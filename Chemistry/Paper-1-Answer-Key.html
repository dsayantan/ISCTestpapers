Read the passage carefully and answer the questions that follow.

Phenol, C6H5OH, was first isolated from coal tar; today most of it is made from cumene (isopropylbenzene). Cumene is oxidised by air to cumene hydroperoxide, which is then decomposed by dilute acid. The process is economical because a second useful compound is obtained along with phenol.

The –OH group activates the benzene ring towards electrophilic substitution, so phenol reacts with bromine water at room temperature, without any Lewis acid, to give a white precipitate. Phenol is also weakly acidic: it dissolves in aqueous NaOH, and electron-withdrawing groups such as –NO2 at the ortho or para position make it a stronger acid. Phenol and its derivatives are used to make dyes, plastics and drugs such as aspirin.

(iii)1 mark

Why is p-nitrophenol a stronger acid than phenol? (Evaluate)

Answer

In p-nitrophenoxide ion the negative charge is delocalised not only over the ring but also on to the oxygen atoms of the –NO2 group (−R and −I effects). The anion is more stabilised than phenoxide ion, so p-nitrophenol loses H+ more readily.

Question 72 marks

Substantiate the following statements with reasons.

(i)1 mark

Mn2+ compounds are more stable than Fe2+ compounds towards oxidation to the +3 state. (Atomic number of Mn = 25 and Fe = 26) (Understand)

Answer

Mn2+ is 3d5, a half-filled and extra-stable configuration; oxidation to Mn3+ (3d4) would destroy it, so the third ionisation enthalpy of Mn is very high. Fe2+ is 3d6: losing one electron gives the stable 3d5 Fe3+, so Fe2+ is easily oxidised.

(ii)1 mark

Zn, Cd and Hg are not regarded as transition elements. (Atomic number of Zn = 30, Cd = 48 and Hg = 80) (Understand)

Answer

Their configuration is (n−1)d10ns2; the d subshell is completely filled both in the atoms and in their common +2 ions. A transition element must have an incompletely filled d subshell in the atom or in a common ion.

S25 Q20(i)(c), B26 Q10(i) (D8) and S24 Q5(ii), S27 Q7(ii) family (D2).
