(ii)1 mark

A dry cell is an example of a __________ cell, whereas a lead storage battery is an example of a __________ cell. (Recall)

Answer

primary, secondary

In a primary cell the cell reaction cannot be reversed and the cell is discarded when the reactants are used up. A secondary cell (lead storage battery, Ni–Cd cell) can be recharged because its reaction is reversed by passing a current in the opposite direction.

(Analyse)

(C)3 marks

Read the passage carefully and answer the questions that follow.

Alcohols and phenols are hydroxy derivatives of hydrocarbons. Ethanol is the most familiar alcohol: it is the active ingredient of hand sanitisers and is blended with petrol as a fuel. The boiling points of alcohols are much higher than those of ethers of comparable molar mass, because alcohol molecules are associated through hydrogen bonds. Aliphatic alcohols are very weak acids, weaker even than water. Phenol is a stronger acid than the alcohols, and the presence of an electron-withdrawing group on the ring increases its acidity further. The following data are taken from a chemistry data book.

CompoundEthanolMethoxymethanePropan-1-olPropan-2-ol
Boiling point (K)351248370356
CompoundEthanolWaterPhenolp-Cresolm-Nitrophenolp-Nitrophenol
pKa15·915·710·010·38·47·2

Alcohols are classified as primary, secondary and tertiary, and the class can be recognised by the rate at which they react with the Lucas reagent.

(i)1 mark

Ethanol and methoxymethane are isomers, yet ethanol boils at 351 K and methoxymethane at only 248 K. Explain. (Understand)

Answer

Ethanol has an O–H group, so its molecules are held together by intermolecular hydrogen bonds; extra energy is needed to break them. Methoxymethane has no hydrogen attached to oxygen: only weak dipole–dipole forces act, so it boils much lower.

(ii)1 mark

Arrange ethanol, phenol and p-nitrophenol in the increasing order of acidic strength and give one reason why p-nitrophenol is more acidic than phenol. (Analyse)

Answer

ethanol < phenol < p-nitrophenol (pKa 15·9 > 10·0 > 7·2). The –NO2 group at the para position withdraws electrons by its −R (and −I) effect and spreads the negative charge of the phenoxide ion onto the nitro group, so the ion is better stabilised and the proton is lost more easily.

(iii)1 mark

Name the reagent that can be used to distinguish propan-1-ol from propan-2-ol and state what is observed with each at room temperature. (Apply)

Answer

Lucas reagent (conc. HCl + anhydrous ZnCl2). Propan-2-ol (secondary) gives turbidity within about five minutes; propan-1-ol (primary) gives no turbidity at room temperature (it appears only on heating).

(CH3)2CHOH + HClanhyd. ZnCl2 (CH3)2CHCl + H2O

Question 72 marks

Substantiate the following statements with reasons.

(i)1 mark

Cr2+ is a reducing agent but Mn3+ is an oxidising agent, although both have the d4 configuration. (Atomic number of Cr = 24, Mn = 25) (Analyse)

Answer

Cr2+ → Cr3+ gives d3 (half-filled t2g, stable), so Cr2+ loses an electron readily and is a reductant. Mn3+ → Mn2+ gives d5 (half-filled, extra stable), so Mn3+ readily gains an electron and is an oxidant.

(ii)1 mark

Zirconium (Z = 40) and hafnium (Z = 72) have very similar properties and are difficult to separate. (Understand)

Answer

Hf follows the lanthanoids, and the poor shielding by the 4f electrons causes the lanthanoid contraction. This cancels the normal increase in size down the group, so Zr and Hf have almost the same atomic and ionic radii (160 and 159 pm), and hence nearly identical chemistry.
