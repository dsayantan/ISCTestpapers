(C)3 marks

Read the passage carefully and answer the questions that follow.

When a solution and pure solvent are separated by a semipermeable membrane, solvent molecules pass into the solution. This is osmosis, and the extra pressure that must be applied to the solution to stop it is the osmotic pressure, π = iCRT. Solutions of equal osmotic pressure are isotonic. The fluid inside red blood cells is isotonic with a 0·9% (w/v) solution of sodium chloride, called normal saline, which is why drugs are injected intravenously in saline. In a more concentrated (hypertonic) solution the cells lose water.

If a pressure larger than the osmotic pressure is applied on the solution side, pure solvent is forced out through the membrane. This is used in coastal cities to obtain drinking water from sea water.

(i)1 mark

What will happen to red blood cells placed in a 2% (w/v) NaCl solution? (Understand)

Answer

2% NaCl is hypertonic: water flows out of the cells, which shrink (crenation).
