(C)3 marks

Read the passage carefully and answer the questions that follow.

Many reactions, including every radioactive decay, follow first-order kinetics: the rate is proportional to the amount of reactant present, and the half-life, t1/2 = 0·693/k, does not depend on the initial amount. Carbon-14 (t1/2 = 5730 years) is made continuously in the upper atmosphere and is taken up by living plants; once the plant dies, no fresh carbon-14 enters and the activity of its remains falls steadily. Comparing the activity of an old sample with that of fresh wood gives its age.

The rate constant of a reaction also depends on temperature, as described by the Arrhenius equation, k = A e−Ea/RT. A graph of log k against 1/T is a straight line from which Ea can be found.

100 → 50 → 25 → 12·5%: three half-lives (12·5% = (1/2)3 is left).

S25 1C(iii) 87.5% from t1/2 (K11).

S25 1C(ii) C-14 dating; S24 Q18(i), B25I Q5 (K4, tier A).

Question 22 marks

On diluting from 0·1 M to 0·001 M, the molar conductivity of KCl increases about 1·1 times, whereas that of acetic acid increases about 9 times. Explain this difference and show it on a graph of Λm against √c. (Analyse)

Answer
KCl is a strong electrolyte, fully ionised at all concentrations; dilution only reduces inter-ionic attraction, so Λm rises only slightly; it falls linearly as √c increases, Λm = Λ°m − A√c (Debye–Hückel–Onsager).1
Acetic acid is a weak electrolyte: its degree of dissociation increases sharply on dilution, so the number of ions per mole rises and Λm increases steeply at low concentration; its Λ°m cannot be found by extrapolation.1
S25 Q2 “1.5 times vs 25 times” → B26 Q17(i)(b) “3 vs 30 times” (E9, in every paper 2023–27).

Why are soda-water bottles sealed under high pressure? (Understand)
