(C)3 marks

Read the passage carefully and answer the questions that follow.

For a reaction to occur, the reacting molecules must collide. According to collision theory, only those collisions in which the molecules have energy equal to or greater than a threshold value and are properly oriented lead to products; these are called effective collisions. The dependence of the rate constant on temperature is given by the Arrhenius equation, k = Ae−Ea/RT, or log k = log A − Ea/2·303RT. A plot of log k against 1/T is therefore a straight line.

A catalyst provides an alternative path of lower activation energy; it increases the rate of the forward and the backward reaction equally.

(i)1 mark

For a reaction, the slope of the plot of log k against 1/T is −5000 K. Calculate the activation energy of the reaction. (Apply)

Answer

Slope = −Ea/2·303R ⇒ Ea = 2·303 × 8·314 × 5000 = 95735 J mol−1 = 95·7 kJ mol−1.

K12 Arrhenius graph: B25I 1C(ii) passage (slope −4621 → Ea); syllabus names the graph.
(ii)1 mark

Why do all collisions between reactant molecules not result in the formation of products? (Understand)

Answer

A collision is effective only if (1) the colliding molecules have energy ≥ the threshold energy (so the activation barrier is crossed) and (2) they are properly oriented; most collisions fail one of these conditions.

(iii)1 mark

How does a catalyst increase the rate of a reaction? Does it change the enthalpy of the reaction? (Analyse)

Answer

It lowers Ea by an alternative path, so a larger fraction of molecules have enough energy (k = Ae−Ea/RT increases). No: ΔH depends only on the initial and final states, which the catalyst does not change.

K13 (S26 Q17(iii), B23 1A(ii), H2425 Q2(ii)).

Question 72 marks

Substantiate the following statements with reasons.

(i)1 mark

Transition elements show variable oxidation states; manganese (Z = 25) shows all states from +2 to +7. (Understand)

Answer

The (n−1)d and ns electrons have very similar energies, so a varying number of them can be lost or shared. Mn is 3d54s2: using 2 to 7 of these electrons gives +2 to +7 (+7 in MnO4−).

(ii)1 mark

Cr2+ is a reducing agent whereas Mn3+ is an oxidising agent, though both are d4. (Atomic number of Cr = 24, Mn = 25) (Analyse)

Cr2+ → Cr3+ (d3, t2g3, half-filled t2g, stable), so Cr2+ loses an electron readily. Mn3+ → Mn2+ (d5, half-filled, extra stable), so Mn3+ gains an electron readily.

Why are haloarenes much less reactive than haloalkanes towards nucleophilic substitution? (Understand)

Resonance gives the C–X bond partial double-bond character (shorter, stronger); the carbon is sp2; the phenyl cation is unstable, and the electron-rich ring repels the nucleophile.

(c)1 mark

Chlorine deactivates the benzene ring, yet chlorobenzene gives ortho and para products on nitration. Explain. (Evaluate)

Answer

The −I effect of Cl lowers the electron density of the whole ring (deactivation), but its +R effect releases electrons specifically to the ortho and para positions, so the electrophile attacks there.
