In Act V of Pygmalion, why does Doolittle blame Higgins for his new prosperity? (Understand)

Answer

Higgins’s joke to the American millionaire Wannafeller led to Doolittle’s legacy. Wealth has made him respectable against his will: he must lecture, marry, support relatives and live by middle-class morality. He feels Higgins has destroyed his carefree freedom.

In Act V of Pygmalion, why is Mrs Higgins angry that Higgins and Pickering have gone to the police? (Understand)

Answer

They have reported Eliza as if she were a lost umbrella or a criminal. Mrs Higgins points out that Eliza is a free woman with every right to leave, and that their conduct shows they never considered her feelings or dignity at all.

The teacher, like the rest of the town, looks down on the Kelveys because their mother is a washerwoman and their father is said to be in prison. Her special voice and smile show a cold, patronising snobbery that the children learn and copy.

In the short story, The Father-Thing, why does Mrs Walton not believe Charles? (Understand)

The thing looks and sounds exactly like her husband, and she has no reason to doubt him. She thinks Charles is being naughty or imagining things. Adults trust surfaces, so the child who sees the truth is disbelieved and left alone with his terror.

It shows his longing for solitude, fresh air and nature, away from the crowded city. Yet he only dreams and never goes, so the dream also reveals his inertia. The city has so conditioned him that his escape remains a fantasy.

PYGMALION: GEORGE BERNARD SHAW

Question 320 marks
(i)5 marks

What part does Freddy Eynsford Hill play in Acts III to V of Pygmalion? What does Eliza see in him?

Incorporate the following details and write your answer in one paragraph of 80 - 100 words. (Understand)

  • His reaction at the ‘at home’
  • His devotion in Act IV and what it offers Eliza
Answer

At the ‘at home’ Freddy is instantly smitten. He finds Eliza’s startling remarks delightful and offers to walk her across the park, earning her famous refusal. Afterwards he haunts Wimpole Street, standing beneath her window at night. When Eliza leaves the house after quarrelling with Higgins, it is Freddy who is there, declaring his love, and they drive about together. He is idle and penniless, but he adores her and treats her gently. For Eliza, wounded by Higgins’s indifference, Freddy offers love, respect and a relationship in which she will lead.

Model answer: 93 words (limit 80–100)

Forecast Tier E 115; specimen 3(iii)(b) (why Freddy) makes him examinable.
(ii)5 marks

Describe Mrs Higgins’s role in Acts III and V of Pygmalion. Why is she important to the play?

Incorporate the following details and write your answer in one paragraph of 80 - 100 words. (Interpret)

  • Her warning in Act III
  • Her handling of Higgins and Eliza in Act V
Answer

Mrs Higgins is the play’s voice of good sense. In Act III she sees through the men’s excitement, calling them babies playing with a live doll, and asks what is to be done with Eliza afterwards. In Act V she shelters Eliza, scolds her son and Pickering for calling the police, and tells Higgins plainly that he has behaved badly. She even arranges for Doolittle to wait out of sight so that Eliza is not shocked. She matters because she judges the experiment from Eliza’s side, speaking for the audience’s conscience.

Model answer: 95 words (limit 80–100)

Forecast Tier B 37.
(iii)10 marks

(a) Higgins claims that he treats a duchess and a flower girl alike. Is this a fair defence of his conduct towards Eliza? Give your opinion with reference to Acts III to V.

Answer should be written in 150 - 200 words. (Evaluate)

Answer

Marking points. Candidates are required to focus on the following areas in their answers:

  • Higgins’s claim in Act V: the same manner for all human souls
  • What it amounts to: equal rudeness, not equal respect
  • Evidence: the at-home, talking about her, the slippers, the police
  • Contrast with Pickering: courtesy that creates self-respect
  • What Eliza asks for: kindness and recognition
  • A reasoned verdict, acknowledging his honesty and his genuine admiration

Model answer

In Act V Higgins argues that the great secret is to have the same manner for all human souls. He has never treated Eliza worse than anyone else, because he treats a duchess exactly as he treats a flower girl.

The claim is honest as far as it goes. Higgins is not a snob in the ordinary sense: at his mother’s ‘at home’ he is as rude to the genteel Eynsford Hills as he ever was to Eliza.

But the defence does not answer her complaint. Equal treatment is worthless if it means equal rudeness. After the reception he talks about her as if she were absent and calls for his slippers; in Act V he has reported her to the police like missing property. He never thanks her or considers her future.

Pickering shows the alternative. His courtesy, calling her Miss Doolittle and standing when she enters, gave Eliza her self-respect. She asks Higgins only for a little kindness, and he cannot give it.

In my opinion, therefore, the defence fails. Higgins is sincere and he genuinely admires the independent woman she becomes, but consistency in bad manners is not respect. It explains his conduct without excusing it.

Model answer: 200 words (limit 150–200)

Forecast Tier B 38.
OR

(b) Why, in your opinion, does Eliza decide to marry Freddy rather than return to Wimpole Street with Higgins?

Answer should be written in 150 - 200 words. (Evaluate)

Answer

Marking points. Candidates should build their answer on the following areas:

  • Eliza’s new self-respect and independence
  • Higgins will not change; he wants a helper, not a partner
  • Wimpole Street means service: slippers and errands
  • Freddy loves her, needs her and treats her kindly
  • She can lead the marriage and earn her own living
  • Shaw’s sequel: marriage to Freddy, the flower shop

Model answer

By Act V Eliza has become a person, and Higgins cannot treat her as one. That is the heart of her decision.

Higgins tells her frankly that he will not change his manners. What he offers is a return to the old arrangement: fetching slippers, remembering appointments and running errands, with her achievement taken for granted. He admires her strength, but as a fellow bachelor might, not as a husband. She asks for kindness, and he offers only his indifferent company.

Freddy offers what Higgins cannot. He loves her devotedly and has haunted the street beneath her window. He is poor and idle, but that means she will be the stronger partner, earning her own living and making her own decisions. With Freddy she will be treated as a lady.

Shaw’s sequel supports this reading. Eliza marries Freddy, they open a flower shop with Pickering’s help, and Shaw explains that she was never likely to marry a man so devoted to his mother and so careless of her needs.

In my view, her choice is sensible and brave: she chooses dignity and affection over dependence on a brilliant but selfish man.

Model answer: 193 words (limit 150–200)

(i)5 marks

Referring to the short story, Death by Scrabble, describe the words that appear on the board and what seems to follow from them.

Incorporate the following details and write your answer in one paragraph of 80 - 100 words. (Interpret)

  • The words played
  • Their apparent consequences
Answer

The narrator opens the game with BEGIN, and as it continues, words such as ZAPS, EXPLODES, FLY and QUAKE appear on the board. Each seems to be followed by an event in the world that matches it. The narrator comes to believe that the game is controlling reality, and he begins to hunt for a word that will kill his wife. The consequences are ironic. His growing obsession feeds his violence, but it is the narrator himself who dies. Whether the words truly have power or he projects meaning onto coincidence is left open.

(iii)10 marks

(a) How does Cyprian turn his aunt’s shopping expedition to his own advantage in the short story, The Dreamer? Discuss the irony and the comic reversal in the story.

Answer should be written in 150 - 200 words. (Analyse)

Answer

Marking points. Candidates are expected to elaborate on the following ideas:

  • Adela’s plan: Cyprian as escort and parcel carrier
  • The dreamy look: poet or house agent; appearance against reality
  • The missing hat: planted detail; passed off as an excuse
  • Mistaken for a shop assistant; the ‘special reduction’; money collected
  • Adela nearly faints; the Canon and two devotional books
  • Irony of the title; power passes from aunt to nephew

Model answer

Adela Chemping takes her young nephew to the winter sales only to use him. He is to be her male escort and carry her parcels, and she assumes that a dreamy boy will be a docile one. Saki warns otherwise: Cyprian’s wondering look belongs to a poet or a house agent.

The hat is the planted detail. Cyprian arrives without one, explaining that a hat is a nuisance when you must raise it to acquaintances with your hands full of parcels. Adela is scandalised, but she misses the real point: a hatless young man in a shop looks like an assistant.

A customer duly mistakes him for one. Instead of correcting her, Cyprian coolly quotes a special reduction and collects her money. His aunt is so shocked that strangers must help her into the fresh air. When she next finds him, he has just sold two books of devotion to an elderly Canon, his dreamy look deeper than ever.

The irony is complete. The boy she meant to exploit exploits the occasion; the ‘dreamer’ is the most practical person in the store. Power passes from aunt to nephew, and Saki’s comic reversal mocks the vanity of adults.

Model answer: 199 words (limit 150–200)

Forecast Tier B 26; specimen 4(i) (Cyprian’s dress) makes his character a prose focus (weight 0.45).

(b) Comment on the significance of the title of the short story, Games at Twilight.

Answer should be written in 150 - 200 words. (Evaluate)

Answer

Marking points. Candidates have to address the following areas in their answers:

  • Games: children’s play taken with deadly seriousness
  • Hide-and-seek as a contest of power; Ravi’s fantasy of victory
  • Twilight: end of the day, fading light, shadows
  • The funeral ring-game at dusk
  • Twilight of innocence: Ravi’s discovery of his insignificance
  • Evaluation: an apt, ironic title

Model answer

The title joins two ideas, and their meeting explains the story.

The ‘games’ are children’s play, but Desai shows how seriously children take them. For Ravi, hide-and-seek is a contest of power. Raghu, the big bully, is ‘It’, and Ravi, hiding in the dark shed, dreams of defeating him and becoming the champion admired by all. The game carries his whole desire to matter.

‘Twilight’ is the time when the game ends and light fades. By the time Ravi emerges, dusk has fallen and the others have forgotten him. They are playing a solemn funeral ring-game, a strange ritual of death at the end of the day.

Twilight is also symbolic. It marks the end of Ravi’s innocent belief that the world will notice and reward him. Crushed by their indifference, he lies silent, aware for the first time of his own insignificance. His childhood hope dies with the daylight, and the funeral game becomes its ironic rite.

In my judgement the title is apt. It captures both the surface event and the inner change: the play of children and the shadow that falls when a child first learns how little he counts.

Model answer: 195 words (limit 150–200)

Forecast Tier D 101; booklet notes ask what the title means.

In the poem, The Black Santa, what does the child ask for, and what does the wish-list reveal about the child’s world?

Incorporate the following details and write your answer in one paragraph of 80 - 100 words. (Interpret)

  • The child’s list and where the ideas come from
  • What it shows about the setting and society
Answer

The child’s wishes are for bright, cheap, heavily advertised goods, such as a record player of gaudy plastic. These are toys the child has seen in television commercials, not things chosen from experience. The list reveals a poor neighbourhood where children are shaped by the same consumer culture as everyone else, while advertising shows only a fat, white, merry Santa. The child’s innocent trust in a thin, shaky Black Santa therefore becomes moving. The poem exposes how commerce and race decide whose Christmas is shown as normal.

(iii)10 marks

(a) In the poem, Ode to Tomatoes, how does the ‘murder’ of the tomato become a ‘wedding’? How does the form of the poem suit its celebration of a humble thing?

Answer should be written in 150 - 200 words. (Analyse)

Answer

Marking points. Candidates are expected to use the following approach to the question:

  • The knife and the ‘murder’: living flesh, red insides, a cool sun
  • Sacrifice turned to nourishment: salads of Chile
  • The wedding: onion, oil, salt, pepper; a festive meal
  • Hyperbole, personification, celestial imagery (star, sun)
  • Form: very short lines, enjambment, a cascade like chopping
  • A modern ode praising the everyday

Model answer

Neruda treats preparing a salad as a drama. Once the tomato has invaded the kitchen, the poem says, it must be murdered. The knife sinks into its living flesh and its red insides are exposed, yet even then it is a cool sun. The violent metaphor is playful: the sacrifice exists to feed people.

The murder at once becomes a wedding. The tomato is joined to the onion, and oil, salt and pepper are poured as if to celebrate the marriage. Throughout Chile the tomato fills the salads, and the meal becomes a communal feast. Death leads to union and nourishment, so an ordinary lunch becomes a ceremony.

The form supports this joy. The ode is written in extremely short lines, some a single word, that tumble down the page through enjambment, like slices falling from a knife. The rapid rhythm feels like chopping and serving.

Other devices add grandeur: hyperbole of streets running with juice, personification of the tomato’s majesty, and celestial images of a star of earth. By giving an everyday vegetable the ceremonial praise once kept for heroes, Neruda honours the simple pleasures of daily life.

Model answer: 190 words (limit 150–200)

Forecast Tier A 10 / Tier D 109; booklet 10(a) and 10(b).

(b) How does Auden use refrain, repetition and contrast in the poem, Refugee Blues, to convey the suffering of the refugees?

Answer should be written in 150 - 200 words. (Analyse)

Answer

Marking points. Candidates are required to focus on the following areas in their answers:

  • Blues form: tercets, AAB rhyme, the refrain in each third line
  • ‘My dear’: intimacy, weariness, quiet despair
  • Repetition of rejection: consul, committee, speaker
  • Contrast: animals admitted and free; refugees shut out
  • Contrast: nature renewed (the yew) against dead passports
  • Climax: the building of a thousand doors; soldiers in the snow

Model answer

Auden borrows the form of the blues, a song of sorrow. Each tercet’s third line works as a refrain addressed to ‘my dear’, a tender phrase that grows heavier as the verses pile up. Its repetition mirrors the monotony of hopeless waiting.

Repetition also shapes the content. Verse after verse brings another door closing: the consul who declares them officially dead, the committee that says come back next year, the speaker who says they will steal the nation’s bread. The pattern makes rejection feel endless and systematic.

Contrast is Auden’s sharpest tool. A city of ten million has no room for two people. The old yew renews itself every spring, but old passports cannot be renewed. A dressed-up poodle and a cat are let in, fish swim freely and birds sing without politicians, while the refugees are hunted. Such contrasts show them valued below animals.

The final contrasts form a climax: a dream building with a thousand doors, none of them theirs, and then ten thousand soldiers searching the snow. The quiet refrain beside such terror makes the suffering both personal and universal.

Model answer: 186 words (limit 150–200)

Forecast Tier A 17; booklet 10(b); specimen 5(iii)(a) (the sorrow of refugees).
