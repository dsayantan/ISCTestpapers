Question 1(A)(i)[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [ethanol, [Fe(H2O)6]2+, anode, d2sp3, Cannizzaro, reducing, cathode, sp3d2, iodine, [Fe(CN)6]4−, methanol, aldol, oxidising, manganese dichloride].
In electrochemical cells, the electrode having negative values of standard reduction potential acts as ______ while the electrode possessing positive values of standard reduction potential acts as ______.
  1. The electrode with the more negative (lower) standard reduction potential is the stronger reducing agent: it loses electrons, i.e. is oxidised, and so is the anode. The electrode with the positive (higher) reduction potential gains electrons, is reduced, and is the cathode.
    [1]
Answer
anode; cathode
Marking note Both words are needed for the mark, in the order asked.

Concepts to recall

  • Anode = oxidation (lower E°); cathode = reduction (higher E°). E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode > 0 for a spontaneous cell.
  • Mnemonic: AN OX, RED CAT; electrons flow from anode to cathode in the external wire.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(ii)
Question 1(A)(ii)[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [ethanol, [Fe(H2O)6]2+, anode, d2sp3, Cannizzaro, reducing, cathode, sp3d2, iodine, [Fe(CN)6]4−, methanol, aldol, oxidising, manganese dichloride].
The coordination compound ______ is an example of inner orbital complex and the hybridisation involved in the complex is ______.
  1. In [Fe(CN)6]4−, Fe2+ is 3d6. CN− is a strong-field ligand, so the six d electrons pair up in three 3d orbitals, leaving two 3d orbitals vacant. Two 3d + one 4s + three 4p orbitals hybridise, giving d2sp3 hybrids: an inner orbital (low-spin, diamagnetic) complex. [Fe(H2O)6]2+ has a weak-field ligand and is outer orbital (sp3d2).
    [1]
Answer
[Fe(CN)6]4−; d2sp3
Marking note The compound and its hybridisation must match: [Fe(CN)6]4− with d2sp3.

Concepts to recall

  • Inner orbital complex: uses (n−1)d orbitals (d2sp3), strong-field ligand (CN−, NH3, en, CO); outer orbital: nd orbitals (sp3d2), weak-field ligand (F−, H2O, Cl−).
  • Spectrochemical series, weakest to strongest: I− < Cl− < F− < H2O < NH3 < en < CN− < CO.

Question 1(A)(iii)[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [ethanol, [Fe(H2O)6]2+, anode, d2sp3, Cannizzaro, reducing, cathode, sp3d2, iodine, [Fe(CN)6]4−, methanol, aldol, oxidising, manganese dichloride].
Formaldehyde lacks α-hydrogen atom and thus forms sodium formate and ______ on reaction with conc. alkali. This reaction is known as ______ reaction.
  1. HCHO has no α-hydrogen, so with concentrated NaOH it undergoes disproportionation, the Cannizzaro reaction: 2HCHO + NaOH → HCOONa + CH3OH (methanol). One molecule is oxidised to sodium formate, the other reduced to methanol.
    [1]
Answer
methanol; Cannizzaro

Concepts to recall

  • Cannizzaro: aldehydes with no α-H (HCHO, C6H5CHO) + conc. alkali → carboxylate salt + alcohol (disproportionation).
  • Aldehydes with α-H (CH3CHO) give the aldol reaction with dilute alkali instead.

SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i)[1]
Ethylamine reacts with nitrous acid at low temperature to form:
(a)C2H5N2+Cl−, H2O
(b)C2H5OH, C2H5NO2
(c)C2H5OH, N2, H2O
(d)C2H5OH, NH3, H2O
  1. Primary aliphatic amines give unstable diazonium salts which decompose at once even at low temperature, giving the alcohol and nitrogen: C2H5NH2 + HNO2 → C2H5OH + N2 + H2O. This is option (c).
    [1]
Answer
(c) C2H5OH, N2, H2O

Concepts to recall

  • Aliphatic 1° amine + HNO2 (NaNO2 + HCl) → ROH + N2 (a stable diazonium salt is NOT formed).
  • Aromatic 1° amine + HNO2 at 273–278 K gives a stable diazonium salt, which is why aromatic and aliphatic amines are distinguished this way.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii)[1]
Which one of the following statements are correct regarding the depression of freezing point?
(P) The depression in freezing point is directly proportional to the amount of solute dissolved in solution.
(Q) The depression in freezing point is inversely proportional to the amount of solute dissolved in solution.
(R) Vapour pressure of the solution is more than that of pure solvent.
(S) Vapour pressure of the solution is less than that of pure solvent.
(a)Only (P) and (S) are correct.
(b)Only (Q) and (R) are correct.
(c)Only (Q) and (S) are correct.
(d)Only (P) and (Q) are correct.
  1. ΔTf = Kfm, so the depression is directly proportional to the amount of solute: (P) is true, (Q) false. A non-volatile solute lowers the vapour pressure of the solvent (Raoult’s law): (S) is true, (R) false. Only (P) and (S) are correct: option (a).
    [1]
Answer
(a) Only (P) and (S) are correct

Concepts to recall

  • ΔTf = Kf × m = Kf × 1000wB/(MBwA): a colligative property, proportional to the number of solute particles.
  • Solute added to a solvent lowers its vapour pressure; the solution therefore meets the solid’s curve at a lower temperature, so it freezes lower.

Question 1(B)(iii)[1]
The amount of bromine required to completely convert 9·4 g of phenol into 2,4,6-tribromophenol is: (Atomic weight of C = 12, H = 1, O = 16 and Br = 80)
(a)24 g
(b)48 g
(c)80 g
(d)96 g
  1. C6H5OH (94 g mol−1) + 3Br2 → C6H2Br3OH + 3HBr. Moles of phenol = 9·4/94 = 0·1 mol.
    [½]
  2. Moles of Br2 = 3 × 0·1 = 0·3 mol; mass = 0·3 × 160 = 48 g: option (b).
    [½]
Answer
(b) 48 g

Concepts to recall

  • Phenol + excess bromine water → 2,4,6-tribromophenol (white ppt): three Br2 per phenol, since –OH activates the ortho and para positions.
  • Br2 = 2 × 80 = 160 g mol−1; the question gives Br = 80.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv)[1]
Which one of the following statements is correct?
(a)All amino acids are optically inactive.
(b)All amino acids are optically active.
(c)All amino acids are optically active except glutamic acid.
(d)All amino acids are optically active except glycine.
  1. The α-carbon of every natural amino acid other than glycine carries four different groups (H, NH2, COOH and R), so it is chiral and the acid is optically active. In glycine R = H, the α-carbon carries two identical H atoms and glycine is optically inactive. Option (d).
    [1]
Answer
(d) All amino acids are optically active except glycine

Concepts to recall

  • A chiral (asymmetric) carbon has four different substituents; glycine, NH2CH2COOH, has two H’s on Cα.
  • Natural α-amino acids belong to the L-series (−NH2 on the left in the Fischer projection).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(v)
Question 1(B)(v)[1]
What type of structural isomerism exists between the following pair of coordination compounds? [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]Cl·2H2O and [Cr(H2O)5Cl]Cl2·H2O
(a)Ionisation isomerism
(b)Hydrate isomerism
(c)Coordination isomerism
(d)Linkage isomerism
  1. Both have the same formula, but the number of water molecules inside the coordination sphere differs: four ligand H2O + 2 Cl in the first, five H2O + 1 Cl in the second, the rest being water of crystallisation. This is hydrate (solvate) isomerism: option (b).
    [1]
Answer
(b) Hydrate isomerism
Marking note Some books call the pair ionisation isomers, since Cl− moves in and out; the exchanged species is water, so hydrate isomerism is the intended answer.

Concepts to recall

  • Hydrate isomers differ in how many water molecules are ligands and how many are lattice water (e.g. violet [Cr(H2O)6]Cl3 and green [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]Cl·2H2O).
  • Ionisation isomers exchange an anion between the sphere and the outside (SO42− / Cl−); here no ionisable group other than Cl− and H2O is exchanged in kind.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vi)
Question 1(B)(vi)[1]
Assertion: The use of phosphorus chlorides is preferred to thionyl chloride in the preparation of alkyl chlorides from alcohol.
Reason: Phosphorus chlorides give pure alkyl chlorides.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Both statements are false. Thionyl chloride is preferred because the by-products SO2 and HCl are gases that escape, leaving a pure alkyl chloride: ROH + SOCl2 → RCl + SO2↑ + HCl↑. Phosphorus chlorides give POCl3 or H3PO3, which must be separated, so the product is not pure. Option (d).
    [1]
Answer
(d) Both Assertion and Reason are false

Concepts to recall

  • ROH + SOCl2 → RCl + SO2 + HCl (Darzens): best method, by-products are gaseous.
  • ROH + PCl5 → RCl + POCl3 + HCl; 3ROH + PCl3 → 3RCl + H3PO3: the product must be separated from the phosphorus compound.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vii)
Question 1(B)(vii)[1]
Assertion: The basic strength of hydroxides of lanthanoids decreases with an increase in their atomic number.
Reason: The size of trivalent cations of lanthanoids increases with an increase in their atomic number.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Assertion is true: because of the lanthanoid contraction the size of Ln3+ decreases from La to Lu, so the Ln–OH bond becomes more covalent and Ln(OH)3 becomes less basic. The Reason is false, since the size of the trivalent ion decreases with atomic number. Option (c).
    [1]
Answer
(c) Assertion is true and Reason is false

Concepts to recall

  • Lanthanoid contraction: Ln3+ radius falls steadily from La3+ (106 pm) to Lu3+ (85 pm), because 4f electrons shield the nucleus poorly.
  • Smaller cation → greater polarising power → more covalent M–OH → weaker base: La(OH)3 is the most basic, Lu(OH)3 the least.

Question 1(C)(i)[1]
Passage. In 1889 Svante Arrhenius proposed that the dependence of the rate constant (k) of a reaction on temperature can be expressed by k = Ae−Ea/RT, the Arrhenius equation. In logarithmic form log10k = log10A − Ea/(2·303RT), which is like y = mx + c. Ea can be found by a graphical method or by the rate-constant method.
The rate of a particular reaction triples when the temperature changes from 30°C to 60°C. Calculate the activation energy (Ea) of the reaction.
  1. T1 = 303 K, T2 = 333 K, k2/k1 = 3. log(k2/k1) = (Ea/2·303R)[(T2 − T1)/(T1T2)]; so Ea = 2·303 × 8·314 × log 3 × (303 × 333/30) = 3·073e+04 J mol−1 ≈ 30·7 kJ mol−1.
    [1]
Answer
Ea ≈ 30·7 kJ mol−1
Marking note Use R = 8·314 J K−1 mol−1 (paper’s data). 30·7 kJ mol−1; 30–31 kJ accepted.

Concepts to recall

  • Two-temperature Arrhenius: log(k2/k1) = (Ea/2·303R)(1/T1 − 1/T2); convert °C to K.
  • “Rate triples” means k2/k1 = 3 (same concentrations); log 3 = 0·4771.

Question 1(C)(iii)[1]
Passage. In 1889 Svante Arrhenius proposed that the dependence of the rate constant (k) of a reaction on temperature can be expressed by k = Ae−Ea/RT, the Arrhenius equation. In logarithmic form log10k = log10A − Ea/(2·303RT), which is like y = mx + c. Ea can be found by a graphical method or by the rate-constant method.
What is the order of reaction whose rate constant (k) has the same unit as the rate of reaction?
  1. Rate has the unit mol L−1 s−1. For order n, k has the unit (mol L−1)1−n s−1; this equals mol L−1 s−1 only when 1 − n = 1, i.e. n = 0: a zero order reaction.
    [1]
Answer
Zero order

Concepts to recall

  • Unit of k: zero order mol L−1 s−1 (same as rate); first order s−1; second order L mol−1 s−1.
  • Rate = k[A]0 = k for a zero order reaction.

Question 2[2]
Sudeshna took her gold plated bangles to a goldsmith for cleaning and electroplating as they had become black due to constant exposure to dust and dirt. Calculate the amount of charge in coulombs needed for plating 1·0 g of gold on the bangles. During electroplating, the following reaction takes place: Au3+ + 3e− → Au (Given: Atomic weight of Au = 197 g mol−1)
  1. Moles of Au to be deposited = 1·0/197 = 0·005076 mol.
    [½]
  2. One mole of Au needs 3 mol of electrons (3F), so charge Q = (1·0/197) × 3 × 96500
    [1]
  3. Q = 1470 C
    [½]
Answer
Q ≈ 1470 C
Marking note About 1470 C is accepted; the unit must be written.

Concepts to recall

  • Faraday: m = (Q/zF) × M, so Q = mzF/M, where z = electrons per ion (3 for Au3+).
  • 1 faraday = 96500 C is the charge of one mole of electrons.

Question 3[2]
An organic compound [A] having molecular formula C3H6O gives a positive iodoform test. On reduction by LiAlH4, compound [A] forms compound [B]. Further, compound [B] reacts with SOCl2 and forms compound [C]. On treatment with alcoholic KOH, compound [C] gives compound [D] which upon ozonolysis yields formaldehyde and acetaldehyde. Identify the compounds [A], [B], [C] and [D].
  1. [A]: C3H6O with a positive iodoform test has a CH3CO– group: propanone, CH3COCH3 (propanal would not respond).
    [½]
  2. [B]: LiAlH4 reduces the ketone: CH3COCH3 → propan-2-ol, CH3CH(OH)CH3.
    [½]
  3. [C]: CH3CH(OH)CH3 + SOCl2 → 2-chloropropane, CH3CHClCH3 + SO2 + HCl.
    [½]
  4. [D]: alc. KOH eliminates HCl: → propene, CH3CH=CH2. Ozonolysis: CH3CH=CH2 → CH3CHO + HCHO, as given.
    [½]
Answer
[A] propanone; [B] propan-2-ol; [C] 2-chloropropane; [D] propene

Concepts to recall

  • Iodoform test: CH3CO– or CH3CH(OH)– group + I2/NaOH → yellow CHI3.
  • Ozonolysis splits C=C and gives two carbonyl compounds; working backwards (HCHO + CH3CHO) fixes the alkene as propene.
  • Alcohol + SOCl2 → alkyl chloride; alkyl halide + alc. KOH → alkene (dehydrohalogenation).

Question 4[2]
Write chemical equations to convert the following: (i) Chlorobenzene to biphenyl (ii) Chloroform to acetylene
  1. (i) Fittig reaction: 2C6H5Cl + 2Na (dry ether) → C6H5–C6H5 + 2NaCl. (Ullmann: 2C6H5Cl + 2Cu, Δ → C6H5–C6H5 + 2CuCl is equally acceptable.)
    [1]
  2. (ii) Heating chloroform with silver powder: 2CHCl3 + 6Ag (Δ) → HC≡CH + 6AgCl.
    [1]
Answer
(i) 2C6H5Cl + 2Na → C6H5–C6H5 + 2NaCl; (ii) 2CHCl3 + 6Ag → C2H2 + 6AgCl
Marking note Balanced equations are needed for the full mark.

Concepts to recall

  • Wurtz–Fittig: Ar–X + R–X + 2Na → Ar–R; Fittig: 2 Ar–X + 2Na → Ar–Ar; Wurtz: 2R–X + 2Na → R–R.
  • Silver (or copper) powder removes halogen from CHCl3 as AgCl, joining two carbons through a C≡C bond.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q5
Question 5[2]
The half-life period for radioactive carbon, C14 is 5730 years. An ancient sample of wood has only 80% of C14 actively left in comparison to a living plant. Estimate the age of the ancient wood sample.
  1. Decay is first order: k = 0·693/t½ = 0·693/5730 = 1·21 × 10-4 yr−1.
    [1]
  2. t = (2·303/k) log([R]0/[R]) = (2·303/1·21 × 10-4) × log(100/80) = 1845 years (about 1850 years).
    [1]
Answer
Age ≈ 1·85e+03 years
Marking note Answers from 1840 to 1850 years are accepted, depending on rounding of log 1·25 and 0·693.

Concepts to recall

  • Radioactive decay is first order: k = 0·693/t½; t = (2·303/k) log(N0/N).
  • The “living plant” value is the initial amount, so 80 % left means N0/N = 100/80.
  • Sense check: 80 % lies between 100 % (0 years) and 50 % (5730 years), so the age is well under one half-life.

Question 6[2]
An aromatic compound [A] on heating with aqueous ammonia forms compound [B]. Upon heating with Br2 and KOH, compound [B] forms compound [C] which has the molecular formula C6H7N. Identify compounds [A], [B] and [C]. Name the reaction involved in conversion of compound [B] to compound [C].
  1. [C] with formula C6H7N is aniline, C6H5NH2.
    [½]
  2. Br2 + KOH converts an amide into an amine with one carbon less (Hofmann bromamide degradation), so [B] is benzamide, C6H5CONH2: C6H5CONH2 + Br2 + 4KOH → C6H5NH2 + K2CO3 + 2KBr + 2H2O.
    [1]
  3. [A] is benzoic acid, C6H5COOH: C6H5COOH + NH3 → C6H5COO−NH4+ →(Δ) C6H5CONH2 + H2O. The B → C step is the Hofmann bromamide (degradation) reaction.
    [½]
Answer
[A] benzoic acid; [B] benzamide; [C] aniline; Hofmann bromamide reaction
Marking note Benzoyl chloride (C6H5COCl) as [A] also gives benzamide with ammonia, but “on heating with aqueous ammonia” points to the acid; benzoic acid is the expected answer, and benzoyl chloride is not penalised.

Concepts to recall

  • Hofmann bromamide: RCONH2 + Br2 + 4NaOH → RNH2 + Na2CO3 + 2NaBr + 2H2O; the amine has one carbon fewer.
  • Carboxylic acid (or acid chloride, ester) + NH3 → ammonium salt → on heating the amide.
  • C6H7N = C6H5NH2 (unsaturation: benzene ring + one N).
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q7
Question 7[2]
Neha and Imran were given an assignment on the coordination complex [Co(NH3)6]Cl3. Neha found that the complex was of sp3d2 hybridisation and paramagnetic whereas Imran found that it was of d2sp3 hybridisation and diamagnetic. Evaluate the responses of Neha and Imran.
  1. In [Co(NH3)6]3+, Co is +3: 3d64s0. NH3 is a strong-field ligand, so the six 3d electrons pair up in three orbitals, leaving two 3d orbitals empty.
    [½]
  2. Two 3d, the 4s and three 4p orbitals hybridise, giving six d2sp3 hybrid orbitals (octahedral, inner orbital) that accept six NH3 lone pairs.
    [½]
  3. There are no unpaired electrons, so the complex is diamagnetic (μ = 0).
    [½]
  4. Imran is correct. Neha is wrong: sp3d2 and paramagnetism belong to a weak-field complex such as [CoF6]3−.
    [½]
Answer
Imran is correct (d2sp3, diamagnetic); Neha is incorrect

Concepts to recall

  • Strong-field ligand → pairing → low spin → (n−1)d orbitals used → d2sp3, inner orbital. Weak-field ligand → sp3d2, outer orbital.
  • Spin-only magnetic moment μ = √(n(n+2)) BM; n = 0 here.
  • Co3+: [Ar]3d6 (Co is [Ar]3d74s2; three electrons are lost).
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q8
Question 8[2]
(i) Identify the compounds [A] and [B]: (a) CH3CHO →(K2Cr2O7 + H2SO4, [O]) CH3COOH →(Ca(OH)2) [A] →(Δ, dry distillation) [B]; (b) CH3COCH3 →(K2Cr2O7 + H2SO4, [O]) [A] →(PCl5) CH3COCl →(H2, Pd/BaSO4, boiling xylene) [B].
OR
(ii) An aromatic organic compound [A] with the molecular formula C7H6O reduces Tollen’s reagent and undergoes Cannizzaro reaction. On vigorous oxidation, it gives compound [B] which has the molecular formula C7H6O2. On reaction with sodium bi-carbonate, compound [B] forms brisk effervescence. Identify the compounds [A] and [B]. Write the chemical reaction of compound [B] with sodium bi-carbonate.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) CH3COOH + Ca(OH)2 → (CH3COO)2Ca (calcium acetate) + 2H2O: [A] = calcium acetate.
    [½]
  2. Dry distillation: (CH3COO)2Ca → CH3COCH3 + CaCO3: [B] = propanone (acetone).
    [½]
  3. (b) Vigorous oxidation of a ketone breaks the C–C bond next to C=O; acetone gives ethanoic acid (and CO2 + H2O): [A] = CH3COOH. PCl5 converts it to CH3COCl.
    [½]
  4. Rosenmund reduction of acetyl chloride: CH3COCl + H2 (Pd/BaSO4) → [B] = CH3CHO (ethanal) + HCl.

Concepts to recall

  • Raoult: p1 = x1p°1; for a non-volatile solute, (p° − ps)/p° = n2/(n1 + n2).
  • Ideal: ΔmixH = ΔmixV = 0. Positive deviation: vapour pressure above Raoult line, ΔmixH > 0; negative: below, ΔmixH < 0.
  • Boiling point is where vapour pressure equals the external pressure: lower b.p. = higher vapour pressure at any given temperature.
  • Colligative formulas: ΔTb = Kbm; ΔTf = Kfm; π = CRT.
