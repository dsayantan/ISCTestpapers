SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i)[1]
Fill in the blank by choosing the appropriate word(s) from the list given with Question 1(A) in the paper [+2, ethane, tetrahedral, square planar, zero, dry cell, nickel-cadmium cell, propane, Wolff-Kishner, Stephen, completely filled, incompletely filled, paramagnetic, diamagnetic]:
(i) ____________ is an example of a primary cell but ____________ is an example of a secondary cell.
  1. A primary cell cannot be recharged: the dry cell (Leclanché cell) is the example.
    [½]
  2. A secondary cell can be recharged by passing current in the reverse direction: the nickel-cadmium cell (like the lead storage battery).
    [½]
Answer
dry cell; nickel-cadmium cell

Concepts to recall

  • Primary cell: reaction is irreversible, cell is discarded when the reactants are used up (dry cell, mercury cell).
  • Secondary cell: reaction can be reversed by an external source, so it is rechargeable (lead storage cell, Ni–Cd cell).
  • Ni–Cd: Cd + 2OH− → Cd(OH)2 + 2e− during discharge.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(ii)
Question 1(A)(ii)[1]
The complex compound [Ni(CO)4] is ____________ in shape and nickel is in ____________ oxidation state in this complex compound.
  1. CO is a neutral ligand, so the oxidation number of Ni = 0 (charge on complex 0 = Ni + 4 × 0) → zero.
    [½]
  2. Ni(0) is 3d84s2, rearranged to 3d10; with four CO ligands the hybridisation is sp3, so the shape is tetrahedral (diamagnetic).
    [½]
Answer
tetrahedral; zero

Concepts to recall

  • Carbonyl ligands are neutral: O.N. of the metal = charge on the complex.
  • Four-coordinate sp3 gives tetrahedral; four-coordinate dsp2 (e.g. [Ni(CN)4]2−) gives square planar.
  • CO is a strong-field ligand: Ni(0) pairs its electrons as d10 so [Ni(CO)4] is diamagnetic.

Question 1(A)(iii)[1]
When acetaldehyde is treated with hydrazine and KOH in a high boiling solvent glycol, ____________ is formed and the reaction is known as ____________ reduction.
  1. CH3CHO + NH2NH2 → CH3CH=NNH2 (hydrazone), which on heating with KOH in ethylene glycol loses N2 to give ethane, CH3CH3.
    [½]
  2. C=O → CH2 by hydrazine/KOH in glycol is the Wolff–Kishner reduction.
    [½]
Answer
ethane; Wolff–Kishner

Concepts to recall

  • Wolff–Kishner: >C=O → >CH2 with NH2NH2, KOH/glycol, heat (basic medium).
  • Clemmensen: the same conversion with Zn–Hg/conc. HCl (acidic medium); Stephen: nitrile → aldehyde with SnCl2/HCl.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(iv)
Question 1(A)(iv)[1]
The transition metal ions having ____________ d-orbitals are colourless and ____________ in nature.
  1. Colour of transition-metal ions comes from d–d transitions, which need an incompletely filled d subshell (and an empty d orbital to jump into). Ions with completely filled d orbitals (d10, e.g. Zn2+, Cu+) are colourless.
    [½]
  2. They have no unpaired electrons, so they are diamagnetic in nature.
    [½]
Answer
completely filled; diamagnetic
Marking note Of the two options on the list, only “completely filled” fits; d0 ions are also colourless but are not among the choices.

Concepts to recall

  • d–d transition: an electron in a lower d level absorbs visible light and moves to a higher d level; colour seen is the complement of the light absorbed.
  • Zn2+ (3d10), Sc3+ (3d0), Ti4+ (3d0): no d–d transition, colourless; unpaired electrons → paramagnetic, none → diamagnetic.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i)[1]
Which one of the following can produce the foul smelling compound methyl isocyanide in presence of alcoholic KOH?
(a)Chloroform and aniline
(b)Chloroform and methanol
(c)Chloroform and dimethyl amine
(d)Chloroform and methyl amine
  1. Carbylamine reaction: a primary amine + CHCl3 + 3KOH (alc.) → isocyanide. CH3NH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH → CH3NC + 3KCl + 3H2O.
    [½]
  2. Methyl isocyanide has the methyl group on N, so the amine must be methylamine. Aniline gives phenyl isocyanide; dimethylamine is secondary and does not react.
    [½]
Answer
(d) Chloroform and methylamine

Concepts to recall

  • Carbylamine test is given only by primary amines (aliphatic and aromatic): R–NH2 → R–NC (offensive smell).
  • The R group of the isocyanide is the R of the amine; the intermediate is dichlorocarbene :CCl2.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii)[1]
The osmotic pressure of a solution: (P) increases with an increase in number of moles of solute; (Q) decreases with an increase in number of moles of solute; (R) increases at a higher temperature; (S) is dependent on the nature of solute. Which one of the following is correct?
(a)Only (P) and (Q) are correct
(b)Only (P) and (R) are correct
(c)Only (P) and (S) are correct
(d)Only (Q) and (S) are correct
  1. π = CRT = (n/V)RT: π ∝ n (P is true, Q is false) and π ∝ T (R is true).
    [½]
  2. Osmotic pressure is a colligative property: it depends on the number of solute particles, not on their nature (S is false).
    [½]
Answer
(b) Only (P) and (R) are correct

Concepts to recall

  • π = iCRT; for a non-electrolyte i = 1. It rises with concentration and with temperature.
  • Colligative properties depend on the number of particles only, not on what the particles are.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii)[1]
Which one of the following alcohols is the strongest acid?
(a)Phenol
(b)Methanol
(c)Ethanol
(d)t-butyl alcohol
  1. The phenoxide ion C6H5O− is stabilised by resonance (delocalisation of the negative charge into the ring), so phenol (pKa ≈ 10) is far more acidic than any aliphatic alcohol (pKa 15–18).
    [½]
  2. In the aliphatic alcohols alkyl groups (+I) destabilise RO−: acidity CH3OH > C2H5OH > (CH3)3COH, all weaker than phenol.
    [½]
Answer
(a) Phenol

Concepts to recall

  • Acid strength = stability of the conjugate base. Resonance stabilisation of phenoxide beats the +I effect of alkyl groups.
  • Order: phenol > methanol > ethanol > 2° alcohols > 3° alcohols.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv)[1]
Which one of the following does NOT form a silver mirror on heating with Tollen’s reagent?
(a)Glucose
(b)Fructose
(c)Sucrose
(d)Lactose
  1. A sugar reduces Tollens’ reagent only if it has a free (hemiacetal) aldehyde/anomeric –OH. Glucose has a free CHO; fructose isomerises to glucose and mannose in the basic reagent (an α-hydroxy ketone, through an enediol); lactose has a free hemiacetal group in its glucose unit.
    [½]
  2. In sucrose the glycosidic link joins C-1 of glucose to C-2 of fructose, so both anomeric carbons are tied up: a non-reducing sugar, no silver mirror.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Sucrose

Concepts to recall

  • Reducing sugars (free anomeric carbon): glucose, fructose, maltose, lactose. Non-reducing: sucrose.
  • Sucrose = α-D-glucose (C-1) –O– β-D-fructose (C-2); hydrolysis gives invert sugar (glucose + fructose).

Question 1(B)(v)[1]
The coordination number and the oxidation state of the central metal ‘D’ in the complex [D(en)2(H2O)2]Cl3 are:
(a)6 and 2 respectively
(b)4 and 2 respectively
(c)3 and 6 respectively
(d)6 and 3 respectively
  1. en is bidentate: coordination number = 2 × 2 (en) + 2 (H2O) = 6.
    [½]
  2. Three Cl− outside the sphere, so the complex ion is [D(en)2(H2O)2]3+; en and H2O are neutral, so the oxidation state of D = +3.
    [½]
Answer
(d) 6 and 3 respectively

Concepts to recall

  • Coordination number = number of donor atoms attached: monodentate counts 1, bidentate (en, ox2−) counts 2.
  • O.N. of metal = charge on the complex ion − sum of charges on ligands; the counter-ions give the charge of the complex ion.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vi)
Question 1(B)(vi)[1]
Assertion: The process of halogenation of benzene takes place in the presence of anhydrous FeCl3.
Reason: Anhydrous FeCl3 prepares nucleophile to attack the benzene ring.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Assertion is true: benzene is chlorinated by Cl2 in the presence of anhydrous FeCl3 (a Lewis acid catalyst).
    [½]
  2. Reason is false: FeCl3 polarises Cl–Cl to generate the electrophile Cl+ (FeCl4− carries the other end); the ring, which is electron rich, acts as the nucleophile.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Assertion is true and Reason is false.

Concepts to recall

  • Electrophilic substitution: Lewis acid (FeCl3, AlCl3) makes an electrophile: Cl2 + FeCl3 → Cl+ + FeCl4−.
  • Benzene is a π-electron-rich nucleophile; it is attacked by electrophiles, not nucleophiles.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vii)
Question 1(B)(vii)[1]
Assertion: The Zr–Hf pair of elements has the same value of atomic radii though Zr and Hf are placed in different periods in the periodic table.
Reason: The lanthanoid contraction prevents the expected increase in atomic radii of Hf.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Assertion is true: Zr (160 pm) and Hf (159 pm) have almost identical radii, although Hf is in the 6th period.
    [½]
  2. Reason is true and explains it: the 4f electrons between La and Hf shield the nuclear charge poorly, so the steady contraction of the lanthanoids cancels the normal increase in size down the group.
    [½]
Answer
(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.

Concepts to recall

  • Lanthanoid contraction: gradual decrease in atomic and ionic radii from La to Lu, because of poor shielding by 4f electrons.
  • Consequences: Zr and Hf (also Nb–Ta, Mo–W) have similar radii and properties and are very hard to separate; 5d elements have much higher densities and ionisation energies than expected.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(C)(i)
Question 1(C)(i)[1]
Passage. The rate of a reaction depends on the concentration of reactants. The rate law for a hypothetical reaction aA + bB → cC + dD is rate = k[A]x[B]y, where x and y are calculated experimentally and known as order of reaction. In most cases, the mechanism of a reaction is not straightforward but is broken down into simple elementary steps. These steps represent the progress of the overall reaction at the molecular level.
(i) What will be the order of reaction if the unit of k is mol−2 L2 s−1?
  1. Unit of k = (mol L−1)1−n s−1. Here mol−2 L2 = (mol L−1)−2, so 1 − n = −2 and n = 3.
    [½]
  2. The reaction is of third order.
    [½]
Answer
Order = 3 (third order)

Concepts to recall

  • Units of k: zero order mol L−1 s−1; first order s−1; second order L mol−1 s−1; third order L2 mol−2 s−1.
  • General: unit of k = (mol L−1)1−n s−1.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(C)(ii)
Question 1(C)(ii)[1]
(ii) State any one difference between order of reaction and molecularity of reaction.
  1. Order is determined experimentally (from the rate law); molecularity is a theoretical quantity, the number of reacting species in an elementary step.
    [1]
Answer
Order: experimental, can be zero, fractional or whole. Molecularity: theoretical, always a whole number (1, 2, 3), never zero.
Marking note Any one correct difference earns the mark (e.g. “order can be fractional, molecularity cannot”).

Concepts to recall

  • Order = sum of the powers of concentrations in the experimentally found rate law; it applies to the overall reaction.
  • Molecularity applies only to an elementary step; a complex reaction has no single molecularity.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(C)(iii)
Question 1(C)(iii)[1]
(iii) For the reaction 2A → B + C, the rate law is rate = k[A]3/2. What is the order and molecularity of the reaction?
  1. Order = the power of [A] in the rate law = 3/2 = 1·5.
    [½]
  2. Molecularity (from the equation 2A, two molecules of A) = 2; a fractional order shows the reaction is not elementary, so strictly the molecularity is meaningful only for its elementary steps.
    [½]
Answer
Order = 3/2 (1·5); molecularity = 2 (from the equation as written).
Common slip Writing the order as 2 from the coefficient of A: order comes only from the rate law.
Marking note The expected answer is order 3/2 and molecularity 2. A candidate who writes “molecularity not defined for a complex reaction” has also stated a correct fact.

Concepts to recall

  • Fractional order ⇒ complex (multi-step) reaction; the rate law cannot be read from the equation.
  • Molecularity of an elementary step is a small whole number: unimolecular, bimolecular, termolecular.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q2
Question 2[2]
Calculate the number of coulombs required to electroplate 4·75 g of aluminium when the electrode reaction is Al3+ + 3e− → Al. (Given: atomic weight of Al = 27 g mol−1, 1 Faraday = 96,500 coulombs)
  1. Moles of Al deposited = 4·75/27 = 0·1759 mol
    [½]
  2. From Al3+ + 3e− → Al, 3 mol of electrons (3 F) are needed per mol of Al: charge per mol = 3 × 96500 = 2·895 × 105 C
    [1]
  3. Charge required = 0·1759 × 2·895 × 105 = 5·09 × 104 C
    [½]
Answer
Q = 5·09 × 104 C (about 50 900 C)

Concepts to recall

  • Faraday’s first law: m = ZQ; mol of substance × z = mol of electrons; Q = nzF.
  • Equivalent route: 27 g needs 3F, so 4·75 g needs (4·75/27) × 3 F.
  • 1 F = 96500 C is the charge on one mole of electrons.

Question 3[2]
An organic compound [A] has molecular formula C7H6O2. When compound [A] is treated with SOCl2, it yields compound [B]. On heating with NH3, compound [B] forms compound [C]. Compound [C] forms compound [D] on reaction with Br2/KOH. Compound [D] responds to carbylamine test. Identify compounds [A], [B], [C] and [D].
  1. [A] reacts with SOCl2 (converts –COOH to –COCl) and C7H6O2 has a benzene ring plus one C=O/OH: [A] = benzoic acid, C6H5COOH.
    [½]
  2. C6H5COOH + SOCl2 → C6H5COCl + SO2 + HCl: [B] = benzoyl chloride.
    [½]
  3. C6H5COCl + 2NH3 → C6H5CONH2 + NH4Cl: [C] = benzamide.
    [½]
  4. C6H5CONH2 + Br2 + 4KOH → C6H5NH2 + K2CO3 + 2KBr + 2H2O (Hofmann bromamide): [D] = aniline, a primary amine, so it gives the carbylamine test.
    [½]
Answer
[A] benzoic acid; [B] benzoyl chloride; [C] benzamide; [D] aniline.

Concepts to recall

  • SOCl2 is the best reagent for RCOOH → RCOCl (by-products SO2 and HCl escape).
  • Hofmann bromamide degradation: RCONH2 + Br2 + 4KOH → RNH2 + K2CO3 + 2KBr + 2H2O; the product has one carbon fewer.
  • Carbylamine test (CHCl3 + alc. KOH) identifies a primary amine.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q4
Question 4[2]
In the reaction 2NO + O2 → 2NO2, the rate law is rate = k[NO][O2]2.
(i) How will the rate of reaction change if [NO] concentration is doubled and [O2] concentration is halved at the same time?
(ii) Write the order of reaction if [NO] concentration is in large excess.
  1. (i) Initial rate r1 = k[NO][O2]2.
    [½]
  2. New rate r2 = k(2[NO])(½[O2])2 = k[NO][O2]2 × 2 × ¼ = 0·5 r1
    [½]
  3. The rate becomes half of its initial value (decreases by 50%).
    [½]
  4. (ii) With [NO] in large excess its concentration stays effectively constant, so rate = k′[O2]2 with k′ = k[NO]: second order (pseudo-second order).
    [½]
Answer
(i) rate becomes 1/2 of the original; (ii) order = 2 (pseudo second order).

Concepts to recall

  • Rate law: substitute the changed concentrations, keep the powers: factor = 21 × (1/2)2.
  • Pseudo-order: a reactant in large excess has an almost constant concentration, so it is absorbed into the rate constant.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q5
Question 5[2]
When an organic compound [A] having molecular formula C4H9Br is treated with aqueous KOH, the rate of reaction depends on concentration of compound [A] only. But when compound [B], with the same molecular formula, reacts with aqueous KOH, the rate of reaction depends on the concentration of compound [B] as well as of KOH. Compound [B] is a structural isomer of compound [A]. Identify compounds [A] and [B].
  1. Rate depends on [A] only: unimolecular, SN1, which is fastest for a tertiary halide (stable carbocation (CH3)3C+): [A] = tert-butyl bromide, (CH3)3CBr (2-bromo-2-methylpropane).
    [1]
  2. Rate depends on [B] and [OH−]: bimolecular, SN2, typical of a primary halide: [B] = n-butyl bromide, CH3CH2CH2CH2Br (1-bromobutane).
    [1]
Answer
[A] (CH3)3CBr; [B] CH3CH2CH2CH2Br
Marking note 1-bromo-2-methylpropane is also accepted for [B] (primary, SN2). 2-Bromobutane is secondary and shows mixed order, so it does not fit either description.

Concepts to recall

  • SN1: rate = k[RX], two steps via a carbocation, 3° > 2° > 1°. SN2: rate = k[RX][Nu], one step with inversion, 1° > 2° > 3°.
  • Four isomers of C4H9Br: 1-bromobutane, 1-bromo-2-methylpropane (both 1°), 2-bromobutane (2°), 2-bromo-2-methylpropane (3°).

Question 7[2]
While conducting an experiment on coordination compounds, Shirin observed a white precipitate when AgNO3 solution was added to the aqueous solution of the complex [Co(NH3)3(en)SO4]Cl. How can the ionisation isomer of this complex be detected by a chemical test? Write the structure and the IUPAC name of the ionisation isomer.
  1. The given complex has Cl− outside the coordination sphere (it gives AgCl, white, with AgNO3) and SO42− inside. Its ionisation isomer exchanges the two: [Co(NH3)3(en)Cl]SO4 (structure of the complex ion in the figure).
    [½]
  2. IUPAC name: triamminechlorido(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(III) sulphate.
    [½]
  3. Test: add BaCl2 solution: the isomer gives a white precipitate of BaSO4 (SO42− is free) and no precipitate of AgCl with AgNO3, whereas the original complex gives AgCl but no BaSO4. Ba2+ + SO42− → BaSO4↓.
    [1]
Answer
Ionisation isomer: [Co(NH3)3(en)Cl]SO4, triamminechlorido(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(III) sulphate; it gives white BaSO4 with BaCl2 and no AgCl with AgNO3.
Marking note The figure shows one geometrical arrangement of the ligands; the paper does not specify one and any octahedral arrangement with correct ligands earns the structure mark.

  • Ionisation isomers give different ions in solution: the ligand inside the sphere is not free to precipitate.
  • Oxidation state of Co here: +3 (NH3, en neutral; SO42− inside, Cl− outside).
  • IUPAC order of ligands is alphabetical: ammine, chlorido, ethane-1,2-diamine; metal oxidation state in Roman numerals.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q8
Question 8[2]
(i) Arrange the following in the increasing order of their acidic strength: CH3CH2OH, CF3CH2OH, CCl3CH2OH.
(ii) Arrange the following in the increasing order of their boiling points: CH3CH2OH, CH2(OH)CH2OH, CH3CH2Cl.
  1. (i) Electron-withdrawing halogens (−I effect) stabilise the alkoxide, so the halogenated alcohols are more acidic than ethanol (which has the +I effect of CH3). F is more electronegative than Cl, so CF3 withdraws more strongly.
    [½]
  2. Increasing acidity: CH3CH2OH < CCl3CH2OH < CF3CH2OH.
    [½]
  3. (ii) CH3CH2Cl has only weak dipole–dipole forces and no hydrogen bonding; ethanol has one –OH, hence hydrogen bonding; ethane-1,2-diol has two –OH groups, so extensive hydrogen bonding.
    [½]
  4. Increasing boiling point: CH3CH2Cl (285 K) < CH3CH2OH (351 K) < CH2(OH)CH2OH (470 K).
    [½]
Answer
(i) CH3CH2OH < CCl3CH2OH < CF3CH2OH; (ii) CH3CH2Cl < CH3CH2OH < CH2(OH)CH2OH
Marking note The marking follows the −I (electronegativity) argument. Measured pKa values of CF3CH2OH (about 12·4) and CCl3CH2OH (about 12·2) are almost equal, but the expected answer at this level is CF3 the more acidic.

Concepts to recall

  • Acidity of an alcohol rises with electron-withdrawing groups near O–H (−I) and falls with alkyl groups (+I).
  • Boiling point: number of hydrogen bonds per molecule and molar mass: diol > alcohol > haloalkane of similar size.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q9
Question 9[2]
(i) Identify the compounds [A], [B], [C] and [D] in the following reaction:
CH3COOH + PCl5 → [A] → [B] → [C] → [D]
[A] → [B]: H2, Pd/BaSO4, boiling xylene; [B] → [C]: K2Cr2O7 + H2SO4, [O]; [C] → [D]: Ca(OH)2, heat.
OR
(ii) Write chemical equations to convert the following: (a) Benzaldehyde to benzene (b) Benzoic acid to benzaldehyde

Solution to the first alternative

  1. CH3COOH + PCl5 → CH3COCl + POCl3 + HCl: [A] = ethanoyl (acetyl) chloride.
    [½]
  2. Rosenmund reduction (H2, Pd/BaSO4, boiling xylene): [B] = ethanal, CH3CHO.
    [½]
  3. Oxidation of the aldehyde with acidified K2Cr2O7: [C] = ethanoic acid, CH3COOH.
    [½]
  4. 2CH3COOH + Ca(OH)2 → (CH3COO)2Ca + 2H2O; on heating (dry distillation) (CH3COO)2Ca → CH3COCH3 + CaCO3: [D] = propanone (acetone).

  5. (c) By Henry’s law the solubility of a gas in a liquid rises with pressure. CO2 is dissolved in soda water under high pressure; on opening the bottle the pressure falls and the gas escapes (fizzing).
    [1]
Answer
(a) p = 32·44 mm Hg; (b) M2 = 53·1 g mol−1; (c) solubility of CO2 increases with pressure (Henry’s law).
Marking note Using the mole-fraction form, the lowering is 32·8 × 0·01099 = 0·36 mm Hg, so the answer is 32·44 mm Hg (accept 32·4 mm Hg).

Concepts to recall

  • π = iCRT. Hypertonic (cells lose water, shrink), hypotonic (cells swell, may burst), isotonic (no net flow; 0·9% NaCl solution is isotonic with blood).
  • van’t Hoff factor for Na2SO4 is 3, for NaCl 2, for K4[Fe(CN)6] 5 (full dissociation).
  • Reverse osmosis: applied P > π; used for desalination and water purification.
  • Raoult’s law for a non-volatile solute: (p° − p)/p° = xsolute.
  • Elevation of boiling point: ΔTb = Kbm = Kb × (w2/M2) × (1000/w1).
  • Henry’s law: p = KHx; higher pressure, more dissolved gas (soda water, scuba divers, bends).
