Question 1(A)(i)[1]
Fill in the blank(s) by choosing from: [first, alc. AgCN, primary, decrease, third, increase, zero, Lucas, alc. KCN, KOH, no change, two, Tollen’s, AgOH, tertiary]
A mixture of anhydrous ZnCl2 and conc. HCl is called ________ reagent which shows maximum reactivity with ________ alcohol.
  1. The mixture is Lucas reagent.
    [½]
  2. It reacts fastest (turbidity at once, by the SN1 route through the most stable carbocation) with a tertiary alcohol.
    [½]
Answer
Lucas; tertiary

Concepts to recall

  • Lucas test: turbidity (alkyl chloride is insoluble) appears immediately with 3° alcohols, in about 5 minutes with 2° alcohols, and only on heating with 1° alcohols.
  • Reactivity order follows carbocation stability: 3° > 2° > 1°.

Question 1(A)(ii)[1]
Fill in the blank(s) by choosing from: [first, alc. AgCN, primary, decrease, third, increase, zero, Lucas, alc. KCN, KOH, no change, two, Tollen’s, AgOH, tertiary]
Methyl chloride on treatment with ________ forms methyl cyanide, whereas on treatment with ________ it forms methyl isocyanide.
  1. Ionic KCN is largely dissociated and the free C end of CN− attacks carbon: CH3Cl + KCN → CH3CN + KCl. Answer: alc. KCN.
    [½]
  2. Covalent AgCN keeps the C–Ag bond, so the nucleophile attacks through the N lone pair: CH3Cl + AgCN → CH3NC + AgCl. Answer: alc. AgCN.
    [½]
Answer
alc. KCN; alc. AgCN

Concepts to recall

  • CN− is an ambident nucleophile (C or N can attack): KCN gives the cyanide (RCN), AgCN the isocyanide (RNC).
  • Ionic KCN → attack by carbon; covalent AgCN → attack by nitrogen.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(iii)
Question 1(A)(iii)[1]
Fill in the blank(s) by choosing from: [first, alc. AgCN, primary, decrease, third, increase, zero, Lucas, alc. KCN, KOH, no change, two, Tollen’s, AgOH, tertiary]
The hydrolysis of ethyl acetate in acidic medium is a pseudo ________ order reaction. However, the molecularity of the reaction is ________ .
  1. CH3COOC2H5 + H2O → CH3COOH + C2H5OH: water is in large excess so its concentration is practically constant; rate = k[ester] and the reaction is pseudo first order.
    [½]
  2. Two molecules (ester and water) collide in the elementary step, so the molecularity is two.
    [½]
Answer
first; two

Concepts to recall

  • Pseudo first order: a higher-order reaction that behaves as first order because one reactant is in large excess (hydrolysis of an ester, inversion of cane sugar).
  • Order is found by experiment; molecularity is the number of species colliding in an elementary step, and is always a whole number.

Fill in the blank(s) by choosing from: [first, alc. AgCN, primary, decrease, third, increase, zero, Lucas, alc. KCN, KOH, no change, two, Tollen’s, AgOH, tertiary]
Dissociation of solute particles leads to a / an ________ in the magnitude of a colligative property and a / an ________ in the value of the observed molecular mass.
  1. Dissociation increases the number of particles, so a colligative property (ΔTf, π, …) shows an increase.
    [½]
  2. Observed molar mass ∝ 1/(colligative property), so it shows a decrease (van’t Hoff factor i > 1).
    [½]
Answer
increase; decrease

Concepts to recall

  • Colligative properties depend on the number of solute particles: i = (observed colligative property)/(calculated) = (normal molar mass)/(observed molar mass).
  • Dissociation: i > 1; association: i < 1.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i)[1]
Which one of the following is a tetrahedral complex as well as diamagnetic in nature?
(a)[Ni(CN)4]2−
(b)[NiCl4]2−
(c)[Cu(NH3)4]2+
(d)[Ni(CO)4]
  1. [Ni(CN)4]2−: Ni2+ (3d8) with strong-field CN− pairs its electrons, dsp2: square planar, diamagnetic. [NiCl4]2−: sp3, tetrahedral but paramagnetic (two unpaired electrons). [Cu(NH3)4]2+: Cu2+ (3d9) has one unpaired electron.
    [½]
  2. [Ni(CO)4]: Ni(0) is 3d10 (4s electrons pushed into 3d by strong-field CO), all paired; sp3 hybridisation gives a tetrahedral, diamagnetic complex.
    [½]
Answer
(d) [Ni(CO)4]

Concepts to recall

  • Tetrahedral complexes are sp3; Ni(0) with CO (carbonyl, strong field) is 3d10, hence diamagnetic.
  • [Ni(CN)4]2− is the stock example of a square planar low-spin d8 complex; [NiCl4]2− the stock tetrahedral paramagnetic one.
  • Magnetic moment μ = √(n(n + 2)) BM: diamagnetic means n = 0.

When H2S gas is passed through acidified K2Cr2O7 solution, the colour of the solution changes to:
(a)green.

(a) green.

  • Half-reaction: Cr2O72− + 14H+ + 6e− → 2Cr3+ + 7H2O.

Question 1(B)(iv)[1]
Rohit is studying the iodoform reaction using (P) CH3OH, (Q) CH3CHO, (R) CH3COCH3, (S) CH3CH2COCH2CH3. Which combination will give the iodoform test when heated separately with iodine and aqueous NaOH?
(a)Only (P) and (Q)
(b)Only (Q) and (R)
(c)Only (P) and (S)
(d)Only (Q) and (S)
  1. The test needs a CH3CO– group (or CH3CH(OH)–): (Q) ethanal and (R) propanone have it.
    [½]
  2. (P) methanol has no CH3CO– or CH3CH(OH)– unit; (S) pentan-3-one has CH3CH2CO–, not CH3CO–. So only (Q) and (R).
    [½]

(b) Only (Q) and (R)

  • Iodoform (haloform) reaction: CH3CO–R (R = H, alkyl, aryl) or CH3CH(OH)–R with I2/NaOH gives yellow CHI3.
  • Ethanal is the only aldehyde and ethanol the only primary alcohol that respond; methanol does not.

Question 1(B)(vi)[1]
Assertion: Phenols are more acidic than aliphatic alcohols.
Reason: Phenoxide ion is stabilised by resonance but alkoxide ion is not stabilised by resonance.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Assertion true: phenol (pKa about 10) is much more acidic than ethanol (about 16).
    [½]
  2. Reason true and it is the explanation: the negative charge of the phenoxide ion is delocalised over O and the ortho/para carbons (resonance), which stabilises it and favours ionisation; an alkoxide ion has no such delocalisation (and the alkyl group even destabilises it by +I effect).
    [½]
Answer
(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.

Concepts to recall

  • Acidity depends on the stability of the conjugate base: more stable anion → stronger acid.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups (NO2) at o/p increase phenol acidity; electron-donating groups decrease it.

Question 1(B)(vii)[1]
Assertion: The depression of freezing point of 0·1 molal aqueous solution of MgCl2 is less than that of 0·1 molal aqueous solution of NaCl.
Reason: The number of particles furnished by 0·1 molal MgCl2 is less than that of 0·1 molal NaCl.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Ideally MgCl2 → Mg2+ + 2Cl− gives 3 particles (i ≈ 3) against 2 for NaCl (i ≈ 2).
    [½]
  2. ΔTf = iKfm is therefore greater for MgCl2: the Assertion is false, and the Reason (fewer particles from MgCl2) is also false. Option (d).
    [½]
Answer
(d) Both Assertion and Reason are false.

Concepts to recall

  • ΔTf = iKfm; equal molalities, so compare i (number of particles per formula unit).
  • Order of ΔTf for equimolal solutions: AlCl3 (4) > MgCl2 (3) > NaCl (2) > glucose (1).

Kohlrausch observed an interesting pattern between the values of molar conductance at infinite dilution (Λ∞m) for strong electrolytes. Different pairs of electrolytes having a common cation or anion had almost the same difference of Λ∞m. On this basis he postulated Kohlrausch’s Law of Independent Migration of ions. Molar conductivities at infinite dilution:
Ionλ∞m (S cm2 mol−1)
Ba2+127·2
Cl−76·3
Ca2+119·0
SO42−160·0
State Kohlrausch’s Law of Independent Migration of ions.
  1. At infinite dilution each ion migrates independently of the other ions present, so the molar conductivity of an electrolyte at infinite dilution is the sum of the molar conductivities of its cation and anion, each multiplied by the number of ions of that kind furnished by one formula unit.

SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q2
Question 2[2]
A reaction is of first order with respect to reactant [A] and second order with regard to reactant [B]. What is the effect on the rate of reaction when: (i) the concentration of only [B] is increased three times? (ii) the concentration of both [A] and [B] is doubled?
  1. Rate law: rate = k[A]1[B]2.
  2. (i) [B] → 3[B]: rate′ = k[A](3[B])2 = 9 k[A][B]2: the rate becomes 9 times.
    [1]
  3. (ii) [A] → 2[A] and [B] → 2[B]: rate′ = k(2[A])(2[B])2 = 8 k[A][B]2: the rate becomes 8 times.
    [1]
Answer
(i) 9 times; (ii) 8 times
Common slip Adding the orders (×5, ×6) instead of multiplying the factors.

Concepts to recall

  • Rate law: rate = k[A]m[B]n; order = m + n (here 3).
  • If a concentration is multiplied by x, the rate is multiplied by xorder in that reactant.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q3
Question 3[2]
(i) 0·680 g of a compound is dissolved in 15·0 g of benzene and the freezing point of the solution is lowered by 1·44°C. Calculate the experimental molecular mass of the compound. (Kf for benzene = 5·12 K kg mol−1)
(ii) If the theoretical molecular mass of the compound is 80·5 g mol−1, suggest whether it is undergoing association or dissociation.
  1. (i) ΔTf = Kf × w2 × 1000/(M2 × w1), so M2 = 5·12 × 0·680 × 1000/(1·44 × 15·0)
    [½]
  2. M2 = 161·2 g mol−1
    [½]
  3. (ii) Observed molar mass (161·2) is about twice the theoretical value (80·5): i = 80·5/161·2 = 2·00 ≈ 0·5 (< 1).
    [½]
  4. Fewer particles than expected: the solute associates (forms dimers) in benzene.
    [½]
Answer
(i) M = 161·2 g mol−1; (ii) association (dimerisation)

Concepts to recall

  • Molar mass from freezing point depression: M2 = 1000Kfw2/(ΔTfw1) (w1 solvent mass in g).
  • Van’t Hoff factor i = normal molar mass / observed molar mass: i < 1 association, i > 1 dissociation.
  • Carboxylic acids associate (dimerise) in benzene through hydrogen bonds.

Question 4[2]
Write the IUPAC names of the following complex compounds: (i) [Pt(NH3)4Cl2]SO4; (ii) K2[Ni(CN)4].
  1. (i) Charge on the complex cation = +2 (balances SO42−); 4 NH3 (neutral) and 2 Cl− give Pt = +2 + 2 = +4. Ligands in alphabetical order (ammine before chlorido): tetraamminedichloridoplatinum(IV) sulfate.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Complex anion [Ni(CN)4]2−: Ni = +2 (4 × (−1) + Ni = −2). Name: potassium tetracyanidonickelate(II).
    [1]
Answer
(i) Tetraamminedichloridoplatinum(IV) sulfate; (ii) Potassium tetracyanidonickelate(II)
Marking note “Tetraamminedichloroplatinum(IV) sulphate” (older spelling) is accepted.

Concepts to recall

  • IUPAC rules: cation named before anion; ligands in alphabetical order (prefixes di, tetra ignored); oxidation state of the metal in Roman numerals in brackets.
  • For an anionic complex the metal name ends in -ate (nickelate, ferrate, cuprate); neutral ligands: ammine, aqua, carbonyl; anionic ones end in -ido (chlorido, cyanido).

Write chemical equations to convert each of the following: (i) Methyl magnesium bromide to propan-2-ol; (ii) Phenol to benzene.

SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q6
Question 6[2]
Calculate the value of ΔG° for the following cell at 25°C: Zn(s) | Zn2+(1M) || Sn2+(1M) | Sn(s). Given E°Zn2+/Zn = −0·76 V, E°Sn2+/Sn = −0·14 V; 1 faraday = 96,500 coulombs.
  1. Cell reaction: Zn + Sn2+ → Zn2+ + Sn, n = 2. E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode = −0·14 − (−0·76) = 0·62 V
    [1]
  2. ΔG° = −nFE° = −2 × 96500 × 0·62 = -119660 J mol−1 = -119·7 kJ mol−1
    [1]
Answer
ΔG° = -119660 J mol−1 (about -119·7 kJ mol−1)
Common slip Using E° = −0·14 + (−0·76) = −0·90 V (adding instead of subtracting).

Concepts to recall

  • ΔG° = −nFE°cell; a negative ΔG° (positive E°) means a spontaneous cell reaction.
  • E°cell = E°right (cathode) − E°left (anode), both as reduction potentials; the anode is written on the left.
  • Electrons flow from Zn (lower E°, anode) to Sn (cathode).

SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q8
Question 8[2]
A first order reaction is 40% complete in 20 minutes. Calculate the time required for 90% completion of the same reaction.
  1. k = (2·303/t) log([A]0/[A]); after 40% reaction [A] = 60 if [A]0 = 100: k = (2·303/20) log(100/60) = (2·303/20) × 0·2218 = 0·0255 min−1
    [1]
  2. t90% = (2·303/k) log(100/10) = (2·303/0·0255) × 1 = 90·2 min
    [1]
Answer
t ≈ 90·2 min (about 90 minutes)
Marking note Answers between 90 and 90·3 min depending on rounding of log 1·667 are accepted.

Concepts to recall

  • First order: k = (2·303/t) log10([A]0/[A]); t1/2 = 0·693/k.
  • % complete tells you [A] = 100 − %: 40% done → 60 left; 90% done → 10 left, and log(100/10) = 1.
  • The time for a given fraction of a first-order reaction is independent of the initial concentration.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q9
Question 9[2]
(i) Write chemical tests to distinguish between the following pairs of compounds: (a) Acetaldehyde and acetone; (b) Acetic acid and benzaldehyde.
OR
(ii) An organic compound [A] having molecular formula C3H8O gives turbidity with Lucas reagent within five minutes of mixing. On heating compound [A] with Cu at 573 K, compound [B] is formed. Compound [B] does not reduce Fehling’s solution but when heated with iodine and sodium hydroxide it gives a yellow precipitate of compound [C]. Identify the compounds [A], [B] and [C]. Write the chemical reaction for the conversion of compound [B] to compound [C].

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) Tollens’ test: warm with ammoniacal silver nitrate. Acetaldehyde gives a silver mirror: CH3CHO + 2[Ag(NH3)2]+ + 3OH− → CH3COO− + 2Ag + 4NH3 + 2H2O; acetone (a ketone) gives none. (Fehling’s solution, red Cu2O with acetaldehyde only, also serves.)
    [1]
  2. (b) Sodium hydrogencarbonate test: acetic acid gives brisk effervescence of CO2: CH3COOH + NaHCO3 → CH3COONa + H2O + CO2; benzaldehyde does not. (Alternatively benzaldehyde, unlike acetic acid, gives a silver mirror with Tollens’ reagent.)
    [1]
Answer
(a) Tollens’ (silver mirror with acetaldehyde); (b) NaHCO3 (CO2 with acetic acid)
Common slip Choosing the iodoform test for (a): both compounds give yellow CHI3.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. [A]: C3H8O is an alcohol; turbidity with Lucas reagent in about five minutes shows a secondary alcohol, so [A] = propan-2-ol, CH3CH(OH)CH3.
    [½]
  2. [B]: dehydrogenation over Cu at 573 K gives a ketone, [B] = propanone (acetone), CH3COCH3; it does not reduce Fehling’s solution (not an aldehyde).
    [½]
  3. [C]: a methyl ketone gives the iodoform test, [C] = iodoform, CHI3 (yellow).
    [½]
  4. CH3COCH3 + 3I2 + 4NaOH → CHI3 + CH3COONa + 3NaI + 3H2O

  • d4–d7 can be high spin (weak field, Δo < P) or low spin (strong field, Δo > P).
  • Isomerism: linkage (ambidentate NO2/ONO, SCN/NCS), ionisation (exchange of ligand and counter ion), coordination, hydrate, geometric and optical.
  • Spectrochemical series: I− < Br− < Cl− < F− < H2O < NH3 < en < CN− < CO.
