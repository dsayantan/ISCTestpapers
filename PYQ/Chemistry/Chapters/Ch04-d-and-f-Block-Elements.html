Question 1(A)(iii) · Specimen 2027[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [methyl alcohol, ethyl alcohol, paired, one, Cannizzaro, d–d, salicylaldehyde, Perkin’s, unpaired, f–f, salicylic acid, Kolbe’s, two, 193000, 96500, Reimer–Tiemann].
The colour of transition metal ions is due to ______ electron(s) in d-subshell and ______ transition.
  1. A transition metal ion is coloured when it has unpaired (partly filled d) electrons: the ion absorbs a part of visible light to promote a d electron from the lower t2g to the higher eg level, a d–d transition, and shows the complementary colour.
    [1]
Answer
unpaired; d–d

Concepts to recall

  • d0 (Sc3+, Ti4+) and d10 (Zn2+, Cu+) ions are colourless: no d–d transition is possible.
  • The colour seen is complementary to the colour absorbed; the d-level splitting Δ depends on the ligand.
  • f–f transitions (not d–d) colour lanthanoid ions.

When H2S gas is passed through acidified K2Cr2O7 solution, the colour of the solution changes to:
(a)green.
(b)orange.
(c)black.
(d)red.

(a) green.

  • Acidified dichromate is orange; it is a strong oxidising agent and goes to green Cr3+ with reductants such as H2S, SO2, Fe2+, I−.

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (9)ISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vii)
Question 1(B)(vii) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
Assertion: The basic strength of hydroxides of lanthanoids decreases with an increase in their atomic number.
Reason: The size of trivalent cations of lanthanoids increases with an increase in their atomic number.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Assertion is true: because of the lanthanoid contraction the size of Ln3+ decreases from La to Lu, so the Ln–OH bond becomes more covalent and Ln(OH)3 becomes less basic. The Reason is false, since the size of the trivalent ion decreases with atomic number. Option (c).
    [1]
Answer
(c) Assertion is true and Reason is false

Concepts to recall

  • Lanthanoid contraction: Ln3+ radius falls steadily from La3+ (106 pm) to Lu3+ (85 pm), because 4f electrons shield the nucleus poorly.
  • Smaller cation → greater polarising power → more covalent M–OH → weaker base: La(OH)3 is the most basic, Lu(OH)3 the least.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (9)ISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(iv)
Question 1(A)(iv) · Board 2025[1]
The transition metal ions having ____________ d-orbitals are colourless and ____________ in nature.
  1. Colour of transition-metal ions comes from d–d transitions, which need an incompletely filled d subshell (and an empty d orbital to jump into). Ions with completely filled d orbitals (d10, e.g. Zn2+, Cu+) are colourless.
    [½]
  2. They have no unpaired electrons, so they are diamagnetic in nature.
    [½]
Answer
completely filled; diamagnetic
Marking note Of the two options on the list, only “completely filled” fits; d0 ions are also colourless but are not among the choices.

Concepts to recall

  • d–d transition: an electron in a lower d level absorbs visible light and moves to a higher d level; colour seen is the complement of the light absorbed.
  • Zn2+ (3d10), Sc3+ (3d0), Ti4+ (3d0): no d–d transition, colourless; unpaired electrons → paramagnetic, none → diamagnetic.

Question 1(B)(vii) · Board 2025[1]
Assertion: The Zr–Hf pair of elements has the same value of atomic radii though Zr and Hf are placed in different periods in the periodic table.
Reason: The lanthanoid contraction prevents the expected increase in atomic radii of Hf.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Assertion is true: Zr (160 pm) and Hf (159 pm) have almost identical radii, although Hf is in the 6th period.
    [½]
  2. Reason is true and explains it: the 4f electrons between La and Hf shield the nuclear charge poorly, so the steady contraction of the lanthanoids cancels the normal increase in size down the group.
    [½]
Answer
(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.

Concepts to recall

  • Lanthanoid contraction: gradual decrease in atomic and ionic radii from La to Lu, because of poor shielding by 4f electrons.
  • Consequences: Zr and Hf (also Nb–Ta, Mo–W) have similar radii and properties and are very hard to separate; 5d elements have much higher densities and ionisation energies than expected.

The variation and magnitude of oxidation state in coordination complexes is due to the participation of inner ______ electrons in addition to outer ______ electrons.

(n−1)d1–10; ns1–2

Question 1(B)(vii) · Specimen 2025[1]
Assertion: There is a continuous increase in size among Lanthanoids with an increase in atomic number.
Reason: Lanthanoids do not show Lanthanoid contraction.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for assertion.
(c)Assertion is true but Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Across the lanthanoids the 4f electrons shield poorly, so the effective nuclear charge rises and the ionic radius decreases steadily from La3+ (106 pm) to Lu3+ (85 pm): the assertion (continuous increase) is false. This steady decrease is the lanthanoid contraction, so the reason (that they do not show it) is false too.
    [1]

(d) Both Assertion and Reason are false.

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (9)ISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii) · Board 2024[1]
When KMnO4 is heated with acidified oxalic acid, gas bubbles are evolved. These gas bubbles are evolved due to the formation of:
(a)SO2
(b)CO2
(c)SO3
(d)O2
  1. Acidified KMnO4 oxidises oxalic acid: 2MnO4− + 5C2O42− + 16H+ → 2Mn2+ + 10CO2 + 8H2O. The bubbles are carbon dioxide.
    [1]
Answer
(b) CO2

Concepts to recall

  • In acid, MnO4− (Mn +7) → Mn2+ (Mn +2): five electrons gained. Oxalate → 2CO2: two electrons lost.
  • Hence 2 MnO4− : 5 C2O42− (ratio from the electron balance).
  • Self-indicator: the purple colour disappears until the first excess drop gives a permanent pink.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (6)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q7
Question 7 · Specimen 2027[2]
Substantiate the following statements with reasons.
(i) Cobalt is attracted in a magnetic field whereas Zinc is not. (Atomic number of Co = 27 and Zn = 30)
(ii) The melting points and boiling points of Zn, Cd and Hg are low. (Atomic number of Zn = 30, Cd = 48 and Hg = 80)
  1. (i) Co: [Ar] 3d74s2 has three unpaired d electrons, so it is paramagnetic (attracted by a magnetic field; in the solid, ferromagnetic). Zn: [Ar] 3d104s2 has completely filled d and s subshells, no unpaired electron, so it is diamagnetic and not attracted.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Zn, Cd and Hg have the filled (n−1)d10ns2 configuration. The d electrons are all paired and do not take part in metallic bonding, which involves only the two ns electrons. The metallic bond is therefore weak, so their melting and boiling points are low (Hg is a liquid).
    [1]
Answer
(i) Co has 3 unpaired electrons (paramagnetic); Zn has none (d10, diamagnetic). (ii) Filled d10: only ns2 electrons bond, so the metallic bond is weak.

Concepts to recall

  • Paramagnetic = attracted by a field (unpaired electrons); diamagnetic = weakly repelled (all electrons paired).
  • Strength of the metallic bond in the d-block rises with the number of unpaired d electrons available for bonding in addition to ns electrons; hence W has the highest melting point and Zn, Cd, Hg the lowest.
  • Zn, Cd, Hg are d-block but not transition elements (d10 in atoms and in their M2+ ions).
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q10
Question 10 · Board 2026[2]
(i) Why is the first ionisation enthalpy of chromium less than that of zinc? (Atomic number of Cr = 24 and Zn = 30)
(ii) Why does the pink colour of aqueous KMnO4 solution disappear when warmed with oxalic acid solution in acidic medium?
  1. (i) Cr is [Ar]3d54s1; loss of the single 4s electron leaves the stable half-filled 3d5. Zn is [Ar]3d104s2: the electron must be removed from a completely filled, stable 4s23d10 configuration, and its nuclear charge is higher (Zeff larger). Hence IE1 of Cr (653 kJ mol−1) < IE1 of Zn (906 kJ mol−1).
    [1]
  2. (ii) MnO4− (purple/pink, Mn +7) is reduced by oxalic acid to colourless Mn2+ (Mn +2): 2MnO4− + 5C2O42− + 16H+ → 2Mn2+ + 10CO2 + 8H2O. The coloured ion is used up, so the colour disappears.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Cr loses 4s1 to reach stable d5; Zn must lose an electron from the filled 4s23d10; (ii) MnO4− → colourless Mn2+

Concepts to recall

  • Half-filled (d5) and completely filled (d10) sub-shells are extra stable; Cr and Cu have the anomalous configurations 3d54s1 and 3d104s1.
  • Permanganate titration: MnO4− + 8H+ + 5e− → Mn2+ + 4H2O; it acts as its own indicator (first permanent pink at the end point). The reaction with oxalate needs warming (about 60°C) and is autocatalysed by Mn2+.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q8
Question 8 · Specimen 2026[2]
(i) Which divalent metal ion in weak field ligand has maximum paramagnetic character among the first transition metal series (3d series)? Why?
(ii) The melting and boiling points of Zn, Cd and Hg are low. Why?
  1. (i) Mn2+ (3d5). In a weak-field ligand the electrons do not pair, so all five 3d orbitals hold one electron each: five unpaired electrons, the maximum for any M2+ of the series (μ = √35 = 5·92 BM).
    [1]
  2. (ii) Zn, Cd and Hg have the configuration (n−1)d10ns2. All d electrons are paired and do not take part in metallic bonding; only the two s electrons do. The metallic bonds are therefore weak, so melting and boiling points are low (Hg is a liquid).
    [1]
Answer
(i) Mn2+, five unpaired electrons; (ii) filled d10 subshell: only ns2 electrons bond, so weak metallic bonding

Concepts to recall

  • Spin-only magnetic moment μ = √(n(n+2)) BM; the more unpaired electrons, the greater the paramagnetism.
  • M2+ of the 3d series: Sc2+ d1… Cr2+ d4 (4), Mn2+ d5 (5, maximum), Fe2+ d6 (4), Co2+ d7 (3), Ni2+ d8 (2), Cu2+ d9 (1), Zn2+ d10 (0).
  • Strength of metallic bonding in the d-block depends on the number of unpaired d electrons available; Zn, Cd, Hg have none, so their m.p. are the lowest of the series (Zn 693 K, Cd 594 K, Hg 234 K).
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q3
Question 3 · Board 2024[2]
Give a reason for each of the following.
(i) Zinc, cadmium and mercury are considered as d-block elements but not regarded as transition elements.
(ii) Transition metals possess a great tendency to form complex compounds.
  1. (i) A transition element has an incomplete d subshell in its atoms or in its common ions. Zn, Cd and Hg have the configuration (n−1)d10ns2; their only common ion (M2+, d10) also has a completely filled d subshell, so they are d-block but not transition elements.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Transition metal ions are small and highly charged (high charge density), and they have vacant (n−1)d orbitals of suitable energy which accept lone pairs from ligands to form coordinate bonds.
    [1]
Answer
(i) d10 configuration in atoms and in M2+: no incomplete d subshell. (ii) Small size, high charge, vacant d orbitals.

Concepts to recall

  • Definition: a transition element has partly filled d orbitals in its ground state or in any of its common oxidation states.
  • Zn, Cd, Hg: d10s2; the d electrons are not used in bonding, hence low melting points and no variable valency.
  • Complex formation needs (a) a small, highly charged central ion and (b) vacant orbitals to accept the ligand lone pairs.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q5
Question 5 · Board 2024[2]
Account for each of the following.
(i) Zirconium (Zr) and Hafnium (Hf) are difficult to separate.
(ii) Salts of Cupric (Cu2+) ion are coloured whereas salts of Cuprous (Cu+) ion are colourless.
  1. (i) Because of the lanthanoid contraction (poor shielding by 4f electrons) Hf (metallic radius 159 pm) has almost the same size as Zr (160 pm), though it is in the next period. They also have the same valence-shell configuration, so their chemical properties are nearly identical and chemical separation is very difficult (it needs special methods such as fractional crystallisation of complex fluorides or ion exchange).
    [1]
  2. (ii) Cu2+ is 3d9: it has one unpaired electron, so d–d transitions can absorb visible light and the ion is coloured. Cu+ is 3d10: the d subshell is full, there is no vacant d orbital for an electron to be promoted into, so no d–d transition occurs and its salts are colourless.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Lanthanoid contraction makes Zr and Hf almost identical in size and properties. (ii) Cu2+ d9 (unpaired electron, d–d transition); Cu+ d10 (none).
Common slip Do not say Hf and Zr have the same radius because they are in the same group; the point is the lanthanoid contraction, which has cancelled the usual increase in size down the group.

Concepts to recall

  • Lanthanoid contraction: steady decrease in size from La to Lu, as 4f electrons shield the nuclear charge poorly; consequences: Zr–Hf, Nb–Ta, Mo–W pairs alike; third-row elements dense and unreactive.
  • Colour of d-block ions arises from d–d transitions, which need unpaired (or at least partly filled) d orbitals; d0 and d10 ions are colourless (Sc3+, Zn2+, Cu+, Ag+).
  • Colour observed is the complement of the colour absorbed.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q11
Question 11 · Board 2024[2]
(i) Why is Mn2+ ion more stable than Fe2+ ion? (Atomic number of Mn = 25 and Fe = 26)
(ii) Trivalent Lanthanoid ions such as La3+ (Z = 57) and Lu3+ (Z = 71) do not show any colour in their solution. Give a reason.
  1. (i) Mn2+ = [Ar] 3d5 has a half-filled d subshell, which is specially stable (maximum exchange energy, symmetrical distribution). Fe2+ = 3d6 can lose one more electron to reach the stable d5 state (Fe3+), so it is oxidised easily and is less stable.
    [1]
  2. (ii) La3+ has the configuration [Xe] 4f0 (empty) and Lu3+ has [Xe] 4f14 (full). Neither has unpaired f electrons or a partly filled f subshell, so no f–f transition can occur and the ions are colourless.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Mn2+ is d5 (half-filled, stable). (ii) La3+ is 4f0, Lu3+ is 4f14: no f–f transitions.

Concepts to recall

  • Half-filled (d5, f7) and fully filled (d10, f14) subshells have extra stability.
  • Fe3+ (d5) is more stable than Fe2+; Mn3+ (d4) is a strong oxidising agent, Mn2+ is stable. Consequently E°(Mn3+/Mn2+) is +1·57 V, very high.
  • Lanthanoid colour: Ln3+ ions with 1 to 13 f electrons are coloured (f–f transitions); 4f0 (La, Ce4+) and 4f14 (Lu) are colourless.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (3)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q15
Question 15 · Specimen 2027[3]
(i) Complete and balance the following chemical equation.
KMnO4 + H2SO4 + H2S → _____ + _____ + _____ + _____
(ii) Why do transition metals form alloys?
(iii) Potassium dichromate (K2Cr2O7) acts as a powerful oxidising agent in acidic medium. Explain.
  1. (i) Half-reactions: MnO4− + 8H+ + 5e− → Mn2+ + 4H2O (×2); H2S → S + 2H+ + 2e− (×5). Balanced: 2KMnO4 + 3H2SO4 + 5H2S → K2SO4 + 2MnSO4 + 5S + 8H2O. (Check: K 2, Mn 2, S 3 + 5 = 8 = 1 + 2 + 5, H 6 + 10 = 16 = 16, O 8 + 12 = 20 = 4 + 8 + 8.)
    [1]
  2. (ii) Transition metals have similar atomic radii (within about 15 %) and similar metallic structures, so the atoms of one metal can occupy positions in the crystal lattice of another in the molten state. On cooling they form homogeneous solid solutions (alloys) such as brass and steel.
    [1]
  3. (iii) In acid Cr2O72− is reduced from Cr(VI) to the more stable Cr(III): Cr2O72− + 14H+ + 6e− → 2Cr3+ + 7H2O, E° = +1·33 V (K2Cr2O7 + 4H2SO4 → K2SO4 + Cr2(SO4)3 + 4H2O + 3[O]). The large positive E° shows a strong tendency to accept electrons, so it oxidises Fe2+, I−, S2−, etc.
    [1]
Answer
(i) 2KMnO4 + 3H2SO4 + 5H2S → K2SO4 + 2MnSO4 + 5S + 8H2O. (ii) Similar atomic size, they replace each other in the lattice. (iii) Cr(VI) → Cr(III) with E° = +1·33 V
Common slip Writing the products as K2MnO4 or MnO2: in acid medium MnO4− always goes to Mn2+.

Concepts to recall

  • Balance redox by the ion–electron method: equalise electrons gained (MnO4−, 5e) and lost (H2S, 2e): 2 : 5.
  • Alloys form when metal atoms differ in radius by less than about 15 %; transition metals (all with similar radii) form many (steel, brass, bronze, nichrome).
  • K2Cr2O7 (orange) → Cr3+ (green) in acid: Cr2O72− + 14H+ + 6e− → 2Cr3+ + 7H2O; the colour change is the end point in redox titrations.

Question 13 · Board 2026[3]
(i) Complete and balance the following reactions: (a) K2Cr2O7 + H2SO4 + KI → ____ + ____ + ____ + ____; (b) KMnO4 + H2SO4 + FeSO4 → ____ + ____ + ____ + ____.
(ii) Which ion, Co2+ or Zn2+, is attracted to a magnetic field? Give reasons. (Atomic number of Co = 27 and Zn = 30)
  1. (i)(a) K2Cr2O7 + 7H2SO4 + 6KI → 4K2SO4 + Cr2(SO4)3 + 3I2 + 7H2O
    [1]
  2. (i)(b) 2KMnO4 + 8H2SO4 + 10FeSO4 → K2SO4 + 2MnSO4 + 5Fe2(SO4)3 + 8H2O
    [1]
  3. (ii) Co2+ is attracted (paramagnetic): Co2+ is [Ar]3d7 with 3 unpaired electrons. Zn2+ is [Ar]3d10, all electrons paired, so it is diamagnetic (weakly repelled).
    [1]
Answer
(a) K2SO4, Cr2(SO4)3, I2, H2O; (b) K2SO4, MnSO4, Fe2(SO4)3, H2O; (ii) Co2+

THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (3)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q13
Question 13 · Specimen 2026[3]
(i) Complete and balance the following equations:
(a) K2Cr2O7 + FeSO4 + H2SO4 → ______ + ______ + ______ + ______
(b) KMnO4 + KI + H2SO4 → ______ + ______ + ______ + ______
(ii) Explain how the colour of K2Cr2O7 solution depends on pH of the solution.
  1. (i)(a) K2Cr2O7 + 6FeSO4 + 7H2SO4 → K2SO4 + Cr2(SO4)3 + 3Fe2(SO4)3 + 7H2O. (Ionic: Cr2O72− + 6Fe2+ + 14H+ → 2Cr3+ + 6Fe3+ + 7H2O; orange → green.) Balance check: 13 sulphate groups on each side.
    [1]
  2. (b) 2KMnO4 + 10KI + 8H2SO4 → 6K2SO4 + 2MnSO4 + 8H2O + 5I2. (Ionic: 2MnO4− + 10I− + 16H+ → 2Mn2+ + 5I2 + 8H2O; purple decolourised, iodine liberated.)
    [1]
  3. (ii) Chromate and dichromate are interconverted by pH: 2CrO42− + 2H+ ⇄ Cr2O72− + H2O. In alkali (pH rises) the equilibrium shifts left and the solution turns yellow (CrO42−); on adding acid (pH falls) it shifts right and the colour returns to orange (Cr2O72−). K2Cr2O7 + 2KOH → 2K2CrO4 + H2O.
    [1]
Answer
(a) K2SO4, Cr2(SO4)3, Fe2(SO4)3, H2O; (b) K2SO4, MnSO4, H2O, I2; (ii) orange in acid, yellow in alkali
Common slip Writing 3Fe2(SO4)3 as Fe2(SO4)3 only, or forgetting that K2SO4 forms (K+ is a spectator).
Marking note Award ½ for correct products and ½ for correct balancing in each of (a) and (b) if the marking is split; the ionic equation is acceptable.

Concepts to recall

  • Balance redox by ion-electron: Cr2O72− takes 6e− (Cr +6 → +3, two atoms); MnO4− (acid) takes 5e− (Mn +7 → +2). Fe2+ and I− each give 1e−: hence 6 Fe2+ : 1 dichromate, 10 I− : 2 permanganate.
  • Dichromate is a good oxidising agent in acid (E° = 1·33 V); permanganate stronger still (1·51 V).
  • Orange dichromate, yellow chromate; pH < 4 dichromate, pH > 4 chromate (the CISCE key says they co-exist near pH 4).

Question 20 · Board 2025 Imp.[5]
(i) Give a reason for each of the following: (a) Transition elements show high melting point. (b) Though Zr (z = 40) belongs to 4d transition series and Hf (z = 72) belongs to 5d transition series, yet the two cannot be separated easily. (c) Cu2+ and Fe3+ generally form coloured compounds.
(ii) Complete and balance the following reactions: (a) K2Cr2O7 + H2SO4 + Na2SO3 → ___ + ___ + ___ + ___ (b) KMnO4 + H2SO4 + H2S → ___ + ___ + ___ + ___
  1. (i)(a) Strong metallic bonding: besides the ns electrons, many unpaired (n−1)d electrons also take part in bonding, so the interatomic forces are strong and the melting points are high.
    [1]
  2. (b) Lanthanoid contraction: the poorly shielding 4f electrons make Hf (144 pm) almost the same size as Zr (145 pm); with the same valence configuration they have nearly identical properties and are difficult to separate chemically.
    [1]

    [1]

    [1]
  5. (b) 2KMnO4 + 3H2SO4 + 5H2S → K2SO4 + 2MnSO4 + 5S + 8H2O (purple → colourless).
    [1]

(i) metallic bonding by unpaired d electrons; lanthanoid contraction; unpaired d electrons; (ii) balanced equations as above

Concepts to recall

  • Ionic form: Cr2O72− + 3SO32− + 8H+ → 2Cr3+ + 3SO42− + 4H2O; 2MnO4− + 5H2S + 6H+ → 2Mn2+ + 5S + 8H2O.

Question 20 · Board 2025[5]
(i) Give a reason for each of the following:
(a) Ti3+ salts are coloured whereas Ti4+ salts are colourless. [Given: Atomic number of Ti = 22]
(b) Transition elements form alloys.
(c) The pink coloured KMnO4 solution turns colourless when reacted with Mohr’s salt (Fe2+) in acidic medium.
(ii) Complete and balance the following reactions:
(a) KMnO4 + H2SO4 + H2C2O4 → ___ + ___ + ___ + ___
(b) K2Cr2O7 + H2SO4 + H2S → ___ + ___ + ___ + ___
  1. (a) Ti (Z = 22) is [Ar]3d24s2. Ti3+ is 3d1: one unpaired d electron can undergo a d–d transition by absorbing visible light, so it is coloured. Ti4+ is 3d0: no d electron, no d–d transition, colourless.
    [1]
  2. (b) Transition metals have similar atomic sizes (and similar crystal structures), so atoms of one metal can replace those of another in the lattice, forming alloys.
    [1]
  3. (c) In acidic medium MnO4− (purple/pink) is reduced by Fe2+ to almost colourless Mn2+: MnO4− + 8H+ + 5Fe2+ → Mn2+ + 5Fe3+ + 4H2O.
    [1]
  4. (ii)(a) 2KMnO4 + 3H2SO4 + 5H2C2O4 → K2SO4 + 2MnSO4 + 10CO2 + 8H2O
    [1]
  5. (ii)(b) K2Cr2O7 + 4H2SO4 + 3H2S → K2SO4 + Cr2(SO4)3 + 7H2O + 3S
    [1]
Answer
(i)(a) 3d1 has an unpaired electron, 3d0 has none; (b) similar atomic sizes; (c) MnO4− → Mn2+. (ii) as balanced above.
Marking note Each balanced equation is checked atom by atom (K, Mn/Cr, S, O, H). Full ionic equations are also accepted.

Concepts to recall

  • Colour needs unpaired d electrons for d–d transition (partly filled d orbitals): 3d0 and 3d10 ions are colourless.
  • Balancing redox: electrons lost = gained. MnO4− + 8H+ + 5e− → Mn2+ + 4H2O (×2); C2O42− → 2CO2 + 2e− (×5). Cr2O72− + 14H+ + 6e− → 2Cr3+ + 7H2O; H2S → S + 2H+ + 2e− (×3).
  • KMnO4 is self-indicator in titrations: the first permanent pink colour marks the end point. Mohr’s salt = FeSO4(NH4)2SO4·6H2O.

(i) Give a reason for each of the following. (a) The purple colour of KMnO4 disappears in the presence of acidified solution of oxalic acid. (b) Some transition metals and their compounds get attracted towards the magnetic field. (c) Why are Mn2+ compounds more stable than Fe2+ towards oxidation to the +3 state? (atomic no. of Mn = 25 and Fe = 26)
(ii) Complete and balance the following reactions. (a) KMnO4 + H2SO4 + FeSO4 → ______ + ______ + ______ + ______
(b) K2Cr2O7 + H2SO4 + KI → ______ + ______ + ______ + ______
  1. (i)(a) Oxalic acid reduces MnO4− (Mn +7, purple) to Mn2+ (Mn +2, almost colourless): 2MnO4− + 5C2O42− + 16H+ → 2Mn2+ + 10CO2 + 8H2O. (1)
    [1]
  2. (b) They have unpaired electrons in their (partly filled) d orbitals; the spin and orbital motion give a permanent magnetic moment, so they are paramagnetic (attracted by the field). (1)
    [1]
  3. (c) Mn2+ is 3d5, a stable half-filled configuration (extra exchange energy), so the third ionisation (to 3d4) is difficult. Fe2+ is 3d6 and loses one electron easily to become the stable half-filled 3d5 of Fe3+. (1)
    [1]
  4. (ii)(a) 2KMnO4 + 8H2SO4 + 10FeSO4 → K2SO4 + 2MnSO4 + 5Fe2(SO4)3 + 8H2O (½ products, ½ balancing). Check: S 8 + 10 = 18 = 1 + 2 + 15; O 80 = 80.
    [1]
  5. (b) K2Cr2O7 + 7H2SO4 + 6KI → 4K2SO4 + Cr2(SO4)3 + 3I2 + 7H2O (½ products, ½ balancing). Check: K 2 + 6 = 8; S 7 = 4 + 3; O 35 = 35.
    [1]

(i) (a) Mn(+7) → Mn(+2); (b) unpaired d electrons; (c) Mn2+ is d5 (half-filled) while Fe2+ is d6. (ii) balanced equations as above.

Concepts to recall

  • KMnO4 in acid: MnO4− + 8H+ + 5e− → Mn2+ + 4H2O. Fe2+ → Fe3+ + e−, so 5 Fe2+ per MnO4−.
  • Cr2O72− + 14H+ + 6e− → 2Cr3+ + 7H2O (orange → green); I− → I2.
  • Magnetic moment μ = √(n(n+2)) BM; half-filled and fully filled subshells are specially stable.
