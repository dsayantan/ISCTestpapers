What to revise

  • Name reactions and conversions: Finkelstein, Wurtz, Fittig, Sandmeyer link-ups, chlorobenzene to biphenyl or aniline, chloroform to acetylene, iodoform to acetylene.
  • Mechanism questions: SN1 against SN2 from a rate that depends on one reactant, alc. KCN against alc. AgCN (cyanide against isocyanide), chloroform stored with ethanol (phosgene).
  • One-markers: halogenation of benzene with FeCl3, chlorobenzene with aqueous ammonia, Markownikoff against anti-Markownikoff, why tertiary halides fail in Wurtz.
  • Habit: state the reagent and condition with every arrow; haloarenes need a catalyst or severe conditions.

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(ii)
Question 1(A)(ii) · Board 2026[1]
Fill in the blank(s) by choosing from: [first, alc. AgCN, primary, decrease, third, increase, zero, Lucas, alc. KCN, KOH, no change, two, Tollen’s, AgOH, tertiary]
Methyl chloride on treatment with ________ forms methyl cyanide, whereas on treatment with ________ it forms methyl isocyanide.
  1. Ionic KCN is largely dissociated and the free C end of CN− attacks carbon: CH3Cl + KCN → CH3CN + KCl. Answer: alc. KCN.
    [½]
  2. Covalent AgCN keeps the C–Ag bond, so the nucleophile attacks through the N lone pair: CH3Cl + AgCN → CH3NC + AgCl. Answer: alc. AgCN.
    [½]
Answer
alc. KCN; alc. AgCN

Concepts to recall

  • CN− is an ambident nucleophile (C or N can attack): KCN gives the cyanide (RCN), AgCN the isocyanide (RNC).
  • Ionic KCN → attack by carbon; covalent AgCN → attack by nitrogen.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(iv)
Question 1(A)(iv) · Specimen 2026[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [electrophilic, four, 4-bromophenol, first, alc. AgCN, decreases, 2,4,6-tribromophenol, nucleophilic, two, zero, alc. KCN, increases].
Methyl chloride on treatment with ______ gives methyl cyanide, whereas on treatment with ______, it gives methyl isocyanide.
  1. KCN is ionic and gives free CN−, which attacks through carbon: CH3Cl + KCN → CH3CN + KCl (methyl cyanide).
    [½]
  2. AgCN is covalent (Ag–C≡N); only the nitrogen lone pair is available for attack, giving CH3Cl + AgCN → CH3NC + AgCl (methyl isocyanide).
    [½]
Answer
alc. KCN; alc. AgCN

Concepts to recall

  • The cyanide ion is an ambident nucleophile: it can attack through C (giving R–CN) or through N (giving R–NC).
  • KCN, NaCN (ionic) → alkyl cyanide (nitrile); AgCN (covalent) → isocyanide (carbylamine).
  • Nitriles hydrolyse to acids (adds one C); isocyanides hydrolyse to primary amines and methanoic acid.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii) · Specimen 2026[1]
When chlorobenzene is heated with aq. ammonia in the presence of Cu2O at high pressure, it gives:
(a)aniline.
(b)phenyl isocyanide.
(c)diphenyl.
(d)diphenylamine.
  1. Aryl halides are unreactive to nucleophiles, but under drastic conditions (Cu2O, 473 K, high pressure) NH3 replaces Cl: C6H5Cl + 2NH3 → C6H5NH2 + NH4Cl. The product is aniline.
    [1]
Answer
(a) aniline.

Concepts to recall

  • Aryl halides resist nucleophilic substitution because of the partial double-bond character of C–Cl (resonance) and the sp2 carbon; they need high temperature, pressure, a catalyst or ring-activating groups.
  • Phenyl isocyanide comes from aniline + CHCl3 + KOH (carbylamine); diphenyl from the Fittig reaction.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vi)
Question 1(B)(vi) · Board 2025[1]
Assertion: The process of halogenation of benzene takes place in the presence of anhydrous FeCl3.
Reason: Anhydrous FeCl3 prepares nucleophile to attack the benzene ring.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Assertion is true: benzene is chlorinated by Cl2 in the presence of anhydrous FeCl3 (a Lewis acid catalyst).
    [½]
  2. Reason is false: FeCl3 polarises Cl–Cl to generate the electrophile Cl+ (FeCl4− carries the other end); the ring, which is electron rich, acts as the nucleophile.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Assertion is true and Reason is false.

Concepts to recall

  • Electrophilic substitution: Lewis acid (FeCl3, AlCl3) makes an electrophile: Cl2 + FeCl3 → Cl+ + FeCl4−.
  • Benzene is a π-electron-rich nucleophile; it is attacked by electrophiles, not nucleophiles.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)CISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii) · Specimen 2025[1]
According to Markownikoff’s rule, “The negative part of the halogen acid attaches itself with that C atom of an unsymmetrical alkene which has least number of H-atom.” Anti-Markownikoff’s addition of HBr is NOT observed in:
(a)Propene
(b)But-2-ene
(c)But-1-ene
(d)Pent-2-ene
  1. Markownikoff and anti-Markownikoff addition only differ for an unsymmetrical alkene. But-2-ene, CH3CH=CHCH3, is symmetrical: both ends of the double bond are equivalent, so HBr gives only 2-bromobutane whichever way it adds, and the peroxide (Kharasch) effect makes no difference. Propene, but-1-ene and pent-2-ene are unsymmetrical, so with peroxide they give 1-bromopropane, 1-bromobutane and 2-bromopentane/3-bromopentane in different ratios.
    [1]
Answer
(b) But-2-ene

Concepts to recall

  • Peroxide effect (Kharasch): HBr adds by a free-radical chain mechanism, giving the anti-Markownikoff product; HCl and HI do not show it.
  • A symmetrical alkene (but-2-ene, ethene) gives a single product either way.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)CISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vi)
Question 1(B)(vi) · Specimen 2025[1]
Assertion: Tertiary butyl bromide undergoes Wurtz reaction to give 2,2,3,3-tetramethylbutane.
Reason: In Wurtz reaction, when same alkyl halides react with sodium in dry ether to give hydrocarbon; the hydrocarbon formed contains double the number of carbon atoms present in the alkyl halide.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true but Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Reason: 2RX + 2Na → R–R + 2NaX in dry ether, so the product has twice the carbon atoms of RX: true. Assertion: 2(CH3)3CBr + 2Na → (CH3)3C–C(CH3)3 + 2NaBr, i.e. 2,2,3,3-tetramethylbutane, is the textbook coupling of the same-halide Wurtz reaction, and the reason is exactly what explains it. CISCE’s key is (a).
    [1]
Answer
(a)
Marking note CISCE’s key is (a). Chemically, tertiary halides give mostly elimination (isobutene) with sodium and a poor yield of the coupled product, so the assertion is idealised; the options offer no choice that makes the reason true and the assertion false, and (a) is the expected answer.

Concepts to recall

  • Wurtz: 2R–X + 2Na (dry ether) → R–R + 2NaX; the two alkyl groups join, so carbon number doubles.
  • Limitations: with different halides a mixture of three alkanes is formed; methane cannot be made.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(iii)
Question 1(A)(iii) · Board 2024[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [lead poisoning, zero, phosgene, dependent, cancer, independent, diethyl ether, first, ethyl carbonate, ethene].
The addition of small quantity of ethanol to chloroform prevents the formation of ______ and converts it into the harmless compound ______.
  1. On exposure to air and light chloroform is oxidised to poisonous phosgene (COCl2): 2CHCl3 + O2 → 2COCl2 + 2HCl. Ethanol (about 1 %) added to the bottle converts any phosgene formed into harmless ethyl carbonate: COCl2 + 2C2H5OH → (C2H5O)2CO + 2HCl.
    [1]
Answer
phosgene; ethyl carbonate (diethyl carbonate)
Marking note The word list gives ‘ethyl carbonate’; the compound is diethyl carbonate, (C2H5O)2CO. Either name earns the mark.

Concepts to recall

  • Chloroform is stored in dark brown, completely filled bottles to keep out light and air.
  • Phosgene (carbonyl chloride) is a poisonous gas; test for its presence: AgNO3 gives a white ppt with the HCl formed.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (5)ISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q11
Question 11 · Board 2026[2]
Also draws on: part (i) gives aniline (Ch 9).
Write chemical equations to convert each of the following: (i) Chlorobenzene to aniline; (ii) Iodoform to acetylene.
  1. (i) Ammonolysis of chlorobenzene (aqueous NH3, Cu2O, about 473 K, high pressure): C6H5Cl + 2NH3 → C6H5NH2 + NH4Cl
    [1]
  2. (ii) Heat iodoform with silver powder: 2CHI3 + 6Ag → HC≡CH + 6AgI
    [1]
Answer
(i) C6H5Cl + 2NH3 → C6H5NH2 + NH4Cl; (ii) 2CHI3 + 6Ag → C2H2 + 6AgI
Marking note A multi-step route (e.g. chlorobenzene → Grignard → …) is not needed; any correct sequence ending in aniline is accepted.

Concepts to recall

  • Aryl halides are inert to nucleophiles (C–Cl has partial double-bond character from resonance); substitution needs drastic conditions, here Cu2O catalyst at high T and P.
  • Haloforms heated with silver powder lose their halogens as AgX and the carbons couple, giving acetylene from CHI3 (and from CHCl3).
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (5)ISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q4
Question 4 · Board 2025 Imp.[2]
Write chemical equations to convert the following: (i) Chlorobenzene to biphenyl (ii) Chloroform to acetylene
  1. (i) Fittig reaction: 2C6H5Cl + 2Na (dry ether) → C6H5–C6H5 + 2NaCl. (Ullmann: 2C6H5Cl + 2Cu, Δ → C6H5–C6H5 + 2CuCl is equally acceptable.)
    [1]
  2. (ii) Heating chloroform with silver powder: 2CHCl3 + 6Ag (Δ) → HC≡CH + 6AgCl.
    [1]
Answer
(i) 2C6H5Cl + 2Na → C6H5–C6H5 + 2NaCl; (ii) 2CHCl3 + 6Ag → C2H2 + 6AgCl
Marking note Balanced equations are needed for the full mark.

Concepts to recall

  • Wurtz–Fittig: Ar–X + R–X + 2Na → Ar–R; Fittig: 2 Ar–X + 2Na → Ar–Ar; Wurtz: 2R–X + 2Na → R–R.
  • Silver (or copper) powder removes halogen from CHCl3 as AgCl, joining two carbons through a C≡C bond.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (5)ISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q5
Question 5 · Board 2025[2]
When an organic compound [A] having molecular formula C4H9Br is treated with aqueous KOH, the rate of reaction depends on concentration of compound [A] only. But when compound [B], with the same molecular formula, reacts with aqueous KOH, the rate of reaction depends on the concentration of compound [B] as well as of KOH. Compound [B] is a structural isomer of compound [A]. Identify compounds [A] and [B].
  1. Rate depends on [A] only: unimolecular, SN1, which is fastest for a tertiary halide (stable carbocation (CH3)3C+): [A] = tert-butyl bromide, (CH3)3CBr (2-bromo-2-methylpropane).
    [1]
  2. Rate depends on [B] and [OH−]: bimolecular, SN2, typical of a primary halide: [B] = n-butyl bromide, CH3CH2CH2CH2Br (1-bromobutane).
    [1]
Answer
[A] (CH3)3CBr; [B] CH3CH2CH2CH2Br
Marking note 1-bromo-2-methylpropane is also accepted for [B] (primary, SN2). 2-Bromobutane is secondary and shows mixed order, so it does not fit either description.

Concepts to recall

  • SN1: rate = k[RX], two steps via a carbocation, 3° > 2° > 1°. SN2: rate = k[RX][Nu], one step with inversion, 1° > 2° > 3°.
  • Four isomers of C4H9Br: 1-bromobutane, 1-bromo-2-methylpropane (both 1°), 2-bromobutane (2°), 2-bromo-2-methylpropane (3°).
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (5)CISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q4
Question 4 · Specimen 2025[2]
Devise a scheme for the synthesis of n-butane using CH3I as the only source of carbon. Is it possible to obtain propane in pure state by applying this scheme? Give a reason for your answer.
  1. Scheme: (1) 2CH3I + 2Na (dry ether) → CH3–CH3 + 2NaI (Wurtz). (2) CH3CH3 + Cl2 (hν) → CH3CH2Cl + HCl; (3) 2CH3CH2Cl + 2Na (dry ether) → CH3CH2CH2CH3 + 2NaCl (Wurtz again). (1)
    [1]
  2. Propane would need CH3I and C2H5X to couple. Wurtz coupling of two different halides gives a mixture of ethane, propane and butane (CH3–CH3, CH3–C2H5, C2H5–C2H5), which have close boiling points, so pure propane cannot be obtained. (1)
    [1]
Answer
Wurtz twice (with free-radical chlorination between); No, propane cannot be got pure (a mixture is formed).

Concepts to recall

  • Wurtz: 2RX + 2Na → R–R + 2NaX in dry ether; the carbon number doubles.
  • Wurtz with two different alkyl halides gives all three possible alkanes, so it is useful only for symmetrical alkanes.
  • Chain growth from one carbon: Wurtz (C1→C2), halogenation, Wurtz again (C2→C4).
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (5)ISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q6
Question 6 · Board 2024[2]
How will you bring the following conversions?
(i) Benzene to biphenyl
(ii) Iodoform to acetylene
  1. (i) Benzene → bromobenzene: C6H6 + Br2 (FeBr3) → C6H5Br + HBr. Then Fittig reaction: 2C6H5Br + 2Na (dry ether) → C6H5–C6H5 + 2NaBr. (Ullmann: 2C6H5I + 2Cu, heat, gives the same product.)
    [1]
  2. (ii) Heating iodoform with finely divided silver removes the iodine: 2CHI3 + 6Ag → HC≡CH + 6AgI.
    [1]
Answer
(i) C6H6 → C6H5Br → C6H5–C6H5 (Fittig). (ii) 2CHI3 + 6Ag → C2H2 + 6AgI

Concepts to recall

  • Wurtz: 2RX + 2Na → R–R; Wurtz–Fittig: ArX + RX + 2Na → Ar–R; Fittig: 2ArX + 2Na → Ar–Ar.
  • Heating a trihalomethane with Ag powder removes the halogen as AgX, joining the two carbons: 2CHX3 + 6Ag → HC≡CH + 6AgX.
  • Step 1 for biphenyl, halogenation of benzene, is electrophilic substitution with a Lewis acid catalyst.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (3)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q12
Question 12 · Specimen 2027[3]
Also draws on: also the nitrile/isonitrile products (Ch 9).
Two students Rehan and Ananya were given the same compound, alkyl halide (C2H5Cl) by their chemistry teacher and asked to perform different reactions.
• Rehan was instructed to use alcoholic KCN as a reagent and Ananya was instructed to use alcoholic AgCN.
• Rehan observed a compound was formed that could be converted to a carboxylic acid on hydrolysis but Ananya could not obtain the same acid.
• The teacher explained that the difference was due to the bonding behaviour of cyanide ions in the reagents.
Write the chemical equations of the reactions and the IUPAC names of the compounds formed by Rehan and Ananya.
  1. Rehan: C2H5Cl + KCN (alc.) → C2H5–CN + KCl. KCN is ionic: the carbon end of CN− attacks, giving the nitrile, propanenitrile. Hydrolysis: C2H5CN + 2H2O → C2H5COOH + NH3 (propanoic acid).
    [1½]
  2. Ananya: C2H5Cl + AgCN (alc.) → C2H5–NC + AgCl. AgCN is covalent: the nitrogen lone pair attacks, giving the isocyanide, isocyanoethane (ethyl isocyanide, ethyl carbylamine). Its hydrolysis gives ethanamine + methanoic acid, not propanoic acid.
    [1½]
Answer
Rehan: propanenitrile, C2H5CN (→ propanoic acid). Ananya: isocyanoethane, C2H5NC

Concepts to recall

  • Cyanide ion is an ambident nucleophile (C or N). Ionic KCN attacks through C; covalent AgCN through N.
  • Nitrile hydrolysis: RCN → RCOOH + NH3; isocyanide hydrolysis: RNC → RNH2 + HCOOH (acid hydrolysis).
  • Isocyanides (carbylamines) have a foul smell and are reduced by LiAlH4 to secondary amines (N-methyl).
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (3)ISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q15
Question 15 · Board 2026[3]
Also draws on: part (ii) is an alcohol reacting with SOCl2 (Ch 7).
Write the chemical reactions when: (i) Chlorobenzene is heated with conc. HNO3 in the presence of conc. H2SO4. (ii) Ethyl alcohol is treated with SOCl2 in the presence of pyridine. (iii) Chloroform is slowly oxidised with oxygen in the presence of sunlight.
  1. (i) Nitration at the o- and p-positions, the para isomer being the major product: C6H5Cl + HNO3 → ClC6H4NO2 + H2O (the product is a mixture of p-ClC6H4NO2, major, and o-ClC6H4NO2, minor)
    [1]
  2. (ii) C2H5OH + SOCl2 → C2H5Cl + SO2 + HCl (pyridine takes up the HCl)
    [1]
  3. (iii) 2CHCl3 + O2 → 2COCl2 + 2HCl (phosgene, carbonyl chloride)
    [1]
Answer
(i) o- and p-nitrochlorobenzene; (ii) C2H5Cl + SO2 + HCl; (iii) COCl2 (phosgene) + HCl

Concepts to recall

  • Halogens are deactivating but o,p-directing in electrophilic substitution: the −I effect slows the reaction, the +M (lone-pair) effect directs to o and p.
  • SOCl2 is the best reagent for R–OH → R–Cl because both by-products (SO2, HCl) are gases.
  • Chloroform is stored in dark bottles filled to the brim because air and light oxidise it to poisonous phosgene.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (3)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q14
Question 14 · Specimen 2026[3]
Also draws on: one part each from Ch 6, Ch 7 (Williamson) and Ch 9 (carbylamine).
Write the structural formula of the major product formed in each of the following reactions.
(i) C6H5ONa + C2H5Cl →
(ii) C2H5NH2 + CHCl3 + KOH(alc.) → (warm)
(iii) CH3CH2CH2Cl + NaI → (acetone + heat)
  1. (i) Williamson ether synthesis: the phenoxide displaces chloride from the primary halide: C6H5ONa + C2H5Cl → C6H5OC2H5 + NaCl. Product: ethoxybenzene (phenetole), C6H5–O–CH2CH3.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Carbylamine reaction (test for primary amines): C2H5NH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH → C2H5NC + 3KCl + 3H2O. Product: ethyl isocyanide, CH3CH2–N+≡C−, foul smelling.
    [1]
  3. (iii) Finkelstein reaction: CH3CH2CH2Cl + NaI → CH3CH2CH2I + NaCl (NaCl precipitates in dry acetone, driving the equilibrium). Product: 1-iodopropane.
    [1]
Answer
(i) C6H5OC2H5 (ethoxybenzene); (ii) CH3CH2NC; (iii) CH3CH2CH2I
Common slip In (i), writing diethyl ether: the phenyl ring of C6H5ONa stays in the product, so the ether is C6H5–O–C2H5.
Marking note CISCE’s key gives CH3CH2–O–CH2CH3 for (i), which is wrong: the reactants contain a phenyl ring and no second ethyl-oxygen unit; sodium phenoxide with ethyl chloride gives ethoxybenzene. Parts (ii) and (iii) of the key are correct.

Concepts to recall

  • Williamson synthesis: RONa + R′X → ROR′ (SN2; use a primary halide, otherwise elimination competes). With sodium phenoxide it gives a phenyl alkyl ether (Ar–O–R).
  • Carbylamine test: only primary amines (aliphatic or aromatic) + CHCl3 + alc. KOH, warm, give the offensive-smelling isocyanide (R–N≡C).
  • Finkelstein (Cl/Br → I, NaI in dry acetone) and Swarts (→ F, AgF or Hg2F2) are halogen-exchange SN2 reactions.
