ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii) · Specimen 2027[1]
Glucose molecules react with (x) number of molecules of phenyl hydrazine to yield glucosazone. The melting point of glucosazone is 206°C. The value of (x) is:
(a)one
(b)two
(c)three
(d)four
  1. Glucose has a CHO group. The first molecule of phenylhydrazine forms the phenylhydrazone; the second oxidises the neighbouring CH(OH) group to C=O (itself giving aniline and NH3); the third condenses with this new carbonyl to give the bis-phenylhydrazone, the osazone. Hence x = 3.
    [1]
Answer
(c) three

Concepts to recall

  • Osazone formation: 3 C6H5NHNH2 per sugar molecule (one is lost as aniline + ammonia).
  • Glucose, fructose and mannose give the same osazone because C-1 and C-2 are the only carbons involved.
  • The osazone is a yellow crystalline solid; its melting point (206 °C) helps identify the sugar.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(v)
Question 1(B)(v) · Board 2026[1]
Which one of the following nitrogen bases is absent in RNA?
(a)Adenine
(b)Guanine
(c)Uracil
(d)Thymine
  1. RNA contains A, G, C and uracil; thymine (5-methyluracil) occurs only in DNA, so thymine is absent in RNA.
    [1]
Answer
(d) Thymine

Concepts to recall

  • DNA: A, G, C, T and deoxyribose; RNA: A, G, C, U and ribose.
  • Base pairing: A=T (two H-bonds) and G≡C (three) in DNA; A pairs with U in RNA.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv) · Specimen 2026[1]
Hydrolysis of sucrose is called:
(a)esterification.
(b)saponification.
(c)hydration.
(d)inversion.
  1. Sucrose is dextrorotatory (+66·5°). On hydrolysis with dilute acid or invertase it gives an equimolar mixture of D-(+)-glucose (+52·5°) and D-(−)-fructose (−92°), whose net rotation is laevo (−20°). The change of sign of rotation is why the hydrolysis is called inversion.
    [1]
Answer
(d) inversion.

Concepts to recall

  • C12H22O11 + H2O → C6H12O6 (glucose) + C6H12O6 (fructose); the mixture is called invert sugar.
  • Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar (the anomeric carbons of both units are joined in the glycosidic link).
  • Saponification is the alkaline hydrolysis of fats; esterification is acid + alcohol.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
Which one of the following statements is correct?
(a)All amino acids are optically inactive.
(b)All amino acids are optically active.
(c)All amino acids are optically active except glutamic acid.
(d)All amino acids are optically active except glycine.
  1. The α-carbon of every natural amino acid other than glycine carries four different groups (H, NH2, COOH and R), so it is chiral and the acid is optically active. In glycine R = H, the α-carbon carries two identical H atoms and glycine is optically inactive. Option (d).
    [1]
Answer
(d) All amino acids are optically active except glycine

Concepts to recall

  • A chiral (asymmetric) carbon has four different substituents; glycine, NH2CH2COOH, has two H’s on Cα.
  • Natural α-amino acids belong to the L-series (−NH2 on the left in the Fischer projection).
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv) · Board 2025[1]
Also draws on: Tollens test is Ch 8.
Which one of the following does NOT form a silver mirror on heating with Tollen’s reagent?
(a)Glucose
(b)Fructose
(c)Sucrose
(d)Lactose
  1. A sugar reduces Tollens’ reagent only if it has a free (hemiacetal) aldehyde/anomeric –OH. Glucose has a free CHO; fructose isomerises to glucose and mannose in the basic reagent (an α-hydroxy ketone, through an enediol); lactose has a free hemiacetal group in its glucose unit.
    [½]
  2. In sucrose the glycosidic link joins C-1 of glucose to C-2 of fructose, so both anomeric carbons are tied up: a non-reducing sugar, no silver mirror.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Sucrose

Concepts to recall

  • Reducing sugars (free anomeric carbon): glucose, fructose, maltose, lactose. Non-reducing: sucrose.
  • Sucrose = α-D-glucose (C-1) –O– β-D-fructose (C-2); hydrolysis gives invert sugar (glucose + fructose).
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)CISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv) · Specimen 2025[1]
Most of the naturally occurring α-amino acids are optically active and have L-configuration. Which of the following amino acids is NOT asymmetric?
(a)Valine
(b)Alanine
(c)Glycine
(d)Leucine
  1. Optical activity needs a chiral α-carbon with four different groups. Glycine is H2N–CH2–COOH: its α-carbon carries two H, so it is achiral. Valine, alanine and leucine have H, NH2, COOH and a different side chain.
    [1]
Answer
(c) Glycine

Concepts to recall

  • Glycine is the only common α-amino acid that is optically inactive (R = H).
  • Natural amino acids are L (NH2 on the left in the Fischer projection with COOH at the top); the L/D label is relative to glyceraldehyde.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q16
Question 16 · Specimen 2027[3]
(i) Identify the non-reducing sugar which on hydrolysis gives two reducing monosaccharides.
(ii) Which structure of protein is normally unaffected during denaturation of protein?
(iii) Name the vitamins whose deficiency cause the following diseases.
  (a) Rickets   (b) Night blindness
  1. (i) Sucrose (C12H22O11): the glycosidic link joins C-1 of α-D-glucose to C-2 of β-D-fructose, so both reducing groups are tied up and it is non-reducing; on hydrolysis (H+, invertase) it gives glucose + fructose, both reducing.
    [1]
  2. (ii) The primary structure (the sequence of amino acids joined by peptide bonds) is unaffected: denaturation destroys the secondary and tertiary (and quaternary) structure by breaking hydrogen bonds and other weak interactions, but not the peptide bonds.
    [1]
  3. (iii) (a) Rickets: vitamin D (½); (b) night blindness: vitamin A (½).
    [1]
Answer
(i) Sucrose; (ii) primary structure; (iii) (a) vitamin D, (b) vitamin A

Concepts to recall

  • Sucrose is dextrorotatory; on hydrolysis (inversion) it gives an equimolar mixture of D-glucose and D-fructose that is laevorotatory (invert sugar).
  • Denaturation = loss of secondary/tertiary structure (egg boiling, curdling of milk); peptide bonds are intact; primary structure unchanged.
  • Vitamins: A – night blindness, xerophthalmia; B1 – beri-beri; B12 – pernicious anaemia; C – scurvy; D – rickets; E – fragility of RBC; K – blood clotting.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q14
Question 14 · Board 2026[3]
(i) Give one example each of fibrous protein and globular protein.
(ii) The structures of two amino acids, glycine (H2N–CH2–COOH) and alanine (H2N–CH(CH3)–COOH), are given. Draw the peptide linkage between these two amino acids.
(iii) Justify that glucose is an aldose form of sugar, with the help of a chemical reaction.
  1. (i) Fibrous: keratin (or collagen, myosin); globular: albumin (or insulin, haemoglobin).
    [1]
  2. (ii) The –COOH of glycine condenses with the –NH2 of alanine with loss of H2O: H2N-CH2-CO-NH-CH(CH3)-COOH (glycylalanine); the –CO–NH– bond is the peptide linkage.
    [1]
  3. (iii) Glucose is oxidised by bromine water to gluconic acid (same six carbons, no loss of carbon): CHO(CHOH)4CH2OH + [O] → COOH(CHOH)4CH2OH. Only an aldehyde group (–CHO) is oxidised to –COOH under these mild conditions, so glucose contains –CHO: it is an aldose.
    [1]
Answer
(i) keratin; albumin; (ii) –CO–NH– bond joining Gly and Ala; (iii) Br2/H2O gives gluconic acid, so glucose has a –CHO group
Marking note Any one correct chemical test (Br2 water, Tollens’, Fehling’s, or HCN) earns the mark. The peptide may be drawn as Gly–Ala or Ala–Gly.

Concepts to recall

  • Fibrous proteins (keratin, collagen, myosin) are insoluble, with chains parallel; globular proteins (albumin, insulin, haemoglobin) are coiled to a sphere and water-soluble.
  • Peptide bond: amide link –CO–NH– formed between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of the next.
  • Evidence for the –CHO group in glucose: Br2/H2O (gluconic acid), Tollens’ and Fehling’s tests, HCN (cyanohydrin), and acetylation to a pentaacetate (five –OH).
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q15
Question 15 · Specimen 2026[3]
Also draws on: part (iii) is osmosis (Ch 1).
(i) Which vitamin deficiency is responsible for xerophthalmia and night blindness?
(ii) What are the products formed upon hydrolysis of lactose?
(iii) When grapes are placed in a salt solution, they tend to shrink. Why?
  1. (i) Vitamin A (retinol); its deficiency causes xerophthalmia (hardening of the cornea) and night blindness.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Lactose (a disaccharide, β-D-galactose 1→4 D-glucose) hydrolyses to D-glucose and D-galactose: C12H22O11 + H2O → C6H12O6 + C6H12O6.
    [1]
  3. (iii) The salt solution is hypertonic to the cell sap of the grape. Water passes out of the grape through its semi-permeable skin by exosmosis, so the grape loses water and shrinks.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Vitamin A; (ii) glucose and galactose; (iii) exosmosis in a hypertonic solution

Concepts to recall

  • Fat-soluble vitamins: A (xerophthalmia, night blindness), D (rickets), E, K (blood clotting). Water-soluble: B (beri-beri, B1), C (scurvy).
  • Hydrolysis of disaccharides: sucrose → glucose + fructose; maltose → 2 glucose; lactose → glucose + galactose.
  • Osmosis: solvent flows from lower to higher solute concentration through a semi-permeable membrane: into a hypotonic surrounding (swelling), out of a hypertonic one (shrinking).
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q17
Question 17 · Board 2025 Imp.[3]
(i) What is observed when D-glucose is treated with bromine water? Write the chemical reaction. (ii) Though vitamin C is found in fruits and vegetables, it cannot be stored in our body. Why? (iii) What is a peptide linkage? Write an example to show peptide linkage.
  1. (i) The brown colour of bromine water is discharged; glucose (aldehyde group) is oxidised to gluconic acid: CH2OH(CHOH)4CHO + Br2 + H2O → CH2OH(CHOH)4COOH + 2HBr. This shows that the carbonyl group is an aldehyde (not a ketone).
    [1]
  2. (ii) Vitamin C is water soluble; the excess is excreted in the urine and cannot be stored, so it must be supplied regularly in the diet (fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K are stored in fat).
    [1]
  3. (iii) The peptide linkage is the amide bond, –CO–NH–, formed between the –COOH of one α-amino acid and the –NH2 of another with loss of water. Example: glycine + alanine → glycylalanine, H2N–CH2–CO–NH–CH(CH3)–COOH + H2O.
    [1]
Answer
(i) bromine colour discharged, gluconic acid formed; (ii) water soluble, excreted; (iii) –CO–NH– amide bond, e.g. glycylalanine

Concepts to recall

  • Br2/H2O oxidises the –CHO of glucose to –COOH (gluconic acid); with HNO3 it gives saccharic acid; this test shows an aldehyde, and fructose does not give it.
  • Water-soluble vitamins (B, C) are excreted; fat-soluble (A, D, E, K) are stored.
  • Peptide bond: planar amide link; polypeptide = many amino acids joined this way (proteins).
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q17
Question 17 · Board 2025[3]
(i) The structure of amino acid exists in the following two forms (see figure: R–CH(NH2)–COOH ⇋ R–CH(+NH3)–COO− in aqueous solution). The above structure is an example of ____________. If the side chain R is replaced by hydrogen, the amino acid is known as ____________.
(ii) Janice notices that her gums bleed while brushing and eating food. Name the water soluble vitamin which she should consume to prevent bleeding of gums.
(iii) Which linkage holds two units of monosaccharides in a disaccharide?
  1. (i) The dipolar ion carrying both +NH3 and COO− is a zwitterion. With R = H, NH2CH2COOH is glycine.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Bleeding gums (scurvy) are due to deficiency of vitamin C (ascorbic acid), a water-soluble vitamin (citrus fruits, amla).
    [1]
  3. (iii) The two monosaccharide units are joined by a glycosidic linkage (C–O–C) formed by loss of water between their hydroxyl groups.
    [1]
Answer
(i) zwitterion; glycine; (ii) vitamin C; (iii) glycosidic linkage.

Concepts to recall

  • Amino acids exist as zwitterions (internal salt) in solution: they are amphoteric and have high melting points. At the isoelectric point the net charge is zero.
  • Vitamin C deficiency → scurvy; B-complex and C are water-soluble, A, D, E, K are fat-soluble.
  • Sucrose (glucose C-1 to fructose C-2), maltose (glucose C-1 to C-4) and lactose (galactose to glucose) all have glycosidic links; peptide bond joins amino acids.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)CISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q17
Question 17 · Specimen 2025[3]
The structures of glycine and alanine are H2N–CH2–COOH and H2N–CH(CH3)–COOH. (i) Show the peptide bond linkage in glycylalanine. (ii) What products would be formed when a nucleotide from DNA containing thymine is hydrolysed? (iii) What are reducing sugars? Give an example.
  1. (i) The COOH of glycine condenses with the NH2 of alanine, losing H2O: H2N–CH2–COOH + H2N–CH(CH3)–COOH → H2N–CH2–CO–NH–CH(CH3)–COOH + H2O; the –CO–NH– link is the peptide bond (glycine is N-terminal). (1)
    [1]
  2. (ii) A DNA nucleotide has phosphate–2-deoxy-β-D-ribose–base. On hydrolysis a thymine-containing nucleotide gives thymine (base), β-D-2-deoxyribose (sugar) and phosphoric acid (phosphate). (1)
    [1]
  3. (iii) Reducing sugars are carbohydrates that reduce Tollens’ reagent and Fehling’s (or Benedict’s) solution because they have a free aldehyde or α-hydroxy ketone group (free hemiacetal OH). Example: glucose (also fructose, maltose, lactose). (1)
    [1]
Answer
(i) H2N–CH2–CO–NH–CH(CH3)–COOH; (ii) thymine + 2-deoxyribose + phosphate; (iii) sugars reducing Tollens’/Fehling’s reagent, e.g. glucose
Marking note CISCE’s key for (ii) lists “the base residue A, G, C and T + phosphate”; for a nucleotide containing thymine, the base is thymine only (the others are in other nucleotides), so thymine, deoxyribose and phosphate is the precise answer.

Concepts to recall

  • Peptide bond = amide link –CO–NH– between the α-COOH of one amino acid and the α-NH2 of the next; named from the N-terminal end.
  • Nucleoside = base + sugar; nucleotide = base + sugar + phosphate. DNA sugar: 2-deoxyribose; bases A, G, C, T; RNA: ribose; A, G, C, U.
  • Sucrose is non-reducing (glycosidic link between both anomeric carbons); maltose, lactose are reducing.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q14
Question 14 · Board 2024[3]
(i) Aradhana visits a physician as she is suffering from rickets and joint pain. Which fat-soluble vitamin should the physician prescribe to her?
(ii) Somesh put few drops of vinegar in milk. What change do you think he observed in the milk after some time? What is this phenomenon known as?
(iii) Name the product of hydrolysis of sucrose. Is it a reducing sugar or a non-reducing sugar?
  1. (i) Vitamin D (calciferol). Its deficiency causes rickets in children (soft, bent bones) and osteomalacia in adults, because it is needed for calcium absorption.
    [1]
  2. (ii) The milk curdles (the protein casein separates as a solid curd). The acid of the vinegar changes the pH and disturbs the hydrogen bonds and the globular structure of the protein: this is denaturation (coagulation) of the protein.
    [1]
  3. (iii) Sucrose is hydrolysed (dilute acid or invertase) to a mixture of D-(+)-glucose and D-(−)-fructose, called invert sugar: C12H22O11 + H2O → C6H12O6 + C6H12O6. The product (glucose and fructose) is a reducing sugar mixture, although sucrose itself is a non-reducing sugar (its glycosidic link uses both anomeric carbons).
    [1]
Answer
(i) Vitamin D; (ii) milk curdles, denaturation of protein; (iii) glucose + fructose (invert sugar), reducing; sucrose itself is non-reducing.
Marking note In (iii) the phrase ‘Is it a reducing sugar’ is read for the hydrolysis product; the answer states both the product’s and sucrose’s character so that either reading earns the mark.

Concepts to recall

  • Fat-soluble vitamins: A (night blindness), D (rickets), E, K (blood clotting). Water-soluble: B (beri-beri B1), C (scurvy).
  • Denaturation: loss of the secondary and tertiary structure (not the primary) on heating, change of pH or addition of salts; examples: boiling an egg, curdling of milk.
  • Sucrose [α-D-glucose 1→2 β-D-fructose]: dextrorotatory (+66·5°); after hydrolysis the mixture is laevorotatory (−20°), hence ‘invert’ sugar.
