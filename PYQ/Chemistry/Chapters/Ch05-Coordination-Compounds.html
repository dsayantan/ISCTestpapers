What to revise

  • Every paper has a coordination question, often as a 5-mark choice: Werner structure from AgNO3 precipitate and conductance (CoCl3·4NH3, CoCl3·6NH3), IUPAC names and formulae, geometrical isomers.
  • VBT and CFT: hybridisation, inner against outer orbital, magnetic behaviour, unpaired electrons, t2g/eg configurations for d4 and d6, CFSE, tetrahedral splitting, spectrochemical series.
  • Isomerism: linkage, ionisation, hydrate and geometrical (cis-platin); a chemical test to tell ionisation isomers apart.
  • Habit: count the oxidation state first, then the ligand field strength, then fill the orbitals.

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii) · Specimen 2027[1]
Cobalt (III) chloride forms several octahedral complexes with ammonia. Which of the following will NOT give a test for chloride ions with silver nitrate solution at 25°C?
(a)CoCl3·6NH3
(b)CoCl3·5NH3
(c)CoCl3·4NH3
(d)CoCl3·3NH3
  1. Each complex is octahedral, so six ligands are in the coordination sphere. Only Cl− outside the sphere is free to precipitate AgCl. CoCl3·6NH3 = [Co(NH3)6]Cl3 (3 free Cl−); CoCl3·5NH3 = [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2 (2); CoCl3·4NH3 = [Co(NH3)4Cl2]Cl (1); CoCl3·3NH3 = [Co(NH3)3Cl3], all three Cl− are bonded to Co: no free chloride, no precipitate.
    [1]
Answer
(d) CoCl3·3NH3

Concepts to recall

  • Werner: primary valence (oxidation state, ionisable) and secondary valence (coordination number, non-ionisable).
  • Ligands inside the square brackets do not ionise; only the counter-ions give a precipitate with Ag+.
  • Number of free Cl− = number of ions − 1 of the complex: this is how conductivity data are used (Q20(iii)).
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i) · Board 2026[1]
Which one of the following is a tetrahedral complex as well as diamagnetic in nature?
(a)[Ni(CN)4]2−
(b)[NiCl4]2−
(c)[Cu(NH3)4]2+
(d)[Ni(CO)4]
  1. [Ni(CN)4]2−: Ni2+ (3d8) with strong-field CN− pairs its electrons, dsp2: square planar, diamagnetic. [NiCl4]2−: sp3, tetrahedral but paramagnetic (two unpaired electrons). [Cu(NH3)4]2+: Cu2+ (3d9) has one unpaired electron.
    [½]
  2. [Ni(CO)4]: Ni(0) is 3d10 (4s electrons pushed into 3d by strong-field CO), all paired; sp3 hybridisation gives a tetrahedral, diamagnetic complex.
    [½]
Answer
(d) [Ni(CO)4]

Concepts to recall

  • Tetrahedral complexes are sp3; Ni(0) with CO (carbonyl, strong field) is 3d10, hence diamagnetic.
  • [Ni(CN)4]2− is the stock example of a square planar low-spin d8 complex; [NiCl4]2− the stock tetrahedral paramagnetic one.
  • Magnetic moment μ = √(n(n + 2)) BM: diamagnetic means n = 0.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i) · Specimen 2026[1]
The sum of coordination number and oxidation number of metal ‘M’ in the complex [M(en)2Cl2] is:
(a)6
(b)8
(c)9
(d)10
  1. Coordination number: en is bidentate, so 2 × 2 + 2 = 6.
    [½]
  2. Oxidation number: en is neutral and each Cl− is −1. With the complex taken as the monopositive [M(en)2Cl2]+ (as in [Fe(en)2Cl2]Cl of Question 21), M is +3. Sum = 6 + 3 = 9.
    [½]
Answer
(c) 9
Marking note The charge of the complex is not printed, so strictly the oxidation number is not fixed (a neutral [M(en)2Cl2] would give M = +2 and a sum of 8). The marking scheme’s (c) corresponds to M(III), the state used in Question 21(ii). Examiners award the mark for (c).

Concepts to recall

  • Coordination number = number of donor atoms attached to the metal (a bidentate ligand counts twice), not the number of ligands.
  • Oxidation number = charge on the complex − sum of the charges of the ligands.
  • Common neutral ligands: NH3, H2O, en, CO; common anionic: Cl−, CN−, C2O42−.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii) · Specimen 2026[1]
Which of the following orders is correct in spectrochemical series of ligands?
(P) I− < NH3 < CN− < CO
(Q) CO < I− < NH3 < CN−
(R) NH3 < CO < CN− < I−
(S) F− < NH3 < CN− < CO
(a)Only (Q) and (S) are correct.
(b)Only (P) and (S) are correct.
(c)Only (P) and (Q) are correct.
(d)Only (P) and (R) are correct.
  1. The series is I− < Br− < Cl− < F− < H2O < NH3 < en < CN− < CO. (P) and (S) both climb the series correctly; (Q) puts the strongest ligand CO first and (R) puts I−, the weakest, last. So only (P) and (S) are correct.
    [1]
Answer
(b) Only (P) and (S) are correct.

Concepts to recall

  • Spectrochemical series, weak to strong field: I− < Br− < Cl− < F− < OH− < H2O < NH3 < en < CN− < CO.
  • Strong-field ligands give a large Δo (low spin complexes); weak-field ligands a small Δo (high spin).
  • Remember it as halides (weakest) → O/N donors → C donors (strongest, π-acceptors).
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(ii)
Question 1(A)(ii) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [ethanol, [Fe(H2O)6]2+, anode, d2sp3, Cannizzaro, reducing, cathode, sp3d2, iodine, [Fe(CN)6]4−, methanol, aldol, oxidising, manganese dichloride].
The coordination compound ______ is an example of inner orbital complex and the hybridisation involved in the complex is ______.
  1. In [Fe(CN)6]4−, Fe2+ is 3d6. CN− is a strong-field ligand, so the six d electrons pair up in three 3d orbitals, leaving two 3d orbitals vacant. Two 3d + one 4s + three 4p orbitals hybridise, giving d2sp3 hybrids: an inner orbital (low-spin, diamagnetic) complex. [Fe(H2O)6]2+ has a weak-field ligand and is outer orbital (sp3d2).
    [1]
Answer
[Fe(CN)6]4−; d2sp3
Marking note The compound and its hybridisation must match: [Fe(CN)6]4− with d2sp3.

Concepts to recall

  • Inner orbital complex: uses (n−1)d orbitals (d2sp3), strong-field ligand (CN−, NH3, en, CO); outer orbital: nd orbitals (sp3d2), weak-field ligand (F−, H2O, Cl−).
  • Spectrochemical series, weakest to strongest: I− < Cl− < F− < H2O < NH3 < en < CN− < CO.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(v)
Question 1(B)(v) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
What type of structural isomerism exists between the following pair of coordination compounds? [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]Cl·2H2O and [Cr(H2O)5Cl]Cl2·H2O
(a)Ionisation isomerism
(b)Hydrate isomerism
(c)Coordination isomerism
(d)Linkage isomerism
  1. Both have the same formula, but the number of water molecules inside the coordination sphere differs: four ligand H2O + 2 Cl in the first, five H2O + 1 Cl in the second, the rest being water of crystallisation. This is hydrate (solvate) isomerism: option (b).
    [1]
Answer
(b) Hydrate isomerism
Marking note Some books call the pair ionisation isomers, since Cl− moves in and out; the exchanged species is water, so hydrate isomerism is the intended answer.

Concepts to recall

  • Hydrate isomers differ in how many water molecules are ligands and how many are lattice water (e.g. violet [Cr(H2O)6]Cl3 and green [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]Cl·2H2O).
  • Ionisation isomers exchange an anion between the sphere and the outside (SO42− / Cl−); here no ionisable group other than Cl− and H2O is exchanged in kind.

Question 1(A)(ii) · Board 2025[1]
The complex compound [Ni(CO)4] is ____________ in shape and nickel is in ____________ oxidation state in this complex compound.
  1. CO is a neutral ligand, so the oxidation number of Ni = 0 (charge on complex 0 = Ni + 4 × 0) → zero.
    [½]
  2. Ni(0) is 3d84s2, rearranged to 3d10; with four CO ligands the hybridisation is sp3, so the shape is tetrahedral (diamagnetic).
    [½]
Answer
tetrahedral; zero

Concepts to recall

  • Carbonyl ligands are neutral: O.N. of the metal = charge on the complex.
  • Four-coordinate sp3 gives tetrahedral; four-coordinate dsp2 (e.g. [Ni(CN)4]2−) gives square planar.
  • CO is a strong-field ligand: Ni(0) pairs its electrons as d10 so [Ni(CO)4] is diamagnetic.

The coordination number and the oxidation state of the central metal ‘D’ in the complex [D(en)2(H2O)2]Cl3 are:
(a)6 and 2 respectively
(b)4 and 2 respectively
(c)3 and 6 respectively
(d)6 and 3 respectively

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)CISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(v)
Question 1(B)(v) · Specimen 2025[1]
Which one of the following complex ions has geometrical isomers?
(a)[Ni(NH3)5Br]+
(b)[Cr(NH3)4en]3+
(c)[Co(NH3)2(en)2]3+
(d)[Co(NH3)6]3+
  1. Octahedral Ma5b, Ma6 and Ma4(AA) (one bidentate ligand spans two adjacent sites, the rest are alike) have a single arrangement. Ma2(AA)2, i.e. [Co(NH3)2(en)2]3+, can have the two NH3 either at 90° (cis) or at 180° (trans): geometrical isomers.
    [1]
Answer
(c) [Co(NH3)2(en)2]3+

Concepts to recall

  • Octahedral types showing cis–trans: Ma4b2, Ma2(AA)2; fac–mer: Ma3b3. Ma5b and Ma6 never do.
  • cis-[Co(en)2X2] is also optically active (it has non-superimposable mirror images); the trans form is not.
  • Tetrahedral complexes show no geometrical isomerism; square planar Ma2b2 does.

  1. EDTA is a hexadentate chelating ligand: it forms stable soluble chelates with Pb2+ and is used in lead poisoning. Cisplatin, cis-[Pt(NH3)2Cl2], is an anticancer drug used in the treatment of cancer.

Question 1(B)(ii) · Board 2024[1]
The metal complex ion that is paramagnetic is: (Atomic number of Fe = 26, Cu = 29, Co = 27 and Ni = 28)
(a)[Fe(CN)4]2−
(b)[Co(NH3)6]3+
(c)[Ni(CN)4]2−
(d)[Cu(NH3)4]2+
  1. Paramagnetism needs unpaired electrons. [Co(NH3)6]3+: Co3+ 3d6, strong-field NH3 (d2sp3), all paired: diamagnetic. [Ni(CN)4]2−: Ni2+ 3d8, CN− strong field, dsp2 square planar, all paired: diamagnetic. [Cu(NH3)4]2+: Cu2+ 3d9, one unpaired electron (n = 1, μ = √3 = 1·73 BM): paramagnetic. [Fe(CN)4]2− is Fe2+ with strong-field CN−; the formula as printed is a non-standard four-coordinate ion, so it cannot be relied on, and the only ion whose single unpaired electron is certain is the d9 copper complex.
    [1]
Answer
(d) [Cu(NH3)4]2+
Marking note The formula [Fe(CN)4]2− as printed is unusual (the common ion is [Fe(CN)6]4−, which is diamagnetic); in either reading the only unambiguous paramagnetic ion is the d9 copper complex, so (d) stands.

Concepts to recall

  • μ = √(n(n+2)) BM, where n is the number of unpaired electrons.
  • Strong-field ligands (CN−, CO, en, NH3 for Co3+) pair electrons; weak-field (F−, H2O) do not.
  • Any d9 ion (Cu2+) always has exactly one unpaired electron, whatever the ligand.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (6)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q4
Question 4 · Specimen 2027[2]
(i) Answer the following questions with respect to the coordination complex ion [Cr(NH3)6]3+
  (a) What is the hybridisation state of the central metal atom?
  (b) Comment on the magnetic nature of the complex ion.
OR
(ii) Observe the diagram of splitting of d-orbitals in octahedral field and answer the following questions.
  (a) Identify the electronic configuration of d5 ion, if Δo < P.
  (b) Comment on the relationship between crystal field stabilisation energy (Δo) and the strength of the ligand.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) Cr (Z = 24): [Ar] 3d54s1; Cr3+ is 3d3. The three electrons occupy three separate 3d orbitals (Hund’s rule), leaving two 3d, the 4s and three 4p orbitals vacant. These six orbitals hybridise to receive six NH3 lone pairs: d2sp3 (inner orbital, octahedral).
    [1]
  2. (b) Three 3d electrons remain unpaired (n = 3), μ = √(n(n+2)) = √15 = 3·87 BM. The complex ion is paramagnetic.
    [1]
Answer
(a) d2sp3; (b) paramagnetic (three unpaired electrons)

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) If Δo < P (the pairing energy), it costs less to promote an electron to eg than to pair it in t2g. The five electrons therefore go one each into all five orbitals (Hund): t2g3 eg2, a high-spin complex with five unpaired electrons.
    [1]
  2. (b) The splitting Δo increases with the strength of the ligand (spectrochemical series, I− < Br− < Cl− < F− < H2O < NH3 < en < CN− < CO): a strong-field ligand gives a large Δo (low-spin complex), a weak-field ligand a small one. CFSE = (−0·4nt2g + 0·6neg)Δo + pairing terms.
    [1]
Answer
(a) t2g3 eg2 (high spin); (b) the stronger the ligand, the larger Δo (and CFSE); strong-field ligands give low-spin complexes
Marking note The paper writes ‘crystal field stabilisation energy (Δo)’; Δo is the crystal field splitting energy, but the intended answer (larger Δo for stronger ligand) is the same.

Concepts to recall

  • Valence bond theory: six ligands, octahedral. Inner orbital (d2sp3) uses (n−1)d; outer orbital (sp3d2) uses nd.
  • Cr3+ (d3) has exactly three unpaired electrons whatever the ligand, so there is no choice between high and low spin for d3.
  • μ = √(n(n+2)) BM; paramagnetic if n > 0, diamagnetic if n = 0.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q4
Question 4 · Board 2026[2]
Write the IUPAC names of the following complex compounds: (i) [Pt(NH3)4Cl2]SO4; (ii) K2[Ni(CN)4].
  1. (i) Charge on the complex cation = +2 (balances SO42−); 4 NH3 (neutral) and 2 Cl− give Pt = +2 + 2 = +4. Ligands in alphabetical order (ammine before chlorido): tetraamminedichloridoplatinum(IV) sulfate.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Complex anion [Ni(CN)4]2−: Ni = +2 (4 × (−1) + Ni = −2). Name: potassium tetracyanidonickelate(II).
    [1]
Answer
(i) Tetraamminedichloridoplatinum(IV) sulfate; (ii) Potassium tetracyanidonickelate(II)
Marking note “Tetraamminedichloroplatinum(IV) sulphate” (older spelling) is accepted.

Concepts to recall

  • IUPAC rules: cation named before anion; ligands in alphabetical order (prefixes di, tetra ignored); oxidation state of the metal in Roman numerals in brackets.
  • For an anionic complex the metal name ends in -ate (nickelate, ferrate, cuprate); neutral ligands: ammine, aqua, carbonyl; anionic ones end in -ido (chlorido, cyanido).
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q4
Question 4 · Specimen 2026[2]
Using IUPAC nomenclature, write the formula for each of the following:
(i) hexaamminecobalt (III) sulphate
(ii) tetraaquadichloridochromium (III) nitrate
  1. (i) Cation [Co(NH3)6]3+ (Co(III), six neutral NH3); anion SO42−. Balancing 2 × (+3) = 3 × (−2) gives [Co(NH3)6]2(SO4)3.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Cr(III) with four neutral H2O and two Cl−: charge +3 − 2 = +1, so the cation is [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]+, balanced by one NO3−: [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]NO3.
    [1]
Answer
(i) [Co(NH3)6]2(SO4)3 (CISCE prints [Co(NH3)6]SO4); (ii) [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]NO3
Common slip Forgetting that Co3+ needs three sulphates for two cations, and writing 1:1.
Marking note CISCE’s key prints [Co(NH3)6]SO4 for (i), which is not electrically neutral (+3 against −2). The correct formula is [Co(NH3)6]2(SO4)3; a candidate who writes it should be credited. Part (ii) of the key is correct.

Concepts to recall

  • Writing the formula: central metal first, then ligands in alphabetical order (ignoring prefixes; anionic and neutral ligands mixed), all in square brackets; counter-ion outside.
  • Charge of complex ion = oxidation state + total charge of the ligands; the formula as a whole must be neutral.
  • Prefixes: di, tri, tetra, penta, hexa; Roman numeral = oxidation state of the metal. aqua = H2O, ammine = NH3, chlorido = Cl−.

Question 7 · Board 2025 Imp.[2]
Neha and Imran were given an assignment on the coordination complex [Co(NH3)6]Cl3. Neha found that the complex was of sp3d2 hybridisation and paramagnetic whereas Imran found that it was of d2sp3 hybridisation and diamagnetic. Evaluate the responses of Neha and Imran.
  1. In [Co(NH3)6]3+, Co is +3: 3d64s0. NH3 is a strong-field ligand, so the six 3d electrons pair up in three orbitals, leaving two 3d orbitals empty.
    [½]
  2. Two 3d, the 4s and three 4p orbitals hybridise, giving six d2sp3 hybrid orbitals (octahedral, inner orbital) that accept six NH3 lone pairs.
    [½]
  3. There are no unpaired electrons, so the complex is diamagnetic (μ = 0).
    [½]
  4. Imran is correct. Neha is wrong: sp3d2 and paramagnetism belong to a weak-field complex such as [CoF6]3−.
    [½]
Answer
Imran is correct (d2sp3, diamagnetic); Neha is incorrect

Concepts to recall

  • Strong-field ligand → pairing → low spin → (n−1)d orbitals used → d2sp3, inner orbital. Weak-field ligand → sp3d2, outer orbital.
  • Spin-only magnetic moment μ = √(n(n+2)) BM; n = 0 here.
  • Co3+: [Ar]3d6 (Co is [Ar]3d74s2; three electrons are lost).

Question 7 · Board 2025[2]
While conducting an experiment on coordination compounds, Shirin observed a white precipitate when AgNO3 solution was added to the aqueous solution of the complex [Co(NH3)3(en)SO4]Cl. How can the ionisation isomer of this complex be detected by a chemical test? Write the structure and the IUPAC name of the ionisation isomer.
  1. The given complex has Cl− outside the coordination sphere (it gives AgCl, white, with AgNO3) and SO42− inside. Its ionisation isomer exchanges the two: [Co(NH3)3(en)Cl]SO4 (structure of the complex ion in the figure).
    [½]
  2. IUPAC name: triamminechlorido(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(III) sulphate.
    [½]
  3. Test: add BaCl2 solution: the isomer gives a white precipitate of BaSO4 (SO42− is free) and no precipitate of AgCl with AgNO3, whereas the original complex gives AgCl but no BaSO4. Ba2+ + SO42− → BaSO4↓.
    [1]
Answer
Ionisation isomer: [Co(NH3)3(en)Cl]SO4, triamminechlorido(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(III) sulphate; it gives white BaSO4 with BaCl2 and no AgCl with AgNO3.
Marking note The figure shows one geometrical arrangement of the ligands; the paper does not specify one and any octahedral arrangement with correct ligands earns the structure mark.

Concepts to recall

  • Ionisation isomers give different ions in solution: the ligand inside the sphere is not free to precipitate.
  • Oxidation state of Co here: +3 (NH3, en neutral; SO42− inside, Cl− outside).
  • IUPAC order of ligands is alphabetical: ammine, chlorido, ethane-1,2-diamine; metal oxidation state in Roman numerals.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (6)CISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q7
Question 7 · Specimen 2025[2]
During chemistry class, a teacher wrote [Ni(CN)4]2− as a coordination complex ion on the board. The students were asked to find out the magnetic behaviour and shape of the complex. Pari, a student, wrote the answer paramagnetic and tetrahedral whereas another student Suhail wrote diamagnetic and square planar. Evaluate Pari’s and Suhail’s responses.
  1. Ni (Z = 28) is 3d84s2; Ni2+ is 3d8 (two unpaired electrons in a free ion). CN− is a strong-field ligand: it forces the two unpaired 3d electrons to pair, vacating one 3d orbital. (1)
    [1]
  2. One 3d, the 4s and two 4p orbitals hybridise to dsp2, giving four hybrid orbitals directed at the corners of a square, so the complex is square planar, with all electrons paired (n = 0): diamagnetic. Suhail is right; Pari is wrong (tetrahedral sp3 with two unpaired electrons would be paramagnetic, as in [NiCl4]2− with a weak-field ligand). (1)
    [1]
Answer
Suhail is correct: [Ni(CN)4]2− is diamagnetic and square planar (dsp2).

Concepts to recall

  • Valence bond theory: strong-field ligand → pairing → inner-orbital/dsp2; weak-field ligand → sp3, outer orbital.
  • μ = √(n(n+2)) BM; n = 0 means diamagnetic.
  • Four-coordinate d8: strong-field (CN−) gives square planar; weak-field (Cl−) gives tetrahedral.

Question 15 · Board 2025 Imp.[3]
(i) [Co(NH3)6]3+ is an inner orbital complex whereas [CoF6]3− is an outer orbital complex. Why? (Given: Atomic number of Co = 27)
(ii) A coordination compound with molecular formula CrCl3·6H2O precipitates two moles of AgCl with AgNO3 solution. Write the structural formula of the compound.
(iii) Explain why an aqueous solution of K4[Fe(CN)6] is not toxic whereas an aqueous solution of KCN is highly toxic.
  1. (i) Co3+ is 3d6. NH3 is a strong-field ligand: the electrons pair up and vacate two 3d orbitals, so d2sp3 hybridisation uses inner 3d orbitals (inner orbital, diamagnetic). F− is a weak-field ligand and cannot force pairing: the electrons stay unpaired, so the outer 4d orbitals are used (sp3d2, outer orbital, paramagnetic with four unpaired electrons).
    [1]
  2. (ii) Two Cl− are free (precipitate as 2AgCl), the third is in the coordination sphere with five H2O; the sixth water is outside: [Cr(H2O)5Cl]Cl2·H2O (Cr coordination number 6).
    [1]
  3. (iii) K4[Fe(CN)6] is a very stable complex: the six CN− ions are held firmly by coordinate bonds and the ionisation gives only K+ and [Fe(CN)6]4−, so practically no free CN− is released. KCN dissociates fully into K+ and free CN−, which is toxic.
    [1]
Answer
(i) strong-field NH3 pairs electrons (d2sp3); weak-field F− does not (sp3d2); (ii) [Cr(H2O)5Cl]Cl2·H2O; (iii) no free CN− from the stable complex

Concepts to recall

  • Strong field ligand: large crystal field splitting Δo > pairing energy → low-spin, inner orbital.
  • Ionisable (free) Cl− precipitates with Ag+; Cl− inside the bracket does not. Number of AgCl = number of free Cl−.
  • High formation constant of [Fe(CN)6]4− (β ~ 1035) explains its safety.

  1. (a) (1) d4 high spin: t2g3 eg1. (2) d6 low spin: t2g6 eg0.

(a) t2g3eg1; t2g6eg0. (b) −0·6 Δo; −2·4 Δo. (c) linkage; ionisation. (d) [Fe(H2O)6]2+

Concepts to recall

  • VBT: inner-orbital (low-spin) octahedral complexes use d2sp3 (3d, 4s, 4p); outer-orbital ones use sp3d2 (4s, 4p, 4d). NH3 is a strong-field ligand but Cr3+ (d3) needs no pairing.
