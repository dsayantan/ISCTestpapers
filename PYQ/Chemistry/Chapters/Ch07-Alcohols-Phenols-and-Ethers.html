ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (13)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(iv)
Question 1(A)(iv) · Specimen 2027[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [methyl alcohol, ethyl alcohol, paired, one, Cannizzaro, d–d, salicylaldehyde, Perkin’s, unpaired, f–f, salicylic acid, Kolbe’s, two, 193000, 96500, Reimer–Tiemann].
The product formed when sodium phenoxide is treated with CO2 at 400 K, under 4–7 atm pressure and then acidified with dilute HCl is ______. The reaction is known as ______ reaction.
  1. Sodium phenoxide + CO2 (400 K, 4–7 atm) gives sodium salicylate (carboxylation at the ortho position); acidification with HCl liberates salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid). This is Kolbe’s (Kolbe–Schmitt) reaction.
    [1]
Answer
salicylic acid; Kolbe’s

Concepts to recall

  • Kolbe: C6H5ONa + CO2 → o-HOC6H4COONa → (H+) salicylic acid. The electrophile is CO2.
  • Reimer–Tiemann: phenol + CHCl3 + aq. NaOH → salicylaldehyde (the other option in the list).
  • Salicylic acid + (CH3CO)2O → aspirin.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (13)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vi)
Question 1(B)(vi) · Specimen 2027[1]
Assertion: When an ether containing a primary alkyl group and tertiary alkyl group is treated with hydrogen iodide, tertiary alkyl iodide is formed.
Reason: The reaction takes place via SN1 mechanism, and the more stable tertiary carbocation is formed easily which combines with iodide ion to form tertiary alkyl iodide.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true, and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true, but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Assertion true: for (CH3)3C–O–CH3 + HI the products are (CH3)3C–I and CH3OH. Reason true: the protonated ether loses CH3OH to give the stable tertiary carbocation (SN1), which is captured by I−. The reason explains the assertion.
    [1]
Answer
(a)

Concepts to recall

  • Ether + HX: with primary/secondary alkyl groups SN2, halide attacks the smaller alkyl group; with a tertiary group SN1, halide ends on the tertiary carbon.
  • Protonation of the ether oxygen first makes it a good leaving group (an alcohol).
  • Test the Assertion and Reason separately, then ask whether the Reason explains the Assertion.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (13)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(C)(i)
Question 1(C)(i) · Specimen 2027[1]
Passage. The hydroxy derivatives of aliphatic hydrocarbons are called alcohols. Ethanol is perhaps the most important aliphatic alcohol. During COVID-19 pandemic alcohol-based sanitizers became an essential protective tool worldwide, reducing the spread of the virus. Nowadays, it is also used as a substitute for petrol in internal combustion engines.
The boiling points of alcohols are considerably higher than those of the corresponding ethers due to the presence of intermolecular hydrogen bonding in alcohols and it increases with increase in molecular mass. For isomeric alcohols the boiling point follows the order: primary (1°) alcohol > secondary (2°) alcohol > tertiary (3°) alcohol, which is evident from the following data:
CompoundPropan-1-olButan-1-olButan-2-ol2-Methylpropan-2-ol
Boiling point370 K391 K371 K356 K
Aliphatic alcohols behave as weak acids and ionize to a small extent. Their acidic strength is even less than that of both water and phenol. Phenols are hydroxy derivatives of arenes and have varied application in manufacturing of dyes, plastics, drugs, explosives, etc. At different concentrations, phenols are used both as a disinfectant and an antiseptic.
The boiling point of 2-methylpropan-2-ol is much lower than that of butan-1-ol, although both have the same molecular formula. Give a reason to explain such observation.
  1. Both are C4H10O and both form hydrogen bonds, but 2-methylpropan-2-ol (tertiary) is branched: its molecule is nearly spherical, so its surface area is smaller and the van der Waals forces between molecules are weaker (the bulky CH3 groups also shield the O–H from hydrogen bonding). Less energy is needed to separate the molecules, so it boils lower (356 K against 391 K).
    [1]
Answer
Branching decreases the surface area (weaker van der Waals forces and less effective hydrogen bonding), so the boiling point falls.

Concepts to recall

  • For isomers, boiling point falls with branching: straight chain > branched, 1° > 2° > 3° (propan-1-ol 370 K, butan-1-ol 391 K, butan-2-ol 371 K, tertiary 356 K).
  • Boiling point rises with molecular mass and with hydrogen bonding; alcohols boil much higher than ethers or alkanes of similar mass.
  • Intermolecular forces act through surface contact; a compact molecule has less contact.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (13)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(C)(ii)
Question 1(C)(ii) · Specimen 2027[1]
Passage. The hydroxy derivatives of aliphatic hydrocarbons are called alcohols. Ethanol is perhaps the most important aliphatic alcohol. During COVID-19 pandemic alcohol-based sanitizers became an essential protective tool worldwide, reducing the spread of the virus. Nowadays, it is also used as a substitute for petrol in internal combustion engines.
The boiling points of alcohols are considerably higher than those of the corresponding ethers due to the presence of intermolecular hydrogen bonding in alcohols and it increases with increase in molecular mass. For isomeric alcohols the boiling point follows the order: primary (1°) alcohol > secondary (2°) alcohol > tertiary (3°) alcohol, which is evident from the following data:
CompoundPropan-1-olButan-1-olButan-2-ol2-Methylpropan-2-ol
Boiling point370 K391 K371 K356 K
Aliphatic alcohols behave as weak acids and ionize to a small extent. Their acidic strength is even less than that of both water and phenol. Phenols are hydroxy derivatives of arenes and have varied application in manufacturing of dyes, plastics, drugs, explosives, etc. At different concentrations, phenols are used both as a disinfectant and an antiseptic.
Although alcohols are weaker acids than water, phenol is more acidic than alcohols. Justify.
  1. On losing H+ phenol gives the phenoxide ion, whose negative charge on oxygen is delocalised into the benzene ring by resonance (over the ortho and para carbons), so the phenoxide ion is stabilised. In an alkoxide the charge stays on oxygen and the +I effect of the alkyl group intensifies it. The O–H bond of phenol is also weakened because the sp2 carbon withdraws electrons. Hence phenol ionises more readily.
    [1]
Answer
The phenoxide ion is resonance stabilised; the alkoxide ion is not (and is destabilised by the +I effect).
Marking note The CISCE key explains this by resonance; the two points (resonance of the phenoxide, +I effect in the alkoxide) are both accepted.

Concepts to recall

  • Acidic strength depends on the stability of the conjugate base: more stable anion = stronger acid.
  • Order of acidity: phenol > water > alcohols (1° > 2° > 3°); alkyl groups push electrons (+I) and destabilise RO−.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups (−NO2) on the ring increase the acidity of phenol further; electron-donating groups (−CH3) decrease it.

In what form (i.e. concentration), does phenol act as a disinfectant or an antiseptic?
  1. Phenol is an antiseptic (applied to living tissue) at 0·2 % concentration and a disinfectant (for floors, drains, instruments) at about 1 % concentration.

Question 1(B)(vi) · Board 2026[1]
Assertion: Phenols are more acidic than aliphatic alcohols.
Reason: Phenoxide ion is stabilised by resonance but alkoxide ion is not stabilised by resonance.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Assertion true: phenol (pKa about 10) is much more acidic than ethanol (about 16).
    [½]
  2. Reason true and it is the explanation: the negative charge of the phenoxide ion is delocalised over O and the ortho/para carbons (resonance), which stabilises it and favours ionisation; an alkoxide ion has no such delocalisation (and the alkyl group even destabilises it by +I effect).
    [½]

(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.

  • Acidity depends on the stability of the conjugate base: more stable anion → stronger acid.

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (13)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(iii)
Question 1(A)(iii) · Specimen 2026[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [electrophilic, four, 4-bromophenol, first, alc. AgCN, decreases, 2,4,6-tribromophenol, nucleophilic, two, zero, alc. KCN, increases].
Phenol when treated with bromine water produces ______, which is an example of ______ substitution reaction.
  1. Bromine water with phenol gives a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol: C6H5OH + 3Br2 → C6H2Br3OH + 3HBr.
    [½]
  2. The −OH group activates the ring at the ortho and para positions and the attacking species Br+ (an electron-seeking species) is an electrophile, so it is electrophilic substitution.
    [½]
Answer
2,4,6-tribromophenol; electrophilic
Marking note ‘4-bromophenol’ is the distractor, formed in a non-polar solvent at low temperature, not in bromine water.

Concepts to recall

  • −OH is a strongly activating, o-, p-directing group: phenol is brominated at all three positions even without a catalyst.
  • In CS2 or CHCl3 at low temperature, bromination stops at the mono-products (o- and p-bromophenol); the aqueous medium favours ionisation to the phenoxide and the tribromo product.
  • White ppt with bromine water is the test for phenol (and aniline).
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (13)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vii)
Question 1(B)(vii) · Specimen 2026[1]
Assertion: Methoxy ethane reacts with HI at 373 K to give ethanol and iodomethane.
Reason: Reaction of unsymmetrical ether with HI follows SN2 mechanism.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. The ether is first protonated: CH3OC2H5 + HI → CH3–O+(H)–C2H5 I−. I− then attacks by SN2 the smaller, less hindered alkyl group (methyl): CH3OC2H5 + HI → CH3I + C2H5OH. So the Assertion is true, the Reason is true, and the SN2 attack on the less hindered carbon is exactly why iodomethane and ethanol (not iodoethane and methanol) form.
    [1]
Answer
(a) Both true and Reason is the correct explanation.

Concepts to recall

  • Cleavage of mixed alkyl ethers with HX: the halide ends up on the smaller / less hindered alkyl group (SN2); if one group is tertiary the cleavage is SN1 and the halide ends on the tertiary carbon.
  • Phenyl alkyl ethers give phenol + alkyl halide (the Caryl–O bond does not break).
  • Excess HI converts the alcohol formed into a second molecule of alkyl iodide.

The amount of bromine required to completely convert 9·4 g of phenol into 2,4,6-tribromophenol is: (Atomic weight of C = 12, H = 1, O = 16 and Br = 80)
(a)24 g
(b)48 g
(c)80 g
(d)96 g

    [½]

(b) 48 g

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (13)ISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vi)
Question 1(B)(vi) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
Assertion: The use of phosphorus chlorides is preferred to thionyl chloride in the preparation of alkyl chlorides from alcohol.
Reason: Phosphorus chlorides give pure alkyl chlorides.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Both statements are false. Thionyl chloride is preferred because the by-products SO2 and HCl are gases that escape, leaving a pure alkyl chloride: ROH + SOCl2 → RCl + SO2↑ + HCl↑. Phosphorus chlorides give POCl3 or H3PO3, which must be separated, so the product is not pure. Option (d).
    [1]
Answer
(d) Both Assertion and Reason are false

Concepts to recall

  • ROH + SOCl2 → RCl + SO2 + HCl (Darzens): best method, by-products are gaseous.
  • ROH + PCl5 → RCl + POCl3 + HCl; 3ROH + PCl3 → 3RCl + H3PO3: the product must be separated from the phosphorus compound.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (13)ISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii) · Board 2025[1]
Which one of the following alcohols is the strongest acid?
(a)Phenol
(b)Methanol
(c)Ethanol
(d)t-butyl alcohol
  1. The phenoxide ion C6H5O− is stabilised by resonance (delocalisation of the negative charge into the ring), so phenol (pKa ≈ 10) is far more acidic than any aliphatic alcohol (pKa 15–18).
    [½]
  2. In the aliphatic alcohols alkyl groups (+I) destabilise RO−: acidity CH3OH > C2H5OH > (CH3)3COH, all weaker than phenol.
    [½]
Answer
(a) Phenol

Concepts to recall

  • Acid strength = stability of the conjugate base. Resonance stabilisation of phenoxide beats the +I effect of alkyl groups.
  • Order: phenol > methanol > ethanol > 2° alcohols > 3° alcohols.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (13)ISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(iv)
Question 1(A)(iv) · Board 2024[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [lead poisoning, zero, phosgene, dependent, cancer, independent, diethyl ether, first, ethyl carbonate, ethene].
The dehydration of ethyl alcohol with conc. H2SO4 at 140°C mainly yields ______ while at 170°C the main product formed is ______.
  1. At 413 K (140 °C), with excess alcohol, intermolecular dehydration gives diethyl ether: 2C2H5OH → C2H5OC2H5 + H2O. At 443 K (170 °C), with excess acid, intramolecular dehydration gives ethene: C2H5OH → CH2=CH2 + H2O.
    [1]
Answer
diethyl ether; ethene

Concepts to recall

  • Low temperature + excess alcohol: ether (substitution, SN2 by a second ROH on protonated ROH). High temperature + excess acid: alkene (elimination, E2/E1).
  • Order of ease of dehydration: 3° > 2° > 1° alcohol (stability of the carbocation).

Question 6 · Specimen 2026[2]
Also draws on: part (ii) uses a Grignard reagent with formaldehyde (Ch 8).
How will you convert the following (write chemical equation):
(i) Phenol to salicylaldehyde
(ii) Formaldehyde to ethanol
  1. (i) Reimer–Tiemann reaction: phenol is heated with chloroform and alkali, and the product acidified: C6H5OH + CHCl3 + 3KOH → o-HOC6H4CHO + 3KCl + 2H2O (salicylaldehyde, 2-hydroxybenzaldehyde).
    [1]
  2. (ii) HCHO + CH3MgBr → CH3CH2OMgBr (dry ether); hydrolysis: CH3CH2OMgBr + H2O → CH3CH2OH + Mg(OH)Br.
    [1]
Answer
(i) o-HOC6H4CHO (Reimer–Tiemann); (ii) CH3CH2OH (Grignard addition then hydrolysis)
Marking note The key writes the reagent as KOH (alc.); the textbook condition is aqueous alkali. Either earns the mark.

  • The Grignard reagent needs dry ether: water destroys it (CH3MgBr + H2O → CH4).

Write chemical tests to distinguish between the following pairs of compounds: (i) 2-methylpropan-2-ol and propan-2-ol (ii) Ethanol and diethyl ether
  1. (i) Lucas test: shake with Lucas reagent (anhydrous ZnCl2 + conc. HCl) at room temperature. 2-Methylpropan-2-ol (3°) turns turbid immediately (tertiary chloride is formed): (CH3)3COH + HCl → (CH3)3CCl + H2O. Propan-2-ol (2°) becomes turbid only after about 5 minutes.

(i) Arrange the following in the increasing order of their acidic strength: CH3CH2OH, CF3CH2OH, CCl3CH2OH.
(ii) Arrange the following in the increasing order of their boiling points: CH3CH2OH, CH2(OH)CH2OH, CH3CH2Cl.

    [½]
  2. Increasing acidity: CH3CH2OH < CCl3CH2OH < CF3CH2OH.
    [½]
  3. (ii) CH3CH2Cl has only weak dipole–dipole forces and no hydrogen bonding; ethanol has one –OH, hence hydrogen bonding; ethane-1,2-diol has two –OH groups, so extensive hydrogen bonding.
    [½]
  4. Increasing boiling point: CH3CH2Cl (285 K) < CH3CH2OH (351 K) < CH2(OH)CH2OH (470 K).
    [½]
Answer
(i) CH3CH2OH < CCl3CH2OH < CF3CH2OH; (ii) CH3CH2Cl < CH3CH2OH < CH2(OH)CH2OH
Marking note The marking follows the −I (electronegativity) argument. Measured pKa values of CF3CH2OH (about 12·4) and CCl3CH2OH (about 12·2) are almost equal, but the expected answer at this level is CF3 the more acidic.

Concepts to recall

  • Acidity of an alcohol rises with electron-withdrawing groups near O–H (−I) and falls with alkyl groups (+I).
  • Boiling point: number of hydrogen bonds per molecule and molar mass: diol > alcohol > haloalkane of similar size.

[A] = butan-2-ol, CH3CH(OH)CH2CH3. Oxidation gives the ketone [B] = butanone, CH3COCH2CH3, which does not reduce Tollens’ reagent.
    [1]
  5. (v) Clemmensen reduction: CH3COCH2CH3 + 4[H] →(Zn–Hg, conc. HCl) CH3CH2CH2CH3 + H2O. [C] = butane.
    [1]
Answer
(i) phenol → picric acid with conc. HNO3/H2SO4; (ii) large hydrophobic phenyl group; (iii) C6H5Cl + NaOH (623 K, 300 atm) → C6H5ONa, then H+ → C6H5OH; (iv) [A] butan-2-ol, [B] butanone; (v) [C] butane.
Marking note The simple C6H5OH + 3HNO3 equation earns the full mark; the two-step route (sulphonation then nitration) is also accepted.

Concepts to recall

  • Lucas test (ZnCl2/conc. HCl): 3° alcohols turbid at once, 2° in about 5 minutes, 1° only on heating. Oxidation: 2° alcohol → ketone (no Tollens), 1° → aldehyde (Tollens positive).
  • Phenol: nitration with dilute HNO3 gives o- and p-nitrophenol; conc. HNO3/H2SO4 gives 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid), a strong acid.
  • Clemmensen (Zn–Hg/HCl) and Wolff–Kishner convert C=O to CH2.
  • Solubility of alcohols in water falls as the hydrocarbon part grows.

Question 19 · Specimen 2025[5]
Phenol contains a hydroxy group directly attached to the aromatic ring (a phenolic group). Phenols show many reactions similar to those of alcohols, but the direct attachment of –OH to the ring makes the behaviour different. Reactions are categorised as: reactions involving the –OH group; reactions involving the benzene ring.
(i) Write the chemical equation for the preparation of phenol from benzene using H2SO4 and NaOH.
(ii) What happens when phenol reacts with Br2/CS2 at low temperature?
(iii) What is observed when phenol is treated with neutral ferric chloride solution?
(iv) Write the reaction when phenol is treated with conc. HNO3 in the presence of conc. H2SO4.
(v) Write the reaction when phenol is treated with chloroform in the presence of aqueous NaOH at 340 K.
  1. (i) C6H6 + H2SO4 (conc.) → C6H5SO3H + H2O (sulphonation); C6H5SO3H + NaOH → C6H5SO3Na + H2O (benzene sulphonic acid → sodium benzenesulphonate); C6H5SO3Na + 2NaOH (fusion, 573 K) → C6H5ONa + Na2SO3 + H2O; C6H5ONa + HCl → C6H5OH + NaCl. (1)
    [1]
  2. (ii) In a non-polar solvent (CS2) at low temperature bromination is limited to mono-substitution: a mixture of o-bromophenol and p-bromophenol (para major) + HBr. (1)
    [1]
  3. (iii) A violet (purple) colouration, due to the iron(III)–phenoxide complex, [Fe(OC6H5)6]3−. (1)
    [1]
  4. (iv) C6H5OH + 3HNO3 (conc., conc. H2SO4) → 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid), C6H2(NO2)3OH + 3H2O. (1)
    [1]
  5. (v) Reimer–Tiemann reaction: C6H5OH + CHCl3 + 3NaOH (aq., 340 K) → (then H+) salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde), o-HOC6H4CHO. (1)
    [1]
Answer
(i) benzene → sulphonic acid → sodium salt → sodium phenoxide → phenol; (ii) o- and p-bromophenol; (iii) violet colour; (iv) 2,4,6-trinitrophenol; (v) salicylaldehyde
Marking note CISCE’s key requires, for (i), the equation with the proper catalyst/conditions and by-product, and gives the other four products as above.

Concepts to recall

  • The –OH of phenol activates the ring (+R) at ortho and para positions: bromine water gives 2,4,6-tribromophenol; Br2/CS2 gives mono-bromophenols.
  • Phenol is acidic (phenoxide is resonance stabilised) and reacts with NaOH but not NaHCO3; it gives a violet complex with neutral FeCl3.
  • Reimer–Tiemann: phenol + CHCl3 + NaOH → salicylaldehyde (via dichlorocarbene :CCl2); Kolbe: sodium phenoxide + CO2 → salicylic acid.
