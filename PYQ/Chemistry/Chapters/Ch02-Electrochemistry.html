  • Numericals: Nernst equation for Ecell, ΔG° = −nFE°, Faraday’s laws for mass deposited by a given current and time (cells in series), conductivity κ, Λm and the cell constant, degree of dissociation α = Λm/Λ°m.

Question 1(A)(ii) · Specimen 2027[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [methyl alcohol, ethyl alcohol, paired, one, Cannizzaro, d–d, salicylaldehyde, Perkin’s, unpaired, f–f, salicylic acid, Kolbe’s, two, 193000, 96500, Reimer–Tiemann].
Electrochemical equivalent (Z) is the quantity of substance deposited or discharged by a charge of ______ coulomb, whereas the equivalent conductance (E) is the amount of substance deposited or discharged by a charge of ______ coulombs.
  1. The electrochemical equivalent Z is the mass deposited by one coulomb, Z = M/(zF). The chemical equivalent (equivalent mass) is the mass deposited by one faraday, i.e. by 96500 coulombs.
    [1]
Answer
one; 96500
Marking note The paper’s word ‘equivalent conductance (E)’ is a slip for the equivalent (chemical equivalent) mass; the only value that fits the blank is 96500.

Concepts to recall

  • Faraday’s first law: m = ZQ = ZIt.
  • Z × 96500 = equivalent mass; one faraday = charge on one mole of electrons = 96500 C.
  • The printed blanks list both 96500 and 193000 (= 2F) as distractors; 193000 C deposits one mole of a divalent metal.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (13)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vii)
Question 1(B)(vii) · Specimen 2027[1]
Assertion: Magnesium metal can liberate H2 gas from an aqueous solution of HCl.
Reason: Magnesium has positive value of standard reduction electrode potential.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true, and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true, but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Assertion true: E°(Mg2+/Mg) = −2·37 V is lower than E°(H+/H2) = 0, so Mg reduces H+: Mg + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2. Reason false: the standard reduction potential of Mg is negative, and that negative value is the cause, not a positive one.
    [1]
Answer
(c) Assertion is true and Reason is false.

Concepts to recall

  • A metal displaces H2 from dilute acids if its E° is below 0 V (above hydrogen in the electrochemical series).
  • E°cell = 0 − (−2·37) = +2·37 V > 0: spontaneous.
  • Cu (+0·34 V) and Ag (+0·80 V) do not liberate H2 from HCl.

Kohlrausch observed an interesting pattern between the values of molar conductance at infinite dilution (Λ∞m) for strong electrolytes. Different pairs of electrolytes having a common cation or anion had almost the same difference of Λ∞m. On this basis he postulated Kohlrausch’s Law of Independent Migration of ions. Molar conductivities at infinite dilution:
Ionλ∞m (S cm2 mol−1)
Ba2+127·2
Cl−76·3
Ca2+119·0
SO42−160·0
State Kohlrausch’s Law of Independent Migration of ions.
  1. At infinite dilution each ion migrates independently of the other ions present, so the molar conductivity of an electrolyte at infinite dilution is the sum of the molar conductivities of its cation and anion, each multiplied by the number of ions of that kind furnished by one formula unit.
    [1]
Answer
Λ∞m = ν+λ∞+ + ν−λ∞−
Marking note Award the mark for either the statement or the equation with symbols defined.

  • Kohlrausch’s law: Λ∞m = ν+λ∞+ + ν−λ∞− (ν = number of ions from one formula unit).
  • Uses: Λ∞m of weak electrolytes, and the degree of dissociation α = Λm/Λ∞m.

Question 1(C)(i) · Specimen 2026[1]
Vishal set up an experiment to find the resistance of aqueous KCl solution for different concentrations at 298 K using a conductivity cell connected to a Wheatstone bridge. He fed the Wheatstone bridge with A.C. power in the audio frequency range 550 to 5000 cycles per second. Once the resistance was calculated from null point, he also calculated the conductivity (κ) and molar conductivity (Λm) and recorded his readings in tabular form, given below.
S.No.Conc. (M)κ (S cm−1)Λm (S cm2 mol−1)
1.1·00111·3 × 10−3111·3
2.0·1012·9 × 10−3129·0
3.0·011·41 × 10−3141·0

(i) Why did the molar conductivity increase though the conductivity decreased with dilution?
  1. Conductivity κ is the conductance of the ions in one unit volume (1 cm3) of solution. On dilution the number of ions per unit volume decreases, so κ decreases (111·3 → 12·9 → 1·41 × 10−3).
    [½]
  2. Molar conductivity is Λm = κ × Vm, where Vm = 1000/M cm3 is the volume containing one mole. Dilution raises Vm by a larger factor than it lowers κ (interionic attraction also falls), so Λm increases.
    [½]
Answer
κ falls because the ions per cm3 fall; Λm = κ × Vm rises because Vm grows more than κ falls
Marking note The key explains both halves: κ falls with fewer ions per unit volume, Λm = κ × Vm rises. Both are required.

Concepts to recall

  • κ = G* / R (S cm−1), with G* the cell constant (l/A).
  • Λm = κ × 1000 / M (S cm2 mol−1) = κ × Vm.
  • Strong electrolytes: Λm = Λ°m − A√c (Debye–Hückel–Onsager), a straight line against √c.
  • A.C. is used so that the electrolysis products do not polarise the electrodes.

(iii) The conductivity of a 0·01 M solution of acetic acid at 298 K is 1·65 × 10−4 S cm−1. Calculate the molar conductivity of the solution.
  1. Λm = κ × 1000 / M = (1·65 × 10−4 × 1000)/0·01
    [½]

Question 1(A)(i) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [ethanol, [Fe(H2O)6]2+, anode, d2sp3, Cannizzaro, reducing, cathode, sp3d2, iodine, [Fe(CN)6]4−, methanol, aldol, oxidising, manganese dichloride].
In electrochemical cells, the electrode having negative values of standard reduction potential acts as ______ while the electrode possessing positive values of standard reduction potential acts as ______.
  1. The electrode with the more negative (lower) standard reduction potential is the stronger reducing agent: it loses electrons, i.e. is oxidised, and so is the anode. The electrode with the positive (higher) reduction potential gains electrons, is reduced, and is the cathode.
    [1]
Answer
anode; cathode
Marking note Both words are needed for the mark, in the order asked.

Concepts to recall

  • Anode = oxidation (lower E°); cathode = reduction (higher E°). E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode > 0 for a spontaneous cell.
  • Mnemonic: AN OX, RED CAT; electrons flow from anode to cathode in the external wire.

(i) ____________ is an example of a primary cell but ____________ is an example of a secondary cell.
  1. A primary cell cannot be recharged: the dry cell (Leclanché cell) is the example.

Question 1(B)(i) · Board 2024[1]
Which one of the following statements is correct regarding the dry cell?
(P) Zinc container acts as an anode in dry cell.
(Q) Zinc container touches the paste of MnO2 and carbon.
(R) Dry cell can be charged easily.
(S) Graphite rod acts as a cathode in dry cell.
(a)Only (P) and (R)
(b)Only (Q) and (R)
(c)Only (P) and (S)
(d)Only (Q) and (S)
  1. (P) true: Zn → Zn2+ + 2e− occurs at the zinc container, the anode. (Q) false: the zinc touches the NH4Cl + ZnCl2 paste; the MnO2 + carbon paste surrounds the graphite rod. (R) false: a dry cell is a primary cell and cannot be recharged easily. (S) true: the graphite rod (cathode) is where MnO2 is reduced. So only (P) and (S) are correct.
    [1]
Answer
(c) Only (P) and (S)

  • Anode (−): Zn → Zn2+ + 2e−. Cathode (+): MnO2 + NH4+ + e− → MnO(OH) + NH3.
  • Primary cells (dry cell, mercury cell) are not rechargeable; secondary cells (lead storage, Ni–Cd) are.
  • Dry cell emf is about 1·5 V and falls steadily as it is used.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (13)ISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vi)
Question 1(B)(vi) · Board 2024[1]
Assertion: Specific conductivity of all electrolytes decreases on dilution.
Reason: On dilution, the number of ions per unit volume decreases.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true but Reason is false.
(d)Assertion is false but Reason is true.
  1. Specific conductivity κ is the conductance of 1 cm3 of the solution, so it depends on the number of ions in that unit volume. Dilution lowers the number of ions per cm3 (for strong and weak electrolytes alike), so κ falls: both statements are true and the reason explains the assertion. (Molar conductivity, by contrast, rises on dilution.)
    [1]
Answer
(a)

Concepts to recall

  • κ (S cm−1) falls on dilution; Λm = 1000κ/M rises on dilution.
  • Λm rises because the total volume containing one mole increases and (for weak electrolytes) ionisation increases; for strong electrolytes interionic attraction falls.
  • Always test the Assertion and the Reason on their own first, then ask whether the Reason explains the Assertion.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (11)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q2
Question 2 · Specimen 2027[2]
The standard reduction electrode potential (E°) of some metals is given below:
MetalE°
Ag+/Ag+0·80 V
Mg2+/Mg−2·37 V
Li+/Li−3·05 V
Cu2+/Cu+0·34 V
Fe2+/Fe−0·44 V
Arrange these metals in the increasing order of their reducing power. Justify the order.
  1. The reducing power of a metal is its tendency to lose electrons (be oxidised), which is the reverse of its reduction potential: the more negative the E°, the stronger the reducing agent.
    [1]
  2. Arranging E° from most positive to most negative: Ag (+0·80) > Cu (+0·34) > Fe (−0·44) > Mg (−2·37) > Li (−3·05). Increasing reducing power: Ag < Cu < Fe < Mg < Li.
    [1]
Answer
Ag < Cu < Fe < Mg < Li (reducing power increases as E° becomes more negative)
Common slip Listing the metals by decreasing E° and calling that the increasing reducing power: the order is the reverse.

Concepts to recall

  • Reduction potential measures tendency to gain electrons; reducing power (tendency to lose them) varies inversely.
  • The electrochemical series: a metal with lower E° reduces the ions of any metal with higher E°, e.g. Fe + Cu2+ → Fe2+ + Cu.
  • Li has the most negative E° of all metals (−3·05 V) although it is not the most reactive in water; E° is a thermodynamic quantity in aqueous solution.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (11)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q8
Question 8 · Specimen 2027[2]
Write the Nernst equation and calculate the emf of the following cell at 298 K.
Mg(s) | Mg2+ (1 × 10−3 M) || Cu2+ (1 × 10−4 M) | Cu(s)
Given: E°(Mg2+/Mg) = −2·373 V, E°(Cu2+/Cu) = +0·337 V
  1. Cell reaction: Mg + Cu2+ → Mg2+ + Cu, n = 2. Nernst equation at 298 K: Ecell = E°cell − (0·0591/n) log([Mg2+]/[Cu2+])
    [½]
  2. E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode = 0·337 − (−2·373) = 2·71 V
    [½]
  3. Ecell = 2·71 − (0·0591/2) log(10−3/10−4) = 2·71 − 0·02955 × log 10 = 2·71 − 0·02955
    [½]
  4. Ecell = 2·6805 V ≈ 2·68 V
    [½]
Answer
Ecell = 2·6805 V (≈ 2·68 V)
Marking note CISCE’s key uses 0·059 and gets 2·6805 V; with 0·0591 the value is 2·6805 V. Both round to 2·68 V and either is accepted.

Concepts to recall

  • Nernst: E = E° − (0·0591/n) log Q at 298 K, where Q = [products]/[reactants] and solids are omitted; for this cell Q = [Mg2+]/[Cu2+].
  • E°cell = E°(cathode) − E°(anode); the anode is on the left, the electrode of lower E°.
  • Anode (oxidation): Mg → Mg2+ + 2e−. Cathode (reduction): Cu2+ + 2e− → Cu. A dilute product ion increases the emf; a dilute reactant ion decreases it.

Question 6 · Board 2026[2]
Calculate the value of ΔG° for the following cell at 25°C: Zn(s) | Zn2+(1M) || Sn2+(1M) | Sn(s). Given E°Zn2+/Zn = −0·76 V, E°Sn2+/Sn = −0·14 V; 1 faraday = 96,500 coulombs.
  1. Cell reaction: Zn + Sn2+ → Zn2+ + Sn, n = 2. E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode = −0·14 − (−0·76) = 0·62 V
    [1]
  2. ΔG° = −nFE° = −2 × 96500 × 0·62 = -119660 J mol−1 = -119·7 kJ mol−1
    [1]
Answer
ΔG° = -119660 J mol−1 (about -119·7 kJ mol−1)
Common slip Using E° = −0·14 + (−0·76) = −0·90 V (adding instead of subtracting).

Concepts to recall

  • ΔG° = −nFE°cell; a negative ΔG° (positive E°) means a spontaneous cell reaction.
  • E°cell = E°right (cathode) − E°left (anode), both as reduction potentials; the anode is written on the left.
  • Electrons flow from Zn (lower E°, anode) to Sn (cathode).

Question 7 · Specimen 2026[2]
The molar conductivities at infinite dilution (Λ°m) for NaI, CH3COONa and (CH3COO)2Mg are 126·9, 91·0 and 187·8 ohm−1 cm2 mol−1 respectively at 25 °C. What is the molar conductivity of MgI2 at infinite dilution?
  1. By Kohlrausch’s law Λ°m(MgI2) = λ°(Mg2+) + 2λ°(I−). From the data: Λ°[(CH3COO)2Mg] + 2Λ°(NaI) − 2Λ°(CH3COONa) = [λ(Mg2+) + 2λ(Ac−)] + 2[λ(Na+) + λ(I−)] − 2[λ(Na+) + λ(Ac−)] = λ(Mg2+) + 2λ(I−).
    [1]
  2. Λ°m(MgI2) = 187·8 + 2 × 126·9 − 2 × 91·0 = 259·6 S cm2 mol−1
    [1]
Answer
Λ°m(MgI2) = 259·6 S cm2 mol−1
Common slip Forgetting the factor 2: two I− ions per MgI2, and two CH3COO− ions to cancel.

Concepts to recall

  • Kohlrausch’s law of independent migration of ions: Λ°m = ν+λ°+ + ν−λ°−.
  • Method: write the target in terms of ions, then combine the given salts so that the unwanted ions (here CH3COO− and Na+) cancel; the coefficients carry the stoichiometry.
  • Uses: Λ°m of weak electrolytes (CH3COOH, NH4OH) and then α = Λm/Λ°m.

Question 10 · Board 2025 Imp.[2]
The specific conductivity of 0·0025 M acetic acid solution is 7·95 × 10−5 ohm−1 cm−1. Calculate the molar conductance of this solution. If molar conductance for acetic acid is 389·5 ohm−1 cm2 mol−1 at infinite dilution (Λ∞m), what would be its degree of dissociation?
  1. Λm = κ × 1000/M = (7·95 × 10−5 × 1000)/0·0025
    [½]
  2. Λm = 31·8 ohm−1 cm2 mol−1
    [1]
  3. α = Λm/Λ∞m = 31·8/389·5 = 0·0816 (about 8·2 %)
    [½]
Answer
Λm = 31·8 ohm−1 cm2 mol−1; α = 0·0816

Concepts to recall

  • Λm = 1000κ/M (κ in S cm−1, M in mol L−1) gives S cm2 mol−1.

A steady current of 1·5 A was passed until 1·45 g of Ag was deposited at the cathode of cell B. (Atomic mass of Ag = 108, Cu = 63·5, Zn = 65·3) (1) How long did the current flow? (2) What weight of Cu and Zn was deposited at cathode?

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a)(1) The diagram marks copper as the negative electrode (anode, oxidation) and silver as the positive electrode (cathode, reduction). Anode: Cu → Cu2+ + 2e−; cathode: 2Ag+ + 2e− → 2Ag. Cell reaction: Cu + 2Ag+ → Cu2+ + 2Ag.
    [1]
  2. (2) E°cell = 0·799 − 0·337 = 0·462 V
    [½]
  3. (3) Nernst: Ecell = E° − (0·0591/2) log([Cu2+]/[Ag+]2).  0·422 = 0·462 − 0·02955 log(0·1/[Ag+]2)
    [1]
  4. log(0·1/[Ag+]2) = (0·462 − 0·422)/0·02955 = 1·354  ⇒  0·1/[Ag+]2 = 22·58  ⇒  [Ag+] = 0·0666 M
    [½]
  5. (4) ΔG° = −nFE° = −2 × 96500 × 0·462 = -89166 J mol−1 = -89·17 kJ mol−1
    [1]
  6. (b) Λ°m(BaCl2) = λ°(Ba2+) + 2λ°(Cl−) = 127·2 + 2 × 76·3 = 279·8 S cm2 mol−1
    [½]
  7. Λ°m(Al2(SO4)3) = 2λ°(Al3+) + 3λ°(SO42−) = 2 × 189 + 3 × 160 = 858 S cm2 mol−1
    [½]
Answer
(1) Cu + 2Ag+ → Cu2+ + 2Ag; (2) 0·462 V; (3) [Ag+] ≈ 0·067 M; (4) ΔG° ≈ -89·2 kJ mol−1; (b) 279·8 and 858 S cm2 mol−1
Marking note The printed value of E°(Cu) is 0·337 V (not the usual 0·34 V) and the Nernst constant 0·0591 V at 25 °C are used. In (4) ΔG° = −nFE° is given; if the non-standard Ecell = 0·422 V of part (3) is used instead, ΔG = −2 × 96500 × 0·422 = −81·4 kJ mol−1, which should also be accepted. In (3) answers from 0·066 to 0·067 M are acceptable; the value depends slightly on whether 0·059 or 0·0591 is used.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a)(1) κ = cell constant/R = 0·343/30·8 = 1·114 × 10-2 S cm−1
    [1]
  2. (2) Λm = κ × 1000/M = 0·01114 × 1000/0·05 = 222·7 S cm2 mol−1
    [1]
  3. (3) α = Λm/Λ°m = 222·7/471·4 = 0·472
    [1]
  4. (b)(1) Moles of Ag = 1·45/108 = 0·01343 mol; Ag+ + e− → Ag, so charge Q = 0·01343 × 96500 = 1295·6 C.  t = Q/I = 1295·6/1·5 = 864 s (≈ 14·4 min)
    [1]
  5. (2) Same charge in series. Cu2+ + 2e− → Cu: mass = (Q/2F) × 63·5 = (1295·6/193000) × 63·5 = 0·426 g. Zn2+ + 2e− → Zn: mass = (Q/2F) × 65·3 = 0·438 g.
    [1]
Answer
(a) κ = 1·11 × 10-2 S cm−1; Λm = 222·7 S cm2 mol−1; α = 0·472. (b) t = 864 s; Cu = 0·426 g; Zn = 0·438 g

Concepts to recall

  • Nernst equation at 298 K: E = E° − (0·0591/n) log Q, where Q = [products]/[reactants] (pure solids omitted); here Q = [Cu2+]/[Ag+]2.
  • ΔG° = −nFE°; E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode; the electrode of lower E° is the anode.
  • Kohlrausch: Λ°m = ν+λ°+ + ν−λ°−; κ = G*/R; Λm = 1000κ/M; α = Λm/Λ°m for a weak electrolyte.
  • Faraday: m = (Q/zF)M = ItM/zF; cells in series carry the same charge, so masses are proportional to equivalent masses.
