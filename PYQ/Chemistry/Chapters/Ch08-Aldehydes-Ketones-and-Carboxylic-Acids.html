What to revise

  • Name reactions: Clemmensen, Wolff–Kishner, Rosenmund, Cannizzaro, aldol and cross aldol, Perkin, benzoin, Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky, Stephen, Etard, Gattermann–Koch.
  • Identify [A], [B], [C]: C2H4O, C3H6O, C4H8O puzzles from Fehling, Tollens, iodoform and aldol clues, and acid → acid chloride → amide sequences.
  • Tests: aldehyde against ketone, acetaldehyde against benzaldehyde, acetic acid against benzaldehyde, reactivity of benzaldehyde against propanal.
  • Habit: list the clues (reduces Tollens, iodoform, forms oxime) and write the formula of the compound they fix before writing any reaction.

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i) · Specimen 2027[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [methyl alcohol, ethyl alcohol, paired, one, Cannizzaro, d–d, salicylaldehyde, Perkin’s, unpaired, f–f, salicylic acid, Kolbe’s, two, 193000, 96500, Reimer–Tiemann].
Formaldehyde reacts with conc. alkali to give sodium formate and ______ due to the absence of α-hydrogen atom. The reaction is known as ______ reaction.
  1. HCHO has no α-hydrogen, so with conc. NaOH it undergoes self oxidation–reduction (disproportionation): 2HCHO + NaOH → HCOONa + CH3OH. One molecule is oxidised to sodium formate, the other reduced to methyl alcohol. This is the Cannizzaro reaction.
    [1]
Answer
methyl alcohol; Cannizzaro

Concepts to recall

  • Cannizzaro reaction: aldehydes with no α-H (HCHO, C6H5CHO, (CH3)3CCHO) + conc. alkali → alcohol + salt of the acid.
  • Aldehydes with α-H give aldol condensation with dilute alkali instead.
  • Benzaldehyde: 2C6H5CHO + NaOH → C6H5CH2OH + C6H5COONa.

Identify the end product [C] in the following sequence of chemical reactions: HC≡CH →(dil. H2SO4/HgSO4) [A] →([O]) [B] →(PCl5) [C].
(a)Acetic anhydride
(b)Acetic acid
(c)Acetyl chloride
(d)Ethyl chloride
  1. Hydration (Kucherov) of ethyne gives ethanal [A] CH3CHO; oxidation gives ethanoic acid [B] CH3COOH.
    [½]
  2. PCl5 replaces the OH of the acid by Cl: CH3COOH + PCl5 → CH3COCl + POCl3 + HCl, so [C] is acetyl chloride.
    [½]

(c) Acetyl chloride

  • Ethyne + water (Hg2+/H2SO4) → vinyl alcohol → tautomerises to ethanal.
  • PCl5, PCl3 or SOCl2 convert a carboxylic acid to its acid chloride.

Question 1(B)(iv) · Board 2026[1]
Also draws on: iodoform test also applies to alcohols (Ch 7).
Rohit is studying the iodoform reaction using (P) CH3OH, (Q) CH3CHO, (R) CH3COCH3, (S) CH3CH2COCH2CH3. Which combination will give the iodoform test when heated separately with iodine and aqueous NaOH?
(a)Only (P) and (Q)
(b)Only (Q) and (R)
(c)Only (P) and (S)
(d)Only (Q) and (S)
  1. The test needs a CH3CO– group (or CH3CH(OH)–): (Q) ethanal and (R) propanone have it.
    [½]
  2. (P) methanol has no CH3CO– or CH3CH(OH)– unit; (S) pentan-3-one has CH3CH2CO–, not CH3CO–. So only (Q) and (R).
    [½]
Answer
(b) Only (Q) and (R)

Concepts to recall

  • Iodoform (haloform) reaction: CH3CO–R (R = H, alkyl, aryl) or CH3CH(OH)–R with I2/NaOH gives yellow CHI3.
  • Ethanal is the only aldehyde and ethanol the only primary alcohol that respond; methanol does not.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(v)
Question 1(B)(v) · Specimen 2026[1]
An organic compound with molecular formula C3H6O does not give silver mirror test with Tollen’s reagent but gives an oxime with hydroxylamine. The compound is:
(a)CH3CH2CHO
(b)CH3–CO–CH3
(c)CH2=CH–CH2OH
(d)CH2=CH–O–CH3
  1. An oxime forms only from a carbonyl compound (aldehyde or ketone); options (c) (an alcohol) and (d) (an ether) cannot. Failing Tollens’ test rules out the aldehyde (a). The compound is the ketone propanone, CH3COCH3: (CH3)2C=O + NH2OH → (CH3)2C=NOH + H2O.
    [1]
Answer
(b) CH3–CO–CH3

Concepts to recall

  • Tollens’ reagent [Ag(NH3)2]+ oxidises aldehydes only (silver mirror); ketones do not respond.
  • All aldehydes and ketones condense with NH2OH to give oximes, with NH2NH2 hydrazones, with 2,4-DNP orange-red 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazones.
  • Check the formula: C3H6O has one degree of unsaturation: it is a C=O or a C=C.

Formaldehyde lacks α-hydrogen atom and thus forms sodium formate and ______ on reaction with conc. alkali. This reaction is known as ______ reaction.
  1. HCHO has no α-hydrogen, so with concentrated NaOH it undergoes disproportionation, the Cannizzaro reaction: 2HCHO + NaOH → HCOONa + CH3OH (methanol). One molecule is oxidised to sodium formate, the other reduced to methanol.
    [1]

methanol; Cannizzaro

  • Cannizzaro: aldehydes with no α-H (HCHO, C6H5CHO) + conc. alkali → carboxylate salt + alcohol (disproportionation).
  • Aldehydes with α-H (CH3CHO) give the aldol reaction with dilute alkali instead.

When acetaldehyde is treated with hydrazine and KOH in a high boiling solvent glycol, ____________ is formed and the reaction is known as ____________ reduction.
  1. CH3CHO + NH2NH2 → CH3CH=NNH2 (hydrazone), which on heating with KOH in ethylene glycol loses N2 to give ethane, CH3CH3.
    [½]
  2. C=O → CH2 by hydrazine/KOH in glycol is the Wolff–Kishner reduction.
    [½]

ethane; Wolff–Kishner

Question 3 · Specimen 2027[2]
Also draws on: starts from an alkene by ozonolysis (hydrocarbons).
An alkene [A] with molecular formula C5H10 on ozonolysis and subsequent hydrolysis gives a mixture of two compounds, [B] and [C]. Compound [B] gives positive Fehling’s test and also reacts with iodine and NaOH solution. Compound [C] does not give Fehling’s test but forms iodoform.
Identify compounds [A], [B] and [C]. Write the iodoform reaction either with compound [B] or [C].
  1. [B] gives Fehling’s test: an aldehyde; it also gives iodoform: CH3CO– or CH3CH(OH)– unit, so [B] = CH3CHO (ethanal, acetaldehyde). [C] gives iodoform but not Fehling’s: a methyl ketone, [C] = CH3COCH3 (propanone, acetone).
    [1]
  2. Joining the two carbonyl carbons through a C=C gives [A] = (CH3)2C=CHCH3, 2-methylbut-2-ene (C5H10).
    [½]
  3. Iodoform reaction of [C]: CH3COCH3 + 3I2 + 4NaOH → CHI3↓ + CH3COONa + 3NaI + 3H2O (or of [B]: CH3CHO + 3I2 + 4NaOH → CHI3 + HCOONa + 3NaI + 3H2O).
    [½]
Answer
[A] 2-methylbut-2-ene; [B] ethanal; [C] propanone
Marking note CISCE’s key writes ‘[B] CH3CHO or acetaldehyde or ethanol’: ethanol is a slip for ethanal; ethanol (an alcohol) would not give Fehling’s test.

Concepts to recall

  • Ozonolysis: R2C=CHR′ → R2C=O + R′CHO (O3, then Zn/H2O): each C=C carbon becomes a C=O.
  • Iodoform test (I2/NaOH, yellow CHI3): CH3CO– or CH3CH(OH)–. Fehling’s/Tollens’: aliphatic aldehydes only (ketones do not).
  • Work backwards: two carbonyl fragments (C2 + C3 = C5) fix the alkene.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (10)ISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q9
Question 9 · Board 2026[2]
(i) Write chemical tests to distinguish between the following pairs of compounds: (a) Acetaldehyde and acetone; (b) Acetic acid and benzaldehyde.
  1. (a) Tollens’ test: warm with ammoniacal silver nitrate. Acetaldehyde gives a silver mirror: CH3CHO + 2[Ag(NH3)2]+ + 3OH− → CH3COO− + 2Ag + 4NH3 + 2H2O; acetone (a ketone) gives none. (Fehling’s solution, red Cu2O with acetaldehyde only, also serves.)
    [1]
  2. (b) Sodium hydrogencarbonate test: acetic acid gives brisk effervescence of CO2: CH3COOH + NaHCO3 → CH3COONa + H2O + CO2; benzaldehyde does not. (Alternatively benzaldehyde, unlike acetic acid, gives a silver mirror with Tollens’ reagent.)
    [1]
Answer
(a) Tollens’ (silver mirror with acetaldehyde); (b) NaHCO3 (CO2 with acetic acid)
Common slip Choosing the iodoform test for (a): both compounds give yellow CHI3.

Concepts to recall

  • Aldehydes reduce Tollens’ reagent (silver mirror); ketones do not. Fehling’s reagent is reduced by aliphatic aldehydes only, not by benzaldehyde or ketones.
  • Carboxylic acids liberate CO2 from NaHCO3; aldehydes and ketones do not.
  • Iodoform cannot separate acetaldehyde from acetone: both give it.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (10)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q9
Question 9 · Specimen 2026[2]
(i) What happens when carbonyl compound is treated with zinc amalgam and concentrated hydrochloric acid? Give chemical equation and write the name of the reaction.
OR
(ii) Write chemical equations to convert each of the following:
(a) Acetic acid to acetaldehyde
(b) Formaldehyde to urotropine

Solution to the first alternative

  1. The C=O group is reduced to CH2: CH3CHO + 4[H] → CH3CH3 + H2O (Zn–Hg, conc. HCl, heat); a ketone behaves in the same way: (CH3)2C=O → CH3CH2CH3.
    [1]
  2. The reaction is the Clemmensen reduction.
    [1]
Answer
RCOR′ → RCH2R′: Clemmensen reduction
Marking note Any correct aldehyde or ketone example earns the equation mark; the name is a separate mark.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) CH3COOH + SOCl2 → CH3COCl + SO2 + HCl; then Rosenmund reduction: CH3COCl + H2 → CH3CHO + HCl (H2, Pd/BaSO4, partly poisoned with quinoline or sulphur).
    [1]
  2. (b) 6HCHO + 4NH3 → (CH2)6N4 + 6H2O (urotropine, hexamethylenetetramine).
    [1]
Answer
(a) CH3COOH → CH3COCl → CH3CHO; (b) 6HCHO + 4NH3 → (CH2)6N4 + 6H2O
Marking note The Pd catalyst is poisoned with BaSO4 so that the aldehyde is not reduced further to the alcohol.

Concepts to recall

  • Clemmensen reduction: Zn–Hg/conc. HCl converts C=O to CH2; Wolff–Kishner (NH2NH2, KOH/ethylene glycol, heat) does the same in basic medium.
  • Other reductions of carbonyls: NaBH4 or LiAlH4 gives alcohols (C=O → CHOH); H2/Pd also gives alcohols.
  • Choose Clemmensen for base-sensitive substrates, Wolff–Kishner for acid-sensitive ones.

An organic compound [A] having molecular formula C3H6O gives a positive iodoform test. On reduction by LiAlH4, compound [A] forms compound [B]. Further, compound [B] reacts with SOCl2 and forms compound [C]. On treatment with alcoholic KOH, compound [C] gives compound [D] which upon ozonolysis yields formaldehyde and acetaldehyde. Identify the compounds [A], [B], [C] and [D].
  1. [A]: C3H6O with a positive iodoform test has a CH3CO– group: propanone, CH3COCH3 (propanal would not respond).
    [½]
  2. [B]: LiAlH4 reduces the ketone: CH3COCH3 → propan-2-ol, CH3CH(OH)CH3.
    [½]
  3. [C]: CH3CH(OH)CH3 + SOCl2 → 2-chloropropane, CH3CHClCH3 + SO2 + HCl.
    [½]
  4. [D]: alc. KOH eliminates HCl: → propene, CH3CH=CH2. Ozonolysis: CH3CH=CH2 → CH3CHO + HCHO, as given.
    [½]

[A] propanone; [B] propan-2-ol; [C] 2-chloropropane; [D] propene

Question 8 · Board 2025 Imp.[2]
(i) Identify the compounds [A] and [B]: (a) CH3CHO →(K2Cr2O7 + H2SO4, [O]) CH3COOH →(Ca(OH)2) [A] →(Δ, dry distillation) [B]; (b) CH3COCH3 →(K2Cr2O7 + H2SO4, [O]) [A] →(PCl5) CH3COCl →(H2, Pd/BaSO4, boiling xylene) [B].
OR
(ii) An aromatic organic compound [A] with the molecular formula C7H6O reduces Tollen’s reagent and undergoes Cannizzaro reaction. On vigorous oxidation, it gives compound [B] which has the molecular formula C7H6O2. On reaction with sodium bi-carbonate, compound [B] forms brisk effervescence. Identify the compounds [A] and [B]. Write the chemical reaction of compound [B] with sodium bi-carbonate.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) CH3COOH + Ca(OH)2 → (CH3COO)2Ca (calcium acetate) + 2H2O: [A] = calcium acetate.
    [½]
  2. Dry distillation: (CH3COO)2Ca → CH3COCH3 + CaCO3: [B] = propanone (acetone).
    [½]
  3. (b) Vigorous oxidation of a ketone breaks the C–C bond next to C=O; acetone gives ethanoic acid (and CO2 + H2O): [A] = CH3COOH. PCl5 converts it to CH3COCl.
    [½]
  4. Rosenmund reduction of acetyl chloride: CH3COCl + H2 (Pd/BaSO4) → [B] = CH3CHO (ethanal) + HCl.
    [½]
Answer
(a) [A] calcium acetate, [B] propanone; (b) [A] ethanoic acid, [B] ethanal

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. [A] is an aldehyde (reduces Tollens’ reagent), has no α-hydrogen (Cannizzaro) and is aromatic with C7H6O: benzaldehyde, C6H5CHO.
    [1]
  2. [B]: oxidation gives benzoic acid, C6H5COOH (C7H6O2); it liberates CO2 with NaHCO3, the test for a carboxylic acid.
    [½]
  3. C6H5COOH + NaHCO3 → C6H5COONa + H2O + CO2↑
    [½]
Answer
[A] benzaldehyde; [B] benzoic acid; C6H5COOH + NaHCO3 → C6H5COONa + H2O + CO2

Concepts to recall

  • Dry distillation of calcium salts of carboxylic acids gives ketones (calcium formate + calcium acetate gives acetaldehyde).
  • Rosenmund reduction: RCOCl + H2 over Pd/BaSO4 (poisoned catalyst, stops at the aldehyde stage).
  • Ketones resist mild oxidants; strong oxidising agents cleave them to acids with fewer carbons (Popoff’s rule).

An organic compound [A] has molecular formula C7H6O2. When compound [A] is treated with SOCl2, it yields compound [B]. On heating with NH3, compound [B] forms compound [C]. Compound [C] forms compound [D] on reaction with Br2/KOH. Compound [D] responds to carbylamine test. Identify compounds [A], [B], [C] and [D].
  1. [A] reacts with SOCl2 (converts –COOH to –COCl) and C7H6O2 has a benzene ring plus one C=O/OH: [A] = benzoic acid, C6H5COOH.
    [½]
  2. C6H5COOH + SOCl2 → C6H5COCl + SO2 + HCl: [B] = benzoyl chloride.
    [½]
  3. C6H5COCl + 2NH3 → C6H5CONH2 + NH4Cl: [C] = benzamide.
    [½]

    [½]

[A] benzoic acid; [B] benzoyl chloride; [C] benzamide; [D] aniline.

TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (10)ISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q9
Question 9 · Board 2025[2]
(i) Identify the compounds [A], [B], [C] and [D] in the following reaction:
CH3COOH + PCl5 → [A] → [B] → [C] → [D]
[A] → [B]: H2, Pd/BaSO4, boiling xylene; [B] → [C]: K2Cr2O7 + H2SO4, [O]; [C] → [D]: Ca(OH)2, heat.
OR
(ii) Write chemical equations to convert the following: (a) Benzaldehyde to benzene (b) Benzoic acid to benzaldehyde

Solution to the first alternative

  1. CH3COOH + PCl5 → CH3COCl + POCl3 + HCl: [A] = ethanoyl (acetyl) chloride.
    [½]
  2. Rosenmund reduction (H2, Pd/BaSO4, boiling xylene): [B] = ethanal, CH3CHO.
    [½]
  3. Oxidation of the aldehyde with acidified K2Cr2O7: [C] = ethanoic acid, CH3COOH.
    [½]
  4. 2CH3COOH + Ca(OH)2 → (CH3COO)2Ca + 2H2O; on heating (dry distillation) (CH3COO)2Ca → CH3COCH3 + CaCO3: [D] = propanone (acetone).
    [½]
Answer
[A] CH3COCl; [B] CH3CHO; [C] CH3COOH; [D] CH3COCH3
Marking note The last arrow is read as lime water followed by heating the calcium ethanoate formed, which is the standard route to acetone.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) Oxidation to the acid: C6H5CHO + [O] → C6H5COOH (K2Cr2O7/H2SO4 or Tollens’ reagent).
    [½]
  2. Decarboxylation with soda lime: C6H5COONa + NaOH → C6H6 + Na2CO3 (CaO, heat), i.e. C6H5COOH → C6H6 via the sodium salt.
    [½]
  3. (b) C6H5COOH + SOCl2 → C6H5COCl + SO2 + HCl
    [½]
  4. C6H5COCl + H2 → C6H5CHO + HCl (Rosenmund: Pd/BaSO4, boiling xylene)
    [½]
Answer
(a) C6H5CHO → C6H5COOH → C6H6; (b) C6H5COOH → C6H5COCl → C6H5CHO
Marking note Any correct sequence is accepted for (a), e.g. aldehyde → acid → benzene; Clemmensen reduction would give toluene, not benzene.

Concepts to recall

  • Rosenmund reduction: RCOCl + H2 → RCHO + HCl over Pd/BaSO4 (poisoned catalyst stops at the aldehyde).
  • Dry distillation of the calcium salt of a carboxylic acid gives a ketone (symmetrical for calcium ethanoate: acetone).
  • Soda lime (NaOH + CaO) decarboxylates the sodium salt of a carboxylic acid: RCOONa → RH + Na2CO3.
  • Carboxylic acid → aldehyde needs two steps: acid chloride, then Rosenmund reduction (direct reduction goes on to the alcohol).

An organic compound [A] which has characteristic odour, reacts with conc. NaOH to give two compounds [B] and [C]. Compound [B] has molecular formula C7H8O which upon oxidation gives back compound [A]. Compound [C] is the sodium salt of the acid and upon treatment with sodalime yields an aromatic hydrocarbon [D]. Question 9 · Specimen 2025[2]
(i) Identify the compounds [A] and [B] in the following reactions.
(a) C6H5CHO → (1) K2Cr2O7/H2SO4, (2) SOCl2 → [A] → C6H6 (anhy. AlCl3) → [B]
(b) CH3–C≡CH → (1) H2SO4/HgSO4, (2) vigorous oxidation, conc. HNO3 → [A] → C2H5OH [conc. H2SO4] → [B]
OR
(ii) Three organic compounds A, B and C are non cyclic functional isomers of carbonyl compounds with molecular formula C4H8O. Isomers A and C give positive Tollen’s test while compound B does not give positive Tollen’s test but gives positive iodoform test. Compounds A and B on reduction with Zn amalgam and conc. HCl give the same product.
(a) Write the structures of the compounds A, B and C.
(b) Out of the compounds A, B and C, which one will be the least reactive towards addition of HCN.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) C6H5CHO → C6H5COOH (benzoic acid) → with SOCl2 [A] = C6H5COCl (benzoyl chloride); Friedel–Crafts acylation with benzene: [B] = C6H5COC6H5 (benzophenone). (½ + ½)

(a) [A] C6H5COCl, [B] C6H5COC6H5; (b) [A] CH3COOH, [B] CH3COOC2H5

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) Open-chain C4H8O carbonyl isomers: butanal, 2-methylpropanal, butanone. Tollens’ test is positive for aldehydes only, so A and C are aldehydes and B is butanone, which has the CH3CO– group (iodoform positive). Clemmensen reduction of A and B gives the same alkane: butanone gives butane, so A (the straight-chain aldehyde) is butanal. Hence C is 2-methylpropanal (gives isobutane). (1)
    [1]
  2. A = CH3CH2CH2CHO; B = CH3COCH2CH3; C = (CH3)2CHCHO. (b) Ketones are less reactive than aldehydes in nucleophilic addition (two electron-releasing alkyl groups reduce the carbonyl carbon’s positive charge, and two groups give steric hindrance): B (butanone) is least reactive towards HCN. (1)
    [1]
Answer
A butanal; B butanone; C 2-methylpropanal; B is least reactive towards HCN.

Concepts to recall

  • Friedel–Crafts acylation: ArH + RCOCl (anhy. AlCl3) → ArCOR + HCl.

  2. (ii) Ethanal (b.p. 294 K) is far more volatile than ethanol (b.p. 351 K), so it can be distilled out of the mixture as it forms. If left in contact with the oxidising agent it would be oxidised further to ethanoic acid.
