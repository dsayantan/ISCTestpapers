  • Conversions and sequences: Hofmann bromamide degradation, diazotisation then Sandmeyer, Gattermann, Balz–Schiemann and coupling, nitrile or nitro reduction, carbylamine reaction, ammonolysis.
  • Orders and tests: basic strength of ammonia, methylamine and aniline; which reaction does not give a primary amine; why aniline fails the Friedel–Crafts reaction; acetylation.
  • 3-mark identify [A], [B], [C] sequences from a bromoalkane or an amide appear in most papers, with each step a named reaction.
  • Habit: count carbons – Hofmann loses one, cyanide adds one.

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(v)
Question 1(B)(v) · Specimen 2027[1]
Which of the following reactions will NOT give primary amine?
(a)C2H5NC → (LiAlH4)
(b)C2H5CN → (LiAlH4)
(c)C2H5CONH2 → (Br2/KOH)
(d)CH3CONH2 → (Br2/KOH)
  1. (b) nitrile reduction: C2H5CN + 4[H] → C3H7NH2 (primary). (c) Hofmann bromamide: C2H5CONH2 → C2H5NH2 (primary). (d) CH3CONH2 → CH3NH2 (primary).
    [½]
  2. (a) An isocyanide R–N≡C is reduced at the carbon, which becomes CH3: C2H5NC + 4[H] → C2H5NHCH3, N-methylethanamine, a secondary amine.
    [½]
Answer
(a) C2H5NC → (LiAlH4)

Concepts to recall

  • Primary amines from: nitrile reduction, amide reduction, Hofmann bromamide (one carbon less), Gabriel phthalimide, ammonolysis (mixture), reduction of nitro compounds.
  • Isocyanide (carbylamine) reduction gives a secondary amine, R–NH–CH3.
  • Nitrile: RCN → RCH2NH2 (one carbon more than R); Hofmann: RCONH2 → RNH2.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
Ethylamine reacts with nitrous acid at low temperature to form:
(a)C2H5N2+Cl−, H2O
(b)C2H5OH, C2H5NO2
(c)C2H5OH, N2, H2O
(d)C2H5OH, NH3, H2O
  1. Primary aliphatic amines give unstable diazonium salts which decompose at once even at low temperature, giving the alcohol and nitrogen: C2H5NH2 + HNO2 → C2H5OH + N2 + H2O. This is option (c).
    [1]
Answer
(c) C2H5OH, N2, H2O

Concepts to recall

  • Aliphatic 1° amine + HNO2 (NaNO2 + HCl) → ROH + N2 (a stable diazonium salt is NOT formed).
  • Aromatic 1° amine + HNO2 at 273–278 K gives a stable diazonium salt, which is why aromatic and aliphatic amines are distinguished this way.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i) · Board 2025[1]
Which one of the following can produce the foul smelling compound methyl isocyanide in presence of alcoholic KOH?
(a)Chloroform and aniline
(b)Chloroform and methanol
(c)Chloroform and dimethyl amine
(d)Chloroform and methyl amine
  1. Carbylamine reaction: a primary amine + CHCl3 + 3KOH (alc.) → isocyanide. CH3NH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH → CH3NC + 3KCl + 3H2O.
    [½]
  2. Methyl isocyanide has the methyl group on N, so the amine must be methylamine. Aniline gives phenyl isocyanide; dimethylamine is secondary and does not react.
    [½]
Answer
(d) Chloroform and methylamine

Concepts to recall

  • Carbylamine test is given only by primary amines (aliphatic and aromatic): R–NH2 → R–NC (offensive smell).
  • The R group of the isocyanide is the R of the amine; the intermediate is dichlorocarbene :CCl2.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)CISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i) · Specimen 2025[1]
The correct order of the increasing basic nature of Ammonia, Methylamine and Aniline is:
(a)Methylamine < Aniline < Ammonia
(b)Aniline < Methylamine < Ammonia
(c)Ammonia < Aniline < Methylamine
(d)Aniline < Ammonia < Methylamine
  1. The +I effect of CH3 pushes electron density onto N, so methylamine holds H+ more readily than NH3. In aniline the N lone pair is delocalised into the benzene ring (resonance), so it is the weakest base. Increasing order: aniline (pKb 9·4) < ammonia (4·75) < methylamine (3·38).
    [1]
Answer
(d) Aniline < Ammonia < Methylamine

Concepts to recall

  • Basic strength = availability of the lone pair on N: alkyl groups (+I) increase it; an aryl group (resonance, −R) decreases it.
  • Aqueous order of aliphatic amines: (C2H5)2NH > (C2H5)3N > C2H5NH2 > NH3 (inductive plus solvation).
  • A larger pKb means a weaker base.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv) · Board 2024[1]
The reaction of ethanamide with alcoholic sodium hydroxide and bromine gives:
(a)ethylamine.
(b)methylamine.
(c)propylamine.
(d)aniline.
  1. This is Hofmann bromamide degradation: CH3CONH2 + Br2 + 4NaOH → CH3NH2 + Na2CO3 + 2NaBr + 2H2O. The amide loses its carbonyl carbon (as carbonate), so the amine has one carbon less: methylamine.
    [1]
Answer
(b) methylamine.

Concepts to recall

  • Hofmann bromamide: RCONH2 → RNH2 (primary amine with one carbon fewer). Intermediate: isocyanate RN=C=O.
  • To step up a carbon, go through the nitrile (KCN) and reduce; to step down, use Hofmann.
  • The migrating R keeps its configuration; only primary amides react.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vii)
Question 1(B)(vii) · Board 2024[1]
Also draws on: ammonolysis of alkyl halides; also Ch 6.
Assertion: Ammonolysis of alkyl halides involves the reaction between alkyl halides and alcoholic ammonia.
Reason: Ammonolysis of alkyl halides produces secondary amines only.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true but Reason is false.
(d)Assertion is false but Reason is true.
  1. Assertion true: ammonolysis is the nucleophilic substitution of the halogen by NH3 (alcoholic ammonia in a sealed tube, heated). Reason false: RX + NH3 → RNH2, which attacks more RX to give R2NH, R3N and R4N+X−: a mixture of primary, secondary, tertiary amines and the quaternary salt, not secondary amines only.
    [1]
Answer
(c) Assertion is true but Reason is false.

Concepts to recall

  • Ammonolysis: RX + NH3 → RNH3+X− → RNH2 with a base; then repeated alkylation.
  • To get mainly the primary amine use a large excess of NH3; the Gabriel phthalimide synthesis gives pure primary aliphatic amines.
  • Aryl halides do not undergo ammonolysis under these conditions.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (8)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q6
Question 6 · Specimen 2027[2]
Also draws on: part (ii) is a Wurtz-type coupling of a haloalkane (Ch 6).
How can the following conversions be carried out? (Write the chemical equations):
(i) Aniline to chlorobenzene
(ii) Chloroethane to n-butane
  1. (i) Diazotisation then Sandmeyer reaction: C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2HCl (273–278 K) → C6H5N2+Cl− + NaCl + 2H2O;  C6H5N2Cl + Cu2Cl2/HCl (or Cu/HCl, Gattermann) → C6H5Cl + N2.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Wurtz reaction: 2C2H5Cl + 2Na (dry ether) → C2H5–C2H5 (n-butane) + 2NaCl.
    [1]
Answer
(i) C6H5NH2 → (NaNO2/HCl, 273 K) C6H5N2Cl → (Cu2Cl2/HCl) C6H5Cl. (ii) 2C2H5Cl + 2Na → C4H10 + 2NaCl

Concepts to recall

  • Diazonium salts are made at 273–278 K (they decompose above 5 °C) and are replaced in Sandmeyer (CuX/HX), Gattermann (Cu/HX), Balz–Schiemann (HBF4) and H3PO2 reactions.
  • Wurtz: 2R–X + 2Na (dry ether) → R–R; the product has twice the number of carbon atoms (even-carbon alkanes).
  • Cl cannot be introduced into the ring by direct nitrogen replacement; the diazonium route is the only practical one from aniline.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (8)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q9
Question 9 · Specimen 2027[2]
(i) Illustrate the following reactions with an example in each case:
  (a) Coupling reaction
  (b) Balz–Schiemann reaction
OR
(ii) An organic compound [A] with molecular formula C3H7NO forms compound [B] on heating with Br2 and KOH. Compound [B] on heating with CHCl3 and alcoholic KOH produces a pungent smelling compound [C]. Compound [B] on reacting with C6H5SO2Cl forms compound [D] which is soluble in alkali. Write the structures of compounds [A], [B], [C] and [D].

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) Coupling: benzenediazonium chloride couples with phenol in alkaline medium (273–278 K) at the para position to give the azo dye p-hydroxyazobenzene: C6H5N2+Cl− + C6H5OH → C6H5–N=N–C6H4OH + HCl (orange dye). (With aniline the product is p-aminoazobenzene, yellow, in weakly acidic medium.)
    [1]
  2. (b) Balz–Schiemann: C6H5N2+Cl− + HBF4 → C6H5N2+BF4− (precipitate) + HCl; heating the fluoroborate: C6H5N2BF4 → C6H5F + BF3 + N2 (fluorobenzene).
    [1]
Answer
(a) C6H5N2Cl + phenol (alkali, 273 K) → p-hydroxyazobenzene. (b) C6H5N2BF4 → (heat) C6H5F + BF3 + N2

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. [A] + Br2/KOH is Hofmann bromamide degradation, so [A] is an amide: C3H7NO = CH3CH2CONH2 (propanamide). [B] has one carbon less: C2H5NH2 (ethanamine).
    [1]
  2. [B] + CHCl3 + alc. KOH is the carbylamine test: [C] = C2H5NC (ethyl isocyanide, foul smell). [B] + C6H5SO2Cl (Hinsberg) gives [D] = C6H5SO2NHC2H5, soluble in alkali because the N–H hydrogen is acidic (the sulfonyl group is strongly electron-withdrawing), as is true for a primary amine.
    [1]
Answer
[A] C2H5CONH2; [B] C2H5NH2; [C] C2H5NC; [D] C6H5SO2NHC2H5
Marking note Only the primary amide fits: the other C3H7NO amides (CH3CONHCH3, HCON(CH3)2) do not undergo Hofmann degradation.

Concepts to recall

  • Diazonium ion = a weak electrophile; it attacks the activated para position of phenols and arylamines (coupling, forming N=N, a chromophore).
  • Fluorine and iodine cannot be introduced by Sandmeyer: use HBF4 (Balz–Schiemann) for F, KI for I.
  • Diazonium salt reactions retaining N: coupling; those losing N2: Sandmeyer, Gattermann, Balz–Schiemann, H3PO2, boiling water (phenol).

Question 7 · Board 2026[2]
Write chemical reactions for the following named organic reactions: (i) Diazotisation reaction; (ii) Sandmeyer’s reaction.
  1. (i) Aniline with NaNO2 and HCl at 273–278 K: C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2HCl → C6H5N2+Cl− + NaCl + 2H2O (benzenediazonium chloride).
    [1]
  2. (ii) The diazonium salt with Cu(I) halide: C6H5N2+Cl− → C6H5Cl + N2 (reagent Cu2Cl2/HCl); similarly Cu2Br2/HBr gives C6H5Br and CuCN/KCN gives C6H5CN.
    [1]
Answer
(i) C6H5NH2 → C6H5N2+Cl−; (ii) C6H5N2+Cl− + Cu2Cl2 → C6H5Cl + N2
Common slip Temperature above 5°C converts the diazonium salt into phenol.

Concepts to recall

  • Diazotisation: a primary aromatic amine + HNO2 (NaNO2 + HCl, 0–5°C) gives a diazonium salt; it must be kept cold or it decomposes.
  • Sandmeyer: Cu(I) salts (CuCl, CuBr, CuCN) replace N2+ by Cl, Br, CN. Gattermann: Cu powder with HX instead.

Question 3 · Specimen 2026[2]
Also draws on: part (ii) is a haloalkane to carboxylic acid conversion (Ch 6, 8).
Write the chemical equations to convert each of the following:
(i) Aniline to bromobenzene
(ii) Ethyl chloride to propanoic acid
  1. (i) Diazotisation: C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2HCl → C6H5N2+Cl− + NaCl + 2H2O (273–278 K). Sandmeyer reaction: C6H5N2+Cl− → C6H5Br + N2 with CuBr/HBr (or Cu/HBr, Gattermann).
    [1]
  2. (ii) CH3CH2Cl + KCN → CH3CH2CN + KCl (alc. KCN); hydrolysis CH3CH2CN + 2H2O → CH3CH2COOH + NH3 (H+/H2O, heat).
    [1]
Answer
(i) C6H5NH2 → C6H5N2+Cl− → C6H5Br; (ii) C2H5Cl → C2H5CN → C2H5COOH
Marking note Names or formulae of the intermediates need not be given, but the reagent and condition of each step are the marks.

Concepts to recall

  • Aryl diazonium salts are made at 273–278 K (they decompose above 5 °C) and replace N2+ by Cl, Br (CuX, Sandmeyer), I (KI), F (HBF4, Balz–Schiemann), H (H3PO2) or OH.
  • Ascending the homologous series by one carbon: R–X → R–CN (KCN) → R–COOH (hydrolysis). The nitrile carbon becomes the carboxyl carbon.
  • Use alcoholic KCN: in aqueous KOH the halide would give the alcohol.

Question 11 · Specimen 2026[2]
Write the chemical equation for each of the following named organic reactions:
(i) Hofmann’s degradation reaction
(ii) Balz–Schiemann reaction
  1. (i) Hofmann bromamide degradation (primary amine with one carbon less): CH3CONH2 + Br2 + 4KOH → CH3NH2 + K2CO3 + 2KBr + 2H2O.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Arenediazonium chloride is treated with fluoroboric acid (HBF4) at 273–278 K to give the diazonium tetrafluoroborate, which on heating gives fluorobenzene: C6H5N2+Cl− + HBF4 → C6H5N2+BF4− + HCl; C6H5N2+BF4− → C6H5F + BF3 + N2.
    [1]
Answer
(i) RCONH2 + Br2 + 4KOH → RNH2 + K2CO3 + 2KBr + 2H2O; (ii) ArN2+Cl− → ArN2+BF4− → ArF
Marking note For (i) any primary amide with its amine (and K2CO3/KBr or Na2CO3/NaBr) is accepted.

Concepts to recall

  • Hofmann bromamide: amide → amine with one carbon less (isocyanate intermediate); the C=O is lost as K2CO3. Balance: 4KOH, 2KBr, 2H2O.
  • Balz–Schiemann is the way to make ArF (direct fluorination is too violent; KI is the route to ArI).
  • Name reactions need the full equation with reagents and conditions: the examiner gives a mark each.

  1. [C] with formula C6H7N is aniline, C6H5NH2.

[A] benzoic acid; [B] benzamide; [C] aniline; Hofmann bromamide reaction

Question 6 · Board 2025[2]
Write a balanced chemical equation for each of the following: (i) Ethyl cyanide is reduced with LiAlH4. (ii) Aniline is treated with bromine water.
  1. (i) CH3CH2CN + 4[H] → CH3CH2CH2NH2 (LiAlH4, ether): propan-1-amine, a primary amine.
    [1]
  2. (ii) C6H5NH2 + 3Br2 → 2,4,6-C6H2Br3NH2 + 3HBr: white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline.
    [1]

TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (8)CISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q6
Question 6 · Specimen 2025[2]
An organic compound [X] of molecular formula C3H5N, in one reaction produced a primary amine with LiAlH4. In another case, the organic compound [Y] of same molecular formula C3H5N produced a secondary amine with LiAlH4. Identify both the compounds [X] and [Y]. Why do these compounds form two different products?
  1. [X] = C2H5–C≡N (propanenitrile): C2H5CN + 4[H] → C2H5CH2NH2 (propan-1-amine, primary). [Y] = C2H5–N≡C (ethyl isocyanide): C2H5NC + 4[H] → C2H5NHCH3 (N-methylethanamine, secondary). (½ for each structure with its product)
    [1]
  2. Reason: in a nitrile the carbon of the C≡N is bonded to R, so adding hydrogen gives R–CH2–NH2 (N has two H); in an isocyanide the nitrogen is bonded to R and the terminal carbon becomes CH3, so N carries R, CH3 and H: R–NH–CH3. (1)
    [1]
Answer
[X] C2H5CN (→ C3H7NH2); [Y] C2H5NC (→ C2H5NHCH3); the groups attached to the nitrogen differ.
Marking note CISCE’s key gives only the structures of [X] and [Y] and says they give primary and secondary amines; the explanation above is the full answer.

Concepts to recall

  • Nitriles: RCN + 4[H] (LiAlH4 or Na/C2H5OH) → RCH2NH2 (primary amine, one carbon more than R).
  • Isocyanides (carbylamines): R–N≡C reduce to R–NH–CH3 (secondary amine).
  • Nitrile and isocyanide are functional (chain/position) isomers; the carbylamine test (CHCl3 + KOH) gives isocyanides from 1° amines.

Question 18 · Specimen 2027[3]
Write the chemical equations for the following conversions:
(i) Nitrobenzene to benzene
(ii) Aniline to bromobenzene
(iii) Methyl cyanide to ethylamine
  1. (i) Reduce, diazotise, then replace by H: C6H5NO2 + 6[H] (Sn/HCl) → C6H5NH2 + 2H2O;  C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2HCl (273–278 K) → C6H5N2+Cl−;  C6H5N2+Cl− + H3PO2 + H2O → C6H6 + N2 + H3PO3 + HCl.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Sandmeyer: C6H5NH2 + HNO2 + HCl (273–278 K) → C6H5N2+Cl−;  C6H5N2+Cl− + Cu2Br2/HBr → C6H5Br + N2.
    [1]
  3. (iii) Reduction of the nitrile (one carbon more in the amine chain): CH3CN + 4[H] (Na/C2H5OH, or H2/Ni, or LiAlH4) → CH3CH2NH2.
    [1]
Answer
(i) C6H5NO2 → C6H5NH2 → C6H5N2Cl → C6H6; (ii) C6H5N2Cl + Cu2Br2/HBr → C6H5Br; (iii) CH3CN + 4[H] → CH3CH2NH2

Concepts to recall

  • Nitro → amine: Sn/HCl, Fe/HCl or H2/Pd. Amine → diazonium: NaNO2 + HCl at 273–278 K. Diazonium → H: H3PO2 (or C2H5OH).
  • Sandmeyer: CuCl/HCl (Cl), CuBr/HBr (Br), CuCN/KCN (CN); Gattermann uses Cu powder.
  • Nitrile reduction gives RCH2NH2; CH3CN has two carbons, so the product CH3CH2NH2 keeps both and the C≡N carbon becomes CH2.

Question 18 · Specimen 2026[3]
Identify the compounds [A], [B] and [C] in the following reactions:
(i) C6H5CONH2 →(Br2/aq. KOH) [A] →(NaNO2 + HCl, ice cold) [B] →(Cu2Cl2 + HCl) [C]
(ii) CH3C≡N →(4[H]) [A] →(HNO2, 0 °C) [B] →(SOCl2, pyridine) [C]
  1. (i) [A] = C6H5NH2 (aniline; Hofmann bromamide degradation: the amine has one carbon less).

    [1]

Question 13 · Board 2024[3]
(i) Illustrate the following reactions by giving one suitable example in each case. (a) Coupling reaction (b) Acetylation of ethylamine
(ii) Aniline does not give Friedel–Crafts reaction. Give a reason.
  1. (i)(a) Coupling: benzenediazonium chloride reacts with phenol in a weakly alkaline medium (pH 9–10) at 273–278 K, the para position of phenol being attacked, to give the orange dye p-hydroxyazobenzene: C6H5N2+Cl− + C6H5OH → C6H5–N=N–C6H4–OH + HCl. (With aniline in weakly acidic medium it gives p-aminoazobenzene, a yellow dye.)
    [1]
  2. (i)(b) Acetylation: the H of the N–H is replaced by CH3CO–: C2H5NH2 + CH3COCl → C2H5NHCOCH3 + HCl (N-ethylethanamide; in presence of pyridine to remove HCl). Acetic anhydride may be used instead of acetyl chloride.
    [1]
  3. (ii) Aniline is a Lewis base and the AlCl3 catalyst is a Lewis acid. They combine through the nitrogen lone pair to form a salt, C6H5NH2→AlCl3, in which N carries a positive charge and strongly deactivates the ring towards electrophilic attack, so the reaction fails.
    [1]
Answer
(i)(a) Diazonium chloride + phenol → p-hydroxyazobenzene; (b) C2H5NH2 + CH3COCl → C2H5NHCOCH3; (ii) AlCl3 forms a salt with the basic NH2 group.

Concepts to recall

  • Coupling: ArN2+ (electrophile) + activated arene (phenol, aniline) → azo dye Ar–N=N–Ar′; para position, mildly alkaline for phenols, mildly acidic for amines.
  • Acylation of a 1° or 2° amine (not 3°) replaces N–H by N–COR. It also protects the NH2 group of aniline before nitration or bromination.
  • Friedel–Crafts fails with strongly deactivated rings and with substituents that react with AlCl3 (–NH2, –NHR, –NR2).
