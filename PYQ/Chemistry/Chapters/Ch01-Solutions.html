Official weightage of this unit in the ISC 2027 syllabus: 25 (Physical, with Ch 2 and 3) marks. Marks are the paper’s own; where a question offers an OR alternative from another chapter, the alternative sits in its own chapter and its marks are not counted, so each paper’s marks add to 70.

What to revise

  • Numericals every paper: osmotic pressure and molar mass of a protein, depression of freezing point, elevation of boiling point, abnormal molar mass with the van’t Hoff factor i (degree of dissociation of BaCl2, association of benzoic acid or phenol as a dimer), vapour pressure by Raoult’s law.
  • Often a 5-mark case study: isotonic, hypotonic and hypertonic solutions, saline for injection, osmosis in eggs and red blood cells. One-markers ask the order of freezing points or osmotic pressures of urea, NaCl, MgCl2, AlCl3.
  • Concepts: colligative properties depend only on the number of particles, ideal and non-ideal solutions, azeotropes, Henry’s law (soda water, aquatic life in cold water).
  • Habit: find i first for any electrolyte; convert grams of solvent to kg and cm3 to litres before substituting.

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i) · Specimen 2027[1]
The correct order of freezing point of 0·5 M solution of urea, AlCl3, KCl and MgCl2 is:
(a)AlCl3 > MgCl2 > KCl > urea
(b)Urea > KCl > MgCl2 > AlCl3
(c)MgCl2 > KCl > AlCl3 > urea
(d)KCl > MgCl2 > AlCl3 > urea
  1. ΔTf = iKfm: for equal molarity the depression is proportional to the van’t Hoff factor i (the number of particles per formula unit). Urea i = 1, KCl i = 2, MgCl2 i = 3, AlCl3 i = 4.
    [½]
  2. The larger the depression, the lower the freezing point, so the freezing points run: urea > KCl > MgCl2 > AlCl3.
    [½]
Answer
(b) Urea > KCl > MgCl2 > AlCl3
Common slip Writing the order of depression (AlCl3 largest) instead of the order of freezing points reverses the answer.

Concepts to recall

  • ΔTf = iKfm is a colligative property: it depends on the number of particles, not their nature.
  • i for complete dissociation = number of ions per formula unit: KCl 2, MgCl2 3, AlCl3 4, urea 1.
  • More particles → larger ΔTf → lower freezing point. Watch that the question asks for the order of freezing points, not depressions.

Fill in the blank(s) by choosing from: [first, alc. AgCN, primary, decrease, third, increase, zero, Lucas, alc. KCN, KOH, no change, two, Tollen’s, AgOH, tertiary]
Dissociation of solute particles leads to a / an ________ in the magnitude of a colligative property and a / an ________ in the value of the observed molecular mass.

Question 1(B)(vii) · Board 2026[1]
Assertion: The depression of freezing point of 0·1 molal aqueous solution of MgCl2 is less than that of 0·1 molal aqueous solution of NaCl.
Reason: The number of particles furnished by 0·1 molal MgCl2 is less than that of 0·1 molal NaCl.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Ideally MgCl2 → Mg2+ + 2Cl− gives 3 particles (i ≈ 3) against 2 for NaCl (i ≈ 2).
    [½]
  2. ΔTf = iKfm is therefore greater for MgCl2: the Assertion is false, and the Reason (fewer particles from MgCl2) is also false. Option (d).
    [½]
Answer
(d) Both Assertion and Reason are false.

Concepts to recall

  • ΔTf = iKfm; equal molalities, so compare i (number of particles per formula unit).
  • Order of ΔTf for equimolal solutions: AlCl3 (4) > MgCl2 (3) > NaCl (2) > glucose (1).
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i) · Specimen 2026[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [electrophilic, four, 4-bromophenol, first, alc. AgCN, decreases, 2,4,6-tribromophenol, nucleophilic, two, zero, alc. KCN, increases].
The addition of a non-volatile solute to a pure solvent ______ its vapour pressure and ______ its boiling point.
  1. Solute particles occupy part of the surface, so fewer solvent molecules escape: the vapour pressure decreases (Raoult’s law).
    [½]
  2. The solution must be heated higher before its vapour pressure equals the atmospheric pressure, so the boiling point increases: ΔTb = Kbm.
    [½]
Answer
decreases; increases
Marking note Both words are needed for the mark; award ½ each if the marking is split.

Concepts to recall

  • Colligative properties depend on the number of solute particles, not their nature: lowering of vapour pressure, elevation of boiling point, depression of freezing point, osmotic pressure.
  • Raoult’s law for a non-volatile solute: (p° − p)/p° = xsolute.
  • Vapour pressure falls, so boiling point rises and freezing point falls: the three move together.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vi)
Question 1(B)(vi) · Specimen 2026[1]
Assertion: 0·1 M sucrose solution has higher depression in the freezing point than 0·1 M urea solution.
Reason: Depression in freezing point is not a colligative property.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Sucrose and urea are both non-electrolytes (i = 1), so equal molarities give the same ΔTf = Kfm: the Assertion is false. Depression of freezing point depends only on the number of solute particles, so it is a colligative property: the Reason is false. Both are false.
    [1]
Answer
(d) Both Assertion and Reason are false.

Concepts to recall

  • ΔTf = iKfm: equal concentrations of non-electrolytes (i = 1) give equal depression whatever the solute.
  • The four colligative properties: relative lowering of vapour pressure, elevation of boiling point, depression of freezing point, osmotic pressure.
  • Judge the Assertion and the Reason separately before asking whether one explains the other.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2025 Improvement Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
Which one of the following statements are correct regarding the depression of freezing point?
(P) The depression in freezing point is directly proportional to the amount of solute dissolved in solution.
(Q) The depression in freezing point is inversely proportional to the amount of solute dissolved in solution.
(R) Vapour pressure of the solution is more than that of pure solvent.
(S) Vapour pressure of the solution is less than that of pure solvent.
(a)Only (P) and (S) are correct.
(b)Only (Q) and (R) are correct.
(c)Only (Q) and (S) are correct.
(d)Only (P) and (Q) are correct.
  1. ΔTf = Kfm, so the depression is directly proportional to the amount of solute: (P) is true, (Q) false. A non-volatile solute lowers the vapour pressure of the solvent (Raoult’s law): (S) is true, (R) false. Only (P) and (S) are correct: option (a).
    [1]
Answer
(a) Only (P) and (S) are correct

Concepts to recall

  • ΔTf = Kf × m = Kf × 1000wB/(MBwA): a colligative property, proportional to the number of solute particles.
  • Solute added to a solvent lowers its vapour pressure; the solution therefore meets the solid’s curve at a lower temperature, so it freezes lower.

Question 1(B)(ii) · Board 2025[1]
The osmotic pressure of a solution: (P) increases with an increase in number of moles of solute; (Q) decreases with an increase in number of moles of solute; (R) increases at a higher temperature; (S) is dependent on the nature of solute. Which one of the following is correct?
(a)Only (P) and (Q) are correct
(b)Only (P) and (R) are correct
(c)Only (P) and (S) are correct
(d)Only (Q) and (S) are correct
  1. π = CRT = (n/V)RT: π ∝ n (P is true, Q is false) and π ∝ T (R is true).
    [½]
  2. Osmotic pressure is a colligative property: it depends on the number of solute particles, not on their nature (S is false).
    [½]
Answer
(b) Only (P) and (R) are correct

Concepts to recall

  • π = iCRT; for a non-electrolyte i = 1. It rises with concentration and with temperature.
  • Colligative properties depend on the number of particles only, not on what the particles are.

Question 1(B)(ii) · Specimen 2025[1]
Colligative properties of a solution are:
(P) Independent of the nature of solute
(Q) Proportional to molecular mass of solute
(R) Proportional to concentration of solute
(S) Independent of the amount of solvent
(a)Only P and Q are correct
(b)Only P and R are correct
(c)Only P and S are correct
(d)Only Q and S are correct
  1. Colligative properties depend only on the number of solute particles per amount of solvent. (P) true: they do not depend on what the solute is. (Q) false: for a given mass, a larger molar mass gives fewer particles and hence a smaller effect, so the property is inversely, not directly, related to molar mass. (R) true: ΔTf = Kfm, π = CRT. (S) false: molality and molarity both involve the amount of solvent or solution.
    [1]
Answer
(b) Only P and R are correct

Concepts to recall

  • The four colligative properties: relative lowering of vapour pressure, elevation of boiling point, depression of freezing point, osmotic pressure.
  • For equimolar solutions the effect is proportional to the van’t Hoff factor i.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(v)
Question 1(B)(v) · Board 2024[1]
An equimolar solution of non-volatile solutes A and B, shows a depression in freezing point in the ratio of 2:1. If A remains in its normal state in the solution, the state of B in the solution will be:
(a)normal.
(b)hydrolysed.
(c)associated.
(d)dissociated.
  1. ΔTf = i Kfm; for equimolar solutions ΔTf ∝ i. A is normal, so iA = 1. ΔTf(A) : ΔTf(B) = 2 : 1 gives iB = 1/2 < 1. A van’t Hoff factor below 1 means fewer particles than formula units: B is associated (for example as a dimer).
    [1]
Answer
(c) associated.

Concepts to recall

  • i = observed colligative property / normal value = normal molar mass / observed molar mass.
  • i > 1: dissociation (KCl, i → 2); i = 1: normal; i < 1: association (ethanoic acid in benzene, i → 0·5).
  • Association increases the observed molar mass.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(C)(i)
Question 1(C)(i) · Board 2024[1]
Passage. When two solutions are separated by a semi-permeable membrane, the solvent molecules move from a solution of lower molar concentration to a solution of higher molar concentration through osmosis.
Samar removed the outer hard shell of two different eggs while cooking at home. He then placed one egg in pure water and the other egg in saturated solution of sucrose. What change is he likely to observe in the eggs after few hours?
  1. Egg in pure water: water enters the egg (the inside has the higher solute concentration) by endosmosis, so the egg swells. Egg in saturated sucrose solution: water leaves the egg (the outside is more concentrated) by exosmosis, so the egg shrinks.
    [1]
Answer
The egg in pure water swells; the egg in the sucrose solution shrinks.

Concepts to recall

  • Osmosis: net flow of solvent through a semi-permeable membrane from lower solute concentration to higher solute concentration.
  • Hypotonic outside (pure water) → cell swells; hypertonic outside → cell shrinks; isotonic → no change.
  • Real-life uses: preserving pickles and jams, wilting of salted slugs, intravenous (isotonic) saline.

Passage. When two solutions are separated by a semi-permeable membrane, the solvent molecules move from a solution of lower molar concentration to a solution of higher molar concentration through osmosis.
Which solution, hypertonic or hypotonic, has a higher amount of solute in same quantity of solution?
  1. A hypertonic solution has the higher osmotic pressure, so for the same quantity of solution it holds the higher amount of solute; the hypotonic solution holds less.

Hypertonic solution

Question 1(C)(iii) · Board 2024[1]
Passage. When two solutions are separated by a semi-permeable membrane, the solvent molecules move from a solution of lower molar concentration to a solution of higher molar concentration through osmosis.
A 5 % aqueous solution of glucose (molar mass = 180 g mol−1) is isotonic with 1·66 % aqueous solution of urea. Calculate the molar mass of urea.
  1. Isotonic: πglucose = πurea, hence (same T) concentrations in mol L−1 are equal: 5/180 = 1·66/M.  M = (1·66 × 180)/5 = 59·76 g mol−1 (about 60 g mol−1).
    [1]
Answer
Molar mass of urea ≈ 59·8 g mol−1 (≈ 60)
Marking note 5 % and 1·66 % are taken as 5 g and 1·66 g per 100 mL (same volume) of solution.

Concepts to recall

  • Isotonic solutions have equal osmotic pressure, hence equal molar concentration at the same temperature (for non-electrolytes).
  • π = (n/V)RT = wBRT/(MBV); a w/v % means the same volume of both solutions, so the volume cancels.
  • Urea is CO(NH2)2: 12 + 16 + 2 × 16 = 60 g mol−1, a quick check.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (4)CISCE Specimen 2027 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q11
Question 11 · Specimen 2027[2]
The students of Class XII were on a study trip. They visited a nearby lake and took some water samples which were rich in sodium chloride salt. The boiling point of lake water was found to be 373·032 K.
If 500 g of lake water sample was used which contained 0·45 g of NaCl, what is the observed molecular weight of NaCl in the lake sample? Assume that NaCl ionises completely in lake water.
(Given: Kb of water = 0·52 K kg mol−1, boiling point of pure water = 373 K)
  1. Elevation of boiling point: ΔTb = 373·032 − 373 = 0·032 K. NaCl ionises completely: NaCl → Na+ + Cl−, so i = 2.
    [½]
  2. ΔTb = i Kb w × 1000/(M W), so Mobs = i × 1000 Kb w/(ΔTb W)
    [½]
  3. Mobs = 2 × 1000 × 0·52 × 0·45/(0·032 × 500)
    [½]
  4. Mobs = 29·25 g mol−1
    [½]
Answer
Mobs = 29·25 g mol−1
Marking note This is the examiner’s intended method (i = 2 as the question instructs). The data are not self-consistent: 0·45 g NaCl in 500 g water would raise the boiling point by only 0·016 K if ionised completely (the stated 0·032 K corresponds to i = 4); without the factor i the same formula gives 14·63 g mol−1. Follow the question’s instruction and give 29·25.

Concepts to recall

  • ΔTb = iKbm, with molality m = (w/M)(1000/W) for w g of solute in W g of solvent.
  • For complete ionisation of NaCl i = 2; the ‘observed’ molar mass from a colligative property of an electrolyte is Mnormal/i (about half of 58·5).
  • Boiling-point elevation is a colligative property: it depends on the number of particles; a dissociating solute gives a lower apparent molar mass.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (4)ISC 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q3
Question 3 · Board 2026[2]
(i) 0·680 g of a compound is dissolved in 15·0 g of benzene and the freezing point of the solution is lowered by 1·44°C. Calculate the experimental molecular mass of the compound. (Kf for benzene = 5·12 K kg mol−1)
(ii) If the theoretical molecular mass of the compound is 80·5 g mol−1, suggest whether it is undergoing association or dissociation.
  1. (i) ΔTf = Kf × w2 × 1000/(M2 × w1), so M2 = 5·12 × 0·680 × 1000/(1·44 × 15·0)
    [½]
  2. M2 = 161·2 g mol−1
    [½]
  3. (ii) Observed molar mass (161·2) is about twice the theoretical value (80·5): i = 80·5/161·2 = 2·00 ≈ 0·5 (< 1).
    [½]
  4. Fewer particles than expected: the solute associates (forms dimers) in benzene.
    [½]
Answer
(i) M = 161·2 g mol−1; (ii) association (dimerisation)

Concepts to recall

  • Molar mass from freezing point depression: M2 = 1000Kfw2/(ΔTfw1) (w1 solvent mass in g).
  • Van’t Hoff factor i = normal molar mass / observed molar mass: i < 1 association, i > 1 dissociation.
  • Carboxylic acids associate (dimerise) in benzene through hydrogen bonds.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (4)ISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q5
Question 5 · Specimen 2026[2]
When 2 g of benzoic acid (C6H5COOH) is dissolved in 25 g of benzene, it shows a depression in freezing point equal to 1·62 K. Molal depression constant for benzene is 4·7 K kg mol−1. What is the percentage association of acid if it forms dimer in solution?
  1. Mnormal (C6H5COOH) = 122 g mol−1.  Mobs = 1000 Kfw/(ΔTfW) = (1000 × 4·7 × 2)/(1·62 × 25) = 9400/40·5 = 232·1 g mol−1
    [½]
  2. i = Mnormal/Mobs = 122/232·1 = 0·526
    [½]
  3. Dimerisation 2A → A2: if α is the degree of association, i = 1 − α/2.
    [½]
  4. α = 2(1 − i) = 2(1 − 0·526) = 0·949, i.e. 94·9 % association
    [½]
Answer
About 94·9 % of the acid is associated (α ≈ 0·949)
Common slip Treating the acid as dissociating (i = 1 + α): the acid associates here, and i comes out less than 1.
Marking note CISCE’s key has an arithmetic slip. It gives Mobs = 241·98, but 1000 × 4·7 × 2/(1·62 × 25) = 232·1, so i = 0·526 and α = 0·949 (94·9 %), not 0·504 and 99·2 %. The key’s method (i = 1 − α/2) is exactly the one used here. Check: ΔTf for no association would be 4·7 × (2/122)/0·025 = 3·08 K; the observed 1·62 K is 0·526 of it. Method marks are safe; for the final value the working above is correct.

Concepts to recall

  • Association: n molecules form one: i = 1 − α(1 − 1/n); for a dimer i = 1 − α/2, so α = 2(1 − i), and i < 1.
  • Van’t Hoff factor from molar masses: i = Mnormal/Mobs = observed ΔTf / calculated ΔTf.
  • ΔTf = Kf × 1000w/(MW) with W in grams (so the 1000).
  • Carboxylic acids associate in non-polar solvents by two hydrogen bonds (a cyclic dimer).

Question 9 · Board 2024[2]
If 200 cm3 of an aqueous solution of a protein contains 1·26 g of protein, the osmotic pressure of the solution at 300 K is found to be 2·57 × 10−3 atm. Calculate the molar mass of protein. (R = 0·0821 L atm K−1 mol−1)
  1. π = (wB/MB)RT/V, so MB = wBRT/(πV)
    [½]
  2. V = 200 cm3 = 0·200 L.  MB = (1·26 × 0·0821 × 300)/(2·57 × 10−3 × 0·200)
    [1]
  3. = 6·04 × 104 g mol−1
    [½]
Answer
M of protein ≈ 6·04 × 104 g mol−1

  • Osmotic pressure: π = CRT = (n/V)RT; it is the colligative property best suited to polymers and proteins because even dilute solutions give a measurable π.
  • Use V in litres with R = 0·0821 L atm K−1 mol−1; T in kelvin.
  • π is measured at constant temperature (not dependent on the nature of the solute, only on the number of particles).

Question 13 · Board 2025 Imp.[3]
The colligative properties are properties of solutions which depend only upon the number of solute particles irrespective of their nature. If the solute dissociates or associates, we get abnormal molecular weights, accounted by van’t Hoff factor (i).
(i) When 1·90 g of benzoic acid is dissolved in 26 g of benzene, it causes a depression in freezing point by 1·60 K. The molal depression constant for benzene is 4·8 K kg mol−1 and the molecular mass of benzoic acid is 122 g mol−1. Calculate the degree of association of benzoic acid if it forms dimer in solution.
(ii) Arrange the aqueous solution of the following compounds in the increasing order of value of van’t Hoff factor: Benzoic acid, Barium chloride, Urea
  1. (i) Molality (calculated, no association) m = (1·90/122)/(26/1000) = 0·599 mol kg−1; ΔTf(calc) = 4·8 × 0·599 = 2·875 K.
    [1]
  2. i = observed/calculated = 1·60/2·875 = 0·556. For dimerisation 2A → A2: i = 1 − α/2.
    [½]
  3. α = 2(1 − i) = 2(1 − 0·556) = 0·887 (about 88·7 %).
    [½]
  4. (ii) Urea does not ionise (i = 1); benzoic acid is a weak acid and ionises slightly in water (i a little above 1); BaCl2 → Ba2+ + 2Cl− (i ≈ 3). Order: Urea < Benzoic acid < Barium chloride.
    [1]
Answer
(i) α ≈ 0·887 (88·7 %); (ii) urea < benzoic acid < barium chloride
Marking note In water benzoic acid does not dimerise, so i > 1 and the order is as given; a scheme that treats benzoic acid as associated (i < 1) would give benzoic acid < urea < barium chloride, but that holds for benzene, not for water.

Concepts to recall

  • ΔTf = iKfm; i = observed colligative property/calculated; i < 1 means association, i > 1 dissociation.
  • Association into n-mers: i = 1 − α(1 − 1/n); dimer: i = 1 − α/2.
  • Carboxylic acids associate by H-bonding in non-polar solvents like benzene, but ionise slightly in water.

Question 13 · Board 2025[3]
The scientist van’t Hoff introduced a factor (i) to account for the extent of association or dissociation of solutes. It is mathematically expressed as i = normal molecular mass / experimental molecular mass. In case of association, i < 1 and in case of dissociation i > 1.
(i) In the calculation of molecular mass of K4[Fe(CN)6] by using a colligative property, what will be the value of van’t Hoff factor if the solute is 25% dissociated?
(ii) Find the value of van’t Hoff factor for a dilute aqueous solution of benzoic acid in water when it is completely associated to form a dimer.
  1. (i) K4[Fe(CN)6] → 4K+ + [Fe(CN)6]4−: each formula unit gives n = 5 particles.
    [½]
  2. i = 1 + (n − 1)α = 1 + (5 − 1) × 0·25
    [½]
  3. i = 2
    [1]
  4. (ii) 2C6H5COOH → (C6H5COOH)2: two molecules combine into one. Complete association (α = 1): i = 1 − α/2 = 1 − 1/2 = 0·5
    [1]
Answer
(i) i = 2; (ii) i = 0·5

Concepts to recall

  • Dissociation: i = 1 + (n − 1)α; association (m molecules → 1): i = 1 − α(1 − 1/m).
  • i = normal (calculated) molar mass / observed molar mass = observed colligative property / calculated one.
  • Benzoic acid dimerises in benzene (hydrogen bonds); in water it is largely ionised, but the question states complete dimerisation.

Question 13 · Specimen 2025[3]
The molar mass calculated through colligative properties is sometimes different from that determined experimentally (abnormal molecular mass), due to association or dissociation of solute.
(A) The freezing point of a solution containing 5·85 g of NaCl in 100 g of water is −3·348 °C. (Kf of water = 1·86 K kg mol−1, molecular mass of NaCl = 58·5)
(B) The freezing point of benzene solution decreases by 0·45 °C when 0·2 g of acetic acid is added to 20 g of benzene. (Kf of benzene = 5·12 K kg mol−1, molecular mass of acetic acid = 60)
(i) Calculate the abnormal molecular mass of solute in both the cases.
(ii) Inferring the value of van’t Hoff factor in both the cases, find out which solution undergoes association and which solution undergoes dissociation.
(iii) Given below is the increasing order of depression in freezing point of water observed for equimolar concentrations of the compounds, acetic acid < trichloro acetic acid < trifluoro acetic acid. Provide a reason to explain if the above ordered arrangement is correct or not.

Concepts to recall

  • π = CRT = (n/V)RT; isotonic = same π; hypertonic = higher π (solute), hypotonic = lower π. Water always flows from the hypotonic to the hypertonic side.
  • Use R = 0·0821 L atm K−1 mol−1 (printed on the paper) when π is in atm, and T in kelvin (27 °C = 300 K, 37 °C = 310 K).
  • Biological use: intravenous fluids are 0·91 % saline or 5 % glucose; red cells burst in pure water (haemolysis) and shrink in strong salt solution.
  • ΔTb = Kbm, ΔTf = Kfm for a non-electrolyte (i = 1); molality m = moles of solute / kg of solvent.
  • Molar mass of ethylene glycol C2H6O2 = 24 + 6 + 32 = 62; urea CO(NH2)2 = 60.
  • Mass-based units (molality, mass %, mole fraction) do not change with temperature; volume-based units (molarity, normality) do.
