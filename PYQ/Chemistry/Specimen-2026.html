Question 1(A)(i)[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [electrophilic, four, 4-bromophenol, first, alc. AgCN, decreases, 2,4,6-tribromophenol, nucleophilic, two, zero, alc. KCN, increases].
The addition of a non-volatile solute to a pure solvent ______ its vapour pressure and ______ its boiling point.
  1. Solute particles occupy part of the surface, so fewer solvent molecules escape: the vapour pressure decreases (Raoult’s law).
    [½]
  2. The solution must be heated higher before its vapour pressure equals the atmospheric pressure, so the boiling point increases: ΔTb = Kbm.
    [½]
Answer
decreases; increases
Marking note Both words are needed for the mark; award ½ each if the marking is split.

Concepts to recall

  • Colligative properties depend on the number of solute particles, not their nature: lowering of vapour pressure, elevation of boiling point, depression of freezing point, osmotic pressure.
  • Raoult’s law for a non-volatile solute: (p° − p)/p° = xsolute.
  • Vapour pressure falls, so boiling point rises and freezing point falls: the three move together.

When the concentration of a reactant of first order reaction is doubled, the rate becomes ______ times but for ______ order reaction the rate remains the same.
  1. First order: rate = k[A]. Doubling [A] doubles the rate, so the rate becomes two times.
    [½]
  2. Zero order: rate = k[A]0 = k, which does not depend on concentration: the rate remains the same for a zero order reaction.
    [½]
Answer
two; zero

Concepts to recall

  • Rate law rate = k[A]n: if [A] is multiplied by x, the rate is multiplied by xn.
  • Zero order: the rate is constant (a surface-catalysed reaction on a saturated surface, e.g. NH3 on hot platinum).

Question 1(A)(iii)[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [electrophilic, four, 4-bromophenol, first, alc. AgCN, decreases, 2,4,6-tribromophenol, nucleophilic, two, zero, alc. KCN, increases].
Phenol when treated with bromine water produces ______, which is an example of ______ substitution reaction.
  1. Bromine water with phenol gives a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol: C6H5OH + 3Br2 → C6H2Br3OH + 3HBr.
    [½]
  2. The −OH group activates the ring at the ortho and para positions and the attacking species Br+ (an electron-seeking species) is an electrophile, so it is electrophilic substitution.
    [½]
Answer
2,4,6-tribromophenol; electrophilic
Marking note ‘4-bromophenol’ is the distractor, formed in a non-polar solvent at low temperature, not in bromine water.

Concepts to recall

  • −OH is a strongly activating, o-, p-directing group: phenol is brominated at all three positions even without a catalyst.
  • In CS2 or CHCl3 at low temperature, bromination stops at the mono-products (o- and p-bromophenol); the aqueous medium favours ionisation to the phenoxide and the tribromo product.
  • White ppt with bromine water is the test for phenol (and aniline).

Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [electrophilic, four, 4-bromophenol, first, alc. AgCN, decreases, 2,4,6-tribromophenol, nucleophilic, two, zero, alc. KCN, increases].
Methyl chloride on treatment with ______ gives methyl cyanide, whereas on treatment with ______, it gives methyl isocyanide.
  1. KCN is ionic and gives free CN−, which attacks through carbon: CH3Cl + KCN → CH3CN + KCl (methyl cyanide).
    [½]
  2. AgCN is covalent (Ag–C≡N); only the nitrogen lone pair is available for attack, giving CH3Cl + AgCN → CH3NC + AgCl (methyl isocyanide).
    [½]
Answer
alc. KCN; alc. AgCN

Concepts to recall

  • The cyanide ion is an ambident nucleophile: it can attack through C (giving R–CN) or through N (giving R–NC).
  • KCN, NaCN (ionic) → alkyl cyanide (nitrile); AgCN (covalent) → isocyanide (carbylamine).
  • Nitriles hydrolyse to acids (adds one C); isocyanides hydrolyse to primary amines and methanoic acid.

Question 1(B)(i)[1]
The sum of coordination number and oxidation number of metal ‘M’ in the complex [M(en)2Cl2] is:
(a)6
(b)8
(c)9
(d)10
  1. Coordination number: en is bidentate, so 2 × 2 + 2 = 6.
    [½]
  2. Oxidation number: en is neutral and each Cl− is −1. With the complex taken as the monopositive [M(en)2Cl2]+ (as in [Fe(en)2Cl2]Cl of Question 21), M is +3. Sum = 6 + 3 = 9.
    [½]
Answer
(c) 9
Marking note The charge of the complex is not printed, so strictly the oxidation number is not fixed (a neutral [M(en)2Cl2] would give M = +2 and a sum of 8). The marking scheme’s (c) corresponds to M(III), the state used in Question 21(ii). Examiners award the mark for (c).

Concepts to recall

  • Coordination number = number of donor atoms attached to the metal (a bidentate ligand counts twice), not the number of ligands.
  • Oxidation number = charge on the complex − sum of the charges of the ligands.
  • Common neutral ligands: NH3, H2O, en, CO; common anionic: Cl−, CN−, C2O42−.

Question 1(B)(ii)[1]
Which of the following orders is correct in spectrochemical series of ligands?
(P) I− < NH3 < CN− < CO
(Q) CO < I− < NH3 < CN−
(R) NH3 < CO < CN− < I−
(S) F− < NH3 < CN− < CO
(a)Only (Q) and (S) are correct.
(b)Only (P) and (S) are correct.
(c)Only (P) and (Q) are correct.
(d)Only (P) and (R) are correct.
  1. The series is I− < Br− < Cl− < F− < H2O < NH3 < en < CN− < CO. (P) and (S) both climb the series correctly; (Q) puts the strongest ligand CO first and (R) puts I−, the weakest, last. So only (P) and (S) are correct.
    [1]
Answer
(b) Only (P) and (S) are correct.

Concepts to recall

  • Spectrochemical series, weak to strong field: I− < Br− < Cl− < F− < OH− < H2O < NH3 < en < CN− < CO.
  • Strong-field ligands give a large Δo (low spin complexes); weak-field ligands a small Δo (high spin).
  • Remember it as halides (weakest) → O/N donors → C donors (strongest, π-acceptors).

Question 1(B)(iii)[1]
When chlorobenzene is heated with aq. ammonia in the presence of Cu2O at high pressure, it gives:
(a)aniline.
(b)phenyl isocyanide.
(c)diphenyl.
(d)diphenylamine.
  1. Aryl halides are unreactive to nucleophiles, but under drastic conditions (Cu2O, 473 K, high pressure) NH3 replaces Cl: C6H5Cl + 2NH3 → C6H5NH2 + NH4Cl. The product is aniline.
    [1]
Answer
(a) aniline.

Concepts to recall

  • Aryl halides resist nucleophilic substitution because of the partial double-bond character of C–Cl (resonance) and the sp2 carbon; they need high temperature, pressure, a catalyst or ring-activating groups.
  • Phenyl isocyanide comes from aniline + CHCl3 + KOH (carbylamine); diphenyl from the Fittig reaction.

Question 1(B)(iv)[1]
Hydrolysis of sucrose is called:
(a)esterification.
(b)saponification.
(c)hydration.
(d)inversion.
  1. Sucrose is dextrorotatory (+66·5°). On hydrolysis with dilute acid or invertase it gives an equimolar mixture of D-(+)-glucose (+52·5°) and D-(−)-fructose (−92°), whose net rotation is laevo (−20°). The change of sign of rotation is why the hydrolysis is called inversion.
    [1]
Answer
(d) inversion.

Concepts to recall

  • C12H22O11 + H2O → C6H12O6 (glucose) + C6H12O6 (fructose); the mixture is called invert sugar.
  • Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar (the anomeric carbons of both units are joined in the glycosidic link).
  • Saponification is the alkaline hydrolysis of fats; esterification is acid + alcohol.

An organic compound with molecular formula C3H6O does not give silver mirror test with Tollen’s reagent but gives an oxime with hydroxylamine. The compound is:
(a)CH3CH2CHO
(b)CH3–CO–CH3
(c)CH2=CH–CH2OH

Question 1(B)(vi)[1]
Assertion: 0·1 M sucrose solution has higher depression in the freezing point than 0·1 M urea solution.
Reason: Depression in freezing point is not a colligative property.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Sucrose and urea are both non-electrolytes (i = 1), so equal molarities give the same ΔTf = Kfm: the Assertion is false. Depression of freezing point depends only on the number of solute particles, so it is a colligative property: the Reason is false. Both are false.
    [1]
Answer
(d) Both Assertion and Reason are false.

Concepts to recall

  • ΔTf = iKfm: equal concentrations of non-electrolytes (i = 1) give equal depression whatever the solute.
  • The four colligative properties: relative lowering of vapour pressure, elevation of boiling point, depression of freezing point, osmotic pressure.
  • Judge the Assertion and the Reason separately before asking whether one explains the other.

Question 1(B)(vii)[1]
Assertion: Methoxy ethane reacts with HI at 373 K to give ethanol and iodomethane.
Reason: Reaction of unsymmetrical ether with HI follows SN2 mechanism.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. The ether is first protonated: CH3OC2H5 + HI → CH3–O+(H)–C2H5 I−. I− then attacks by SN2 the smaller, less hindered alkyl group (methyl): CH3OC2H5 + HI → CH3I + C2H5OH. So the Assertion is true, the Reason is true, and the SN2 attack on the less hindered carbon is exactly why iodomethane and ethanol (not iodoethane and methanol) form.
    [1]
Answer
(a) Both true and Reason is the correct explanation.

Concepts to recall

  • Cleavage of mixed alkyl ethers with HX: the halide ends up on the smaller / less hindered alkyl group (SN2); if one group is tertiary the cleavage is SN1 and the halide ends on the tertiary carbon.
  • Phenyl alkyl ethers give phenol + alkyl halide (the Caryl–O bond does not break).
  • Excess HI converts the alcohol formed into a second molecule of alkyl iodide.

Question 1(C)(i)[1]
Vishal set up an experiment to find the resistance of aqueous KCl solution for different concentrations at 298 K using a conductivity cell connected to a Wheatstone bridge. He fed the Wheatstone bridge with A.C. power in the audio frequency range 550 to 5000 cycles per second. Once the resistance was calculated from null point, he also calculated the conductivity (κ) and molar conductivity (Λm) and recorded his readings in tabular form, given below.
S.No.Conc. (M)κ (S cm−1)Λm (S cm2 mol−1)
1.1·00111·3 × 10−3111·3
2.0·1012·9 × 10−3129·0
3.0·011·41 × 10−3141·0

(i) Why did the molar conductivity increase though the conductivity decreased with dilution?
  1. Conductivity κ is the conductance of the ions in one unit volume (1 cm3) of solution. On dilution the number of ions per unit volume decreases, so κ decreases (111·3 → 12·9 → 1·41 × 10−3).
    [½]
  2. Molar conductivity is Λm = κ × Vm, where Vm = 1000/M cm3 is the volume containing one mole. Dilution raises Vm by a larger factor than it lowers κ (interionic attraction also falls), so Λm increases.
    [½]
Answer
κ falls because the ions per cm3 fall; Λm = κ × Vm rises because Vm grows more than κ falls
Marking note The key explains both halves: κ falls with fewer ions per unit volume, Λm = κ × Vm rises. Both are required.

Concepts to recall

  • κ = G* / R (S cm−1), with G* the cell constant (l/A).
  • Λm = κ × 1000 / M (S cm2 mol−1) = κ × Vm.
  • Strong electrolytes: Λm = Λ°m − A√c (Debye–Hückel–Onsager), a straight line against √c.
  • A.C. is used so that the electrolysis products do not polarise the electrodes.

Question 2[2]
Consider yourself a research scholar in the forensic science department studying the age of a dead biological sample. During one of the studies, you found that the sample decomposed by following first order kinetics. If 50 % of the sample is decomposed in 120 minutes, how long will it take for 90 % of the sample to decompose?
  1. t½ = 120 min, so k = 0·693/t½ = 0·693/120 = 5·775 × 10-3 min−1
    [1]
  2. a = 100, a − x = 100 − 90 = 10.  t = (2·303/k) log [a/(a − x)] = (2·303/5·775 × 10-3) log 10 = 398·8 min
    [1]
Answer
t ≈ 398·8 min (about 6·6 hours)
Common slip Putting a − x = 90 (the amount decomposed) instead of 10 gives a very small time.
Marking note Answers from 398 to 399 min are accepted (the key prints 398·79 min); using ln 2 and ln 10 exactly gives 398·6 min.

Concepts to recall

  • First order integrated rate law: k = (2·303/t) log [a/(a − x)]; half-life t½ = 0·693/k, independent of the initial amount.
  • Check by half-lives: 90 % decomposed leaves 10 %, which is 2−3·32, so t = 3·32 × 120 ≈ 398 min.
  • Percentage decomposed is not what is left: [A] = a − x is 10, not 90.

Question 3[2]
Write the chemical equations to convert each of the following:
(i) Aniline to bromobenzene
(ii) Ethyl chloride to propanoic acid
  1. (i) Diazotisation: C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2HCl → C6H5N2+Cl− + NaCl + 2H2O (273–278 K). Sandmeyer reaction: C6H5N2+Cl− → C6H5Br + N2 with CuBr/HBr (or Cu/HBr, Gattermann).

  2. (ii) CH3CH2Cl + KCN → CH3CH2CN + KCl (alc. KCN); hydrolysis CH3CH2CN + 2H2O → CH3CH2COOH + NH3 (H+/H2O, heat).
    [1]
Answer
(i) C6H5NH2 → C6H5N2+Cl− → C6H5Br; (ii) C2H5Cl → C2H5CN → C2H5COOH
Marking note Names or formulae of the intermediates need not be given, but the reagent and condition of each step are the marks.

Question 4[2]
Using IUPAC nomenclature, write the formula for each of the following:
(i) hexaamminecobalt (III) sulphate
(ii) tetraaquadichloridochromium (III) nitrate
  1. (i) Cation [Co(NH3)6]3+ (Co(III), six neutral NH3); anion SO42−. Balancing 2 × (+3) = 3 × (−2) gives [Co(NH3)6]2(SO4)3.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Cr(III) with four neutral H2O and two Cl−: charge +3 − 2 = +1, so the cation is [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]+, balanced by one NO3−: [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]NO3.
    [1]
Answer
(i) [Co(NH3)6]2(SO4)3 (CISCE prints [Co(NH3)6]SO4); (ii) [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]NO3
Common slip Forgetting that Co3+ needs three sulphates for two cations, and writing 1:1.
Marking note CISCE’s key prints [Co(NH3)6]SO4 for (i), which is not electrically neutral (+3 against −2). The correct formula is [Co(NH3)6]2(SO4)3; a candidate who writes it should be credited. Part (ii) of the key is correct.

Concepts to recall

  • Writing the formula: central metal first, then ligands in alphabetical order (ignoring prefixes; anionic and neutral ligands mixed), all in square brackets; counter-ion outside.

  • Prefixes: di, tri, tetra, penta, hexa; Roman numeral = oxidation state of the metal. aqua = H2O, ammine = NH3, chlorido = Cl−.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q5
Question 5[2]
When 2 g of benzoic acid (C6H5COOH) is dissolved in 25 g of benzene, it shows a depression in freezing point equal to 1·62 K. Molal depression constant for benzene is 4·7 K kg mol−1. What is the percentage association of acid if it forms dimer in solution?
  1. Mnormal (C6H5COOH) = 122 g mol−1.  Mobs = 1000 Kfw/(ΔTfW) = (1000 × 4·7 × 2)/(1·62 × 25) = 9400/40·5 = 232·1 g mol−1
    [½]
  2. i = Mnormal/Mobs = 122/232·1 = 0·526
    [½]
  3. Dimerisation 2A → A2: if α is the degree of association, i = 1 − α/2.
    [½]
  4. α = 2(1 − i) = 2(1 − 0·526) = 0·949, i.e. 94·9 % association
    [½]
Answer
About 94·9 % of the acid is associated (α ≈ 0·949)
Common slip Treating the acid as dissociating (i = 1 + α): the acid associates here, and i comes out less than 1.
Marking note CISCE’s key has an arithmetic slip. It gives Mobs = 241·98, but 1000 × 4·7 × 2/(1·62 × 25) = 232·1, so i = 0·526 and α = 0·949 (94·9 %), not 0·504 and 99·2 %. The key’s method (i = 1 − α/2) is exactly the one used here. Check: ΔTf for no association would be 4·7 × (2/122)/0·025 = 3·08 K; the observed 1·62 K is 0·526 of it. Method marks are safe; for the final value the working above is correct.

Concepts to recall

  • Association: n molecules form one: i = 1 − α(1 − 1/n); for a dimer i = 1 − α/2, so α = 2(1 − i), and i < 1.
  • Van’t Hoff factor from molar masses: i = Mnormal/Mobs = observed ΔTf / calculated ΔTf.
  • ΔTf = Kf × 1000w/(MW) with W in grams (so the 1000).
  • Carboxylic acids associate in non-polar solvents by two hydrogen bonds (a cyclic dimer).

Question 6[2]
How will you convert the following (write chemical equation):
(i) Phenol to salicylaldehyde
(ii) Formaldehyde to ethanol
  1. (i) Reimer–Tiemann reaction: phenol is heated with chloroform and alkali, and the product acidified: C6H5OH + CHCl3 + 3KOH → o-HOC6H4CHO + 3KCl + 2H2O (salicylaldehyde, 2-hydroxybenzaldehyde).
    [1]
  2. (ii) HCHO + CH3MgBr → CH3CH2OMgBr (dry ether); hydrolysis: CH3CH2OMgBr + H2O → CH3CH2OH + Mg(OH)Br.
    [1]
Answer

(iii) Outer hard shells of two eggs are removed. One of the eggs is placed in saturated solution of sodium chloride and the other egg is placed in pure water. What change will be observed and why?
(iv) What would be the value of van’t Hoff factor for a dilute solution of K2SO4 in water. Assume that K2SO4 is completely ionised.
  1. (i) πV = (w/M)RT, so M = wRT/(πV)
    [½]
  2. V = 300 cm3 = 0·3 L: M = (0·63 × 0·0821 × 300)/(1·29 × 10−3 × 0·3) = 15·517/3·87 × 10−4
    [½]
  3. M = 40095 g mol−1 (4·01 × 104 g mol−1)
    [½]
  4. (ii) ΔTf = 10·0 K = Kf × (w/M)/W(kg), so w = ΔTf × M × W/Kf
    [½]
  5. w = (10·0 × 62·0 × 5·50)/1·86 = 1833 g ≈ 1·833 kg
    [1]
  6. (iii) The egg in pure water swells (endosmosis: water enters through the semi-permeable inner membrane towards the more concentrated egg contents).
    [½]
  7. The egg in saturated NaCl shrinks (exosmosis: water leaves towards the more concentrated solution). Solvent always flows to the higher solute concentration.
    [½]
  8. (iv) K2SO4 → 2K+ + SO42−: three ions per formula unit, so with complete ionisation (α = 1) i = 1 + α(n − 1) = 3.
    [1]
Answer
(i) M ≈ 4·01 × 104 g mol−1; (ii) 1833 g (1·83 kg); (iii) egg in water swells, egg in brine shrinks; (iv) i = 3
Marking note Units: cm3 must be converted to litres (the most common slip) and the mass of solvent to kg. The key gives 40,095·35 g mol−1 and 1833 g (1·833 kg), as here.

Concepts to recall

  • Osmotic pressure (Van’t Hoff): π = CRT = (wB/MB)(RT/V), V in litres with R = 0·0821 L atm K−1 mol−1. Used for macromolecules because π is measurable even at very low concentration.
  • Depression of freezing point: ΔTf = Kfm; m = moles of solute per kg of solvent (W in kg).
  • Osmosis: solvent flows through a semi-permeable membrane from the dilute (hypotonic) to the concentrated (hypertonic) side; endosmosis = into the cell, exosmosis = out.
  • Van’t Hoff factor i = number of particles after dissociation / formula units: NaCl 2, K2SO4 3, glucose 1; i = 1 + α(n − 1).

Question 21[5]
(i) Answer the following:
(a) When the coordination compound CoCl3·6NH3 is mixed with AgNO3 solution, three moles of AgCl are precipitated per mole of the compound. Write the structural formula of the complex compound.
(b) Predict the number of unpaired electrons in [Fe(H2O)6]3+ and [Fe(CN)6]3− based on crystal field theory.
(c) Draw a diagram to show the splitting of d-orbitals in a tetrahedral crystal field.
(d) Write the formulae for the following: (1) Linkage isomer of [CoCl(en)2(NO2)]Cl2 (2) Ionisation isomer of [CoBr(NH3)5]SO4
OR
(ii) (a) For the complex compound [Fe(en)2Cl2]Cl, identify the following: (1) Oxidation state (2) Hybridisation of the central metal atom (3) Magnetic behaviour of the complex (4) Geometry of the complex
(b) Draw the structures of geometrical isomers of complex [Pt(en)2Cl2]2+.
(c) On the basis of crystal field theory, write the electronic configuration for d4 ion in octahedral crystal field, if Δo > P.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) Three moles of AgCl per mole means all three Cl− are free (ionisable): [Co(NH3)6]Cl3 → [Co(NH3)6]3+ + 3Cl−; 3AgNO3 + 3Cl− → 3AgCl + 3NO3−. Structural formula: [Co(NH3)6]Cl3 (all six NH3 inside the coordination sphere).
    [1]
  2. (b) Fe3+ is 3d5. H2O is a weak-field ligand (Δo < P): t2g3eg2, 5 unpaired electrons. CN− is strong-field (Δo > P): t2g5eg0, 1 unpaired electron.
    [1]
  3. (c) In a tetrahedral field the five d orbitals split into a lower set e (dz², dx²−y²), 3/5 Δt below the barycentre, and a higher set t2 (dxy, dyz, dzx), 2/5 Δt above it (see the figure). Δt = (4/9)Δo.
    [1]
  4. (d)(1) Linkage isomer: NO2 bonded through N (nitro) becomes bonded through O (nitrito): [CoCl(en)2(ONO)]Cl2.
    [1]
  5. (d)(2) Ionisation isomer: the Br− and SO42− exchange places between coordination sphere and counter-ion: [Co(NH3)5SO4]Br.
    [1]
Answer
(a) [Co(NH3)6]Cl3; (b) 5 and 1; (c) e (lower, 2 orbitals), t2 (higher, 3 orbitals); (d) [CoCl(en)2(ONO)]Cl2; [Co(NH3)5SO4]Br
Marking note The key labels the tetrahedral sets t2g and eg on its diagram; the correct labels are t2 and e (no g, because a tetrahedron has no centre of symmetry). The orbital arrangement in the key (two orbitals below, three above) is right. Either label is credited.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a)(1) The outer Cl− carries the charge: [Fe(en)2Cl2]+, with en neutral and two Cl− inside: oxidation state of Fe = +1 + 2 = +3.
    [½]
  2. (2) Fe3+ is 3d5; en is treated as a strong-field ligand, so the electrons pair (one unpaired in t2g5), two 3d orbitals are vacated and the hybridisation is d2sp3 (inner orbital).  (3) One unpaired electron: paramagnetic (μ = √3 = 1·73 BM).  (4) Six donor atoms (2 en × 2 + 2 Cl): octahedral.
    [1½]
  3. (b) Two geometrical isomers: trans (the two Cl− opposite, at 180°) and cis (the two Cl− adjacent, at 90°); the cis form also exists as a pair of optical isomers. Draw both octahedral structures, each with its two bidentate en rings (1 mark for each correct isomer).
    [2]
  4. (c) With Δo > P the fourth electron pairs in t2g rather than going to eg: configuration t2g4 eg0 (low spin, 2 unpaired electrons).
    [1]
Answer
(a) +3; d2sp3; paramagnetic; octahedral. (b) cis and trans. (c) t2g4eg0
Marking note Hybridisation and magnetism depend on treating en as strong field. The marking scheme gives d2sp3 and paramagnetic. Note that the two Cl− ligands are weak-field, so the spin state of this particular complex is not fixed by theory alone; in either spin state it has unpaired electrons, so ‘paramagnetic’ is safe. Examiners follow the key.

Concepts to recall

  • Werner: ionisable (primary) valency is precipitated by AgNO3; ligands inside [ ] are not. AgCl moles = number of free Cl−.
  • Crystal field: octahedral Δo > P (strong field) gives low spin (electrons pair in t2g); Δo < P gives high spin. Unpaired electron count gives μ = √(n(n+2)) BM.
  • Tetrahedral: Δt = (4/9)Δo, always smaller than P, so tetrahedral complexes are always high spin; the order of sets is reversed (e below t2).
  • Isomerism: linkage (ambidentate ligand NO2−/ONO−, SCN−/NCS−); ionisation (counter-ion swaps with a ligand); hydrate; coordination; geometrical; optical.
