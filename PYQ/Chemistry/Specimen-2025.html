SECTION A – 14 MARKSCISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i)[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [four, three, remains same, propane, ns1–2, increases, six, propan-2-ol, (n−1)d1–10, two, decreases, Clemmensen’s, Wolff–Kishner].
The molar conductance of a solution ______ on dilution but the specific conductance of a solution ______ on dilution.
  1. Molar conductance Λm = 1000κ/M: on dilution the volume containing one mole increases (and ionisation increases for weak electrolytes), so it increases (½). Specific conductance κ is the conductance of 1 cm3, and dilution lowers the number of ions in it, so it decreases (½).
    [1]
Answer
increases; decreases
Marking note Both words are needed for the mark.

Concepts to recall

  • κ (S cm−1) falls on dilution; Λm = 1000κ/M (S cm2 mol−1) rises.
  • Strong electrolytes: Λm rises slowly towards Λ°m (less interionic attraction); weak electrolytes: rises steeply (more ionisation).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSCISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(ii)
Question 1(A)(ii)[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [four, three, remains same, propane, ns1–2, increases, six, propan-2-ol, (n−1)d1–10, two, decreases, Clemmensen’s, Wolff–Kishner].
In the complex ion [CoCl(en)2ONO]+, the coordination number and the oxidation number of the central metal ion are ______ and ______.
  1. en is bidentate: 2 × 2 = 4, plus Cl− (1) and ONO− (1): coordination number = six (½). Oxidation number x + (−1) + 2(0) + (−1) = +1, so x = +3 (½).
    [1]
Answer
six; three

Concepts to recall

  • Coordination number = number of donor atoms bonded to the metal (bidentate ligand counts 2). Oxidation number = charge on complex − sum of ligand charges.
  • en (ethane-1,2-diamine) is neutral; Cl− and ONO− (nitrito-O) are each −1.

Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [four, three, remains same, propane, ns1–2, increases, six, propan-2-ol, (n−1)d1–10, two, decreases, Clemmensen’s, Wolff–Kishner].
Propanone on reaction with zinc amalgam in the presence of concentrated HCl gives ______ and the reaction is called ______ reduction.
  1. CH3COCH3 + 4[H] → CH3CH2CH3 + H2O with Zn–Hg/conc. HCl: the product is propane (½) and the method is Clemmensen’s reduction (½).

propane; Clemmensen’s

Question 1(A)(iv)[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [four, three, remains same, propane, ns1–2, increases, six, propan-2-ol, (n−1)d1–10, two, decreases, Clemmensen’s, Wolff–Kishner].
The variation and magnitude of oxidation state in coordination complexes is due to the participation of inner ______ electrons in addition to outer ______ electrons.
  1. The (n−1)d electrons lie close in energy to the ns electrons, so both take part in bonding: inner (n−1)d1–10 (½) electrons in addition to outer ns1–2 (½) electrons.
    [1]
Answer
(n−1)d1–10; ns1–2

  • General configuration of d-block elements: (n−1)d1–10ns1–2 (Pd is 4d105s0, an exception).
  • Variable oxidation states differ by units of one (Fe2+, Fe3+), unlike p-block elements which differ by two.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSCISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i)[1]
The correct order of the increasing basic nature of Ammonia, Methylamine and Aniline is:
(a)Methylamine < Aniline < Ammonia
(b)Aniline < Methylamine < Ammonia
(c)Ammonia < Aniline < Methylamine
(d)Aniline < Ammonia < Methylamine
  1. The +I effect of CH3 pushes electron density onto N, so methylamine holds H+ more readily than NH3. In aniline the N lone pair is delocalised into the benzene ring (resonance), so it is the weakest base. Increasing order: aniline (pKb 9·4) < ammonia (4·75) < methylamine (3·38).
    [1]
Answer
(d) Aniline < Ammonia < Methylamine

Concepts to recall

  • Basic strength = availability of the lone pair on N: alkyl groups (+I) increase it; an aryl group (resonance, −R) decreases it.
  • Aqueous order of aliphatic amines: (C2H5)2NH > (C2H5)3N > C2H5NH2 > NH3 (inductive plus solvation).
  • A larger pKb means a weaker base.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSCISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii)[1]
Colligative properties of a solution are:
(P) Independent of the nature of solute
(Q) Proportional to molecular mass of solute
(R) Proportional to concentration of solute
(S) Independent of the amount of solvent
(a)Only P and Q are correct
(b)Only P and R are correct
(c)Only P and S are correct
(d)Only Q and S are correct
  1. Colligative properties depend only on the number of solute particles per amount of solvent. (P) true: they do not depend on what the solute is. (Q) false: for a given mass, a larger molar mass gives fewer particles and hence a smaller effect, so the property is inversely, not directly, related to molar mass. (R) true: ΔTf = Kfm, π = CRT. (S) false: molality and molarity both involve the amount of solvent or solution.
    [1]
Answer
(b) Only P and R are correct

Concepts to recall

  • The four colligative properties: relative lowering of vapour pressure, elevation of boiling point, depression of freezing point, osmotic pressure.
  • For equimolar solutions the effect is proportional to the van’t Hoff factor i.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSCISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii)[1]
According to Markownikoff’s rule, “The negative part of the halogen acid attaches itself with that C atom of an unsymmetrical alkene which has least number of H-atom.” Anti-Markownikoff’s addition of HBr is NOT observed in:
(a)Propene
(b)But-2-ene
(c)But-1-ene
(d)Pent-2-ene
  1. Markownikoff and anti-Markownikoff addition only differ for an unsymmetrical alkene. But-2-ene, CH3CH=CHCH3, is symmetrical: both ends of the double bond are equivalent, so HBr gives only 2-bromobutane whichever way it adds, and the peroxide (Kharasch) effect makes no difference. Propene, but-1-ene and pent-2-ene are unsymmetrical, so with peroxide they give 1-bromopropane, 1-bromobutane and 2-bromopentane/3-bromopentane in different ratios.
    [1]
Answer
(b) But-2-ene

Concepts to recall

  • Peroxide effect (Kharasch): HBr adds by a free-radical chain mechanism, giving the anti-Markownikoff product; HCl and HI do not show it.
  • A symmetrical alkene (but-2-ene, ethene) gives a single product either way.

Question 1(B)(iv)[1]
Most of the naturally occurring α-amino acids are optically active and have L-configuration. Which of the following amino acids is NOT asymmetric?
(a)Valine
(b)Alanine
(c)Glycine
(d)Leucine
  1. Optical activity needs a chiral α-carbon with four different groups. Glycine is H2N–CH2–COOH: its α-carbon carries two H, so it is achiral. Valine, alanine and leucine have H, NH2, COOH and a different side chain.
    [1]
Answer
(c) Glycine

Concepts to recall

  • Glycine is the only common α-amino acid that is optically inactive (R = H).
  • Natural amino acids are L (NH2 on the left in the Fischer projection with COOH at the top); the L/D label is relative to glyceraldehyde.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSCISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(v)
Question 1(B)(v)[1]
Which one of the following complex ions has geometrical isomers?
(a)[Ni(NH3)5Br]+
(b)[Cr(NH3)4en]3+
(c)[Co(NH3)2(en)2]3+
(d)[Co(NH3)6]3+
  1. Octahedral Ma5b, Ma6 and Ma4(AA) (one bidentate ligand spans two adjacent sites, the rest are alike) have a single arrangement. Ma2(AA)2, i.e. [Co(NH3)2(en)2]3+, can have the two NH3 either at 90° (cis) or at 180° (trans): geometrical isomers.
    [1]
Answer
(c) [Co(NH3)2(en)2]3+

Concepts to recall

  • Octahedral types showing cis–trans: Ma4b2, Ma2(AA)2; fac–mer: Ma3b3. Ma5b and Ma6 never do.
  • cis-[Co(en)2X2] is also optically active (it has non-superimposable mirror images); the trans form is not.
  • Tetrahedral complexes show no geometrical isomerism; square planar Ma2b2 does.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSCISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vi)
Question 1(B)(vi)[1]
Assertion: Tertiary butyl bromide undergoes Wurtz reaction to give 2,2,3,3-tetramethylbutane.
Reason: In Wurtz reaction, when same alkyl halides react with sodium in dry ether to give hydrocarbon; the hydrocarbon formed contains double the number of carbon atoms present in the alkyl halide.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true but Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Reason: 2RX + 2Na → R–R + 2NaX in dry ether, so the product has twice the carbon atoms of RX: true. Assertion: 2(CH3)3CBr + 2Na → (CH3)3C–C(CH3)3 + 2NaBr, i.e. 2,2,3,3-tetramethylbutane, is the textbook coupling of the same-halide Wurtz reaction, and the reason is exactly what explains it. CISCE’s key is (a).
    [1]
Answer
(a)
Marking note CISCE’s key is (a). Chemically, tertiary halides give mostly elimination (isobutene) with sodium and a poor yield of the coupled product, so the assertion is idealised; the options offer no choice that makes the reason true and the assertion false, and (a) is the expected answer.

Concepts to recall

  • Wurtz: 2R–X + 2Na (dry ether) → R–R + 2NaX; the two alkyl groups join, so carbon number doubles.
  • Limitations: with different halides a mixture of three alkanes is formed; methane cannot be made.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSCISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vii)
Question 1(B)(vii)[1]
Assertion: There is a continuous increase in size among Lanthanoids with an increase in atomic number.
Reason: Lanthanoids do not show Lanthanoid contraction.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for assertion.
(c)Assertion is true but Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Across the lanthanoids the 4f electrons shield poorly, so the effective nuclear charge rises and the ionic radius decreases steadily from La3+ (106 pm) to Lu3+ (85 pm): the assertion (continuous increase) is false. This steady decrease is the lanthanoid contraction, so the reason (that they do not show it) is false too.
    [1]
Answer
(d) Both Assertion and Reason are false.

Concepts to recall

  • Lanthanoid contraction: gradual decrease in size of Ln3+ with increasing atomic number because 4f electrons shield the nuclear charge imperfectly.
  • Consequences: Zr and Hf (and Nb, Ta) have almost identical radii and are hard to separate; the third-row d-block elements are denser and have higher ionisation energies than expected.

Question 1(C)(i)[1]
Passage. C-14 is used to determine the age of fossils. In the upper atmosphere, neutrons in cosmic rays produce 147N + 10n → 146C + 11H. After death the C-14 level falls by 146C → 147N + 0−1e + γ rays. The half-life of C-14 is 5770 years; the ratio C-14 : C-12 in a living being is 1 : 1012.
Write the relation between the decay constant and half-life period.
  1. For a first order (radioactive) decay N = N0e−kt; at t = t½, N = N0/2, so ln 2 = k t½ and k = 0·693/t½ (statement and symbols, 1).
    [1]
Answer
k = 0·693 / t½

Concepts to recall

  • First order: ln([R]0/[R]) = kt, i.e. k = (2·303/t) log([R]0/[R]).
  • Half-life of a first order reaction is independent of the initial amount.

Question 1(C)(ii)[1]
Passage. C-14 is used to determine the age of fossils. In the upper atmosphere, neutrons in cosmic rays produce 147N + 10n → 146C + 11H. After death the C-14 level falls by 146C → 147N + 0−1e + γ rays. The half-life of C-14 is 5770 years; the ratio C-14 : C-12 in a living being is 1 : 1012.
In a piece of dead wood, the activity or concentration of C-14 is found to be one third of its initial activity. Calculate the age of the old wood.
  1. k = 0·693/5770 = 1·201 × 10-4 y−1.  t = (2·303/k) log([R]0/[R]) = (2·303/1·201 × 10-4) log 3 = (2·303 × 0·4771)/1·201 × 10-4 = 9149 years.
    [1]
Answer
Age ≈ 9149 years (about 9·1 × 103 y)
Marking note CISCE’s key prints 9146·5 years; rounding of 0·693, 2·303 and log 3 gives values from 9145 to 9150, all of which are acceptable.

Concepts to recall

  • t = (2·303/k) log([R]0/[R]) with k = 0·693/t½; log 3 = 0·4771.
  • Radiocarbon dating assumes the C-14 : C-12 ratio of living matter (1 : 1012) was constant and that the decay is first order.

Question 2[2]
The solution of two electrolytes A and B are diluted. Λm of B increases 1·5 times while that of A increases 25 times. Which of the two is a strong electrolyte? Give a reason.
  1. B is the strong electrolyte. (1)
    [1]
  2. A strong electrolyte is completely ionised at every concentration, so on dilution Λm rises only a little (less interionic attraction), towards Λ°m. A weak electrolyte ionises more and more on dilution, so its Λm rises steeply (A, 25 times). (1)
    [1]
Answer
B is the strong electrolyte; its Λm rises only slightly on dilution.

Concepts to recall

  • Strong: Λm = Λ°m − A√C (Debye–Hückel–Onsager); almost linear against √C, small slope.
  • Weak: Λm rises sharply at low concentration because the degree of dissociation α → 1; Λ°m can only be found by Kohlrausch’s law.

Question 3[2]
An organic compound [A] which has characteristic odour, reacts with conc. NaOH to give two compounds [B] and [C]. Compound [B] has molecular formula C7H8O which upon oxidation gives back compound [A]. Compound [C] is the sodium salt of the acid and upon treatment with sodalime yields an aromatic hydrocarbon [D]. Identify the compounds [A], [B], [C] and [D].
  1. [A] = benzaldehyde, C6H5CHO (almond odour; no α-H, so it undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction with conc. NaOH): 2C6H5CHO + NaOH → C6H5CH2OH + C6H5COONa. [B] = benzyl alcohol, C6H5CH2OH (C7H8O; oxidation gives back [A]). [C] = sodium benzoate. (½ each)
    [2]
  2. [D]: C6H5COONa + NaOH (CaO) → C6H6 + Na2CO3: [D] = benzene.
Answer
[A] C6H5CHO; [B] C6H5CH2OH; [C] C6H5COONa; [D] C6H6

Concepts to recall

  • Cannizzaro: aldehydes with no α-hydrogen (HCHO, C6H5CHO) disproportionate in conc. NaOH into an alcohol and the sodium salt of the acid.
  • Decarboxylation: RCOONa + NaOH/CaO (soda lime) → RH + Na2CO3.
  • Clue-chasing: formula C7H8O with aromatic ring + oxidation back to the aldehyde = benzyl alcohol.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSCISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q4
Question 4[2]
Devise a scheme for the synthesis of n-butane using CH3I as the only source of carbon. Is it possible to obtain propane in pure state by applying this scheme? Give a reason for your answer.
  1. Scheme: (1) 2CH3I + 2Na (dry ether) → CH3–CH3 + 2NaI (Wurtz). (2) CH3CH3 + Cl2 (hν) → CH3CH2Cl + HCl; (3) 2CH3CH2Cl + 2Na (dry ether) → CH3CH2CH2CH3 + 2NaCl (Wurtz again). (1)
    [1]
  2. Propane would need CH3I and C2H5X to couple. Wurtz coupling of two different halides gives a mixture of ethane, propane and butane (CH3–CH3, CH3–C2H5, C2H5–C2H5), which have close boiling points, so pure propane cannot be obtained. (1)
    [1]
Answer
Wurtz twice (with free-radical chlorination between); No, propane cannot be got pure (a mixture is formed).

Concepts to recall

  • Wurtz: 2RX + 2Na → R–R + 2NaX in dry ether; the carbon number doubles.
  • Wurtz with two different alkyl halides gives all three possible alkanes, so it is useful only for symmetrical alkanes.
  • Chain growth from one carbon: Wurtz (C1→C2), halogenation, Wurtz again (C2→C4).

Question 5[2]
It is generally observed that the rate of a chemical reaction becomes double with every 10°C rise in temperature. If the generalisation holds true for a reaction in the temperature range of 298 K to 308 K, what would be the value of activation energy (Ea) for the reaction?
  1. k2/k1 = 2, T1 = 298 K, T2 = 308 K. log(k2/k1) = (Ea/2·303R)[(T2 − T1)/(T1T2)] (1)
    [1]
  2. Ea = 2·303 × 8·314 × log 2 × (298 × 308)/10 = 2·303 × 8·314 × 0·301 × 9178·4 = 52898 J mol−1 (≈ 52·9 kJ mol−1) (1)
    [1]
Answer
Ea ≈ 52898 J mol−1 (52·9 kJ mol−1)
Common slip Using R = 1·987 cal gives Ea in calories (12·6 kcal); the unit must match the R chosen.
Marking note CISCE’s key: Ea = 52897·78 J.

Concepts to recall

  • Arrhenius two-temperature form: log(k2/k1) = (Ea/2·303R)(1/T1 − 1/T2).
  • Temperature coefficient = k(T+10)/kT ≈ 2 to 3 for most reactions.
  • Use R = 8·314 J K−1 mol−1 (given), so Ea comes out in joules.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSCISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q6
Question 6[2]
An organic compound [X] of molecular formula C3H5N, in one reaction produced a primary amine with LiAlH4. In another case, the organic compound [Y] of same molecular formula C3H5N produced a secondary amine with LiAlH4. Identify both the compounds [X] and [Y]. Why do these compounds form two different products?
  1. [X] = C2H5–C≡N (propanenitrile): C2H5CN + 4[H] → C2H5CH2NH2 (propan-1-amine, primary). [Y] = C2H5–N≡C (ethyl isocyanide): C2H5NC + 4[H] → C2H5NHCH3 (N-methylethanamine, secondary). (½ for each structure with its product)
    [1]
  2. Reason: in a nitrile the carbon of the C≡N is bonded to R, so adding hydrogen gives R–CH2–NH2 (N has two H); in an isocyanide the nitrogen is bonded to R and the terminal carbon becomes CH3, so N carries R, CH3 and H: R–NH–CH3. (1)
    [1]
Answer
[X] C2H5CN (→ C3H7NH2); [Y] C2H5NC (→ C2H5NHCH3); the groups attached to the nitrogen differ.
Marking note CISCE’s key gives only the structures of [X] and [Y] and says they give primary and secondary amines; the explanation above is the full answer.

Concepts to recall

  • Nitriles: RCN + 4[H] (LiAlH4 or Na/C2H5OH) → RCH2NH2 (primary amine, one carbon more than R).
  • Isocyanides (carbylamines): R–N≡C reduce to R–NH–CH3 (secondary amine).
  • Nitrile and isocyanide are functional (chain/position) isomers; the carbylamine test (CHCl3 + KOH) gives isocyanides from 1° amines.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSCISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q7
Question 7[2]
During chemistry class, a teacher wrote [Ni(CN)4]2− as a coordination complex ion on the board. The students were asked to find out the magnetic behaviour and shape of the complex. Pari, a student, wrote the answer paramagnetic and tetrahedral whereas another student Suhail wrote diamagnetic and square planar. Evaluate Pari’s and Suhail’s responses.
  1. Ni (Z = 28) is 3d84s2; Ni2+ is 3d8 (two unpaired electrons in a free ion). CN− is a strong-field ligand: it forces the two unpaired 3d electrons to pair, vacating one 3d orbital. (1)
    [1]
  2. One 3d, the 4s and two 4p orbitals hybridise to dsp2, giving four hybrid orbitals directed at the corners of a square, so the complex is square planar, with all electrons paired (n = 0): diamagnetic. Suhail is right; Pari is wrong (tetrahedral sp3 with two unpaired electrons would be paramagnetic, as in [NiCl4]2− with a weak-field ligand). (1)
    [1]
Answer
Suhail is correct: [Ni(CN)4]2− is diamagnetic and square planar (dsp2).

Concepts to recall

  • Valence bond theory: strong-field ligand → pairing → inner-orbital/dsp2; weak-field ligand → sp3, outer orbital.
  • μ = √(n(n+2)) BM; n = 0 means diamagnetic.
  • Four-coordinate d8: strong-field (CN−) gives square planar; weak-field (Cl−) gives tetrahedral.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSCISCE Specimen 2025 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q8
Question 8[2]
An organic compound ‘X’ with molecular formula C4H10O is found to be soluble in conc. H2SO4 and does not react with sodium metal or KMnO4. Compound ‘X’ when heated with excess of HI gives a single alkyl halide. Deduce the structure of the compound ‘X’. Explain all the reactions involved.
  1. C4H10O is saturated (no unsaturation). It does not react with Na, so there is no –OH (not an alcohol); it is not oxidised by KMnO4; it dissolves in conc. H2SO4 by forming an oxonium salt. These show an ether. (1)
    [1]
  2. Excess HI gives only one alkyl halide, so the two alkyl groups are identical: X = diethyl ether, C2H5OC2H5. C2H5OC2H5 + 2HI → 2C2H5I + H2O. (Step: ether + HI → C2H5I + C2H5OH, then the alcohol + HI → C2H5I + H2O.) Also: C2H5OC2H5 + H2SO4 → [(C2H5)2OH]+HSO4−. (1)
    [1]

(i) Osmosis and osmotic pressure play a very significant role in biological processes. The osmotic pressure of human blood is 8·21 atm at 27 °C. A 0·91 % (mass/volume) solution of NaCl, known as saline water, is isotonic with fluids inside the human red blood cells; medicines are mixed with saline water before being injected into the veins.

Is this statement true or false? Give a reason. (e) Name the disease caused by taking a lot of salt or salty food, which results in water retention in tissue cells causing puffiness or swelling.

(b) Ethylene glycol is used as an antifreeze agent. Calculate the amount of ethylene glycol to be added to four kilogram of water to prevent it from freezing at −6 °C. (Kf of water = 1·86 K kg mol−1) Assume that ethylene glycol (CH2OH·CH2OH) does not dissociate or associate in aqueous solution.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) Isotonic solutions are solutions having the same osmotic pressure at the same temperature (so there is no net flow of solvent across a semi-permeable membrane between them). (1)
    [1]
  2. (b) π = CRT: C = π/RT = 8·21/(0·0821 × 300) = 0·3333 mol L−1. Mass of glucose (M = 180) per litre = 0·3333 × 180 = 60 g, i.e. 6 g per 100 mL. (1)
    [1]

    [1]

    [1]

    [1]

(a) solutions of equal osmotic pressure; (b) 6 g per 100 mL; (c) 8·48 atm; (d) False (it is hypotonic); (e) oedema

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) m = (8·0/60)/0·250 = 0·5333 mol kg−1; ΔTb = Kbm = 0·52 × 0·5333 = 0·277 K; boiling point = 373 + 0·277 = 373·277 K. (2)

Concepts to recall

  • π = CRT = (n/V)RT; isotonic = same π; hypertonic = higher π (solute), hypotonic = lower π. Water always flows from the hypotonic to the hypertonic side.
  • Use R = 0·0821 L atm K−1 mol−1 (printed on the paper) when π is in atm, and T in kelvin (27 °C = 300 K, 37 °C = 310 K).
  • Biological use: intravenous fluids are 0·91 % saline or 5 % glucose; red cells burst in pure water (haemolysis) and shrink in strong salt solution.
  • ΔTb = Kbm, ΔTf = Kfm for a non-electrolyte (i = 1); molality m = moles of solute / kg of solvent.
  • Molar mass of ethylene glycol C2H6O2 = 24 + 6 + 32 = 62; urea CO(NH2)2 = 60.
  • Mass-based units (molality, mass %, mole fraction) do not change with temperature; volume-based units (molarity, normality) do.
