SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i)[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [lead poisoning, zero, phosgene, dependent, cancer, independent, diethyl ether, first, ethyl carbonate, ethene].
For a particular reaction, the value of rate constant is 0·05 sec−1. The reaction is of ______ order and will be ______ of the initial concentration.
  1. The unit of k is sec−1, which is the unit of a first order rate constant (unit = (mol L−1)1−n sec−1, n = 1). For first order, k = (2·303/t) log([R]0/[R]) contains only a ratio, so the half-life 0·693/k is independent of the initial concentration.
    [1]
Answer
first; independent
Marking note Both blanks are needed for the mark; ‘first’ and ‘independent’ belong together.

Concepts to recall

  • Units of k: zero order mol L−1 s−1; first order s−1; second order L mol−1 s−1.
  • First order: t½ = 0·693/k, independent of [R]0; zero order: t½ = [R]0/2k (depends on it).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(ii)
Question 1(A)(ii)[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [lead poisoning, zero, phosgene, dependent, cancer, independent, diethyl ether, first, ethyl carbonate, ethene].
EDTA is used in the treatment of ______ while Cisplatin is used in the treatment of ______.
  1. EDTA is a hexadentate chelating ligand: it forms stable soluble chelates with Pb2+ and is used in lead poisoning. Cisplatin, cis-[Pt(NH3)2Cl2], is an anticancer drug used in the treatment of cancer.
    [1]
Answer
lead poisoning; cancer

Concepts to recall

  • Chelate therapy: EDTA removes Pb2+ and Ca2+; D-penicillamine removes Cu2+ (Wilson’s disease).
  • Cisplatin: cis-diamminedichloridoplatinum(II), square planar; the trans isomer is inactive.
  • Other applications of coordination compounds: Ni and Co by solvent extraction, silver/gold by cyanide process, hardness of water by EDTA titration.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(iii)
Question 1(A)(iii)[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [lead poisoning, zero, phosgene, dependent, cancer, independent, diethyl ether, first, ethyl carbonate, ethene].
The addition of small quantity of ethanol to chloroform prevents the formation of ______ and converts it into the harmless compound ______.
  1. On exposure to air and light chloroform is oxidised to poisonous phosgene (COCl2): 2CHCl3 + O2 → 2COCl2 + 2HCl. Ethanol (about 1 %) added to the bottle converts any phosgene formed into harmless ethyl carbonate: COCl2 + 2C2H5OH → (C2H5O)2CO + 2HCl.
    [1]
Answer
phosgene; ethyl carbonate (diethyl carbonate)
Marking note The word list gives ‘ethyl carbonate’; the compound is diethyl carbonate, (C2H5O)2CO. Either name earns the mark.

Concepts to recall

  • Chloroform is stored in dark brown, completely filled bottles to keep out light and air.
  • Phosgene (carbonyl chloride) is a poisonous gas; test for its presence: AgNO3 gives a white ppt with the HCl formed.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(A)(iv)
Question 1(A)(iv)[1]
Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets [lead poisoning, zero, phosgene, dependent, cancer, independent, diethyl ether, first, ethyl carbonate, ethene].
The dehydration of ethyl alcohol with conc. H2SO4 at 140°C mainly yields ______ while at 170°C the main product formed is ______.
  1. At 413 K (140 °C), with excess alcohol, intermolecular dehydration gives diethyl ether: 2C2H5OH → C2H5OC2H5 + H2O. At 443 K (170 °C), with excess acid, intramolecular dehydration gives ethene: C2H5OH → CH2=CH2 + H2O.
    [1]
Answer
diethyl ether; ethene

Concepts to recall

  • Low temperature + excess alcohol: ether (substitution, SN2 by a second ROH on protonated ROH). High temperature + excess acid: alkene (elimination, E2/E1).
  • Order of ease of dehydration: 3° > 2° > 1° alcohol (stability of the carbocation).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i)[1]
Which one of the following statements is correct regarding the dry cell?
(P) Zinc container acts as an anode in dry cell.
(Q) Zinc container touches the paste of MnO2 and carbon.
(R) Dry cell can be charged easily.
(S) Graphite rod acts as a cathode in dry cell.
(a)Only (P) and (R)
(b)Only (Q) and (R)
(c)Only (P) and (S)
(d)Only (Q) and (S)
  1. (P) true: Zn → Zn2+ + 2e− occurs at the zinc container, the anode. (Q) false: the zinc touches the NH4Cl + ZnCl2 paste; the MnO2 + carbon paste surrounds the graphite rod. (R) false: a dry cell is a primary cell and cannot be recharged easily. (S) true: the graphite rod (cathode) is where MnO2 is reduced. So only (P) and (S) are correct.
    [1]
Answer
(c) Only (P) and (S)

Concepts to recall

  • Anode (−): Zn → Zn2+ + 2e−. Cathode (+): MnO2 + NH4+ + e− → MnO(OH) + NH3.
  • Primary cells (dry cell, mercury cell) are not rechargeable; secondary cells (lead storage, Ni–Cd) are.
  • Dry cell emf is about 1·5 V and falls steadily as it is used.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii)[1]
The metal complex ion that is paramagnetic is: (Atomic number of Fe = 26, Cu = 29, Co = 27 and Ni = 28)
(a)[Fe(CN)4]2−
(b)[Co(NH3)6]3+
(c)[Ni(CN)4]2−
(d)[Cu(NH3)4]2+
  1. Paramagnetism needs unpaired electrons. [Co(NH3)6]3+: Co3+ 3d6, strong-field NH3 (d2sp3), all paired: diamagnetic. [Ni(CN)4]2−: Ni2+ 3d8, CN− strong field, dsp2 square planar, all paired: diamagnetic. [Cu(NH3)4]2+: Cu2+ 3d9, one unpaired electron (n = 1, μ = √3 = 1·73 BM): paramagnetic. [Fe(CN)4]2− is Fe2+ with strong-field CN−; the formula as printed is a non-standard four-coordinate ion, so it cannot be relied on, and the only ion whose single unpaired electron is certain is the d9 copper complex.
    [1]
Answer
(d) [Cu(NH3)4]2+
Marking note The formula [Fe(CN)4]2− as printed is unusual (the common ion is [Fe(CN)6]4−, which is diamagnetic); in either reading the only unambiguous paramagnetic ion is the d9 copper complex, so (d) stands.

Concepts to recall

  • μ = √(n(n+2)) BM, where n is the number of unpaired electrons.
  • Strong-field ligands (CN−, CO, en, NH3 for Co3+) pair electrons; weak-field (F−, H2O) do not.
  • Any d9 ion (Cu2+) always has exactly one unpaired electron, whatever the ligand.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii)[1]
When KMnO4 is heated with acidified oxalic acid, gas bubbles are evolved. These gas bubbles are evolved due to the formation of:
(a)SO2
(b)CO2
(c)SO3
(d)O2
  1. Acidified KMnO4 oxidises oxalic acid: 2MnO4− + 5C2O42− + 16H+ → 2Mn2+ + 10CO2 + 8H2O. The bubbles are carbon dioxide.
    [1]
Answer
(b) CO2

Concepts to recall

  • In acid, MnO4− (Mn +7) → Mn2+ (Mn +2): five electrons gained. Oxalate → 2CO2: two electrons lost.
  • Hence 2 MnO4− : 5 C2O42− (ratio from the electron balance).
  • Self-indicator: the purple colour disappears until the first excess drop gives a permanent pink.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv)[1]
The reaction of ethanamide with alcoholic sodium hydroxide and bromine gives:
(a)ethylamine.
(b)methylamine.
(c)propylamine.
(d)aniline.
  1. This is Hofmann bromamide degradation: CH3CONH2 + Br2 + 4NaOH → CH3NH2 + Na2CO3 + 2NaBr + 2H2O. The amide loses its carbonyl carbon (as carbonate), so the amine has one carbon less: methylamine.
    [1]
Answer
(b) methylamine.

Concepts to recall

  • Hofmann bromamide: RCONH2 → RNH2 (primary amine with one carbon fewer). Intermediate: isocyanate RN=C=O.
  • To step up a carbon, go through the nitrile (KCN) and reduce; to step down, use Hofmann.
  • The migrating R keeps its configuration; only primary amides react.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(v)
Question 1(B)(v)[1]
An equimolar solution of non-volatile solutes A and B, shows a depression in freezing point in the ratio of 2:1. If A remains in its normal state in the solution, the state of B in the solution will be:
(a)normal.
(b)hydrolysed.
(c)associated.
(d)dissociated.
  1. ΔTf = i Kfm; for equimolar solutions ΔTf ∝ i. A is normal, so iA = 1. ΔTf(A) : ΔTf(B) = 2 : 1 gives iB = 1/2 < 1. A van’t Hoff factor below 1 means fewer particles than formula units: B is associated (for example as a dimer).
    [1]
Answer
(c) associated.

Concepts to recall

  • i = observed colligative property / normal value = normal molar mass / observed molar mass.
  • i > 1: dissociation (KCl, i → 2); i = 1: normal; i < 1: association (ethanoic acid in benzene, i → 0·5).
  • Association increases the observed molar mass.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vi)
Question 1(B)(vi)[1]
Assertion: Specific conductivity of all electrolytes decreases on dilution.
Reason: On dilution, the number of ions per unit volume decreases.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true but Reason is false.
(d)Assertion is false but Reason is true.
  1. Specific conductivity κ is the conductance of 1 cm3 of the solution, so it depends on the number of ions in that unit volume. Dilution lowers the number of ions per cm3 (for strong and weak electrolytes alike), so κ falls: both statements are true and the reason explains the assertion. (Molar conductivity, by contrast, rises on dilution.)
    [1]
Answer
(a)

Concepts to recall

  • κ (S cm−1) falls on dilution; Λm = 1000κ/M rises on dilution.
  • Λm rises because the total volume containing one mole increases and (for weak electrolytes) ionisation increases; for strong electrolytes interionic attraction falls.
  • Always test the Assertion and the Reason on their own first, then ask whether the Reason explains the Assertion.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(B)(vii)
Question 1(B)(vii)[1]
Assertion: Ammonolysis of alkyl halides involves the reaction between alkyl halides and alcoholic ammonia.
Reason: Ammonolysis of alkyl halides produces secondary amines only.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true but Reason is false.
(d)Assertion is false but Reason is true.
  1. Assertion true: ammonolysis is the nucleophilic substitution of the halogen by NH3 (alcoholic ammonia in a sealed tube, heated). Reason false: RX + NH3 → RNH2, which attacks more RX to give R2NH, R3N and R4N+X−: a mixture of primary, secondary, tertiary amines and the quaternary salt, not secondary amines only.
    [1]
Answer
(c) Assertion is true but Reason is false.

Concepts to recall

  • Ammonolysis: RX + NH3 → RNH3+X− → RNH2 with a base; then repeated alkylation.
  • To get mainly the primary amine use a large excess of NH3; the Gabriel phthalimide synthesis gives pure primary aliphatic amines.
  • Aryl halides do not undergo ammonolysis under these conditions.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(C)(i)
Question 1(C)(i)[1]
Passage. When two solutions are separated by a semi-permeable membrane, the solvent molecules move from a solution of lower molar concentration to a solution of higher molar concentration through osmosis.
Samar removed the outer hard shell of two different eggs while cooking at home. He then placed one egg in pure water and the other egg in saturated solution of sucrose. What change is he likely to observe in the eggs after few hours?
  1. Egg in pure water: water enters the egg (the inside has the higher solute concentration) by endosmosis, so the egg swells. Egg in saturated sucrose solution: water leaves the egg (the outside is more concentrated) by exosmosis, so the egg shrinks.
    [1]
Answer
The egg in pure water swells; the egg in the sucrose solution shrinks.

Concepts to recall

  • Osmosis: net flow of solvent through a semi-permeable membrane from lower solute concentration to higher solute concentration.
  • Hypotonic outside (pure water) → cell swells; hypertonic outside → cell shrinks; isotonic → no change.
  • Real-life uses: preserving pickles and jams, wilting of salted slugs, intravenous (isotonic) saline.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(C)(ii)
Question 1(C)(ii)[1]
Passage. When two solutions are separated by a semi-permeable membrane, the solvent molecules move from a solution of lower molar concentration to a solution of higher molar concentration through osmosis.
Which solution, hypertonic or hypotonic, has a higher amount of solute in same quantity of solution?
  1. A hypertonic solution has the higher osmotic pressure, so for the same quantity of solution it holds the higher amount of solute; the hypotonic solution holds less.
    [1]
Answer
Hypertonic solution

Concepts to recall

  • Hyper = more solute (higher π); hypo = less solute (lower π).
  • Osmotic pressure π = CRT is directly proportional to concentration.
  • Two solutions with the same π are isotonic.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q1(C)(iii)
Question 1(C)(iii)[1]
Passage. When two solutions are separated by a semi-permeable membrane, the solvent molecules move from a solution of lower molar concentration to a solution of higher molar concentration through osmosis.
A 5 % aqueous solution of glucose (molar mass = 180 g mol−1) is isotonic with 1·66 % aqueous solution of urea. Calculate the molar mass of urea.
  1. Isotonic: πglucose = πurea, hence (same T) concentrations in mol L−1 are equal: 5/180 = 1·66/M.  M = (1·66 × 180)/5 = 59·76 g mol−1 (about 60 g mol−1).
    [1]
Answer
Molar mass of urea ≈ 59·8 g mol−1 (≈ 60)
Marking note 5 % and 1·66 % are taken as 5 g and 1·66 g per 100 mL (same volume) of solution.

Concepts to recall

  • Isotonic solutions have equal osmotic pressure, hence equal molar concentration at the same temperature (for non-electrolytes).
  • π = (n/V)RT = wBRT/(MBV); a w/v % means the same volume of both solutions, so the volume cancels.
  • Urea is CO(NH2)2: 12 + 16 + 2 × 16 = 60 g mol−1, a quick check.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q2
Question 2[2]
(i) Write a chemical test to distinguish between ethanol and phenol.
(ii) Give a chemical reaction to convert acetaldehyde into secondary propyl alcohol.
  1. (i) Neutral FeCl3 test. To a little of each compound add a few drops of neutral ferric chloride solution. Phenol gives a violet (purple) colour: 6C6H5OH + Fe3+ → [Fe(OC6H5)6]3− + 6H+. Ethanol gives no colour (it stays yellow).
    [1]
  2. (ii) Acetaldehyde is treated with methylmagnesium bromide in dry ether and the adduct hydrolysed: CH3CHO + CH3MgBr → CH3CH(CH3)OMgBr;  CH3CH(CH3)OMgBr + H2O → (CH3)2CHOH + Mg(OH)Br. Product: propan-2-ol (secondary propyl alcohol).
    [1]
Answer
(i) Phenol: violet colour with neutral FeCl3; ethanol: none. (ii) CH3CHO + CH3MgBr, then H2O → (CH3)2CHOH
Marking note Other valid tests for (i): bromine water (phenol gives white ppt), or phenol dissolves in aqueous NaOH. Ethanol gives the iodoform test, which phenol does not.

Concepts to recall

  • Phenol: violet with FeCl3; also dissolves in NaOH and decolourises bromine water (white ppt of 2,4,6-tribromophenol). Ethanol: none of these; gives iodoform test.
  • Grignard + HCHO → 1° alcohol; + other RCHO → 2° alcohol; + ketone → 3° alcohol.
  • The Grignard reagent must be prepared and used under strictly dry conditions (it is destroyed by water).
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q3
Question 3[2]
Give a reason for each of the following.
(i) Zinc, cadmium and mercury are considered as d-block elements but not regarded as transition elements.
(ii) Transition metals possess a great tendency to form complex compounds.
  1. (i) A transition element has an incomplete d subshell in its atoms or in its common ions. Zn, Cd and Hg have the configuration (n−1)d10ns2; their only common ion (M2+, d10) also has a completely filled d subshell, so they are d-block but not transition elements.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Transition metal ions are small and highly charged (high charge density), and they have vacant (n−1)d orbitals of suitable energy which accept lone pairs from ligands to form coordinate bonds.
    [1]
Answer
(i) d10 configuration in atoms and in M2+: no incomplete d subshell. (ii) Small size, high charge, vacant d orbitals.

Concepts to recall

  • Definition: a transition element has partly filled d orbitals in its ground state or in any of its common oxidation states.
  • Zn, Cd, Hg: d10s2; the d electrons are not used in bonding, hence low melting points and no variable valency.
  • Complex formation needs (a) a small, highly charged central ion and (b) vacant orbitals to accept the ligand lone pairs.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q4
Question 4[2]
Convert the following by giving chemical equations for each.
(i) Ethyl bromide to diethyl ether
(ii) Phenol to salicylaldehyde
  1. (i) Williamson synthesis: C2H5Br + C2H5ONa → C2H5OC2H5 + NaBr (sodium ethoxide from ethanol and Na). Equally, 2C2H5Br + Ag2O (dry) → C2H5OC2H5 + 2AgBr.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Reimer–Tiemann reaction: C6H5OH + CHCl3 + 3NaOH (aq, 340 K) → sodium salicylaldehyde (the ortho product, via the dichlorocarbene :CCl2) → on acidification (H+) salicylaldehyde, 2-HO–C6H4–CHO.
    [1]
Answer
(i) C2H5Br + C2H5ONa → C2H5OC2H5. (ii) Phenol + CHCl3/NaOH, then H+ → salicylaldehyde.

Concepts to recall

  • Williamson synthesis: R–X + R′ONa → R–O–R′ (SN2; use a primary halide, a 3° halide only eliminates).
  • Reimer–Tiemann: phenol + CHCl3 + aq. NaOH → salicylaldehyde (ortho-CHO); with CCl4 in place of CHCl3 the product is salicylic acid.
  • The electrophile in Reimer–Tiemann is dichlorocarbene, :CCl2, generated by NaOH from CHCl3.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q5
Question 5[2]
Account for each of the following.
(i) Zirconium (Zr) and Hafnium (Hf) are difficult to separate.
(ii) Salts of Cupric (Cu2+) ion are coloured whereas salts of Cuprous (Cu+) ion are colourless.
  1. (i) Because of the lanthanoid contraction (poor shielding by 4f electrons) Hf (metallic radius 159 pm) has almost the same size as Zr (160 pm), though it is in the next period. They also have the same valence-shell configuration, so their chemical properties are nearly identical and chemical separation is very difficult (it needs special methods such as fractional crystallisation of complex fluorides or ion exchange).
    [1]
  2. (ii) Cu2+ is 3d9: it has one unpaired electron, so d–d transitions can absorb visible light and the ion is coloured. Cu+ is 3d10: the d subshell is full, there is no vacant d orbital for an electron to be promoted into, so no d–d transition occurs and its salts are colourless.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Lanthanoid contraction makes Zr and Hf almost identical in size and properties. (ii) Cu2+ d9 (unpaired electron, d–d transition); Cu+ d10 (none).
Common slip Do not say Hf and Zr have the same radius because they are in the same group; the point is the lanthanoid contraction, which has cancelled the usual increase in size down the group.

Concepts to recall

  • Lanthanoid contraction: steady decrease in size from La to Lu, as 4f electrons shield the nuclear charge poorly; consequences: Zr–Hf, Nb–Ta, Mo–W pairs alike; third-row elements dense and unreactive.
  • Colour of d-block ions arises from d–d transitions, which need unpaired (or at least partly filled) d orbitals; d0 and d10 ions are colourless (Sc3+, Zn2+, Cu+, Ag+).
  • Colour observed is the complement of the colour absorbed.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q6
Question 6[2]
How will you bring the following conversions?
(i) Benzene to biphenyl
(ii) Iodoform to acetylene
  1. (i) Benzene → bromobenzene: C6H6 + Br2 (FeBr3) → C6H5Br + HBr. Then Fittig reaction: 2C6H5Br + 2Na (dry ether) → C6H5–C6H5 + 2NaBr. (Ullmann: 2C6H5I + 2Cu, heat, gives the same product.)
    [1]
  2. (ii) Heating iodoform with finely divided silver removes the iodine: 2CHI3 + 6Ag → HC≡CH + 6AgI.
    [1]
Answer
(i) C6H6 → C6H5Br → C6H5–C6H5 (Fittig). (ii) 2CHI3 + 6Ag → C2H2 + 6AgI

Concepts to recall

  • Wurtz: 2RX + 2Na → R–R; Wurtz–Fittig: ArX + RX + 2Na → Ar–R; Fittig: 2ArX + 2Na → Ar–Ar.
  • Heating a trihalomethane with Ag powder removes the halogen as AgX, joining the two carbons: 2CHX3 + 6Ag → HC≡CH + 6AgX.
  • Step 1 for biphenyl, halogenation of benzene, is electrophilic substitution with a Lewis acid catalyst.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q7
Question 7[2]
Calculate the maximum possible electrical work that can be obtained from a galvanic cell under standard conditions at 298 K.
Zn | Zn2+(aq) || Ag+(aq) | Ag
Given E°(Zn2+/Zn) = −0·76 V; E°(Ag+/Ag) = +0·80 V
  1. Maximum electrical work = −ΔG° = nFE°cell. Cell reaction: Zn + 2Ag+ → Zn2+ + 2Ag, so n = 2.
    [½]
  2. E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode = 0·80 − (−0·76) = 1·56 V
    [½]
  3. Wmax = 2 × 96500 C mol−1 × 1·56 V
    [½]
  4. = 3·01 × 105 J mol−1 = 301·1 kJ mol−1 (of Zn dissolved)
    [½]
Answer
Wmax = 3·01 × 105 J mol−1 ≈ 301 kJ mol−1
Common slip Writing ΔG° = +301 kJ (sign error): the cell is spontaneous, so ΔG° is negative and the work obtained is +301 kJ.

Concepts to recall

  • ΔG° = −nFE°; the maximum work a cell can do is −ΔG (non-expansion work), and Wmax is positive for a spontaneous cell.
  • E°cell = E°(right, reduction) − E°(left, oxidation); do not multiply E° by the stoichiometric coefficient.
  • Cell notation: anode (oxidation) on the left, cathode (reduction) on the right; || is the salt bridge.
SECTION B – 20 MARKSISC 2024 Chemistry Paper 1 (862A), Q8
Question 8[2]
(i) Give a reason for each of the following.
  (a) Ethoxy ethane does not react with sodium, but ethanol does.
  (b) Methoxy ethane with conc. HI at 373 K gives C2H5OH and CH3I but not CH3OH and C2H5I.
OR
(ii) An organic compound [A] having molecular formula C4H10O forms a compound [B] with molecular formula C4H8O on oxidation. Compound [B] gives a positive iodoform test. The reaction of compound [B] with CH3MgBr followed by hydrolysis gives compound [C] with molecular formula C5H12O. Identify the compounds [A], [B] and [C]. Write the reaction for the conversion of compound [A] to compound [B].

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) Ethanol has an acidic hydrogen on oxygen (the O–H bond is polar); sodium displaces it to give sodium ethoxide and H2: 2C2H5OH + 2Na → 2C2H5ONa + H2. Ethoxyethane has no O–H bond, only C–H bonds (not acidic), so there is nothing for sodium to replace.
    [1]
  2. (b) The ether is first protonated: CH3–O+(H)–C2H5. The iodide ion then attacks by an SN2 mechanism at the smaller, less hindered alkyl group, the methyl carbon, and C2H5OH is the leaving group: CH3OC2H5 + HI → CH3I + C2H5OH.
    [1]
Answer
(a) Ethanol has O–H (acidic H), ether has none. (b) I− attacks the less hindered CH3 (SN2) of the protonated ether.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. [B] (C4H8O) gives the iodoform test, so it contains CH3CO–: [B] = butan-2-one, CH3COCH2CH3. It was obtained by oxidising a secondary alcohol: [A] = butan-2-ol, CH3CH(OH)CH2CH3.
    [1]

Concepts to recall

  • Nernst equation at 298 K: E = E° − (0·0591/n) log Q, where Q = [products]/[reactants] (pure solids omitted); here Q = [Cu2+]/[Ag+]2.
  • ΔG° = −nFE°; E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode; the electrode of lower E° is the anode.
  • Kohlrausch: Λ°m = ν+λ°+ + ν−λ°−; κ = G*/R; Λm = 1000κ/M; α = Λm/Λ°m for a weak electrolyte.
  • Faraday: m = (Q/zF)M = ItM/zF; cells in series carry the same charge, so masses are proportional to equivalent masses.
