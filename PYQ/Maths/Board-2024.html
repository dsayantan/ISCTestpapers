SECTION A – 65 MARKSISC 2024 Mathematics (860), Q1(i)
Question 1(i)[1]
Let L be the set of all straight lines in a plane. The relation R on L defined as ‘perpendicular to’ is:
(a)Symmetric and Transitive
(b)Transitive
(c)Symmetric
(d)Equivalence
  1. Reflexive? A line is not perpendicular to itself, so R is not reflexive.
    [½]
  2. Symmetric: if l1 ⊥ l2 then l2 ⊥ l1. Transitive? If l1 ⊥ l2 and l2 ⊥ l3 then l1 ∥ l3, not ⊥; so it is not transitive.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Symmetric
Common slip Choosing (a): the pair of lines l1, l3 is parallel, not perpendicular, so transitivity fails.

Concepts to recall

  • An equivalence relation is reflexive, symmetric and transitive; test all three, one counter-example kills a property.
  • For “perpendicular to” the usual counter-example for transitivity is three lines l1 ⊥ l2 ⊥ l3 with l1 ∥ l3.
SECTION A – 65 MARKSISC 2024 Mathematics (860), Q1(ii)
Question 1(ii)[1]
The order and the degree of the differential equation 1 + (dydx)2 = d2ydx2 are:
(a)2 and 32
(b)2 and 3
(c)3 and 4
(d)2 and 1
  1. Highest derivative is d2y/dx2, so the order is 2.
    [½]
  2. The equation is a polynomial in the derivatives: y″ = 1 + (y′)2. The power of y″ is 1, so the degree is 1.
    [½]
Answer
(d) 2 and 1
Common slip Giving degree 2 from the squared first derivative.

Concepts to recall

  • Order = order of the highest derivative; degree = its power once the equation is a polynomial in the derivatives (free of radicals and fractions of derivatives).
  • The square of dy/dx does not change the degree, because it is not the highest derivative.

Question 1(iii)[1]
Let A be a non-empty set.
Statement 1: Identity relation on A is Reflexive.
Statement 2: Every Reflexive relation on A is an Identity relation.
(a)Both the statements are true.
(b)Both the statements are false.
(c)Statement 1 is true and Statement 2 is false.
(d)Statement 1 is false and Statement 2 is true.
  1. Statement 1: the identity relation I = {(a, a) : a ∈ A} contains (a, a) for every a, so it is reflexive. True.
    [½]
  2. Statement 2: for A = {1, 2}, R = {(1,1), (2,2), (1,2)} is reflexive but is not the identity relation. False.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Statement 1 is true and Statement 2 is false.

Concepts to recall

  • Reflexive: (a, a) ∈ R for every a. The identity relation is the smallest reflexive relation.
  • A reflexive relation may contain extra pairs; the converse of Statement 2 is the true one.

Question 1(iv)[1]
The graph of the function f is shown below. Of the following options, at what values of x is the function f NOT differentiable?
(a)At x = 0 and x = 2
(b)At x = 1 and x = 3
(c)At x = −1 and x = 1
(d)At x = −1·5 and x = 1·5
  1. At x = 0 the left branch ends at the open dot (0, −1) while f(0) = 0: f is not even continuous there, hence not differentiable.
    [½]
  2. At x = 2 the lower semicircle meets the upper semicircle with a vertical tangent (slope infinite), so the derivative does not exist. At 1, 3, −1, ±1·5 the curve is smooth.
    [½]
Answer
(a) At x = 0 and x = 2

Concepts to recall

  • Differentiable ⇒ continuous. A break (jump, hole) is a point of non-differentiability.
  • A corner (unequal one-sided slopes) or a vertical tangent (infinite slope) also fails; read these off the graph.
  • Crossing the x-axis smoothly (as near −1·5) is no problem.

Question 1(vii)[1]
Assertion: Let the matrices A = −32−54 and B = 4−25−3 be such that A100B = BA100.
Reason: AB = BA implies AnB = BAn for all positive integers n.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Assertion is false and Reason is true.
  1. Reason: if AB = BA then AnB = An−1(AB) = An−1BA = … = BAn (move B across one A at a time). True.

(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.

In which one of the following intervals is the function f(x) = x3 − 12x increasing?
(a)(−2, 2)
(b)(−∞, −2) ∪ (2, ∞)

If A and B are symmetric matrices of the same order, then AB − BA is:

Question 1(xv)[1]
Given that 1y + 1x = 112 and y decreases at a rate of 1 cm s−1, find the rate of change of x when x = 5 cm and y = 1 cm.
  1. Differentiate with respect to time: −(1/y2) dy/dt − (1/x2) dx/dt = 0, so dx/dt = −(x2/y2) dy/dt.
    [½]
  2. With dy/dt = −1, x = 5, y = 1: dx/dt = −(25)(−1) = 25 cm s−1 (x increases).
    [½]

Question 2[2]
(i) Let f : R − {−1/3} → R − {0} be defined as f(x) = 53x + 1 is invertible. Find f−1(x).
OR
(ii) If f : R → R is defined by f(x) = 2x − 74, show that f(x) is one-one and onto.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Put y = f(x) = 5/(3x + 1). Then 3x + 1 = 5/y.
    [1]
  2. x = (5 − y)/(3y), so f−1(x) = 5 − x3x, x ∈ R − {0}.
    [1]
Answer
f−1(x) = (5 − x)/(3x)

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. One-one: f(x1) = f(x2) ⇒ (2x1 − 7)/4 = (2x2 − 7)/4 ⇒ x1 = x2.
    [1]
  2. Onto: let y ∈ R (co-domain). Solving (2x − 7)/4 = y gives x = (4y + 7)/2, a real number, and f((4y + 7)/2) = (4y + 7 − 7)/4 = y. Every y has a pre-image, so f is onto.
    [1]
Answer
f is one-one and onto (a bijection).

Concepts to recall

  • To find the inverse: write y = f(x), solve for x in terms of y, then rename y as x.
  • f is invertible iff it is one-one and onto; the problem tells us it is.

Find the value of the determinant given below, without expanding it at any stage.
βγ1α(β + γ)γα1β(γ + α)αβ1γ(α + β)
  1. Apply C1 → C1 + C3. The first column becomes βγ + αβ + αγ, γα + βγ + βα, αβ + γα + γβ: each entry equals αβ + βγ + γα = s.

SECTION A – 65 MARKSISC 2024 Mathematics (860), Q4
Question 4[2]
(i) Determine the value of k for which the following function is continuous at x = 3: f(x) = {(x + 3)2 − 36x − 3; x ≠ 3k; x = 3
OR
(ii) Find a point on the curve y = (x − 2)2 at which the tangent is parallel to the line joining the chord through the points (2, 0) and (4, 4).

Solution to the first alternative

  1. For continuity at 3: limx→3 f(x) = f(3) = k.
    [½]
  2. (x + 3)2 − 36 = (x + 3 − 6)(x + 3 + 6) = (x − 3)(x + 9).
    [½]
  3. For x ≠ 3, f(x) = x + 9, so the limit = 12. Hence k = 12.
    [1]
Answer
k = 12

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Slope of the chord = (4 − 0)/(4 − 2) = 2.
    [½]
  2. Slope of the tangent = dy/dx = 2(x − 2). Parallel ⇒ 2(x − 2) = 2, so x = 3.
    [1]
  3. y = (3 − 2)2 = 1. The point is (3, 1).
    [½]
Answer
(3, 1)

Concepts to recall

  • f continuous at a ⇔ limx→a f(x) = f(a).
  • Factorise a difference of squares to remove the 0/0 form: a2 − b2 = (a − b)(a + b).
  • Parallel lines have equal slopes; slope of the tangent is dy/dx.
  • This is the mean value theorem: the tangent at some interior point is parallel to the chord (here at the mid-point of the chord’s x-range).

Question 7[4]
If y = 3 cos(log x) + 4 sin(log x), show that x2 d2ydx2 + x dydx + y = 0.
  1. dy/dx = 3(−sin(log x))(1/x) + 4 cos(log x)(1/x), so x dy/dx = −3 sin(log x) + 4 cos(log x).
    [1½]
  2. Differentiate again (product rule on the left): x d2y/dx2 + dy/dx = −3 cos(log x)(1/x) − 4 sin(log x)(1/x).
    [1½]
  3. Multiply by x: x2 d2y/dx2 + x dy/dx = −[3 cos(log x) + 4 sin(log x)] = −y.
    [½]
  4. Hence x2 d2y/dx2 + x dy/dx + y = 0.
    [½]

Proved: x2y″ + xy′ + y = 0.

  • Chain rule: d/dx[sin(log x)] = cos(log x) · 1/x.
  • Differentiating x y′ by the product rule gives y′ + x y″.
  • Strategy: multiply y′ by x first, so the second differentiation stays clean.

Question 8[4]
(i) Solve for x: sin−1(x2) + cos−1 x = π6
OR
(ii) If sin−1x + sin−1y + sin−1z = π, show that x2 − y2 − z2 + 2yz√(1 − x2) = 0.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Write sin−1(x/2) = π/6 − cos−1x and take sine: x/2 = sin(π/6) cos(cos−1x) − cos(π/6) sin(cos−1x).
    [1½]
  2. cos(cos−1x) = x, sin(cos−1x) = √(1 − x2). So x/2 = x/2 − (√3/2)√(1 − x2), giving √(1 − x2) = 0 and x = ±1.
    [1½]
  3. Check: x = 1 gives π/6 + 0 = π/6 (valid); x = −1 gives −π/6 + π = 5π/6 ≠ π/6 (rejected).
    [1]
Answer
x = 1
Marking note Full marks for x = 1 with the rejection of −1 shown; a candidate who stops at ±1 loses the last mark.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Let sin−1x = A, sin−1y = B, sin−1z = C, so A + B + C = π and B + C = π − A.
    [1]
  2. Take cosine: cos(B + C) = −cos A, i.e. cosB cosC − sinB sinC = −cosA.
    [1]
  3. cos B = √(1 − y2), cos C = √(1 − z2), cos A = √(1 − x2): √(1−y2)√(1−z2) = yz − √(1−x2).
    [1]
  4. Square: (1 − y2)(1 − z2) = y2z2 − 2yz√(1−x2) + 1 − x2. Expanding: 1 − y2 − z2 + y2z2 = y2z2 + 1 − x2 − 2yz√(1−x2), so x2 − y2 − z2 + 2yz√(1−x2) = 0.
    [1]
Answer
Proved.

Concepts to recall

  • sin(A − B) = sinA cosB − cosA sinB.
  • sin(cos−1x) = √(1 − x2) because the angle lies in [0, π].
  • Taking a sine can introduce extra roots; always verify in the original equation.
  • Substitute angles A, B, C so that sine is x, y, z and the radicals are the cosines.
  • cos(π − A) = −cos A.
  • The radical √(1−x2) is the target, so use the cosine form (not the sine form) of B + C.

Question 9[4]
(i) Evaluate: ∫ x2 cos x dx
OR
(ii) Evaluate: ∫ x + 7x2 + 4x + 7 dx

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Integration by parts with u = x2, dv = cos x dx: ∫x2cos x dx = x2 sin x − ∫ 2x sin x dx.
    [2]
  2. ∫ x sin x dx = −x cos x + ∫ cos x dx = −x cos x + sin x.
    [1]
  3. Result: x2 sin x + 2x cos x − 2 sin x + C.
    [1]

x2 sin x + 2x cos x − 2 sin x + C

  1. Write x + 7 = ½(2x + 4) + 5 (derivative of denominator is 2x + 4).
    [1]

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) n = 7, Σx = 56, Σy = 56, so x̄ = 8 and ȳ = 8. With dx = x − 8, dy = y − 8: Σdxdy = 6 + 0 − 2 + 9 + 0 + 2 + 6 = 21, Σdx2 = 9 + 1 + 1 + 9 + 0 + 4 + 4 = 28.
    [1½]
  2. byx = Σdxdy/Σdx2 = 21/28 = 3/4.
    [1]
  3. Regression of Y on X: y − 8 = (3/4)(x − 8), i.e. y = (3/4)x + 2.
    [½]
  4. (b) For x = 13: y = (3/4)(13) + 2 = 11·75, i.e. a monthly salary of ₹ 11,750.
    [1]
Answer
(a) y = 0·75x + 2 (Y on X); (b) ₹ 11,750

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) The regression line passes through (x̄, ȳ) with ȳ = 44: 3(44) − 5x̄ + 180 = 0 ⇒ 5x̄ = 312, x̄ = 62·4.
    [1]
  2. (b) σx2 = (9/16)σy2 ⇒ σx/σy = 3/4. The line, solved for x: x = (3/5)y + 36, so bxy = 3/5 (line of X on Y; the other reading, byx = 5/3, would give |r| = (5/3)(3/4) = 5/4 > 1, impossible).
    [1]
  3. bxy = rσx/σy ⇒ r = (3/5)/(3/4) = 4/5.
    [1]
  4. r = 0·8 (positive, since the regression coefficient 3/5 is positive).
    [1]
Answer
(a) 62·4; (b) r = 4/5 = 0·8
Marking note The question does not say which variable the given line has as dependent. Only the reading X on Y gives a valid r (|r| ≤ 1); the mean 62·4 is the same either way.

Concepts to recall

  • Regression of Y on X: y − ȳ = byx(x − x̄), with byx = Σ(x − x̄)(y − ȳ)/Σ(x − x̄)2.
  • Salary is the dependent variable, so regress Y on X to predict it.
  • Both means are whole numbers here (8, 8), so deviations from the mean are the easy route.
  • Both lines of regression pass through the point of means, so substitute the known mean into the given equation.
  • bxy = rσx/σy, byx = rσy/σx; |r| ≤ 1 decides which line is which.

Question 22[4]
Aman has ₹ 1500 to purchase rice and wheat for his grocery shop. Each sack of rice and wheat costs ₹ 180 and ₹ 120 respectively. He can store a maximum number of 10 bags in his shop. He will earn a profit of ₹ 11 per bag of rice and ₹ 9 per bag of wheat. (i) Formulate a Linear Programming Problem to maximise Aman’s profit. (ii) Calculate the maximum profit.
  1. (i) Let Aman buy x bags of rice and y bags of wheat. Maximise Z = 11x + 9y subject to 180x + 120y ≤ 1500 (i.e. 3x + 2y ≤ 25), x + y ≤ 10, x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0.
    [2]
  2. (ii) Corner points of the feasible region: O(0, 0), A(25/3, 0), P(5, 5) [3x + 2y = 25 meets x + y = 10], B(0, 10).
    [1]
  3. Z: O 0; A 11(25/3) = 91·67; P 55 + 45 = 100; B 90. Maximum profit = ₹ 100 at (5, 5).
    [1]
Answer
Maximise Z = 11x + 9y subject to 3x + 2y ≤ 25, x + y ≤ 10, x, y ≥ 0; maximum profit ₹ 100 (5 bags of rice, 5 of wheat).

Concepts to recall

  • LPP: decision variables, objective function, constraints (budget, storage), non-negativity.
  • Corner point method: the optimum of a linear objective on a bounded feasible region is at a vertex.
  • Bags must be whole numbers; the optimum (5, 5) is integral, so no rounding is needed (A is not integral and gives less anyway).
