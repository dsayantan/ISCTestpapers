What to revise

  • Dropped. Linear regression (lines of regression, regression coefficients, bxybyx = r2, mean from two lines) and the commerce calculus of cost, revenue, average and marginal functions and break-even are not in the ISC 2027 syllabus. They were Section C of the 2024–2026 papers.

Question 19(i) · Board 2026[1]
Section C of the old paper pattern: the alternative to Section B, not counted in the 80 marks. Not in the ISC 2027 syllabus.
Statement 1: Two regression coefficients cannot have the same sign.
Statement 2: Both the regression coefficients can be numerically greater than unity.
(a)Statement 1 is true and Statement 2 is false.
(b)Statement 2 is true and Statement 1 is false.
(c)Both the statements are true.
(d)Both the statements are false.
  1. byx = rσy/σx and bxy = rσx/σy both carry the sign of r (σ’s are positive), so the two regression coefficients always have the same sign: Statement 1 is false.
    [½]
  2. bxy·byx = r2 ≤ 1, so both cannot exceed 1 numerically (their product would exceed 1): Statement 2 is false.
    [½]
Answer
(d) Both the statements are false.

Concepts to recall

  • byx = rσy/σx, bxy = rσx/σy; same sign as r.
  • r2 = bxybyx ≤ 1; so if one coefficient is numerically greater than 1, the other is numerically less than 1.

Question 19(ii) · Board 2026[1]

Which one of the following statements is true about Marginal Revenue?
(a)It is always constant for all firms.
(b)It is always equal to the average revenue.
(c)It is the revenue gained from decreasing output by 1 unit.
(d)MR at x = a is the additional revenue obtained by increasing the output from a to a + 1.
  1. Marginal revenue is the rate of change of total revenue with output, MR = dR/dx. In practical terms, MR at x = a is the extra revenue from producing one more unit, i.e. from a to a + 1.
    [½]
  2. (a) it varies with output in general; (b) it equals AR only when R is proportional to x (constant price); (c) refers to decreasing output. So only (d) is true.
    [½]
Answer
(d) MR at x = a is the additional revenue obtained by increasing the output from a to a + 1.

Concepts to recall

  • MR = dR/dx; AR = R/x; marginal cost MC = dC/dx.
  • Approximate meaning: MR at x = a ≈ R(a + 1) − R(a).

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (20)ISC Specimen 2026 Mathematics, Q19(i)
Question 19(i) · Specimen 2026[1]
Section C of the old paper pattern: the alternative to Section B, not counted in the 80 marks. Not in the ISC 2027 syllabus.
Which condition is true if Average Cost (AC) is constant at all levels of output?
(a)MC > AC
(b)MC = AC
(c)MC < AC
(d)MC = ½ AC
  1. AC = C(x)/x. AC is constant ⇒ d(AC)/dx = 0.
    [½]
  2. d(AC)/dx = [xC′(x) − C(x)]/x2 = 0 ⇒ C′(x) = C(x)/x, i.e. MC = AC.
    [½]
Answer
(b) MC = AC

Concepts to recall

  • MC = C′(x), AC = C(x)/x. MC < AC where AC is falling, MC = AC where AC is flat (at its minimum or constant), MC > AC where AC is rising.
  • Example: C = 5x gives AC = 5 (constant) and MC = 5.

Which of the following statement(s) is/are correct with respect to regression coefficients?
Statement 1: It measures the degree of linear relationship between two variables.
Statement 2: It gives the value by which one variable changes for a unit change in the other variable.
(a)Statement 1 is true and Statement 2 is false.
(b)Statement 2 is true and Statement 1 is false.
(c)Both the statements are true.
(d)Both the statements are false.
  1. Statement 1 describes the correlation coefficient r (the degree of linear relationship, −1 ≤ r ≤ 1), not the regression coefficient. False.
    [½]
  2. Statement 2: the regression coefficient byx is the slope, the change in y for a unit change in x. True. So only Statement 2 is correct: option (b).
    [½]

(b) Statement 2 is true and Statement 1 is false.
Marking note CISCE’s key prints “(b)” but its words describe “both statements true, Statement 2 not the explanation”, which matches none of the printed options. Option (b), Statement 2 true and Statement 1 false, is what the concept requires and what the key’s letter names.

Concepts to recall

  • Correlation coefficient r = strength and direction of the linear relationship; regression coefficient b = slope = change in one variable per unit change in the other.
  • r2 = byx · bxy links the two.

Question 19(ii) · Specimen 2025[1]
Section C of the old paper pattern: the alternative to Section B, not counted in the 80 marks. Not in the ISC 2027 syllabus.
Read the following statements and choose the correct option: (I) If r = 0, then regression lines are not defined. (II) If r = 0, then regression lines are parallel. (III) If r = 0, then regression lines are perpendicular. (IV) If r = ±1, then regression lines coincide.
(a)Only IV is correct
(b)Only I and II are correct
(c)Only I and IV are correct
(d)Only III and IV are correct
  1. r = 0 ⇒ both regression coefficients are 0, so the lines are y = ȳ and x = x̄: they exist and are perpendicular. (I), (II) false; (III) true.
    [½]
  2. r = ±1 ⇒ byxbxy = 1, the two lines have the same slope and the same point (x̄, ȳ): they coincide. (IV) true. So only III and IV are correct.
    [½]
Answer
(d) Only III and IV are correct

Concepts to recall

  • Angle between the regression lines: tan θ = ((1 − r2)/|r|)(σxσy/(σx2 + σy2)): r = 0 ⇒ 90°, r = ±1 ⇒ 0°.
  • Uncorrelated variables: the best estimate of y is just ȳ, whatever x is.

Question 20 · Board 2026[2]
Section C of the old paper pattern: the alternative to Section B, not counted in the 80 marks. Not in the ISC 2027 syllabus.
(i) A company produces a commodity with ₹36,000 as a fixed cost. The variable cost is estimated to be 25% of the total revenue earned. The selling price of the product is ₹20 per unit. Find the following: (a) Cost function [1] (b) Profit function [1]
OR
(ii) A school is organising an art and craft exhibition. The management has decided to donate the profit earned from the sale of exhibition items to an NGO. Total cost function for organising the exhibition is: C(x) = −x2 + 11x + 50. Each item is sold for ₹6. Find the condition for the number of items to be sold to earn profit.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) Revenue R(x) = 20x. Variable cost = 25% of 20x = 5x. Cost function C(x) = 36000 + 5x.
    [1]
  2. (b) Profit P(x) = R(x) − C(x) = 20x − 36000 − 5x = 15x − 36000.
    [1]
Answer
(a) C(x) = 36000 + 5x; (b) P(x) = 15x − 36000

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Revenue R(x) = 6x. Profit P(x) = 6x − (−x2 + 11x + 50) = x2 − 5x − 50.
    [1]
  2. Profit requires P > 0: x2 − 5x − 50 = (x − 10)(x + 5) > 0 ⇒ x > 10 (since x > 0). More than 10 items must be sold.
    [1]
Answer
x > 10: at least 11 items must be sold.

Concepts to recall

  • Cost = fixed + variable. Here variable cost is tied to revenue (25% of 20x), not to units.
  • Profit = Revenue − Cost; break-even when P = 0 (x = 2400 units).
  • Profit = Revenue − Cost; “to earn profit” means P(x) > 0.
  • Solve the quadratic inequality by factorising and discard the negative root (x ≥ 0).
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC Specimen 2026 Mathematics, Q20
Question 20 · Specimen 2026[2]
Section C of the old paper pattern: the alternative to Section B, not counted in the 80 marks. Not in the ISC 2027 syllabus.
(i) A real estate company is going to build a new residential complex. The land they have purchased can hold at most 500 apartments. If they make x apartments, the monthly maintenance cost for the whole complex is: fixed cost ₹4000, variable cost ₹(14x − 0·04x2). How many apartments should the complex have in order to minimise the maintenance cost?
OR
(ii) The demand function of a monopoly is given by x = 100 − 4p. Find the quantity at which the marginal revenue will be zero.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Total cost C(x) = 4000 + 14x − 0·04x2, 0 ≤ x ≤ 500. C′(x) = 14 − 0·08x = 0 ⇒ x = 175; C″ = −0·08 < 0, so x = 175 gives a maximum (cost 5225), not a minimum.
    [1]
  2. The minimum on [0, 500] is therefore at an end-point: C(0) = 4000, C(500) = 4000 + 7000 − 10000 = 1000. The least cost is at x = 500 apartments.
    [1]
Answer
500 apartments (maintenance cost ₹1000, the least)
Common slip Stopping at the critical point x = 175, which maximises the cost.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. p = (100 − x)/4, so revenue R(x) = px = (100x − x2)/4.
    [1]
  2. MR = dR/dx = (100 − 2x)/4 = 0 ⇒ x = 50.
    [1]
Answer
x = 50

Concepts to recall

  • On a closed interval, the absolute extrema are at critical points or end-points: compare all of them.
  • A zero of C′ with C″ < 0 is a maximum: the cost rises to 175 apartments and then falls.
  • Revenue = price × quantity; express the price in terms of x from the demand function before differentiating.
  • MR = 0 at the quantity that maximises revenue (R″ < 0 here).

(i) Consider the data given below: x: arithmetic mean 36, standard deviation 11; y: arithmetic mean 85, standard deviation 8; and r = 0·66. Find the regression coefficients.
OR
(ii) Out of the two regression lines given below, find the line of regression of x on y: x + 2y − 5 = 0 and 2x + 3y − 8 = 0.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. byx = rσy/σx = 0·66 × 8/11 = 0·48.
    [1]
  2. bxy = rσx/σy = 0·66 × 11/8 = 0·9075.
    [1]
Answer
byx = 0·48; bxy = 0·9075
Common slip Interchanging σx and σy.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Suppose x + 2y = 5 is x on y: x = 5 − 2y, bxy = −2; then 2x + 3y = 8 is y on x: byx = −2/3. r2 = (−2)(−2/3) = 4/3 > 1, which is impossible. Reject.
    [1]
  2. So 2x + 3y = 8 is x on y: x = 4 − 3y/2, bxy = −3/2, and x + 2y = 5 is y on x: byx = −1/2; r2 = 3/4 < 1, valid (r = −√3/2). The line of regression of x on y is 2x + 3y − 8 = 0.
    [1]

Concepts to recall

  • Actual (not marginal) revenue from the nth unit = R(n) − R(n − 1). Marginal revenue R′(n) is only an approximation.
  • Regression coefficients: byx = rσy/σx, bxy = rσx/σy; same sign as r; r2 = bxybyx; r is the GM, not the AM.
  • bxy = Σ(x − x̄)(y − ȳ)/Σ(y − ȳ)2: the denominator is for the variable you are regressing on.
