What to revise

  • One 4-mark question in every paper and always with a sketch: area under y2 = 4ax and x = a, between a parabola and a line, y = √(4 − x2) with the x-axis (a semicircle), y = 4 − x2, y = x|x|, y = x(4 − x) from 0 to 5 (the part below the axis counts positive).
  • Specimen 2027 sets it with a tangent at the origin and with a parabola through P and Q on a line.
  • Habit: draw first, shade the region, split at intersections and where the curve crosses the axis.

Question 1(viii) · Specimen 2027[1]
The expression for finding the area of the shaded region is:
(a)∫82(4 − x2 − 2) dx
(b)∫82(x2 + 4) dx
(c)∫82(y2 + 4) dy
(d)∫82(4 − x2) dx
  1. The line is x + 2y = 8, i.e. x/8 + y/4 = 1, so y = 4 − x/2.
    [½]
  2. The shaded region is bounded by x = 2, this line and the x-axis (which meets the line at x = 8). Area = ∫28 (4 − x/2) dx.
    [½]
Answer
(d) ∫82(4 − x2) dx
Marking note Check by area: the triangle has base 6 and height 3, area 9; ∫28 (4 − x/2) dx = [4x − x2/4]28 = 16 − 7 = 9.

Concepts to recall

  • Area under a curve y = f(x) from x = a to x = b above the x-axis is ∫ab y dx.
  • Rewrite the given line as y = … first; the limits are where the region starts (x = 2) and where the line meets the x-axis (x = 8).

Question 15 · Specimen 2027[3]
Also draws on: the tangent at the origin (Ch 6) is needed to find the triangle.
(i) The diagram below shows the graph of f(x) = 2x√(a2 − x2), for −1 ≤ x ≤ a, where a > 1. The line L is the tangent to the graph of f(x) at the origin O. Given that f′(x) = (2a2 − 4x2)/√(a2 − x2), for −1 ≤ x < a. Using integration, find the area of ΔOPQ in terms of a.
OR
(ii) A line with equation y = −3x + 9 intersects the axes at the points P and Q. A parabola of the form y = ax2 + c, where a, c ∈ ℤ, also passes through the points P and Q as shown in the diagram. (a) Obtain the equation of the parabola. [1] (b) Using integration find the area of the shaded region. [2]

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Slope of the tangent at O: f′(0) = 2a2/√(a2) = 2a. Tangent L: y = 2ax.
    [1]
  2. P(a, b) lies on L directly above Q(a, 0), so ΔOPQ is the region under L from x = 0 to x = a. Area = ∫0a 2ax dx.
    [1]
  3. = 2a [x2/2]0a = 2a · a2/2 = a3 square units.
    [1]
Answer
Area of ΔOPQ = a3 square units
Marking note The curve itself returns to the x-axis at Q (f(a) = 0); P is on the tangent L, with b = 2a2. CISCE’s key also gives a3 square units.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) Putting x = 0: y = 9, P(0, 9); putting y = 0: x = 3, Q(3, 0). The parabola passes through (0, 9): c = 9; through (3, 0): 9a + 9 = 0, a = −1. Parabola: y = 9 − x2.
    [1]
  2. (b) Between x = 0 and 3 the parabola lies above the line. Area = ∫03 [(9 − x2) − (9 − 3x)] dx = ∫03 (3x − x2) dx.
    [1]
  3. = [3x2/2 − x3/3]03 = 27/2 − 9 = 9/2 square units.
    [1]
Answer
(a) y = 9 − x2; (b) 9/2 square units
Marking note The paper’s diagram is redrawn in the solution figure; the shaded region lies between the chord PQ and the arc.

Concepts to recall

  • The slope of the tangent at a point is the derivative there; the tangent at the origin passes through O: y = f′(0)x.
  • Area of a region under a straight line y = mx from 0 to a is ∫0a mx dx = ma2/2.
  • Check with ½ × base × height: ½ · a · 2a2 = a3.
  • Find the intercepts of the line by putting x = 0 and y = 0; two points fix the two unknowns a and c of the parabola.
  • Area between two curves = ∫ (upper − lower) dx between the points where they meet.
  • The limits are the x-coordinates of P and Q: 0 and 3.

Question 18 · Board 2026[4]
Find the area of the region bounded by y = √(4 − x2) and x axis using integration.
  1. y = √(4 − x2) ⇒ x2 + y2 = 4, y ≥ 0: the upper half of a circle of radius 2 centred at the origin; it meets the x-axis at x = ±2.
    [1]
  2. Area = ∫−22 √(4 − x2) dx = 2∫02 √(4 − x2) dx (symmetry about the y-axis).
    [1]
  3. = 2[ (x/2)√(4 − x2) + (4/2) sin−1(x/2) ]02 = 2[(0 + 2·π/2) − (0 + 0)].
    [1]
  4. = 2π square units. (Check: half the area π(2)2 = 4π.)
    [1]
Answer
Area = 2π square units

Concepts to recall

  • ∫√(a2 − x2) dx = (x/2)√(a2 − x2) + (a2/2) sin−1(x/a) + C.
  • Sketch first: the region is a semicircle; area of a circle πr2 gives an immediate check.
  • Even integrand over a symmetric interval: ∫−aa = 2∫0a.
FOUR-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC Specimen 2026 Mathematics, Q18
Question 18 · Specimen 2026[4]
Find the area bounded by the curve y = x(4 − x) and the x-axis from x = 0 to x = 5, as shown in the figure given.
  1. The curve cuts the x-axis at x = 0 and x = 4. On (0, 4), y ≥ 0 (above the axis); on (4, 5), y ≤ 0 (below the axis). The area is taken in two parts, each positive.
    [1]
  2. A1 = ∫04 (4x − x2) dx = [2x2 − x3/3]04 = 32 − 64/3 = 32/3.
    [1]
  3. A2 = |∫45 (4x − x2) dx| = |[2x2 − x3/3]45| = |(50 − 125/3) − (32 − 64/3)| = |25/3 − 32/3| = 7/3.
    [1]
  4. Total area = A1 + A2 = 32/3 + 7/3 = 39/3 = 13 square units.
    [1]
Answer
Area = 13 square units
Common slip Integrating from 0 to 5 in one go and getting 25/3.

Concepts to recall

  • When the curve crosses the axis, split the integral at the crossing; area is always positive, so take the modulus of the part below the axis.
  • A single integral ∫05 y dx = 32/3 − 7/3 = 25/3 would subtract the lower part: that is the signed area, not the area asked for.
FOUR-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2025 Improvement Mathematics (860), Q18
Question 18 · Board 2025 Imp.[4]
(i) Using integration, find the area of the region bounded by the line x − y + 2 = 0, the curve x = √y and the y-axis.
OR
(ii) Find the area enclosed by the parabola y2 = x and the line y = 2x.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Line: y = x + 2. Curve: x = √y, i.e. y = x2 with x ≥ 0. Intersection: x2 = x + 2, (x − 2)(x + 1) = 0, x = 2 (the root x = −1 is rejected as x ≥ 0); point (2, 4). Figure: [1]
    [1½]
  2. Between x = 0 (the y-axis) and x = 2 the line lies above the parabola. Area = ∫02 [(x + 2) − x2] dx.
    [1]
  3. = [x2/2 + 2x − x3/3]02 = 2 + 4 − 8/3 = 10/3 square units.
    [1½]
Answer
10/3 square units
Common slip Taking the limits −1 to 2 (using both roots) includes the left half of the parabola, which is not part of x = √y.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Solve y2 = x with y = 2x: y2 = y/2, so y = 0 or y = 1/2. Points O(0, 0) and (1/4, 1/2). Figure: [1]
    [1½]
  2. Integrating with respect to y (the line x = y/2 lies to the right of the parabola x = y2 for 0 < y < 1/2): Area = ∫01/2 (y/2 − y2) dy.
    [1]
  3. = [y2/4 − y3/3]01/2 = 1/16 − 1/24 = 1/48 square units.
    [1½]
Answer
1/48 square units
Marking note Same answer with x-strips: ∫01/4(√x − 2x) dx = 1/12 − 1/16 = 1/48.

Concepts to recall

  • Area between two curves with yupper > ylower: ∫ (yupper − ylower) dx between the intersection abscissae.
  • x = √y means x ≥ 0 only: it is the right half of the parabola y = x2; the y-axis is the boundary x = 0.
  • Sketch first: it settles which curve is above and the limits.
  • For a parabola opening along x, integrate with respect to y (horizontal strips); or equivalently ∫01/4 (√x − 2x) dx = 1/48.
  • Find the intersection points first; they give the limits.

Question 18 · Board 2025[4]
(i) The equation y = 4 − x2 represents a parabola. (a) Make a rough sketch of the graph of the given function. [1] (b) Determine the area enclosed between the curve, the x-axis, the lines x = 0 and x = 2. [2] (c) Hence, find the area bounded by the parabola and the x-axis. [1]
OR
(ii) A farmer has a field bounded by three lines x + 2y = 2, y − x = 1, 2x + y = 7. Using integration, find the area of the region bounded by these lines.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) Vertex (0, 4); opens downwards (coefficient of x2 is −1); symmetric about the y-axis; meets the x-axis at x = ±2 (see the sketch).
    [1]
  2. (b) The curve is above the x-axis on [0, 2]: area = ∫02(4 − x2)dx = [4x − x3/3]02
    [1]
  3. = 8 − 8/3 = 16/3 sq. units.
    [1]
  4. (c) The region between the parabola and the x-axis is symmetric about the y-axis, so its area = 2 × 16/3 = 32/3 sq. units.
    [1]
Answer
(b) 16/3 sq. units; (c) 32/3 sq. units.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Vertices: x + 2y = 2 and y = x + 1 meet at P(0, 1); y = x + 1 and 2x + y = 7 meet at Q(2, 3); x + 2y = 2 and 2x + y = 7 meet at R(4, −1).
    [1]
  2. For 0 ≤ x ≤ 2 the region lies between y = x + 1 (above) and y = 1 − x/2 (below): area1 = ∫02(3x/2)dx = [3x2/4]02 = 3.
    [1]
  3. For 2 ≤ x ≤ 4 it lies between y = 7 − 2x (above) and y = 1 − x/2: area2 = ∫24(6 − 3x/2)dx = [6x − 3x2/4]24 = 12 − 9 = 3.
    [1]
  4. Total area = 3 + 3 = 6 sq. units (check by the triangle formula: ½|0(3 + 1) + 2(−1 − 1) + 4(1 − 3)| = 6).
    [1]
Answer
Area = 6 sq. units

Concepts to recall

  • Area under y = f(x) from x = a to b (curve above the axis) = ∫aby dx.
  • Sketch checklist: vertex, direction of opening, intercepts, symmetry. Use symmetry to double a half-area.
  • Area between two curves: ∫ab(yupper − ylower)dx; split where the upper or lower boundary changes (here at the vertex Q, x = 2).
  • Find vertices by solving the lines in pairs; sketch to see which line is on top.

Question 18 · Specimen 2025[4]
(i) Sketch the region enclosed bounded by the curve y = x|x| and the ordinates x = −1 and x = 1. [1]
(ii) Evaluate: ∫01 x2 dx. [1]
(iii) Hence find the area bounded by the curve y = x|x| and the ordinates x = −1 and x = 1. [2]
  1. (i) y = x|x| = −x2 for x < 0 and x2 for x ≥ 0: an S-shaped curve through O, below the axis for −1 ≤ x < 0 and above it for 0 < x ≤ 1; the region lies between the curve, the x-axis and the lines x = ±1 (figure).
    [1]
  2. (ii) ∫01 x2 dx = [x3/3]01 = 1/3.
    [1]
  3. (iii) The two pieces are mirror images in O, each of area ∫01 x2 dx = 1/3 (the left piece lies below the axis, and its area is taken as positive).
    [1]
  4. Total area = 2 × 1/3 = 2/3 square units. (Note ∫−11 x|x| dx = 0, but the area is not that integral.)
    [1]
Answer
(ii) 1/3; (iii) area = 2/3 square units
Common slip Writing the area as ∫−11 x|x| dx = 0.

Concepts to recall

  • Area under a curve: take |y| where the curve is below the x-axis (split at the crossings).
  • x|x| is an odd function, so the signed integral is 0 but the area is twice the right half.
FOUR-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2024 Mathematics (860), Q18
Question 18 · Board 2024[4]
(i) Using integration, find the area bounded by the curve y2 = 4ax and the line x = a.
OR
(ii) Using integration, find the area of the region bounded by the curves y2 = 4x and x2 = 4y.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. The curve is a parabola symmetric about the x-axis, vertex O, and it meets x = a at y = ±2a. Required area = 2 × (area above the x-axis from 0 to a).
    [1]
  2. Area = 2 ∫0a 2√a √x dx = 4√a ∫0a x1/2 dx.
    [1½]
  3. = 4√a · (2/3)a3/2 = 8a2/3.
    [1½]
Answer
8a2/3 square units

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Solve y2 = 4x, x2 = 4y: x4/16 = 4x ⇒ x = 0 or 4. The curves meet at (0, 0) and (4, 4).
    [1]
  2. For 0 ≤ x ≤ 4 the parabola y = 2√x lies above y = x2/4. Area = ∫04 (2√x − x2/4) dx.
    [1]
  3. = [(4/3)x3/2 − x3/12]04 = 32/3 − 16/3 = 16/3.
    [2]
Answer
16/3 square units

Concepts to recall

  • Area between a curve and a line: draw the region, choose vertical strips, and exploit symmetry about the axis.
  • ∫ xn dx = xn+1/(n+1).
  • Points of intersection give the limits; area = ∫ (upper curve − lower curve) dx.
  • Write each curve as y in terms of x (first quadrant): y = 2√x and y = x2/4.
