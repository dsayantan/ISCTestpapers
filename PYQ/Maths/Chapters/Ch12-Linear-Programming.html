Official weightage in the ISC 2027 syllabus: 5 marks. Papers 2024–2026 and specimens 2025, 2026 ask Section A (65) and then Section B or Section C (15); Section B is the counted choice, so every paper’s counted marks add to 80. Where a question offers an OR alternative from another chapter, the alternative sits in its own chapter and is not counted.

What to revise

  • In the 2024–2026 pattern LPP sits in Section C (4 marks, the alternative to Section B) so it is not counted in those papers' 80; the ISC 2027 syllabus gives LP 5 marks and specimen 2027 sets it in Question 9 (3 marks) plus two MCQs.
  • Formulate and solve graphically: crop-land and herbicide, rice and wheat with a budget, book shelf by weight, a feasible region drawn for you.
  • Theory points set again and again: Z = px + qy maximum at two corner points means p and q are in a fixed ratio and every point of the segment is optimal; a problem need not have an optimal solution (unbounded region).
  • Habit: list corner points in a table and evaluate Z at each.

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (2)ISC Specimen 2027 Mathematics, Q1(xiii)
Question 1(xiii) · Specimen 2027[1]
An objective function Z = ax + by is maximum at points (8, 10) and (7, 12). If a, b ≥ 0 and ab = 72, then the maximum value of the function is equal to:
(a)84
(b)132
(c)156
(d)176
  1. Equal maximum at both points: 8a + 10b = 7a + 12b ⇒ a = 2b. With ab = 72: 2b2 = 72, b = 6 (b ≥ 0), a = 12.
    [½]
  2. Maximum value Z = 12(8) + 6(10) = 156 (check: 12(7) + 6(12) = 84 + 72 = 156).
    [½]
Answer
(c) 156

Concepts to recall

  • If an LPP has the same optimal value at two corner points, it has the same value at every point of the segment joining them.
  • Equate Z at the two corner points, then use the extra condition to fix a and b.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (2)ISC Specimen 2027 Mathematics, Q1(xiv)
Question 1(xiv) · Specimen 2027[1]
Statement I: Every Linear Programming Problem has at least one optimal solution.
Statement II: If a Linear Programming Problem has two optimal solutions, then it has infinitely many solutions.
Which of the statements is correct?
(a)Statement I is true and Statement II is false.
(b)Statements I is false and Statement II is true.
(c)Both the statements are true.
(d)Both the statements are false.
  1. Statement I is false: an LPP may be infeasible (no feasible region) or unbounded, and then has no optimal solution.
    [½]
  2. Statement II is true: if two points give the same optimal value, every point on the segment joining them gives the same value, so there are infinitely many optimal solutions.
    [½]
Answer
(b) Statements I is false and Statement II is true.

Concepts to recall

  • An LPP may have a unique optimum, infinitely many optima, or none (infeasible, or an unbounded objective).
  • Feasible region convex: the segment between two optimal points stays in the region and keeps the objective value constant.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (1)ISC Specimen 2027 Mathematics, Q9
Question 9 · Specimen 2027[3]
The feasible region determined by some constraints is represented by the shaded region in the graph given below (vertices A(1, 1), B(3, 3), C(1, 7); the boundary lines pass through (0, 9) and (9/2, 0)). (i) Formulate the constraints which represent the above feasible region. [1] (ii) Hence, maximise the objective function given by Z = x + y. [1] (iii) What change in the constraints will make the feasible region unbounded? [1]
  1. (i) The three boundary lines: x = 1; y = x (through A and B); and 2x + y = 9 (through (0, 9), (9/2, 0), B(3, 3) and C(1, 7)). Testing the origin side for the shaded triangle ABC gives the constraints x ≥ 1, y ≥ x, 2x + y ≤ 9.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Evaluate Z = x + y at the corner points: A(1, 1): 2; B(3, 3): 6; C(1, 7): 8. The maximum value is Z = 8 at C(1, 7).
    [1]
  3. (iii) Reverse the inequality 2x + y ≤ 9 to 2x + y ≥ 9: the region x ≥ 1, y ≥ x, 2x + y ≥ 9 extends to infinity and is unbounded.
    [1]
Answer
(i) x ≥ 1, y ≥ x, 2x + y ≤ 9; (ii) maximum Z = 8 at (1, 7); (iii) change 2x + y ≤ 9 to 2x + y ≥ 9
Marking note Another correct answer to (iii) is to drop the constraint 2x + y ≤ 9 altogether. Note that (0, 9) and (9/2, 0) are only the axis intercepts of the third line; they are not corners of the shaded region.

Concepts to recall

  • To write a constraint from a graph: find the equation of the boundary line, then take a test point (the origin, if the line misses it) to choose ≤ or ≥.
  • Corner-point method: for a bounded feasible region the extreme value of Z is at a corner point.
  • A feasible region is unbounded when it is not enclosed by the constraints, e.g. when the only upper limit is removed.
FOUR-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2026 Mathematics (860), Q22
Question 22 · Board 2026[4]
Section C of the old paper pattern: the alternative to Section B, not counted in the 80 marks.
Raunak is a small-scale entrepreneur who sells sewing machines in a rural market. He wants to expand his business but has two main constraints: Capital and Storage. He has a total capital of ₹5,760 to invest. The godown can store a maximum number of 20 sewing machines. Raunak sells two types of machines: Electronic sewing machine, each costs him ₹360; Manually operated sewing machine, each costs him ₹240. His wife Radhika suggests selling an electronic machine at a profit of ₹22 and a manually operated sewing machine at a profit of ₹18. Using the concept of Linear Programming Problem, find the number of sewing machines of each type that Raunak should sell to maximise his profit.
  1. Let x electronic and y manual machines be bought and sold. Maximise Z = 22x + 18y subject to 360x + 240y ≤ 5760 (i.e. 3x + 2y ≤ 48), x + y ≤ 20, x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0.
    [1]
  2. Draw the lines 3x + 2y = 48 (through (16, 0), (0, 24)) and x + y = 20 (through (20, 0), (0, 20)); the feasible region is the shaded quadrilateral OBCD in the first quadrant (bounded).
    [1]
  3. Corner points: O(0, 0); B(16, 0); D(0, 20); C is where 3x + 2y = 48 meets x + y = 20: subtract 2 × (second) to get x = 8, y = 12, so C(8, 12).
    [1]
  4. Z(O) = 0, Z(B) = 22·16 = 352, Z(D) = 18·20 = 360, Z(C) = 22·8 + 18·12 = 176 + 216 = 392. The maximum is ₹392 at C(8, 12): he should sell 8 electronic and 12 manually operated machines.
    [1]
Answer
8 electronic and 12 manual machines; maximum profit ₹392

Concepts to recall

  • LPP: write the objective and constraints, plot the feasible region, evaluate Z at each corner point of a bounded region; the extreme value is at a corner (Corner-Point Method).
  • Simplify constraints before plotting: 360x + 240y ≤ 5760 divides by 120 into 3x + 2y ≤ 48.
  • Machines are whole numbers; the optimum (8, 12) happens to be an integer point, so no rounding is needed.
FOUR-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC Specimen 2026 Mathematics, Q22
Question 22 · Specimen 2026[4]
Section C of the old paper pattern: the alternative to Section B, not counted in the 80 marks.
(i) A linear programming problem is given by Z = px + qy where p, q > 0, subject to the constraints x + y ≤ 60, 5x + y ≤ 100, x ≥ 0 and y ≥ 0. (a) Solve graphically to find the corner points of the feasible region. (b) If Z = px + qy is maximum at (0, 60) and (10, 50), find the relation between p and q. Also mention the number of optimal solutions in this case.
OR
(ii) The feasible region for an L.P.P. is shown in the figure (lines through D(0, 50) and A(25, 0), and through C(0, 20) and E(40, 0); the shaded region OABC). (a) Write the constraints for the L.P.P. (b) Find the co-ordinates of the point B. (c) Find the maximum value of the objective function Z = x + y.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) Draw x + y = 60 (intercepts (60, 0), (0, 60)) and 5x + y = 100 (intercepts (20, 0), (0, 100)). Both constraints are ≤ and contain the origin, so the feasible region lies on the origin’s side of each line in the first quadrant: the bounded polygon OCBA.
    [1]
  2. Corner points: O(0, 0); C(20, 0) on the x-axis; A(0, 60) on the y-axis; B where the lines meet: subtract, 4x = 40, x = 10, y = 50, so B(10, 50).
    [1]
  3. (b) Z has the same (maximum) value at A and B: Z(0, 60) = 60q and Z(10, 50) = 10p + 50q.
    [1]
  4. 60q = 10p + 50q ⇒ 10q = 10p ⇒ p = q. The objective line is then parallel to the side AB, so Z is maximum at every point of the segment AB: infinitely many optimal solutions.
    [1]
Answer
(a) O(0, 0), C(20, 0), B(10, 50), A(0, 60); (b) p = q, infinitely many optimal solutions
Marking note The key writes the equality as “0·p + 60·p = 10·p + 50·q”; the first 60p is a slip for 60q, and the conclusion p = q stands.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) Line DA: x/25 + y/50 = 1 ⇒ 2x + y = 50. Line CE: x/40 + y/20 = 1 ⇒ x + 2y = 40. The origin is in the shaded region and satisfies 0 ≤ 50 and 0 ≤ 40, so the constraints are 2x + y ≤ 50, x + 2y ≤ 40, x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0.
    [1]
  2. (b) B is the intersection of 2x + y = 50 and x + 2y = 40. Multiply the first by 2: 4x + 2y = 100; subtract the second: 3x = 60, x = 20, y = 10. B = (20, 10).
    [1]
  3. (c) Corner points of the region: O(0, 0), A(25, 0), B(20, 10), C(0, 20). Z = x + y: Z(O) = 0, Z(A) = 25, Z(B) = 30, Z(C) = 20.
    [1]
  4. The maximum value of Z is 30, at B(20, 10).
    [1]
Answer
(a) 2x + y ≤ 50, x + 2y ≤ 40, x, y ≥ 0; (b) B(20, 10); (c) maximum Z = 30

Concepts to recall

  • Corner-point method: the optimum of a linear objective over a bounded feasible region is at a corner point.
  • If the objective has the same maximum value at two adjacent corners, it has that value at every point of the edge joining them: infinitely many optimal solutions.
  • Equal values at A and B means the objective line px + qy = Z is parallel to AB (slope −1), which is why p = q.
  • Intercept form x/a + y/b = 1 gives the line’s equation from its two intercepts; test the origin to choose ≤ or ≥.
  • The corner point B is where two boundary lines meet: solve the two equations simultaneously.
  • Evaluate Z at every corner of the bounded region; the largest value is the maximum.

Question 22 · Board 2025 Imp.[4]
Section C of the old paper pattern: the alternative to Section B, not counted in the 80 marks.
A cooperative society of farmers has 50 hectares of land to grow two crops, X and Y. The profits from crops X and Y per hectare are estimated as ₹ 10500 and ₹ 9000 respectively. To control weeds, a liquid herbicide has to be used on crops X and Y in the quantity of 20 litres and 10 litres per hectare respectively. Further, the quantity of herbicide to be used on both the crops should not exceed 800 litres to protect the environment. How much land should be allocated to each crop to maximise the total profit of the society? Formulate the above Linear Programming Problem mathematically and then solve it graphically.
  1. Let x hectares be used for X and y hectares for Y. Maximise Z = 10500x + 9000y subject to x + y ≤ 50 (land), 20x + 10y ≤ 800, i.e. 2x + y ≤ 80 (herbicide), x, y ≥ 0.
    [1½]
  2. Graph: the lines x + y = 50 and 2x + y = 80 meet at P(30, 20). The feasible region is the bounded polygon OAPB with O(0, 0), A(40, 0), P(30, 20), B(0, 50) (the point (40, 0) is where 2x + y = 80 meets the x-axis, (0, 50) where x + y = 50 meets the y-axis). Figure: [1]
    [1]
  3. Corner values of Z: O: 0; A(40, 0): 420 000; P(30, 20): 315 000 + 180 000 = 495 000; B(0, 50): 450 000.
    [1]
  4. Maximum profit ₹ 4 95 000 at P(30, 20): 30 hectares of crop X and 20 hectares of crop Y.
    [½]
Answer
30 hectares of X and 20 hectares of Y; maximum profit ₹ 4,95,000.
Marking note Intersections: x + y = 50 and 2x + y = 80 give x = 30, y = 20.

Concepts to recall

  • LPP: objective function, linear constraints and non-negativity; the optimum of a bounded feasible region lies at a corner point (corner point method).
  • Formulation: one variable per decision (hectares of each crop), one inequality per limited resource (land, herbicide). The herbicide constraint may be simplified by dividing by 10.
  • Evaluate Z at every corner point; the corner where two constraint lines meet is found by solving them together.

Question 22 · Board 2025[4]
Section C of the old paper pattern: the alternative to Section B, not counted in the 80 marks.
Two different types of books have to be stacked in the shelf of a library. The first type of book weighs 1 kg and has a thickness of 6 cm. The second type of book weighs 1·5 kg and has a thickness of 4 cm. The shelf is 96 cm long and can support a maximum weight of 21 kg. How should both the types of books be placed in the shelf to include the maximum number of books? Formulate a Linear Programming Problem and solve it graphically.
  1. Let there be x books of the first type and y of the second. Maximise Z = x + y (number of books).
    [½]
  2. Constraints: length 6x + 4y ≤ 96, i.e. 3x + 2y ≤ 48; weight x + 1·5y ≤ 21, i.e. 2x + 3y ≤ 42; x, y ≥ 0.
    [1]
  3. Draw the lines 3x + 2y = 48 (through (16, 0), (0, 24)) and 2x + 3y = 42 (through (21, 0), (0, 14)). They meet where 9x + 6y = 144 and 4x + 6y = 84, i.e. 5x = 60: P(12, 6). The feasible region is OAPC with corners O(0, 0), A(16, 0), P(12, 6), C(0, 14).
    [1½]
  4. Z at the corners: O 0; A 16; P 12 + 6 = 18; C 14.
    [½]
  5. Maximum Z = 18 at P(12, 6): 12 books of the first type and 6 of the second (length 72 + 24 = 96 cm, weight 12 + 9 = 21 kg: both limits just used).
    [½]
Answer
12 books of the first type and 6 of the second: maximum number of books = 18.
Marking note The corner (12, 6) is integer-valued, so no rounding argument is needed.

Concepts to recall

  • LPP steps: define variables, write the objective and constraints (with x, y ≥ 0), draw the feasible region, evaluate Z at every corner, choose the optimum.
  • A bounded feasible region always has its optimum at a corner point (corner-point theorem).
  • Convert units consistently: here cm for thickness, kg for weight; 1·5y may be cleared by × 2.

Question 22 · Specimen 2025[4]
Section C of the old paper pattern: the alternative to Section B, not counted in the 80 marks.
A linear programming problem is given by Z = px + qy where p, q > 0 subject to the constraints x + y ≤ 60, 5x + y ≤ 100, x ≥ 0 and y ≥ 0.
(i) Solve graphically to find the corner points of the feasible region. [2]
(ii) If Z = px + qy is maximum at (0, 60) and (10, 50), find the relation of p and q. Also mention the number of optimal solution(s) in this case. [2]
  1. (i) Draw x + y = 60 (through (60, 0), (0, 60)) and 5x + y = 100 (through (20, 0), (0, 100)); the feasible region is the bounded polygon in the first quadrant under both lines.
    [1]
  2. They meet where (5x + y) − (x + y) = 40 ⇒ x = 10, y = 50. Corner points: O(0, 0), C(20, 0), B(10, 50), A(0, 60).
    [1]
  3. (ii) Z is maximum at both A(0, 60) and B(10, 50), so the values are equal: 0·p + 60q = 10p + 50q ⇒ 10q = 10p ⇒ p = q.
    [1]
  4. With p = q, Z = p(x + y) is parallel to the edge AB (x + y = 60): the maximum 60p is attained at every point of the segment AB, so there are infinitely many optimal solutions.
    [1]
Answer
(i) O(0, 0), C(20, 0), B(10, 50), A(0, 60); (ii) p = q; infinitely many optimal solutions

Concepts to recall

  • Corner-point method: the optimum of a bounded region is at a corner; if it occurs at two adjacent corners it occurs at every point of the edge joining them.
  • Equal objective values at two corners give the relation of the coefficients.
  • Check Z at the other corners: Z(C) = 20p < 60p, so A and B really are the maximum.

Question 22 · Board 2024[4]
Section C of the old paper pattern: the alternative to Section B, not counted in the 80 marks.
Aman has ₹ 1500 to purchase rice and wheat for his grocery shop. Each sack of rice and wheat costs ₹ 180 and ₹ 120 respectively. He can store a maximum number of 10 bags in his shop. He will earn a profit of ₹ 11 per bag of rice and ₹ 9 per bag of wheat. (i) Formulate a Linear Programming Problem to maximise Aman’s profit. (ii) Calculate the maximum profit.
  1. (i) Let Aman buy x bags of rice and y bags of wheat. Maximise Z = 11x + 9y subject to 180x + 120y ≤ 1500 (i.e. 3x + 2y ≤ 25), x + y ≤ 10, x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0.
    [2]
  2. (ii) Corner points of the feasible region: O(0, 0), A(25/3, 0), P(5, 5) [3x + 2y = 25 meets x + y = 10], B(0, 10).
    [1]
  3. Z: O 0; A 11(25/3) = 91·67; P 55 + 45 = 100; B 90. Maximum profit = ₹ 100 at (5, 5).
    [1]
Answer
Maximise Z = 11x + 9y subject to 3x + 2y ≤ 25, x + y ≤ 10, x, y ≥ 0; maximum profit ₹ 100 (5 bags of rice, 5 of wheat).

Concepts to recall

  • LPP: decision variables, objective function, constraints (budget, storage), non-negativity.
  • Corner point method: the optimum of a linear objective on a bounded feasible region is at a vertex.
  • Bags must be whole numbers; the optimum (5, 5) is integral, so no rounding is needed (A is not integral and gives less anyway).
