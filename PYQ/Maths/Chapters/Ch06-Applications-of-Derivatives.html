Papers 2024–2026 and specimens 2025, 2026 ask Section A (65) and then Section B or Section C (15); Section B is the counted choice, so every paper’s counted marks add to 80. Where a question offers an OR alternative from another chapter, the alternative sits in its own chapter and is not counted. 6 OR alternative(s) in this chapter come from questions whose main part is in another chapter.

What to revise

  • Tangents and normals (2 marks): tangent parallel to the x-axis or to a chord (use the Mean Value idea), tangent at the origin, normal at a point.
  • Rates of change: kite on a string, balloon, popcorn tub, 1/x + 1/y = 1/12, water draining from a cone (specimen 2027).
  • Increasing and decreasing: intervals, f′(x) > 0 read from a graph, a for which f is increasing on ℝ; and maxima/minima with the second-derivative test (f″(0) = 0 for y = x4 needs the first-derivative test).
  • Optimisation (6 marks): window of maximum light, cone inscribed in a sphere, closed cylinder of given volume, ATM locations on a cubic hill path. Name the variable, form one equation, differentiate, and prove it is a maximum.

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (9)ISC Specimen 2027 Mathematics, Q1(iv)
Question 1(iv) · Specimen 2027[1]
A particle moves along the curve 6y = x3 + 2. At what point(s) on the curve is the y-coordinate changing 8 times as fast as the x-coordinate?
(a)(2, 5/3) only
(b)(4, 11) only
(c)(4, 11) and (−4, −31/3)
(d)(8, 86) and (−8, −85)
  1. Differentiate w.r.t. time t: 6 dy/dt = 3x2 dx/dt. Put dy/dt = 8 dx/dt: 48 = 3x2, so x2 = 16 and x = ±4.
    [½]
  2. x = 4 gives y = 66/6 = 11; x = −4 gives y = (−64 + 2)/6 = −31/3. Points: (4, 11) and (−4, −31/3).
    [½]
Answer
(c) (4, 11) and (−4, −31/3)
Common slip Stopping at x = 4 and choosing (b); x2 = 16 has the root −4 as well.

Concepts to recall

  • Rates of change: dy/dt = (dy/dx)(dx/dt); “y changes 8 times as fast as x” means dy/dt = 8 dx/dt, i.e. dy/dx = 8.
  • A square equation gives two roots (±); both must be tested against the curve and both are valid here.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (9)ISC Specimen 2027 Mathematics, Q1(v)
Question 1(v) · Specimen 2027[1]
Statement I: If a satellite’s altitude function h(t) has a positive derivative (h′(t) > 0), the satellite is moving away from the Earth.
Statement II: At the apogee (highest point), the instantaneous velocity (dh/dt) of the satellite relative to its altitude is zero.
Which of the following is correct?
(a)Statement I is true and Statement II is false.
(b)Statements I is false and Statement II is true.
(c)Both the statements are true.
(d)Both the statements are false.
  1. Statement I: h′(t) > 0 means the altitude increases with time, so the satellite is moving away from the Earth: true.
    [½]
  2. Statement II: at the highest point the altitude is a maximum, where dh/dt = 0: true. Both statements are true.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Both the statements are true.

Concepts to recall

  • Sign of the derivative: h′ > 0 means increasing, h′ < 0 means decreasing.
  • At a local maximum of a differentiable function the derivative is zero (and changes sign from + to −).
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (9)ISC Specimen 2027 Mathematics, Q1(vi)
Question 1(vi) · Specimen 2027[1]
For what value(s) of k do the tangents of the two curves x = y2 and xy = k cut at right angles?
(a)k = ±1/4
(b)k = ±1
(c)k = 0
(d)k = ±1/(2√2)
  1. x = y2 gives dy/dx = 1/(2y); xy = k gives x dy/dx + y = 0, so dy/dx = −y/x.
    [½]
  2. At right angles the product of the slopes is −1: (1/(2y))(−y/x) = −1/(2x) = −1, so x = ½ and y2 = ½. Then k2 = x2y2 = ¼ · ½ = 1/8, so k = ±1/(2√2).
    [½]
Answer
(d) k = ±1/(2√2)

Concepts to recall

  • Two curves cut at right angles at a point if the product of their slopes there is −1.
  • Work with the common point: the same (x, y) satisfies both curves, which fixes x and y and then k = xy.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (9)ISC Specimen 2027 Mathematics, Q1(vii)
Question 1(vii) · Specimen 2027[1]
Evaluate the nature of the point (0, 0) for the curve y = x4, given that f″(0) = 0.
(a)The function at the point (0, 0) is undefined because the second derivative test fails.
(b)It is a point of local maximum because the first derivative is changing its sign from positive to negative as x increases through 0.
(c)It is a local minimum because the second derivative is positive for all x ≠ 0.
(d)It is a point of neither local maximum nor local minimum because the second derivative is zero.
  1. y′ = 4x3 = 0 at x = 0, and y″ = 12x2 = 0 at x = 0: the second-derivative test is inconclusive there, but the function is perfectly defined, so (a) is false.
    [½]
  2. y″ = 12x2 > 0 for all x ≠ 0 (equivalently y′ changes from negative to positive at 0), so the curve is concave up on both sides and (0, 0) is a local minimum: option (c).
    [½]
Answer
(c) It is a local minimum because the second derivative is positive for all x ≠ 0.
Marking note CISCE’s key argues only from the sign of f″ for x ≠ 0; the clean reason is the first-derivative test (4x3 changes sign from − to +). Both lead to option (c). “f″ is zero so it is neither” (d) is the classic wrong idea: a zero second derivative proves nothing.

Concepts to recall

  • Second-derivative test: f′(c) = 0 and f″(c) > 0 gives a minimum; if f″(c) = 0 the test fails and the first-derivative test decides.
  • First-derivative test: f′ changes sign − to + at c ⇒ local minimum. Here y′ = 4x3 is negative for x < 0 and positive for x > 0.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (9)ISC 2026 Mathematics (860), Q1(viii)
Question 1(viii) · Board 2026[1]
Observe the graph given below and answer the question that follows.
Statement 1: f(x) increases in (−∞, −1) and (1, ∞)
Statement 2: f(x) decreases in (−∞, 0) and (1, ∞)
Which one of the following is correct?
(a)Statement 1 is true and Statement 2 is false.
(b)Statement 2 is true and Statement 1 is false.
(c)Both the statements are true.
(d)Both the statements are false.
  1. Read the graph (y = |x2 − 1|): it falls on (−∞, −1), rises on (−1, 0), falls on (0, 1) and rises on (1, ∞).
    [½]
  2. Statement 1 claims f increases on (−∞, −1): it decreases there, so Statement 1 is false. Statement 2 claims it decreases on (−∞, 0) and (1, ∞): it increases on (−1, 0) and on (1, ∞), so Statement 2 is false. Both are false.
    [½]
Answer
(d) Both the statements are false.
Marking note Statement 1 would be true if it read “(−1, 0) and (1, ∞)”; as printed, (−∞, −1) is a decreasing stretch.

Concepts to recall

  • Increasing: the graph rises as x increases (f′ > 0); decreasing: it falls (f′ < 0). Read left to right.
  • A statement about a union of intervals is true only if it holds on every interval in it.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (9)ISC 2025 Mathematics (860), Q1(iii)
Question 1(iii) · Board 2025[1]
Consider the graph of the function f(x) shown (redrawn below). Statement 1: The function f(x) is increasing in (½, 2). Statement 2: The function f(x) is strictly increasing in (½, 1). Which is correct?
(a)Statement 1 is true and Statement 2 is false.
(b)Statement 2 is true and Statement 1 is false.
(c)Both the statements are true.
(d)Both the statements are false.
  1. From the graph, f rises from 0 at x = ½ to 1 at x = 1, and is constant (= 1) on [1, 2].
    [½]
  2. “Increasing” means x1 < x2 ⇒ f(x1) ≤ f(x2); a flat piece is allowed, so Statement 1 is true. On (½, 1) the values rise strictly, so Statement 2 is true. Both are true.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Both the statements are true.
Common slip Rejecting Statement 1 because of the flat part on (1, 2): in the ISC/NCERT definition a flat part does not stop a function being increasing.

Concepts to recall

  • Increasing (non-decreasing): x1 < x2 ⇒ f(x1) ≤ f(x2). Strictly increasing: f(x1) < f(x2).
  • A constant stretch is increasing but not strictly increasing.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (9)ISC 2025 Mathematics (860), Q1(vii)
Question 1(vii) · Board 2025[1]
A cylindrical popcorn tub of radius 10 cm is being filled with popcorns at the rate of 314 cm3 per minute. The level of the popcorns in the tub is increasing at the rate of:
(a)1 cm/minute
(b)0·1 cm/minute
(c)1·1 cm/minute
(d)0·5 cm/minute
  1. V = πr2h with r = 10 constant, so dV/dt = π(10)2 dh/dt = 100π dh/dt.
    [½]
  2. dh/dt = 314/(100 × 3·14) = 1 cm/min.
    [½]
Answer
(a) 1 cm/minute
Marking note π = 3·14 is the value that makes 314 divisible; with π = 22/7 the answer is 1·0001 cm/min, still option (a).

Concepts to recall

  • Related rates: write the quantity (volume) in terms of the variable, differentiate with respect to t by the chain rule.
  • Only the quantities that change are differentiated: here h changes, r does not.

Question 1(ix) · Board 2024[1]
In which one of the following intervals is the function f(x) = x3 − 12x increasing?
(a)(−2, 2)
(b)(−∞, −2) ∪ (2, ∞)
(c)(−2, ∞)
(d)(−∞, 2)
  1. f′(x) = 3x2 − 12 = 3(x − 2)(x + 2).
    [½]
  2. f′ > 0 when x < −2 or x > 2, so f is increasing on (−∞, −2) ∪ (2, ∞).
    [½]
Answer
(b) (−∞, −2) ∪ (2, ∞)

  • f′(x) > 0 on an interval ⇒ f increasing there; f′ < 0 ⇒ decreasing.

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (9)ISC 2024 Mathematics (860), Q1(xv)
Question 1(xv) · Board 2024[1]
Given that 1y + 1x = 112 and y decreases at a rate of 1 cm s−1, find the rate of change of x when x = 5 cm and y = 1 cm.
  1. Differentiate with respect to time: −(1/y2) dy/dt − (1/x2) dx/dt = 0, so dx/dt = −(x2/y2) dy/dt.
    [½]
  2. With dy/dt = −1, x = 5, y = 1: dx/dt = −(25)(−1) = 25 cm s−1 (x increases).
    [½]
Answer
25 cm s−1 (increasing)
Marking note The given values do not satisfy 1/x + 1/y = 1/12 (1/5 + 1 ≠ 1/12), a misprint in the paper. The answer depends only on the differentiated relation, so 25 cm s−1 is the intended answer.

Concepts to recall

  • Related rates: differentiate the relation with respect to t by the chain rule, then substitute.
  • “Decreases at 1 cm s−1” means dy/dt = −1.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (9)ISC 2026 Mathematics (860), Q6
Question 6 · Board 2026[2]
(i) The surface of a spherical balloon is increasing at the rate of 4 cm2/sec. Find the rate of change of volume when its radius is 12 cm.
OR
(ii) Find the equation of the normal at (1, 2) to the curve x2 = 4y.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. S = 4πr2 ⇒ dS/dt = 8πr dr/dt = 4, so dr/dt = 1/(2πr).
    [1]
  2. V = (4/3)πr3 ⇒ dV/dt = 4πr2 dr/dt = 4πr2/(2πr) = 2r. At r = 12: dV/dt = 24 cm3/sec.
    [1]
Answer
dV/dt = 24 cm3/s

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Misprint: the point (1, 2) does not lie on x2 = 4y (1 ≠ 8). The point meant is (2, 1) (coordinates written the other way round), and that reading is solved here.
  2. 2x = 4 dy/dx ⇒ dy/dx = x/2; slope of tangent at (2, 1) is 1.
    [½]
  3. Slope of normal = −1/(slope of tangent) = −1.
    [½]
  4. Normal: y − 1 = −1(x − 2), i.e. x + y − 3 = 0.
    [1]
Answer
x + y = 3 (at the point (2, 1))
Marking note As printed, (1, 2) is not on the curve. If the intended point was (1, 1/4) the normal is 8x + 4y − 9 = 0 (slope of normal −2). Award full marks for a correct method on any consistent reading, and credit a candidate who points out that the point is not on the curve.

Concepts to recall

  • Related rates: write the formula connecting the quantities, differentiate with respect to t using the chain rule, then substitute the instant’s value.
  • Sphere: S = 4πr2, V = (4/3)πr3; note dV/dt = (r/2)(dS/dt) always.
  • Slope of tangent = dy/dx at the point; slope of normal = −1/(dy/dx).
  • Equation of normal at (x1, y1): y − y1 = −(x − x1)/(dy/dx). Always check that the given point lies on the curve.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (9)ISC Specimen 2026 Mathematics, Q2 (OR alternative)
Question 2 · Specimen 2026 (OR)[2]
OR alternative of Question 2; not counted in the paper’s 80 marks.
(ii) Find the values of ‘a’ for which the function f(x) = b − ax + sin x is increasing on ℝ.
  1. f′(x) = −a + cos x. For f to be increasing on ℝ we need f′(x) ≥ 0 for every real x, i.e. cos x ≥ a for all x.
    [1]
  2. The smallest value of cos x is −1, so the condition holds for all x exactly when a ≤ −1 (at a = −1, f′ = 0 only at isolated points x = (2n+1)π, so f is still increasing).
    [1]
Answer
a ≤ −1, i.e. a ∈ (−∞, −1]

Concepts to recall

  • f is increasing on an interval if f′(x) ≥ 0 there (strictly increasing if f′ > 0).
  • A condition required for all x must hold at the worst case: here the minimum of cos x, which is −1.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (9)ISC Specimen 2026 Mathematics, Q3
Question 3 · Specimen 2026[2]
Find the point on the curve y = (x − 2)2 at which the tangent is parallel to the chord joining the end points (2, 0) and (4, 4).
  1. Slope of the chord = (4 − 0)/(4 − 2) = 2. Slope of the tangent at (x, y) is dy/dx = 2(x − 2).
    [1]
  2. Parallel: 2(x − 2) = 2 ⇒ x = 3, then y = (3 − 2)2 = 1. The point is (3, 1).
    [1]
Answer
The point is (3, 1)

Concepts to recall

  • Parallel lines have equal slopes. The slope of the tangent is the derivative; the slope of a chord is (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1).
  • This is the Mean Value Theorem: some point c in (2, 4) has f′(c) = [f(4) − f(2)]/(4 − 2); here c = 3 lies in (2, 4) as required.

(ii) If f(x) = log(1 + x) + 1/(1 + x), show that f(x) attains its minimum value at x = 0.

TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (9)CISCE Specimen 2025 Mathematics, Q4
Question 4 · Specimen 2025[2]
Find the equation to the tangent at (0, 0) on the curve y = 4x2 − 2x3.
  1. dy/dx = 8x − 6x2; the slope at (0, 0) is 0.
    [1]
  2. Tangent: y − 0 = 0(x − 0), i.e. y = 0 (the x-axis).
    [1]
Answer
y = 0 (tangent at the origin); the tangent drawn from the origin, which CISCE’s key solves for, is y = 2x
Marking note CISCE’s key works a different question, “the tangent drawn from the origin”: it lets the point of contact (x1, y1) vary and solves for it, getting (1, 2), i.e. the tangent y = 2x. FOUR-MARK QUESTIONS (3)ISC 2025 Improvement Mathematics (860), Q8 (OR alternative)
Question 8 · Board 2025 Imp. (OR)[4]
OR alternative of Question 8; not counted in the paper’s 80 marks.
(ii) In a particular forest, the population density (P) of wild animals (in thousands of animals per sq. km) at a distance ‘r’ km from the centre of the forest is approximately given by P = 10 + 20r − 10r2.
(a) What is the population density in the centre of the forest? (b) Calculate the rate of change of population density. (c) For what value of ‘r’ is the population density maximum? (d) Prove that the population density of animals varies with distance from the centre.
  1. (a) At the centre r = 0: P = 10, i.e. 10 thousand animals per sq. km.
    [1]
  2. (b) Rate of change of density with distance: dP/dr = 20 − 20r = 20(1 − r) thousand per sq. km per km.
    [1]
  3. (c) dP/dr = 0 gives r = 1; d2P/dr2 = −20 < 0, so P is maximum at r = 1 km, where P = 10 + 20 − 10 = 20 thousand per sq. km.
    [1]
  4. (d) dP/dr = 20(1 − r) is not identically zero: P increases for 0 < r < 1 (derivative > 0) and decreases for r > 1 (derivative < 0). So the density is not constant: it varies with the distance from the centre.
    [1]
Answer
(a) 10 thousand/km2; (b) dP/dr = 20 − 20r; (c) r = 1 km (maximum density 20 thousand/km2); (d) dP/dr ≠ 0 in general, so P varies.
Marking note Part (d) is worded "varies": the proof is that dP/dr is not identically zero (the sign change at r = 1 is a bonus).

Concepts to recall

  • Rate of change of a quantity = its derivative with respect to the variable.
  • Maxima: dP/dr = 0 and d2P/dr2 < 0 (second-derivative test). Completing the square: P = 20 − 10(r − 1)2.
  • A quantity varies if its derivative is not zero throughout; the sign of dP/dr tells whether it rises or falls.
FOUR-MARK QUESTIONS (3)ISC 2025 Mathematics (860), Q9 (OR alternative)
Question 9 · Board 2025 (OR)[4]
OR alternative of Question 9; not counted in the paper’s 80 marks.
(ii) The graph of f(x) = −x3 + 27x − 2 is given (redrawn below), with turning points A(a, b) and B(c, d). (a) Find the slope of the above graph. [1] (b) Find the co-ordinates of turning points, A and B. [2] (c) Evaluate f″(−2), f(0) and f′(3) and arrange them in ascending order. [1]
  1. (a) The slope of the graph at any point is f′(x) = −3x2 + 27 (for example 27 at the y-axis, x = 0).
    [1]
  2. (b) Turning points: f′(x) = 0 ⇒ x2 = 9, x = ±3.
    [1]
  3. f(−3) = 27 − 81 − 2 = −56 and f(3) = −27 + 81 − 2 = 52. So A = (−3, −56) (minimum, f″(−3) = 18 > 0) and B = (3, 52) (maximum, f″(3) = −18 < 0).
    [1]
  4. (c) f″(x) = −6x, so f″(−2) = 12; f(0) = −2; f′(3) = −27 + 27 = 0. Ascending order: f(0) = −2 < f′(3) = 0 < f″(−2) = 12.
    [1]
Answer
(a) f′(x) = 27 − 3x2; (b) A(−3, −56), B(3, 52); (c) f(0) < f′(3) < f″(−2), i.e. −2, 0, 12.
Marking note The printed graph labels the crossing of the axes (0, 0), but f(0) = −2 (part (c) asks for it), so the label is a drawing convention and the graph is not to scale. “The slope of the above graph” is taken as the slope function f′(x) (the graph is a curve, so its slope varies). An answer that gives the slope at the origin, 27, also earns the mark.

Concepts to recall

  • Slope of the tangent to y = f(x) at a point is f′(x); turning points are where f′ = 0.
  • f″ > 0 at a turning point: minimum; f″ < 0: maximum.
  • Read the notation carefully: f″(−2), f(0) and f′(3) are values of three different functions.

Question 9 · Specimen 2025[4]
(i) A kite is being pulled down by a string that goes through a ring on the ground 8 metres away from the person pulling it. If the string is pulled in at 1 metre per second, how fast is the kite coming down when it is 15 metres high?
  1. Let h m be the height of the kite and y m the length of string from the kite to the ring; the ring is 8 m (fixed) from the point below the kite: h2 + 82 = y2.
    [1]
  2. Differentiate w.r.t. time t: 2h dh/dt = 2y dy/dt ⇒ h dh/dt = y dy/dt.
    [1]
  3. When h = 15: y = √(225 + 64) = 17. The string is pulled in at 1 m/s: dy/dt = −1.
    [1]
  4. 15 dh/dt = 17(−1) ⇒ dh/dt = −17/15 m/s: the kite comes down at 17/15 m/s ≈ 1·13 m/s.
    [1]

The kite is coming down at 17/15 m/s (≈ 1·13 m/s)
Marking note CISCE’s key reaches 8·dx/dt = 17, i.e. dx/dt = 17/8, by differentiating with the 15 m kept constant and the 8 m allowed to vary. That is the speed of a horizontal distance, not of the kite coming down. Concepts to recall

  • Related rates: write one equation connecting the changing quantities, differentiate w.r.t. t, then substitute the instant’s values.
  • A quantity that is shrinking has a negative rate (dy/dt = −1); the answer asked is the speed, so state “coming down”.

Marking note The track is physical only for x ≥ 0, so “climbing” starts at x = 0. As a polynomial the function also increases on (−∞, 1).

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) tan α = r/h = 0·5, so r = h/2. V = 1/3πr2h = 1/3π(h/2)2h = πh3/12.
    [1]
  2. dV/dt = (πh2/4)(dh/dt) = −5. At h = 4: (π · 16/4) dh/dt = 4π dh/dt = −5, so dh/dt = −5/(4π) m/min: the level drops at 5/(4π) ≈ 0·40 m/min.
    [1]
  3. (b) Slant height l = √(r2 + h2) = (√5/2)h. Wetted (curved) surface S = πrl = π(h/2)(√5h/2) = (√5π/4)h2. dS/dh = (√5π/2)h > 0 for every depth h > 0, so S is strictly increasing.
    [1]
  4. (c) C = KS2 = K(5π2/16)h4. By the chain rule dC/dt = (dC/dh)(dh/dt) = (5Kπ2h3/4) · (−20/(πh2)) = −25πKh.
    [1]
  5. So the rate of change of cost is a linear function of h, with magnitude 25πKh: it is greatest in size at the greatest depth (at the start of draining, h = H, the full depth of the tank). The sign is negative: the cost falls as the tank drains.
    [1]
Answer
(a) the level drops at 5/(4π) m/min ≈ 0·40 m/min; (b) dS/dh = (√5π/2)h > 0; (c) dC/dt = −25πKh, largest in magnitude at the greatest depth
Marking note Part (c) is thin in the key. CISCE’s key writes dC/dt = 25Kπh (dropping the sign of dh/dt) and concludes only that the cost changes most rapidly “at the maximum possible depth”: no numerical depth is derivable because the full depth of the tank is not given (the figure’s h = 4 m is the depth at the instant of part (a)). Concepts to recall

  • f′ > 0 ⇒ increasing, f′ < 0 ⇒ decreasing: factorise f′ and test the sign in each interval.
  • Absolute extrema on a closed interval [a, b]: compare f at the end points and at the critical points inside.
  • Normal at a point: slope = −1/f′; when f′ = 0 the normal is vertical, x = x0 (not “slope = ∞”).
  • Related rates: express the volume in one variable using the geometry (r = h tan α), then differentiate w.r.t. time: dV/dt = (dV/dh)(dh/dt).
  • Curved surface of a cone = πrl, slant height l = √(r2 + h2). Wetted area is the curved surface (no top).
  • A function is strictly increasing when its derivative is positive on its whole domain.
  • A draining quantity has a negative rate: say so; the size of the rate is what “most rapidly” compares.

    [1]

(ii) A dolphin jumps and takes a path given by the equation h(t) = ½(−7t2 + 3t + 2), t ≥ 0, where h(t) is the height of the dolphin at any point of time. (a) Is the function differentiable for t ≥ 0?     [1]

    [1½]

    [1]
  3. dV/dh = ⅓π(4Rh − 3h2) = ⅓πh(4R − 3h) = 0 ⇒ h = 4R/3 (h ≠ 0).
    [1½]

    [1]

    [1]
Answer
Maximum volume at h = 4R/3, so h : diameter = 2 : 3 (proved)

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Let the radius of the semicircular ends be r and the length h. Volume fixed: V = ½πr2h ⇒ h = 2V/(πr2).
    [1]
  2. Total surface S = curved face πrh + rectangular base 2rh + two semicircular ends πr2 = πr2 + (π + 2)rh.
    [1]
  3. Substitute h: S = πr2 + 2(π + 2)V/(πr). dS/dr = 2πr − 2(π + 2)V/(πr2).
    [1]
  4. dS/dr = 0 ⇒ π2r3 = (π + 2)V = (π + 2)·½πr2h ⇒ 2πr = (π + 2)h.
    [1]

    [1]

    [1]

Minimum surface when h : 2r = π : π + 2 (proved)

Concepts to recall

  • Maxima / minima: express the quantity in one variable, set the first derivative to zero, test with the second derivative.
  • In a section through the axis, a right triangle with hypotenuse R links r, h and R: r2 = R2 − (h − R)2.
  • Choose the variable that makes the algebra short: using h avoids the product rule the key’s Method I needs.
  • Surface of a half-cylinder: ½(2πrh) curved + 2rh flat base + 2 × ½πr2 ends; volume ½πr2h.

  • Second-derivative test: positive ⇒ minimum.
