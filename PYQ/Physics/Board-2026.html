Question 1(A)(i)[1]
A fish called an electric eel can generate a voltage up to 800 V. If it can supply a current of 5 mA, the maximum power it can deliver is:
(a)4000 W
(b)400 W
(c)40 W
(d)4 W
  1. Maximum power P = VI = 800 V × 5 × 10−3 A = 4 W
    [1]
Answer
(d) 4 W
Common slip Leaving the current in mA gives 4000, option (a).

Concepts to recall

  • Electrical power P = VI = I2R = V2/R.
  • Convert units first: 5 mA = 5 × 10−3 A (the usual slip is to use 5 A).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(ii)
Question 1(A)(ii)[1]
A collimated beam of protons is travelling with a constant velocity along the X-axis. A current-carrying solenoid has its axis along the X-axis too. If the beam now enters the solenoid along its axis, it will:
(a)describe a circular path.
(b)get deflected along the Z-axis.
(c)get deflected along the Y-axis.
(d)continue to travel undeflected.
  1. Inside a long solenoid the field B is uniform and parallel to the axis, i.e. along the X-axis.
    [½]
  2. The protons move along the X-axis, so the angle between v and B is 0°. Force F = qvB sin 0° = 0, so the beam is not deflected.
    [½]
Answer
(d) continue to travel undeflected.

Concepts to recall

  • Magnetic force on a moving charge: F = q(v × B), magnitude qvB sinθ.
  • No force when v is parallel or antiparallel to B; a circle needs v ⊥ B; a helix when oblique.
  • Field inside a long solenoid is uniform and along its axis (B = μ0nI).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(iii)
Question 1(A)(iii)[1]
An electron, a proton, a deuteron and an alpha particle are all accelerated from rest through the same potential difference. Which one will have the maximum de Broglie wavelength?
(a)Proton
(b)Deuteron
(c)Electron
(d)Alpha particle
  1. Kinetic energy gained = qV, so momentum p = √(2mqV) and λ = h/p = h/√(2mqV).
    [½]
  2. For the same V, λ ∝ 1/√(mq). The electron has by far the smallest mass (9·1 × 10−31 kg, against about 10−27 kg) with the same magnitude of charge as the proton, so mq is least for the electron and its λ is greatest.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Electron

Concepts to recall

  • de Broglie wavelength λ = h/p; for a charge q accelerated through V: λ = h/√(2mqV).
  • Compare mq: electron mee; proton mpe; deuteron 2mpe; alpha 4mp(2e).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(iv)
Question 1(A)(iv)[1]
Magnetic susceptibility (χ) of a material is found to decrease with the rise in its temperature. The material is:
(a)diamagnetic.
(b)paramagnetic.
(c)ferromagnetic.
(d)not magnetic.
  1. Curie’s law for a paramagnetic material: χ = C/T, so χ falls as the temperature rises. Diamagnetic susceptibility is (nearly) independent of temperature.
    [1]
Answer
(b) paramagnetic.
Marking note The textbook pairing is “χ ∝ 1/T → paramagnetic”; a ferromagnet is not described by the simple Curie law.

Concepts to recall

  • Diamagnetic: χ small, negative, independent of T. Paramagnetic: χ small, positive, χ = C/T (Curie law). Ferromagnetic: χ large, follows χ = C/(T − Tc) above the Curie temperature.
  • A material whose χ varies as 1/T is the paramagnet.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(v)
Question 1(A)(v)[1]
When a p-n junction diode is forward-biased:
(a)it acts as an insulator and no current flows through it.
(b)it offers a low resistance and a large current flows through it.
(c)it offers a high resistance and a small current flows through it.
(d)the width of the depletion region increases.
  1. Forward bias (p to +, n to −) opposes the barrier potential, so the depletion region narrows, the resistance is low and a large current flows once the applied voltage exceeds the knee (cut-in) voltage.
    [1]
Answer
(b) it offers a low resistance and a large current flows through it.

Concepts to recall

  • Forward bias: depletion region narrows, low resistance, majority-carrier current. Reverse bias: depletion region widens, high resistance, tiny leakage current.
  • Knee voltage: about 0·7 V for Si, 0·3 V for Ge.

Question 1(A)(vi)[1]
When a biconvex lens of glass of refractive index 1·6 was dipped in a certain transparent liquid, it was found to behave like a plane sheet of glass. So, the refractive index of the liquid is:
(a)1·0
(b)1·4
(c)1·5
(d)1·6
  1. Lens-maker’s formula in a medium: 1/f = (gnl − 1)(1/R1 − 1/R2) with gnl = nglass/nliquid.
    [½]
  2. A plane sheet has no power, so 1/f = 0, which needs gnl = 1, i.e. nliquid = nglass = 1·6.
    [½]
Answer
(d) 1·6

Concepts to recall

  • 1/f = (n2/n1 − 1)(1/R1 − 1/R2), the lens of index n2 in a medium n1.
  • If nlens = nmedium the lens is invisible: no refraction, f → ∞.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(vii)
Question 1(A)(vii)[1]
Assertion: In Young’s double slit experiment, the first bright band for blue light is closer to the centre than the first bright band for green light.
Reason: Blue light has a longer wavelength than green light.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Assertion is false and Reason is true.
  1. Fringe width β = λD/d; the first bright band is at y = β ∝ λ.
    [½]
  2. Blue light has the shorter wavelength (about 450 nm against about 530 nm for green), so its first bright band is nearer the centre: the Assertion is true. The Reason says the opposite, so it is false.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Assertion is true and Reason is false.

Concepts to recall

  • Fringe width β = λD/d. Position of the nth bright fringe yn = nλD/d.
  • Order of wavelengths: violet/blue (short) < green < yellow < red (long).
  • For assertion-reason: judge each statement alone first, then ask whether the Reason explains the Assertion.

Question 1(B)(iii)[1]
Calculate the work done in moving a point charge ‘q’ through a distance ‘d’ along the perpendicular bisector of an electric dipole, which consists of two point charges −Q and +Q separated by a distance ‘L’.
  1. Every point on the perpendicular bisector is equidistant from +Q and −Q, so the potentials cancel: V = 0 at all its points (it is an equipotential).
    [½]
  2. Work W = q(Vfinal − Vinitial) = q(0 − 0) = 0.
    [½]
Answer
W = 0
Marking note Answer “zero” with the reason (equipotential) earns the mark; the numerical values of d, L and q are not needed.

  • Work done by an external agent between two points: W = qΔV.
  • The perpendicular bisector of a dipole is an equipotential at zero potential (the field there is parallel to p, i.e. perpendicular to the path).

SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q2
Question 2[2]
(i) Four point charges Q1 = +17·7 μC, Q2 = −8·85 μC, Q3 = −17·7 μC and Q4 = 35·4 μC are kept as shown in Figure 1. Calculate the electric flux emanating from the closed surface S.
OR
(ii) (a) Find the value of the current ‘I’ in Figure 2.
(b) State the conservation principle on which your calculation is based.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Gauss’s law: Φ = qenclosed/ε0. Only Q1 and Q2 lie inside S; Q3 and Q4 are outside and do not contribute.
    [½]
  2. qenc = 17·7 − 8·85 = 8·85 μC = 8·85 × 10−6 C
    [½]
  3. Φ = (8·85 × 10−6)/(8·85 × 10−12) = 1·0 × 106 N m2 C−1
    [1]
Answer
Φ = 1·0 × 106 N m2 C−1
Common slip Adding all four charges.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Right-hand junction: current entering = current leaving = 7 + 5 + 3 = 15 A, so 15 A flows in through the middle wire.
    [½]
  2. Left-hand junction (Kirchhoff’s current law): the 15 A leaves along the middle wire, so 8 + I = 15, i.e. I = 7 A (directed towards the junction).
    [½]
  3. (b) The calculation rests on conservation of electric charge (Kirchhoff’s first law: the algebraic sum of currents at a junction is zero).
    [1]
Answer
(a) I = 7 A, flowing into the junction; (b) conservation of charge (Kirchhoff’s junction rule).

Concepts to recall

  • Gauss’s law: Φ = ∮E·dS = qenc/ε0.
  • Charges outside the surface contribute zero net flux (what enters also leaves).
  • Use ε0 = 8·85 × 10−12 F m−1 from the constants table; unit N m2 C−1 or V m.
  • Kirchhoff’s current law: ΣIin = ΣIout at a junction (charge does not pile up).
  • Read arrow directions from the figure first; then pick a junction where only one current is unknown.

You are provided with three identical capacitors, each of capacitance ‘C’. If Cp is the equivalent capacitance when they are connected in parallel and Cs is the equivalent capacitance when they are connected in series, calculate the ratio Cs/Cp.
  1. Parallel: Cp = C + C + C = 3C
    [½]
  2. Series: 1/Cs = 1/C + 1/C + 1/C = 3/C, so Cs = C/3
    [1]
  3. Cs/Cp = (C/3)/(3C) = 1/9

SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q4
Question 4[2]
(i) What is the effect of doubling the current flowing through a metallic wire on: (a) the drift speed of free electrons? (b) the relaxation time of free electrons?
OR
(ii) Draw a labelled circuit diagram of a potentiometer used to compare the emfs of two given cells X and Y. (Procedure and formula are NOT required.)

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) I = neAvd: for the same wire (n, A unchanged), vd ∝ I, so the drift speed doubles.
    [1]
  2. (b) The relaxation time τ (mean time between collisions) depends on the material and temperature, not on the current, so it is unchanged (the random thermal speed is far larger than vd).
    [1]
Answer
(a) Drift speed doubles. (b) Relaxation time remains the same.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Primary circuit: driver cell E, plug key K and rheostat Rh in series with the uniform wire AB; the positive terminal of the driver cell is joined to A. Labelled.
    [1]
  2. Secondary circuit: positive terminals of cells X and Y joined to A; their negative terminals to terminals 1 and 3 of a two-way key; common terminal 2 through a galvanometer G and a high resistance R to the jockey J on the wire. Labelled.
    [1]
Answer
Circuit diagram as drawn.
Marking note Both circuits must be shown. Diagram marks are for the labelled diagram; omission of the driver-cell polarity or of the high series resistance costs ½.

Concepts to recall

  • I = neAvd; vd = (eE/m)τ; conductivity σ = ne2τ/m.
  • τ falls with rising temperature but is independent of the current (assuming heating is negligible).
  • Principle: potential drop across a wire length is proportional to the length (uniform wire, steady current): V ∝ l.
  • Comparison of emfs: E1/E2 = l1/l2 (balance lengths), provided the driver emf exceeds both.
  • Every cell must be connected with its + terminal to A; mark K, Rh, G, R, J.

(i) Write an expression of the Biot–Savart law in vector form.
(ii) State Ampère’s circuital law.
  1. (i) dB = (μ0/4π) I (dl × r)/r3, where dB is the field at a point at position vector r from the current element I dl.

  2. (ii) The line integral of the magnetic field B around any closed path equals μ0 times the total current threading the surface bounded by the path: ∮B·dl = μ0Ienc.

SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q6
Question 6[2]
A bi-convex lens of focal length f1 is placed in air as shown in Figure 3(a). The radii of curvature of its first and second surfaces are R and 2R respectively. The lens is cut along the plane CD. Compare the focal length f2 of the resulting lens shown in Figure 3(b) with that of the original lens shown in Figure 3(a).
  1. Original lens: R1 = +R, R2 = −2R: 1/f1 = (n − 1)(1/R + 1/2R) = (n − 1)(3/2R)
    [1]
  2. Cut lens (plano-convex, flat face and curved face of radius 2R): 1/f2 = (n − 1)(1/∞ + 1/2R) = (n − 1)/(2R). Dividing: f2/f1 = 3, i.e. f2 = 3f1.
    [1]
Answer
f2 = 3f1 (the focal length becomes three times as large)
Common slip Cutting across the axis does not just halve the power: the power of the retained curved face is that of one surface only, (n − 1)/2R.

Concepts to recall

  • Lens-maker’s formula: 1/f = (n − 1)(1/R1 − 1/R2), with the sign convention (distances measured from the lens, along the incident light as +).
  • For a plane surface R = ∞, and only the curved face contributes.
  • Cutting perpendicular to the axis changes the surfaces (new focal length); cutting along the axis leaves f unchanged (only the brightness drops).

SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q8
Question 8[2]
The work functions for metals M1 and M2 are 1·9 eV and 5·0 eV respectively. Perform necessary calculations to find out which metal emits photoelectrons when monochromatic light of wavelength 410 nm is incident on them.
  1. Photon energy E = hc/λ = (6·6 × 10−34 × 3 × 108)/(410 × 10−9) = 4·83 × 10-19 J
    [½]
  2. In eV: E = 4·83 × 10-19/(1·6 × 10−19) = 3·02 eV
    [½]
  3. Photoelectric emission needs E ≥ φ. For M1: 3·02 eV > 1·9 eV, so photoelectrons are emitted (Kmax = 3·02 − 1·9 = 1·12 eV).
    [½]
  4. For M2: 3·02 eV < 5·0 eV, so no emission.
    [½]
Answer
Only metal M1 emits photoelectrons (photon energy 3·02 eV exceeds 1·9 eV but not 5·0 eV).
Common slip Comparing a value in joules with a work function in eV.

Concepts to recall

  • Einstein: hν = φ + Kmax; emission only if ν > ν0 (i.e. E > φ).
  • E (eV) = 1240/λ (nm): 1240/410 = 3·02 eV, a quick check.
  • Use the constants table: h = 6·6 × 10−34 J s, 1 eV = 1·6 × 10−19 J.
SECTION C – 27 MARKSISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q9
Question 9[3]
Show that the intensity of the electric field at a point in end-on position, i.e. the axial position, of an electric dipole is given by
E = 14πε0 · 2pr(r2 − l2)2
where the terms have their usual meaning.
  1. Let the dipole consist of −q at A and +q at B, separated by 2l, with centre O. P is on the axis at distance r from O (r > l). Draw the figure.
    [1]
  2. Field of +q (at distance r − l): E1 = 14πε0q(r − l)2, directed along p (away from +q). Field of −q (at distance r + l): E2 = 14πε0q(r + l)2, directed towards −q, opposite to p.
    [1]
  3. Both lie on the axis, so E = E1 − E2 = q4πε0[1(r − l)2 − 1(r + l)2] = q4πε04rl(r2 − l2)2. With p = q(2l): E = 14πε02pr(r2 − l2)2, along p.
    [1]
Answer
Eaxial = (1/4πε0) · 2pr/(r2 − l2)2 → 2p/(4πε0r3) for r >> l.
Common slip Writing ‘q’ for the dipole moment, or forgetting that the nearer charge (+q) dominates.

Concepts to recall

  • Dipole moment p = q(2l), directed from −q to +q.
  • Axial: E = 2pr/[4πε0(r2 − l2)2] along p. Equatorial: E = p/[4πε0(r2 + l2)3/2], opposite to p.
  • Short dipole (r >> l): axial field = 2 × equatorial field.
  • Superposition: add the fields of the two charges as vectors.
SECTION C – 27 MARKSISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q10
Question 10[3]
(i) In the circuit shown in Figure 4, how much resistance should be connected to a 10 Ω resistor so that the points M and N are at the same potential?
OR
(ii) Figure 5 shows a battery consisting of three cells. Their emfs and internal resistances are also shown. Calculate: (a) the emf of the battery; (b) the internal resistance of the battery.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. M and N are at the same potential when the bridge is balanced: 3 Ω : 6 Ω = 4 Ω : Reff.
    [1]
  2. Reff = (6 × 4)/3 = 8 Ω. This is less than 10 Ω, so the extra resistor x goes in parallel with the 10 Ω resistor.
    [1]
  3. 1/8 = 1/10 + 1/x, so 1/x = 1/8 − 1/10 = 1/40 and x = 40 Ω.
    [1]
Answer
A 40 Ω resistor must be connected in parallel with the 10 Ω resistor.
Common slip Putting the new resistor in series: that can only increase the resistance above 10 Ω, but 8 Ω is needed, so it must be in parallel.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. 2 V, 1 Ω and 4 V, 3 Ω are in series and aid each other: E1 = 2 + 4 = 6 V, r1 = 1 + 3 = 4 Ω. This is in parallel with the 6 V, 12 Ω cell (E2, r2).
    [1]
  2. (a) For cells in parallel (same polarity): E = (E1r2 + E2r1)/(r1 + r2) = (6 × 12 + 6 × 4)/(4 + 12) = 96/16 = 6 V.
    [1]
  3. (b) r = r1r2/(r1 + r2) = (4 × 12)/16 = 3 Ω.
    [1]
Answer
(a) emf = 6 V; (b) internal resistance = 3 Ω.

Concepts to recall

  • No current flows between M and N when the potential drops in the two branches are in the same ratio: R1/R2 = R3/R4 (Wheatstone balance).
  • Parallel: 1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2 lowers the resistance, series raises it. Here 10 Ω must be reduced to 8 Ω, so parallel.
  • Cells in series (aiding): E = ΣE, r = Σr.
  • Two cells in parallel: E = (E1r2 + E2r1)/(r1 + r2), r = r1r2/(r1 + r2).
  • In the symbol the longer plate is positive; all three long plates face the left terminal.

(i) Obtain an expression for the magnetic field (B) at the centre of a circular coil having ‘n’ turns of radius ‘R’ when it is carrying a current ‘I’.

(ii) (a) Two moving coil galvanometers G1 and G2 are identical except for the following features:

  1. Consider an element dl of the coil. By the Biot–Savart law, at the centre O: dB = (μ0/4π) I dl sin 90°/R2, since dl ⊥ r and r = R for every element.
    [1]

    [1]

    [1]
Answer
B = μ0nI/2R

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) Current sensitivity Is = θ/I = NBA/k; B and k are the same for both, so Is ∝ NA.
    [1]
  2. NA for G1 = 50 × 30 = 1500 cm2; for G2 = 60 × 40 = 2400 cm2. So G2 has the greater current sensitivity (ratio 1·6). The coil resistance does not enter.
    [1]
  3. (b) The coil is wound on a soft-iron core and the magnet has concave (cylindrical) pole pieces, so the field lines are radial and the plane of the coil is always parallel to B.
    [1]
Answer
(a) G2 (NA = 2400 cm2 against 1500 cm2); (b) concave pole pieces with a soft-iron core.
Marking note Do not penalise a candidate who also computes voltage sensitivity (G1: 375, G2: 400 in the same units) provided the conclusion for current sensitivity is G2.

Concepts to recall

  • Biot–Savart law: dB = (μ0/4π) I dl sinθ/r2.
  • On the axis at distance x: B = μ0nIR2/[2(R2 + x2)3/2]; set x = 0 for the centre.
  • Right-hand thumb rule: curl the fingers along the current; the thumb gives B at the centre.
  • Deflection θ = NBAI/k. Current sensitivity NBA/k; voltage sensitivity NBA/(kR) — the resistance matters only for the latter.
  • Radial field: torque τ = NIAB stays constant as the coil turns, so θ ∝ I (linear scale).
  • Soft-iron core: concentrates the field and makes it radial.

    [1]

    [1]

    [1]
