What to revise

  • Worth 2 marks a year and always a recall question: name the wave (radar, X-ray, remote control, satellite, security scan) and one common property.
  • Learn: the spectrum in order of frequency with the uses and sources, c = νλ, transverse nature, same speed in vacuum.
  • Numerical: frequency from wavelength (e.g. 6 nm).

TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q6
Question 6 · Specimen 2027[2]
There are seven types of electromagnetic waves having different frequency ranges. Arrange them in the increasing order of their frequencies.
  1. Radio waves < microwaves < infrared < visible light
    [1]
  2. < ultraviolet < X-rays < gamma rays.
    [1]
Answer
Radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays (lowest to highest frequency).
Marking note Reverse order (decreasing wavelength) is not what was asked; the order must be from lowest to highest frequency.

Concepts to recall

  • All EM waves travel at c = 3 × 108 m s−1 in vacuum, so f ∝ 1/λ: frequency increases as wavelength falls.
  • Memory aid: “Radio Microwave Infrared Visible Ultraviolet X-ray Gamma”.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q7
Question 7 · Board 2026[2]
(i) Name the electromagnetic wave that scans the contents of the luggage of a traveller during a security check.
(ii) Which physical quantity is the same for UV rays, red light and radio waves when they are travelling in vacuum?
  1. (i) X-rays (high penetrating power; absorbed differently by materials of different density).
    [1]
  2. (ii) Their speed, c = 3 × 108 m s−1 (all electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed in vacuum).
    [1]
Answer
(i) X-rays. (ii) Speed in vacuum (3 × 108 m s−1).

Concepts to recall

  • EM spectrum in order of increasing frequency: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays.
  • All EM waves: c = 1/√(μ0ε0) in vacuum; they differ in wavelength and frequency, not speed.
  • X-rays: medical imaging, airport security, crystallography.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q7
Question 7 · Specimen 2026[2]
Name the electromagnetic wave used in: (a) radars    (b) crystallography
  1. (a) Radars use microwaves (short wavelength, about 1 mm to 30 cm, which can be beamed and reflected from aircraft, ships and rain).
    [1]
  2. (b) Crystallography uses X-rays: their wavelength (about 0·1 nm) is comparable with the spacing of atoms in a crystal, so the crystal diffracts them.
    [1]
Answer
(a) Microwaves; (b) X-rays.

Concepts to recall

  • Spectrum in order of decreasing wavelength: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays.
  • Uses: radio/TV communication; microwaves for radar and ovens; infrared for remote controls and heating; X-rays for medical imaging and crystal structure; gamma rays for cancer therapy.
  • Diffraction needs wavelength of the order of the spacing: X-rays and atoms.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q7
Question 7 · Board 2025 Imp.[2]
(Picture: a hand pointing a remote control at a television set.) (i) Name the electromagnetic wave emitted by the remote control operating the TV set.
(ii) State any one property common to all electromagnetic waves.
  1. (i) Infrared radiation (a pulse-coded infrared beam from an infrared LED).
    [1]
  2. (ii) Any one: all electromagnetic waves travel with the speed of light c = 3 × 108 m s−1 in vacuum; they are transverse waves; they are not deflected by electric or magnetic fields (they carry no charge); they carry energy and momentum; they need no medium.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Infrared waves; (ii) all travel at c in vacuum (or are transverse).

Concepts to recall

  • Order by wavelength: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays.
  • Common properties: transverse, speed c = 1/√(μ0ε0) in vacuum, E ⊥ B ⊥ direction of travel, E/B = c, uncharged.
  • Infrared: remote controls, night-vision, heating; produced by hot bodies and molecular vibrations.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q7
Question 7 · Board 2025[2]
A satellite sends signals to a dish antenna on the ground (see the image).
(i) Name the electromagnetic wave travelling from the satellite to the dish antenna shown in the image above.
(ii) If the wavelength of an electromagnetic wave is 6 nm, what is its frequency?
  1. (i) The signals are microwaves (wavelength about 1 mm to 30 cm, frequencies in the GHz range) used for satellite communication; they pass through the ionosphere and are received by the dish.
    [1]
  2. (ii) f = c/λ = (3 × 108)/(6 × 10−9)
    [½]
  3. = 5·0 × 1016 Hz.
    [½]
Answer
(i) Microwaves; (ii) f = 5·0 × 1016 Hz.
Marking note Only the 1 nm = 10−9 m conversion is needed; the wave in (ii) is a different wave from the one in (i).

Concepts to recall

  • c = fλ for all electromagnetic waves in vacuum (c = 3 × 108 m s−1).
  • Uses: radio waves (broadcast), microwaves (radar, satellite links, ovens), infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, γ-rays in order of increasing frequency.
  • A 6 nm wave lies in the X-ray / ultraviolet range, not the microwave range.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC Specimen 2025 Physics Paper 1, Q7
Question 7 · Specimen 2025[2]
(i) Name the electromagnetic radiation that has been used in obtaining the image of a chest (a chest radiograph is shown in the paper).
(ii) What is the wavelength range of electromagnetic radiation used in radio broadcast?
  1. (i) X-rays: they pass through soft tissue but are absorbed by bone, so a radiograph (shadow image of the ribs and spine) is obtained.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Radio waves, with wavelength greater than 1 m (CISCE’s key): FM broadcast is about 3 m, AM broadcast about 200–600 m.
    [1]
Answer
(i) X-rays; (ii) greater than 1 m.
Marking note The wavelength of radio waves is quoted differently in books (0·1 m upwards in NCERT, > 1 m in CISCE’s key); either is credited for broadcast bands, which lie from about 3 m (FM) to several hundred metres (AM).

Concepts to recall

  • Electromagnetic spectrum in order of increasing frequency: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, γ-rays; all travel at c in vacuum.
  • X-rays (0·01–10 nm): medical imaging, crystallography, airport scanners. Radio waves: communication; microwaves: radar and ovens.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q5
Question 5 · Board 2024[2]
(i) Name the electromagnetic radiation whose frequency is 1011 Hz.
(ii) What is the speed of radio waves in vacuum?
  1. (i) Wavelength λ = c/f = (3 × 108)/1011 = 0·003 m = 3 mm. This lies in the range of about 1 mm to 1 m (109 to 1012 Hz): microwaves.
    [1]
  2. (ii) All electromagnetic waves, radio waves included, travel in vacuum with the same speed c = 3 × 108 m s−1.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Microwaves (λ = 3 mm). (ii) 3 × 108 m s−1.

Concepts to recall

  • c = fλ; EM spectrum by frequency: radio < 109 Hz < microwave (109–1012) < infrared < visible (~1014) < UV < X-ray < gamma.
  • In vacuum c = 1/√(μ0ε0) for all EM waves; they differ in λ and f only.
