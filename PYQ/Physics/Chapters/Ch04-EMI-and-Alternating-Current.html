ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (4)ISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii) · Board 2026[1]
What is motional emf?
  1. The emf induced in a conductor of length l because it moves with velocity v in a magnetic field B (cutting the field lines), given by e = Blv when B, l and v are mutually perpendicular.
    [1]
Answer
Motional emf is the emf induced across a conductor moving in a magnetic field; e = Blv.

Concepts to recall

  • Magnetic force on the free electrons of a moving rod, qvB, separates charge until E = vB, giving emf Blv.
  • Direction from Fleming’s right-hand rule or Lenz’s law.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (4)ISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii) · Specimen 2026[1]
State one method to minimise flux loss in a transformer.
  1. Wind the primary and secondary coils one over the other (interleaving) so that nearly all the flux of the primary links the secondary; or use a core of high permeability (soft iron) which confines the flux.
    [1]
Answer
Winding primary and secondary on top of each other, on a high-permeability core.
Marking note Key: interleaved windings and a core of high permeability. A different loss (laminating the core) is a different answer and earns no mark here.

Concepts to recall

  • Flux leakage: not all of the primary’s flux links the secondary. Remedy: close coupling and a high-permeability closed core.
  • Do not confuse with eddy-current loss (laminated core), hysteresis loss (soft iron), copper loss (thick wire), humming (tight clamping).
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (4)ISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii) · Board 2025[1]
Figure 3 below shows an ideal transformer. Explain why current flowing through secondary coil is greater than that in primary coil.
  1. The figure shows fewer turns on the secondary than on the primary (Ns < Np): a step-down transformer. For an ideal transformer there is no power loss, VpIp = VsIs, and Vs/Vp = Ns/Np < 1. Hence Is = Ip(Np/Ns) > Ip: the voltage is stepped down, so the current is stepped up by the same factor.
    [1]
Answer
Ns < Np, so Vs < Vp and, since VpIp = VsIs, Is > Ip.

Concepts to recall

  • Ideal transformer: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np = Ip/Is; output power = input power.
  • Step-down: Ns < Np, lower voltage and higher current. Step-up: the opposite.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (4)ISC Specimen 2025 Physics Paper 1, Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii) · Specimen 2025[1]
Explain why core of a transformer is always laminated.
  1. The changing flux induces eddy currents in the iron core. A core built of thin insulated sheets (laminations) breaks up the paths of these currents and raises the resistance of each loop, so the eddy currents are small and the loss of energy as heat (I2R) is reduced.
    [1]
Answer
To reduce the loss of power (heat) due to eddy currents.

Concepts to recall

  • Eddy currents: induced circulating currents in a bulk conductor in a changing flux (Lenz’s law: they oppose the change).
  • Laminate the core to cut eddy-current loss; use soft iron (narrow hysteresis loop) to cut hysteresis loss; thick copper wire cuts the I2R loss in the windings.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (2)ISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q5
Question 5 · Specimen 2027[2]
Explain why a blue coloured spark appears in a switch contact when a circuit containing a load like a heater, geyser or an electric iron is switched off.
  1. When the switch is opened the current falls very rapidly. The wiring and load have self-inductance, so a large induced (back) emf e = −LdI/dt appears across the gap between the contacts.
    [1]
  2. This large emf produces a discharge through the air: the air molecules are ionised and the excited molecules emit light (blue) as they return to their ground states.
    [1]
Answer
A large self-induced emf on break causes an electric discharge in the air at the contacts; the ionised air gives off a blue light.
Marking note Mention of back emf / self-induction and of ionisation of air gets the two marks.

Concepts to recall

  • Self-induction: emf = −L dI/dt; it opposes the change of current, and dI/dt is very large when a circuit is broken suddenly.
  • Excited atoms or molecules emit light of definite colours when they return to lower levels.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (2)ISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q4
Question 4 · Board 2024[2]
(i) What type of transformer is used in a mobile phone charger?
(ii) Why is the core of a transformer made of soft iron and not of steel?
  1. (i) A step-down transformer: it reduces the 220/230 V mains to a few volts (about 5 V) before the rectifier in the charger.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Soft iron has high permeability and low retentivity with a narrow hysteresis loop, so the hysteresis (energy) loss per cycle is small and it is easily magnetised and demagnetised by the ac. Steel, with high retentivity and a wide loop, would waste much energy as heat.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Step-down transformer. (ii) Soft iron has a small hysteresis loss (low retentivity, high permeability); steel would lose much energy as heat.

Concepts to recall

  • Transformer: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np = Ip/Is (ideal). Step-down: Ns < Np.
  • Energy losses: copper loss (I2R), eddy currents (laminated core), hysteresis (soft iron), flux leakage.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (1)ISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q12 (OR alternative)
Question 12 · Board 2025 Imp. (OR)[3]
OR alternative of Question 12; not counted in the paper's 70 marks. Also draws on: motional emf on rails (induction); also uses the magnetic force on a moving charge from Ch 3.
(ii) Figure 9 below shows a metallic rod PQ resting on two parallel thick and smooth metallic rails CS and DR. There is a uniform magnetic field B which is perpendicular to the rails (into the page; PQ has length l and is at a distance x from RS and moves with velocity v to the right).
(a) How much magnetic flux is linked to the closed circuit PQRS?
(b) If the rod PQ is moved on the rails with a constant velocity v, how much emf will be induced in it?
(c) How much power has to be spent by an external agency to maintain the motion of the rod PQ on the rails? Write your answer in terms of B, l, v and resistance R of the rod PQ.
  1. (a) Area of loop PQRS = lx and B is perpendicular to it: Φ = Blx.
    [1]
  2. (b) |e| = dΦ/dt = Bl(dx/dt) = Blv (the rails have negligible resistance; direction by Lenz’s law).
    [1]
  3. (c) Current I = e/R = Blv/R; magnetic force on the rod F = BIl = B2l2v/R, opposing the motion. Power needed P = Fv = B2l2v2/R (equal to the heat I2R produced).
    [1]
Answer
(a) Φ = Blx; (b) e = Blv; (c) P = B2l2v2/R.
Common slip Giving the power as Blv × I but leaving I unexpressed; the question asks for it in terms of B, l, v and R.

Concepts to recall

  • Faraday’s law: e = −dΦ/dt; motional emf e = Blv for a rod moving perpendicular to B.
  • Force on a current-carrying rod F = BIl; Lenz’s law: it opposes the motion, so an external force is needed to keep v constant.
  • Energy conservation: mechanical power supplied = electrical power dissipated, Fv = I2R.
FIVE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q18
Question 18 · Specimen 2027[5]
(i) (a) The graph below shows variation of current (I) flowing through an electrical device with time (t), when an ac source e = 240 sin(ωt) V is connected to it.
(1) Identify the device. (resistor, capacitor or an inductor)
(2) Calculate the frequency of the current flowing through the device.
(3) How much is the opposition offered by this device?
(b) A train is moving on horizontal tracks that are 1·2 m apart. Its displacement (x) depends on time (t) as x(m) = 3t2 + 6. Vertical component of earth’s magnetic field in its region of motion is 2 × 10−5 T. Calculate the emf induced between two ends of its axle rod 10 s after the train starts moving.
OR
(ii) An ac generator generating an emf e = 300 sin(100πt) V is connected to a 100 Ω resistor.
(a) Draw a labelled graph of current (I) flowing through it versus time (t). Draw one cycle only.
(b) Calculate the average power consumed by the resistor.
(c) Why ac is preferred to dc, though it has varying values whereas the latter has a fixed value?
(d) State any one difference between a transformer used at the power generating station and the transformer in a city which is used to supply power to households.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a)(1) At t = 0, when the emf e = 240 sinωt is zero, the current is at its maximum (6 A): i = 6 cosωt leads the emf by 90°. Only a capacitor does this.
    [1]
  2. (2) The current repeats after T = 80 ms (maxima at 0 and 80 ms): f = 1/T = 1/(80 × 10−3 s) = 12·5 Hz.
    [1]
  3. (3) The opposition is the capacitive reactance XC = e0/i0 = 240/6 = 40 Ω.
    [1]
  4. (b) Speed v = dx/dt = 6t; at t = 10 s, v = 60 m s−1.
    [½]
  5. The axle (length l = 1·2 m) cuts the vertical field: e = Blv = 2 × 10−5 × 1·2 × 60 = 1·44 × 10−3 V (= 1·44 mV).
    [1½]
Answer
(a) capacitor; f = 12·5 Hz; XC = 40 Ω. (b) e = 1·44 × 10−3 V.
Common slip Reading the period as the time between the zero crossings (40 ms for a half-cycle) and getting 25 Hz; the period is between successive maxima, 80 ms.
Marking note The graph is read from the figure: current maximum +6 A at t = 0, zero near 20 ms, minimum at 40 ms, maximum again at 80 ms. The marks for the device include the reason (leading by 90°).

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) Peak current I0 = e0/R = 300/100 = 3 A; 2πf = 100π, so f = 50 Hz and T = 1/f = 0·02 s. Current and emf are in phase: I = 3 sin(100πt) A.
    [1]
  2. Graph: one cycle of a sine curve from 0 to 0·02 s, between +3 A and −3 A, axes labelled with the values (see figure).
    [1]
  3. (b) Average power ⟨P⟩ = I02R/2 = (32 × 100)/2 = 450 W.
    [1]
  4. (c) ac can be stepped up or down easily by a transformer (so it can be transmitted at high voltage with little loss), but dc cannot.
    [1]
  5. (d) The transformer at the power station is a step-up transformer; the one supplying households is a step-down transformer.
    [1]
Answer
(a) graph of 3 sin(100πt) A, T = 0·02 s; (b) ⟨P⟩ = 450 W; (c) ac can be stepped up/down by transformers; (d) step-up at the station, step-down in the city.
Marking note The key writes the period as “0·02 Hz”: the unit of T is the second. In the graph the axis values and one full cycle must be shown; the marks for (a) include the values I0 and T.

Concepts to recall

  • Phase in ac circuits: resistor, current in phase with e; capacitor, i leads e by 90°; inductor, i lags by 90°.
  • XC = 1/ωC = e0/i0; f = 1/T, ω = 2πf.
  • Motional emf e = Blv for B, l, v mutually perpendicular; for a train on a horizontal track it is the vertical component of the earth’s field that is cut.
  • Resistor in ac: I = (e0/R) sinωt, in phase; Irms = I0/√2; average power = Irms2R = I02R/2.
  • Transformer: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np; step-up (Ns > Np) before transmission to reduce I2R loss, step-down for the consumer.
FIVE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q18
Question 18 · Board 2026[5]
(i) (a) When a coil is connected to a 200 V dc supply, the current flowing through it is found to be 1 A. However, when it is connected to the 200 V, 50 Hz ac supply, the current is found to be 0·5 A. (1) Explain why the current flowing is less when the coil is connected to an ac supply. (2) Calculate the coefficient of self-inductance (L) of the coil.
(b) If the power factor in an ac circuit is 0·5, what is the phase difference between the voltage and the current in the circuit?
OR
(ii) (a) A 70 Ω resistor is connected to an ac source generating an emf ‘e’ given by e(V) = 495 sin(100πt). Calculate the rms value of the current (Irms) flowing through the resistor.
(b) A 20 cm length of a long iron-cored solenoid has 25 turns. If the area of cross-section of the solenoid is 1 × 10−4 m2, calculate its coefficient of self-inductance (L).
(c) State why soft iron is preferred to steel as a material for the core of a transformer.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a)(1) With dc only the resistance R of the coil opposes the steady current. With ac the changing current induces a back emf in the coil, which is represented by the inductive reactance XL = 2πfL; the impedance Z = √(R2 + XL2) is larger than R, so the current is less.
    [1]
  2. (a)(2) R = V/I = 200/1 = 200 Ω; Z = 200/0·5 = 400 Ω
    [1]
  3. XL = √(Z2 − R2) = √(4002 − 2002) = 346·4 Ω
    [1]
  4. L = XL/(2πf) = 346·4/(2 × 3·14 × 50) = 1·10 H
    [1]
  5. (b) cos φ = 0·5, so φ = 60° (voltage leads the current by 60° in an inductive circuit).
    [1]
Answer
(a)(2) L ≈ 1·10 H; (b) φ = 60°.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) Peak emf e0 = 495 V; I0 = 495/70 = 7·07 A; Irms = I0/√2 = 5 A
    [2]
  2. (b) L = μ0μrN2A/l = (4π × 10−7 × 3000 × 252 × 1 × 10−4)/0·20 = 1·18 × 10-3 H
    [2]
  3. (c) Soft iron has high permeability and low retentivity, so its hysteresis loss per cycle (area of the B–H loop) is small and it is easily magnetised and demagnetised; steel has large hysteresis loss.
    [1]
Answer
(a) Irms = 5·0 A; (b) L ≈ 1·18 × 10-3 H; (c) low hysteresis loss.
Marking note Numbers taken with π = 3·14 as in the constants table; π = 3·14159 gives the same value to three figures (1·18 × 10−3 H).

Concepts to recall

  • dc: V = IR. ac: Vrms = IrmsZ, Z = √(R2 + XL2), XL = ωL = 2πfL.
  • Power factor cos φ = R/Z (here 200/400 = 0·5, consistent with φ = 60°).
  • An inductor passes dc freely but chokes ac: back emf = −L dI/dt.
  • e = e0 sin ωt; erms = e0/√2, Irms = erms/R for a pure resistor.
  • Solenoid with core: L = μ0μrN2A/l (μr = 3000 from the constants table).
  • Transformer core: soft iron → low hysteresis loss, high permeability, low eddy-current loss when laminated.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) At resonance I0 = E/R; the same emf gives the tallest peak for the smallest R. X has the tallest peak: X used the least resistance (and Z the greatest).
    [1]
  2. (b) Q = ω0L/R (same L, same ω0): maximum for the least R, so Q is maximum for X (the sharpest curve).
    [1]
  3. (c) At resonance XL = XC, so the impedance Z = √[R2 + (XL − XC)2] = R: it is minimum and purely resistive; the current is maximum and in phase with the emf.
    [1]

Concepts to recall

  • Self-inductance: L = Φ/I; induced emf e = −L dI/dt; energy stored ½LI2; 1 H = 1 Wb A−1.
  • Inductive reactance XL = ωL = 2πfL; for an ideal coil Vrms = IrmsXL. It is zero for d.c.

  • Series LCR: Z = √[R2 + (XL − XC)2]; resonance f0 = 1/(2π√LC), where XL = XC, Z = R, current maximum, phase angle zero.
  • Quality factor Q = ω0L/R = 1/(ω0CR) = f0/bandwidth: sharper resonance for smaller R.
  • Capacitor: XC = 1/(2πfC), so current is proportional to frequency (d.c. blocked, high-frequency passed).

Question 18 · Board 2025 Imp.[5]
(i) A choke coil has a self-inductance of 0·5 H and a resistance of 50 Ω. It is connected to a 220 V, 50 Hz a.c. supply.
(a) Calculate rms value of the current flowing through the coil.
(b) What is the phase difference between the supply voltage and the current?
(c) What is the power factor for this circuit?
OR
(ii) (a) When current flowing through a solenoid increases from 0 to 10 A in 0·2 s, an emf of 20 V is induced in it. Calculate the coefficient of self-inductance (L) of the solenoid.
(b) An ideal step-up transformer is shown in Figure 11 below (primary 440 V, secondary 44000 V):
(1) Calculate its turns ratio.
(2) Which of the windings: primary or secondary, carries larger current? Give a reason for your answer.
(c) An ac source generates an emf ‘e’ given by e = 311 sin(100πt) V. Calculate rms value of the emf generated by the source.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) Inductive reactance XL = 2πfL = 2 × 3·14 × 50 × 0·5 = 157 Ω.
    [1]
  2. Impedance Z = √(R2 + XL2) = √(502 + 1572) = 164·8 Ω.
    [1]
  3. Irms = Vrms/Z = 220/164·8 = 1·34 A.
    [1]
  4. (b) tanφ = XL/R = 157/50 = 3·14, so φ = 72·3°: the voltage leads the current by this angle.
    [1]
  5. (c) Power factor cosφ = R/Z = 50/164·8 = 0·303.
    [1]
Answer
(a) Irms = 1·34 A; (b) φ = 72·3° (voltage leads); (c) cosφ = 0·303.
Common slip Dividing the voltage by R or by XL alone instead of by Z.
Marking note Taking π = 22/7 gives XL = 157·1 Ω and the same answers to three figures (1·33 A, 72·3°, 0·303).

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) |e| = L(dI/dt), so L = 20/(10/0·2) = 20/50 = 0·4 H.
    [1½]
  2. (b)(1) Ns/Np = Vs/Vp = 44 000/440 = 100 (turns ratio 1 : 100, primary : secondary).
    [1]
  3. (2) The primary carries the larger current: for an ideal transformer VpIp = VsIs, so Ip/Is = Vs/Vp = 100: a step-up transformer steps the current down.
    [1]
  4. (c) Peak emf e0 = 311 V; erms = e0/√2 = 311/1·414 = 220 V ≈ 220 V.
    [1½]
Answer
(a) L = 0·4 H; (b)(1) turns ratio 100; (2) the primary carries the larger current (100 times); (c) erms ≈ 220 V.
Common slip Taking the larger voltage side to carry the larger current, or quoting 311 V as the rms value.

Concepts to recall

  • Series L–R circuit: XL = ωL = 2πfL; Z = √(R2 + XL2); Irms = Vrms/Z.
  • Phase angle tanφ = XL/R; in an inductive circuit the voltage leads the current. Power factor cosφ = R/Z; mean power VrmsIrms cosφ.
  • A choke has a low power factor: it limits the ac current with little power loss.
  • Self-induction: e = −L(dI/dt); L in henry (V s A−1).
  • Ideal transformer: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np = Ip/Is; power in = power out. Step-up: Ns > Np.
  • Peak and rms: e = e0 sinωt, erms = e0/√2; the mains 220 V corresponds to 311 V peak.
FIVE-MARK QUESTIONS (7)ISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q18
Question 18 · Board 2025[5]
(i) (a) Figure 9 shows a metallic rod MN of length l = 3 m moving in an external uniform magnetic field, B = (1/π) T which is directed into the plane of this paper. If the position of the rod changes with time as x = πt, where x is in metre and t is in second, then: (1) Calculate the motional emf developed in the rod. (2) Name the law used to find the direction of induced current.
(b) When current flowing through a solenoid decreases from 15 A to 0 in 0·2 s, an emf of 30 V is induced in it. Calculate the coefficient of self-inductance of the solenoid.
OR
(ii) An alternating emf E = 5·0 sin(314t) V is applied to a circuit containing a resistor connected in series with an unknown component X. The current in the circuit is found to be I = 3·0 sin(314t − π/3) A.
(a) Identify the component X. (b) Calculate rms value (Irms) of the current flowing through the circuit. (c) Find the frequency of the source. (d) Calculate power factor. (e) Find the impedance (Z) of the circuit.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a)(1) The rod moves with speed v = dx/dt = π m s−1 (perpendicular to its length and to B); motional emf ε = Blv.
    [1]
  2. ε = (1/π) × 3 × π = 3 V.
    [1]
  3. (a)(2) The direction of the induced current is found by Lenz’s law (equivalently Fleming’s right-hand rule): here v × B points up the page, so the current in the rod flows from N to M and M is the positive end.
    [1]
  4. (b) Induced emf |ε| = L|dI/dt|, so L = ε/(dI/dt).
    [1]
  5. dI/dt = 15/0·2 = 75 A s−1, L = 30/75 = 0·4 H.
    [1]
Answer
(a)(1) ε = 3 V; (a)(2) Lenz’s law; (b) L = 0·4 H.
Marking note The printed figure labels the rod ends B and A, while the question text names the rod MN; the direction asked for is only the law. The sign of ε in (b) is dropped because only the magnitude is given.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) The current lags the emf by π/3 (the phase constant is −π/3). In a purely resistive circuit the phase difference is zero; current lags in an inductive circuit and leads in a capacitive one. So X is an inductor.
    [1]
  2. (b) Irms = I0/√2 = 3/√2 = 2·12 A.
    [1]
  3. (c) ω = 314 rad s−1 = 2πf, so f = 314/(2 × 3·14) = 50 Hz.
    [1]
  4. (d) Power factor = cos φ = cos(π/3) = 0·5.
    [1]
  5. (e) Z = E0/I0 = 5·0/3·0 = 1·67 Ω.
    [1]
Answer
(a) Inductor; (b) 2·12 A; (c) 50 Hz; (d) 0·5; (e) 1·67 Ω.
Marking note With π = 3·14 as in the constants table, f = 50 Hz exactly.

Concepts to recall

  • Motional emf: ε = Blv when v, B and the rod are mutually perpendicular; the speed is the time-derivative of the position.
  • Lenz’s law: the induced current opposes the change in flux that produces it (conservation of energy); Fleming’s right-hand rule: thumb v, forefinger B, middle finger current.
  • Self-inductance: ε = −L dI/dt; the henry is the induced emf per unit rate of change of current (1 V per A s−1).
  • Compare with E = E0 sin ωt, I = I0 sin(ωt − φ): φ > 0 means the current lags (inductive), φ < 0 means it leads (capacitive).
  • Irms = I0/√2; f = ω/2π; power factor cos φ = R/Z; Z = E0/I0 = Erms/Irms.
  • For this circuit R = Z cos φ = 0·83 Ω and XL = Z sin φ = 1·44 Ω.

Question 18 · Specimen 2025[5]
(i) The magnetic field through a single loop of wire, 12 cm in radius and 8·5 Ω resistance, changes with time as shown in the graph (Figure 9). The magnetic field is perpendicular to the plane of the loop.
(a) Find the induced emf for the time intervals 0 to 2·0 s, 2·0 to 4·0 s, and 4·0 to 6·0 s.

(ii) Three students, X, Y and Z performed an experiment for studying the variation of a.c. with frequency in a series LCR circuit and obtained the graphs as shown. They all used an AC source of the same emf and inductance of the same value.
(a) Who used minimum resistance?
(b) In which case will the quality Q factor be maximum?
(c) What did the students conclude about the nature of impedance at resonant frequency (f0)?
(d) An ideal capacitor is connected across 220 V, 50 Hz, and 220 V, 100 Hz supplies. Find the ratio of current flowing through it in the two cases.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) Faraday’s law for one turn: e = −dΦ/dt = −A (dB/dt), cos θ = 1 (B perpendicular to the plane).
    [½]
  2. Area A = πr2 = 3·14 × (0·12)2 = 0·0452 m2.
    [½]
  3. 0 – 2·0 s: dB/dt = (1·0 − 0)/2·0 = 0·5 T s−1, so e = −0·0452 × 0·5 = −0·0226 V
    [1]
  4. 2·0 – 4·0 s: B is constant (1·0 T), dB/dt = 0, so e = 0.
    [½]
  5. 4·0 – 6·0 s: dB/dt = (0 − 1·0)/2·0 = −0·5 T s−1, so e = +0·0226 V (opposite sense).
    [½]
  6. (b) I = e/R = 0·0226/8·5: I = −2·66 mA for 0–2 s, 0 for 2–4 s, +2·66 mA for 4–6 s (≈ 2·7 mA).
    [1]
  7. Graph: I (mA) against t (s): a horizontal step at −2·7 mA from 0 to 2 s, zero from 2 to 4 s and a step at +2·7 mA from 4 to 6 s; axes labelled with units.
    [1]
Answer
(a) −0·0226 V (≈ −0·023 V), 0, +0·0226 V; (b) I = −2·7 mA, 0, +2·7 mA in the three intervals (graph).
Marking note CISCE’s key (e = −0·023 V, i = −2·7 × 10−3 A, 0, +2·7 × 10−3 A) agrees. With the printed convention the first sign is negative; either sign convention is accepted as long as the two outer intervals have opposite signs. π is not in the constants table: 3·14 is used.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) At resonance I0 = V/R (same V): the tallest peak means the smallest R. X has the highest peak, so X used the minimum resistance (and Z the maximum).
    [1]
  2. (b) Q = ω0L/R = (1/R)√(L/C); with the same L and f0 the smallest R gives the largest Q: X (the sharpest, narrowest curve).
    [1]
  3. (c) At resonance XL = XC, so Z = √(R2 + (XL − XC)2) = R: the impedance is minimum and purely resistive (equal to the ohmic resistance); current is a maximum and in phase with the emf.
    [1]
  4. (d) For a capacitor XC = 1/2πfC, so I = V/XC = 2πfCV ∝ f for the same V and C.
    [1]
  5. I1/I2 = f1/f2 = 50/100 = 1 : 2.
    [1]
Answer
(a) X; (b) X; (c) Z = R, minimum, purely resistive; (d) I1:I2 = 1:2.

Concepts to recall

  • Faraday’s law: e = −NdΦ/dt; with the loop normal parallel to B, Φ = BA. A straight-line B–t graph gives a constant emf, its slope times A.
  • Lenz’s law: the minus sign: rising flux induces a current opposing the rise; falling flux the opposite. So the current reverses between the first and third intervals.
  • No change of flux (B constant), no emf, no current – the flat middle section is not a “maximum” of anything.
  • Series LCR: Z = √(R2 + (XL − XC)2); resonance at ω0 = 1/√(LC), f0 = 1/2π√(LC).
  • Quality factor Q = ω0L/R = ω0/(2Δω): sharpness of resonance; low R gives a tall, narrow peak.
  • XC = 1/ωC and XL = ωL: a capacitor passes more current at higher frequency, an inductor less.
