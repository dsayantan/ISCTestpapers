In an astronomical telescope of refracting type,
(a)Objective lens has smaller focal length, and eyepiece lens has greater focal length.
(b)Objective lens has greater focal length, and eyepiece lens has smaller focal length.
(c)Both objective lens and eyepiece lens have large focal length.
(d)Both objective lens and eyepiece lens have small focal length.
  1. Angular magnification in normal adjustment m = fo/fe, which must be large, so fo is large and fe is small.
    [1]
Answer
(b) Objective lens has greater focal length, and eyepiece lens has smaller focal length.

Concepts to recall

Question 1(A)(vii) · Specimen 2027[1]
Assertion: The focal length of a convex mirror will increase, if the mirror is placed in water.
Reason: The focal length of a convex mirror is equal to half its radius of curvature.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true, and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true, but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Assertion is false and Reason is true.
  1. Reason: for a spherical mirror f = R/2, which is true.
    [½]
  2. Reflection does not depend on the refractive index of the surrounding medium, so f = R/2 is unchanged in water: the Assertion is false.
    [½]
Answer
(d) Assertion is false and Reason is true.
Common slip Carrying over what happens to a lens in water (its focal length increases) to a mirror.

Concepts to recall

  • Mirror: f = R/2 in any medium, because only reflection is involved. A lens is different: its f depends on nlens/nmedium.
  • Assertion–reason: test each statement alone, then ask whether the reason explains the assertion.

Question 1(B)(iii) · Specimen 2027[1]
How can the focal length of a given convex lens be measured in Physics laboratory using only a metre scale?
  1. Distant object method: focus the image of a distant object (a tree or a window across the street) on a screen (wall or paper) by moving the lens; the parallel rays from the distant object meet at the focus.
    [½]
  2. Measure the distance between the lens and the sharp image with the metre scale: this is the focal length f.
    [½]
Answer
f = distance from lens to the sharp image of a distant object.
Marking note The 2027 key names the “Distant Object Method”; the explanation of the procedure is what earns the mark.

Concepts to recall

  • Rays from a distant object are parallel, so the image forms at the principal focus: v = f when u → ∞.
  • It is a rough value (error of a few mm); the u–v method or the displacement method is more accurate.

Question 1(A)(vi) · Board 2026[1]
When a biconvex lens of glass of refractive index 1·6 was dipped in a certain transparent liquid, it was found to behave like a plane sheet of glass. So, the refractive index of the liquid is:
(a)1·0
(b)1·4
(c)1·5
(d)1·6
  1. Lens-maker’s formula in a medium: 1/f = (gnl − 1)(1/R1 − 1/R2) with gnl = nglass/nliquid.
    [½]
  2. A plane sheet has no power, so 1/f = 0, which needs gnl = 1, i.e. nliquid = nglass = 1·6.
    [½]
Answer
(d) 1·6

Concepts to recall

  • 1/f = (n2/n1 − 1)(1/R1 − 1/R2), the lens of index n2 in a medium n1.
  • If nlens = nmedium the lens is invisible: no refraction, f → ∞.

Question 1(A)(vi) · Specimen 2026[1]
When a beam of white light is incident on a prism, the prism:
(a)only disperses the incident light.
(b)only deviates the incident light.
(c)deviates as well as disperses incident light.
(d)neither deviates nor disperses incident light.
  1. Refraction through a prism bends every colour towards the base, so the beam is deviated.
    [½]
  2. The refractive index differs for each wavelength (n larger for violet), so the colours are bent by different amounts and spread into a spectrum: it also disperses the light.
    [½]
Answer
(c) deviates as well as disperses incident light.

Concepts to recall

  • Deviation δ = (n − 1)A for a thin prism; δviolet > δred because nviolet > nred.
  • Dispersion is the splitting of white light into its colours because n depends on wavelength (Cauchy).

Question 1(B)(iii) · Specimen 2026[1]
Why are giant telescopes of reflecting type? Give any one scientific reason.
  1. Any one: images formed by a reflecting telescope are free from chromatic aberration (a mirror does not disperse light); or free from spherical aberration (a parabolic mirror); or brighter, because a large mirror can be made and supported from behind, so light-gathering power is high.
    [1]
Answer
A mirror gives images free from chromatic aberration (or spherical aberration / brighter images).
Marking note Any one of the three reasons printed in the key earns the mark.

Concepts to recall

  • Resolving power ∝ aperture D/λ; light gathered ∝ D2. A large lens sags under its weight and is supported only at its rim; a mirror is supported from behind.
  • Mirrors have no chromatic aberration and a parabolic mirror removes spherical aberration.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (20)ISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv) · Specimen 2026[1]
Give any one example where a ray of light travelling from one optical medium to another travels undeviated.
  1. A ray incident along the normal to the interface (angle of incidence 0°) passes into the second medium without change of direction; or when the two media have the same refractive index, μ1 = μ2.
    [1]
Answer
A ray incident normally on the surface of separation (or μ1 = μ2).

Concepts to recall

  • Snell’s law: μ1 sin i = μ2 sin r. No deviation if i = 0 (so r = 0) or μ1 = μ2 (so r = i).
  • The speed changes on entering the second medium but the direction need not.

Question 1(A)(vii) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
Given below are two statements marked, Assertion and Reason. Read the two statements and choose the correct option.
Assertion: When a beam of white light is passed through a prism, it gets split into its constituent colours.
Reason: Refractive index of glass is different for different wavelengths of light.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Both statements are true: white light splits into its colours (dispersion), and the refractive index of glass varies with wavelength (n larger for violet than for red, Cauchy: n = A + B/λ2).
    [½]
  2. Because n differs, the colours are refracted through different angles; this is precisely why the beam splits, so the Reason explains the Assertion.
    [½]
Answer
(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.

Concepts to recall

  • Dispersion: n depends on λ; nviolet > nred, so violet deviates most.
  • In a vacuum all colours travel at the same speed and do not disperse; dispersion occurs only in a material medium.

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (20)ISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
Figure 3 below shows an optical pin kept in front of a lens mirror combination. It is found that the final image formed by the lens mirror combination coincides with the object pin. Draw the ray diagram showing the formation of the final image (I).
  1. The final image coincides with the pin only if the rays return along their own paths. Diagram (a): the pin P is at the principal focus F of the convex lens L, so the rays from P emerge from the lens as a parallel beam, fall normally on the plane mirror M, are reflected straight back along the same paths and the lens converges them to P again: the final image I is at P.
    [½]
  2. Diagram (b), equally acceptable: P is at a distance for which the lens forms a real image I′ exactly on the mirror; M reflects the rays as if from a luminous point at I′, and the lens, by reversibility of rays, converges them back on P, so I coincides with P. (Labelled rays with arrows.)
    [½]
Answer
Ray diagram as drawn: rays retrace their paths and the final image I coincides with the pin P.
Marking note The question does not give the distances, so either construction earns the marks; the labelled diagram with arrows on the rays, the pin, lens and mirror marked, and I shown coinciding with P is what is credited.

Concepts to recall

  • Coincidence of image and object (no parallax) in the lens-mirror method: the rays must retrace their path, which happens when they fall normally on the mirror (pin at the focus of the lens, so the beam between lens and mirror is parallel).
  • Reversibility of light: a ray returned along its path goes back to its source; a plane mirror at the image I′ makes I′ a luminous object for the return journey.
  • In the laboratory the distance from the pin to the lens, when there is no parallax with its image, is the focal length of the lens.

Question 1(A)(vi) · Board 2025[1]
Given below are two statements marked, Assertion and Reason. Read the two statements and choose the correct option.
Assertion: When a convex lens made of glass is completely immersed in water, its focal length increases.
Reason: Refractive index of glass with respect to water is greater than that of glass with respect to air.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Lens-maker’s formula: 1/f = (mng − 1)(1/R1 − 1/R2), where mng = ng/nm is the index of glass relative to the surrounding medium.
    [½]
  2. Air: ang = 1·5. Water: wng = 1·5/1·33 = 1·13, which is smaller. So (n − 1) falls from 0·5 to about 0·13 and f becomes about four times larger: the Assertion is true. The Reason says the relative index is greater in water than in air; that is false.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Assertion is true and Reason is false.
Marking note With nglass = 1·5 and nwater = 1·33, fwater/fair = 0·5/0·13 ≈ 4.

Concepts to recall

  • Focal length of a lens in a medium: 1/f = (nlens/nmedium − 1)(1/R1 − 1/R2); the closer the two indices, the weaker the lens.
  • A lens with the same index as its surroundings has infinite focal length (it disappears).
  • Assertion–reason: check each statement on its own first, then whether the second explains the first.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (20)ISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii) · Board 2025[1]
State any one difference between a primary rainbow and a secondary rainbow.
  1. Any one: (1) A primary rainbow is formed by one internal reflection inside each drop; a secondary rainbow by two internal reflections. (2) In the primary the red is on the outer edge and violet on the inner; in the secondary the order is reversed (violet outer, red inner). (3) The primary is brighter; the secondary is fainter (more light lost in the extra reflection). (4) The primary subtends about 42° (red) at the observer’s eye, the secondary about 51°.
    [1]
Answer
Primary: one internal reflection, red outside; secondary: two internal reflections, violet outside, fainter.
Marking note Award the mark for any one correct distinction.

Concepts to recall

  • A rainbow is dispersion plus total internal reflection (internal reflection) inside raindrops; the observer has the Sun behind.
  • Each extra internal reflection reverses the colour order and costs intensity.

Question 1(B)(iv) · Board 2025[1]
Hubble telescope employs a large parabolic mirror as an objective. State any one advantage of using a mirror in place of a lens in such a telescope.
  1. Any one: a mirror has no chromatic aberration (reflection does not depend on wavelength); a parabolic mirror is free of spherical aberration; a large mirror can be supported from the back and is lighter than a large lens, and it has only one surface to be ground and polished, so a large aperture (more light, better resolution) is possible.
    [1]
Answer
No chromatic aberration (or: large aperture is easier to make and support; no spherical aberration with a parabolic shape).
Marking note Any one of the listed advantages earns the mark.

Concepts to recall

  • Reflecting telescope: objective is a concave (parabolic) mirror; resolving power ∝ aperture, so a large aperture is needed for faint, distant objects.
  • Lens defects: chromatic aberration (n depends on λ) and spherical aberration; mirrors do not show the first.

Assertion: An astronomical telescope has an objective lens having large focal length.
Reason: Magnifying power of an astronomical telescope varies directly with focal length of the objective lens.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (20)ISC Specimen 2025 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(vii)
Question 1(A)(vii) · Specimen 2025[1]
Assertion: If critical angle of glass–air pair (μg = 3/2) is θ1 and that of water–air pair (μw = 4/3) is θ2, then the critical angle for the water–glass pair will lie between θ1 and θ2.
Reason: A medium is optically denser if its refractive index is greater.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is false and Reason is true.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. sin θc = 1/μ for a medium against air: sin θ1 = 2/3, θ1 = 41·8°; sin θ2 = 3/4, θ2 = 48·6°.
    [½]
  2. For the water–glass pair (light going from glass to water): sin θc = μw/μg = (4/3)/(3/2) = 8/9, θc = 62·7°, which is greater than both, not between them: the Assertion is false. The Reason (denser = larger μ) is true.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Assertion is false and Reason is true.

Concepts to recall

  • Critical angle at the boundary from denser (1) to rarer (2): sin θc = μ2/μ1 = μrarer/μdenser.
  • Two media with nearly equal indices have a critical angle close to 90°; air against a medium gives a much smaller one.
  • Total internal reflection needs light going from the denser to the rarer medium and an angle of incidence above θc.

Question 1(A)(v) · Board 2024[1]
When a ray of white light is incident obliquely on the first surface of a prism, then:
(a)red colour is deviated most.
(b)green colour is deviated most.
(c)yellow colour is deviated most.
(d)violet colour is deviated most.
  1. Deviation δ = (n − 1)A for a thin prism, and in general increases with the refractive index.
    [½]
  2. Refractive index is greatest for the shortest wavelength (Cauchy: n = a + b/λ2), so violet (nv > nr) is deviated most and red least.

(d) violet colour is deviated most.

Question 6 · Board 2026[2]
A bi-convex lens of focal length f1 is placed in air as shown in Figure 3(a). The radii of curvature of its first and second surfaces are R and 2R respectively. The lens is cut along the plane CD. Compare the focal length f2 of the resulting lens shown in Figure 3(b) with that of the original lens shown in Figure 3(a).
  1. Original lens: R1 = +R, R2 = −2R: 1/f1 = (n − 1)(1/R + 1/2R) = (n − 1)(3/2R)
    [1]
  2. Cut lens (plano-convex, flat face and curved face of radius 2R): 1/f2 = (n − 1)(1/∞ + 1/2R) = (n − 1)/(2R). Dividing: f2/f1 = 3, i.e. f2 = 3f1.
    [1]
Answer
f2 = 3f1 (the focal length becomes three times as large)
Common slip Cutting across the axis does not just halve the power: the power of the retained curved face is that of one surface only, (n − 1)/2R.

Concepts to recall

  • Lens-maker’s formula: 1/f = (n − 1)(1/R1 − 1/R2), with the sign convention (distances measured from the lens, along the incident light as +).
  • For a plane surface R = ∞, and only the curved face contributes.
  • Cutting perpendicular to the axis changes the surfaces (new focal length); cutting along the axis leaves f unchanged (only the brightness drops).

Question 6 · Specimen 2026[2]
Two similar convex lenses are made up of two different materials as shown in Figures 7 and 8. Find the number of images formed in the following set-ups: (i) Figure 7   (ii) Figure 8

TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (4)ISC Specimen 2025 Physics Paper 1, Q6
Question 6 · Specimen 2025[2]
(i) The focal length of a double convex lens is equal to the radius of curvature of either surface. What is the refractive index of its material?
(ii) What is meant by a thin prism?
  1. (i) Lens-maker’s formula: 1/f = (n − 1)(1/R1 − 1/R2) with R1 = +R, R2 = −R and R = f:
    [½]
  2. 1/f = (n − 1)(1/f + 1/f) = 2(n − 1)/f, so n − 1 = 1/2 and n = 1·5.
    [½]
  3. (ii) A thin prism is a prism whose refracting angle is small (less than about 4°, i.e. a few degrees), so that the deviation δ = (n − 1)A for rays of small incidence.
    [1]
Answer
(i) n = 1·5; (ii) a prism of very small refracting angle (A < 4°).

Read the passage given below and answer the questions that follow.
There are two types of optical instruments: Microscopes and Telescopes. Microscopes are used to magnify very tiny objects whereas telescopes are used to study distant objects. Both of them deploy convex lenses. In his telescope, Newton used a large parabolic mirror to collect light from the stars and reduce aberrations.
(i) Rohit observed the launch of Chandrayan 3 with the help of an optical instrument. Name the instrument used by him.
(ii) State any one advantage of a reflecting telescope over a refracting telescope.
(iii) Which instrument is used to study the structure of a virus?
(iv) What is the ability of an optical instrument to form enlarged images called?
(v) What is the difference between a compound microscope and an astronomical telescope (refracting type), as far as their lenses are concerned?
  1. (i) A telescope (astronomical telescope): the launch is a distant object.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Any one: a mirror has no chromatic aberration; a large-aperture mirror can be made and supported from behind (a large lens would sag), giving brighter images and better resolution; a parabolic mirror removes spherical aberration; it is lighter and cheaper.
    [1]
  3. (iii) An electron microscope, because a virus (about 20–300 nm) is smaller than the wavelength of visible light, and electrons have a far smaller de Broglie wavelength.
    [1]
  4. (iv) Magnifying power (magnification) of the instrument.
    [1]
  5. (v) In a compound microscope the objective has a very short focal length and a small aperture, while the eyepiece has a larger focal length and aperture (fo < fe). In an astronomical telescope the objective has a large focal length and a large aperture, and the eyepiece a small focal length (fo > fe).
    [1]
Answer
(i) Telescope. (ii) No chromatic aberration (or larger aperture, brighter image). (iii) Electron microscope. (iv) Magnifying power. (v) Microscope: fo < fe; telescope: fo > fe.
Marking note For (iii) the passage mentions only optical microscopes; an answer “(compound) microscope” is likely to be accepted, but a virus is below the resolution limit of a light microscope, so electron microscope is the physically correct answer. For (i) both “telescope” and “astronomical telescope” score.

Concepts to recall

  • Compound microscope: m = mome = (vo/uo)(1 + D/fe); object just outside fo. Telescope: m = fo/fe in normal adjustment, L = fo + fe.
  • Resolving power of a telescope ∝ D/λ; of a microscope ∝ 2n sinθ/λ, so a smaller λ (electrons) resolves finer detail.
  • Reflecting telescope: Newtonian, Cassegrain; the primary is a concave (parabolic) mirror.
