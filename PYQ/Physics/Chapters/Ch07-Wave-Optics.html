ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(vii)
Question 1(A)(vii) · Board 2026[1]
Assertion: In Young’s double slit experiment, the first bright band for blue light is closer to the centre than the first bright band for green light.
Reason: Blue light has a longer wavelength than green light.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Assertion is false and Reason is true.
  1. Fringe width β = λD/d; the first bright band is at y = β ∝ λ.
    [½]
  2. Blue light has the shorter wavelength (about 450 nm against about 530 nm for green), so its first bright band is nearer the centre: the Assertion is true. The Reason says the opposite, so it is false.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Assertion is true and Reason is false.

Concepts to recall

  • Fringe width β = λD/d. Position of the nth bright fringe yn = nλD/d.
  • Order of wavelengths: violet/blue (short) < green < yellow < red (long).
  • For assertion-reason: judge each statement alone first, then ask whether the Reason explains the Assertion.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(vi)
Question 1(A)(vi) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
Given below are two statements marked, Assertion and Reason. Read the two statements and choose the correct option.
Assertion: Phase difference between two overlapping waves to produce a bright band 0 is 2π, 4π, 6π, 8π, …… (2m−1)π.
Reason: When crest of one wave falls on crest of another wave, amplitude of the resulting wave decreases.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Assertion: bright bands need constructive interference, phase difference 0, 2π, 4π, … = 2mπ. The general term printed, (2m−1)π, is an odd multiple of π, which gives dark bands. As written the Assertion is false.
    [½]
  2. Reason: crest on crest is constructive interference, so the amplitude increases (to a1 + a2); the Reason is false.
    [½]
Answer
(d) Both Assertion and Reason are false.
Marking note The printed series ends in (2m−1)π, which describes dark bands, so the Assertion is taken as false and the answer is (d). If the line is read as intending 2mπ it would be option (c); the Reason is false either way.

Concepts to recall

  • Constructive interference: Δφ = 2mπ (path difference mλ), amplitude a1 + a2. Destructive: Δφ = (2m−1)π (path difference (2m−1)λ/2), amplitude |a1 − a2|.
  • For assertion-reason questions test each statement on its own, down to the last term of a series.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(vii)
Question 1(A)(vii) · Board 2025[1]
Given below are two statements marked, Assertion and Reason. Read the two statements and choose the correct option.
Assertion: Diffraction of light is difficult to observe in everyday situations but can be observed in laboratory conditions.
Reason: To produce diffraction of waves, size of an obstacle must be comparable to the wavelength of the waves.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Reason: appreciable diffraction needs an obstacle or aperture of size comparable to λ; this is true. Light has λ of about 5 × 10−7 m, whereas everyday obstacles are enormously larger, so bending is not noticed; in the laboratory, slits of width of the order of λ are used. So the Assertion is true and the Reason is exactly why: (a).
    [1]
Answer
(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.

Concepts to recall

  • Diffraction is marked when the aperture width a is comparable to λ (a ∼ λ); the first minimum of a single slit is at sinθ = λ/a.
  • Ray optics holds when a ≫ λ; that is why light seems to travel in straight lines (and sound, with λ of metres, bends round corners).

Question 1(B)(v) · Board 2024[1]
What type of wavefronts are associated with a point source of light?
  1. A point source in an isotropic medium sends light equally in all directions, so all points at the same distance from it are in the same phase.
    [½]
  2. The wavefronts are spherical (concentric spheres centred on the source; they look like circles in a plane section).
    [½]
Answer
Spherical wavefronts.

Concepts to recall

  • Wavefront: locus of points in the same phase. Point source: spherical; line (slit) source: cylindrical; a distant source, or the emergent beam of a lens with the source at its focus: plane.
  • Rays are perpendicular to the wavefront.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (2)ISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q6
Question 6 · Board 2025 Imp.[2]
A parallel beam of monochromatic light of wavelength 450 nm is incident normally on a rectangular slit of width 0·9 μm. Calculate angular width of the central bright band.
  1. Single slit: first minimum at a sinθ = λ, so sinθ = λ/a = (450 × 10−9)/(0·9 × 10−6) = 0·5.
    [1]
  2. θ = 30°; angular width of the central band = 2θ = 60° (= 1·05 rad).
    [1]
Answer
Angular width 2θ = 60° ≈ 1·05 rad
Marking note With the small-angle formula 2λ/a the answer is 1 rad (57·3°); this is the answer many books give. Either value is accepted, but the exact one is 60° since θ = 30° is not a small angle.

Concepts to recall

  • Single-slit diffraction: minima at a sinθ = nλ; central maximum lies between the first minima on either side, angular width 2θ = 2λ/a (small angles).
  • Here λ/a = 0·5 is not small, so use sinθ = 0·5 exactly.
  • Linear width on a screen at distance D: 2λD/a.

Draw a labelled graph showing the variation in intensity of diffracted light with diffracting angle in a single slit Fraunhofer diffraction experiment.
  1. Axes labelled: intensity I on the vertical axis, diffraction angle θ (or sin θ) on the horizontal axis; a central maximum of greatest intensity at θ = 0 that is twice as wide as the others.
    [1]
  2. Subsidiary maxima on both sides, rapidly decreasing in intensity, separated by minima (zero intensity) marked at sin θ = ±λ/a, ±2λ/a, …
    [1]
Answer
Graph as drawn: a broad central maximum with weak, narrower secondary maxima on both sides.
Marking note Marks are for the labelled axes and the correct shape with the central peak dominating; the first minimum should be marked at λ/a.

THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q13
Question 13 · Specimen 2027[3]
A physics teacher drew a ray diagram on the board as shown below and asked the following questions to the students. State whether the answer of the student is correct or incorrect. Correct the incorrect response with an explanation in brief.
(i) Teacher: There is a point P on the screen where the path difference between two interfering light waves is exactly 1·2 × 10−6 m. If the light emitted by the source has a wavelength of 600 nm, can anyone tell me whether a bright or a dark fringe will form at the point P? Student: Dark fringe will be obtained.
(ii) Teacher: Two students performed the same Young’s double slit experiment. One student obtained wider fringes. What is the possible reason for this observation? Student: The student who obtained wider fringes must have increased the distance between the two slits.
(iii) Teacher: If one of the slits is closed with a finger while performing the experiment, what change will be noticed on the screen? Student: The screen will get illuminated uniformly.
  1. (i) Incorrect. Path difference / λ = (1·2 × 10−6)/(600 × 10−9) = 2, a whole number of wavelengths (nλ with n = 2): constructive interference, so a bright fringe forms at P.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Incorrect. Fringe width β = λD/d: wider fringes come from a decrease in the slit separation d (or an increase in D or λ). Increasing d makes the fringes narrower.
    [1]
  3. (iii) Incorrect. With one slit closed there is no interference; the single open slit gives a diffraction pattern on the screen (a broad central maximum with weaker secondary maxima), not a uniform illumination.
    [1]
Answer
(i) incorrect, bright fringe (path difference = 2λ); (ii) incorrect, d must have been decreased; (iii) incorrect, a diffraction pattern is seen.
Common slip Reading 2 as an even number and calling the fringe dark: dark needs a half-integral number of wavelengths.
Marking note In (ii) any of: decrease d, increase D, use longer wavelength; but the student’s statement (increase of d) is wrong.

Concepts to recall

  • Constructive interference: path difference = nλ (bright); destructive: (n + ½)λ (dark).
  • Fringe width β = λD/d (D slit–screen distance, d slit separation).
  • A single slit of width comparable to λ diffracts the light: central maximum twice as wide as the others and much brighter.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q15
Question 15 · Board 2026[3]
Kabir and Neha conducted experiments on light as part of their school project. Both worked with the same source of monochromatic yellow light. Both obtained patterns on the screen which consisted of alternate bright and dark bands. The images depict their results (Kabir’s: bands of equal width and spacing; Neha’s: a wide central band with narrower, unequal bands on either side).
(i) Identify the phenomenon observed by Neha.
(ii) The teacher asked Kabir to replace monochromatic yellow light with white light. What would be Kabir’s observation now?
(iii) What conclusion would be drawn by Kabir and Neha about the nature of light?
  1. (i) Neha’s pattern (central band widest, side bands narrower and fainter) is diffraction (single-slit diffraction); Kabir’s equally spaced bands are interference (Young’s double slit).
    [1]
  2. (ii) With white light Kabir would see a white central bright fringe with coloured fringes on both sides (violet nearest the centre, red farthest), and only a few such fringes are seen as they overlap further out.
    [1]
  3. (iii) Both patterns (interference and diffraction) show that light has a wave nature.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Diffraction. (ii) White central fringe flanked by coloured fringes (violet inner, red outer). (iii) Light behaves as a wave.

Concepts to recall

  • Young’s fringes: equal width β = λD/d. Single-slit diffraction: central maximum twice as wide as the others, width 2λD/a, intensity falls off sharply.
  • White light: each colour has its own β ∝ λ; at the centre all colours have zero path difference, hence white; fringes beyond are coloured.
  • Interference and diffraction are evidence for the wave theory; the photoelectric effect for the particle nature.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q16
Question 16 · Board 2026[3]
Using Huygens’ wave theory and a labelled diagram, show that the angle of reflection (r) is equal to the angle of incidence (i).
  1. Let a plane wavefront AB be incident on the reflecting surface MN, making angle i with it. While B travels to C in time t (BC = vt), the secondary wavelet from A grows into a sphere of radius AE = vt; the tangent CE from C is the reflected wavefront. Draw the labelled figure.
    [1]
  2. In right-angled triangles ABC and AEC: AC is common, BC = AE = vt and ∠ABC = ∠AEC = 90°; so the triangles are congruent (RHS) and ∠BAC = ∠ECA.
    [1]
  3. ∠BAC = i (the angle between the incident wavefront and the surface equals the angle of incidence) and ∠ECA = r likewise. Therefore i = r.
    [1]
Answer
Angle of reflection r = angle of incidence i.

Concepts to recall

  • Huygens’ principle: every point on a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets; the new wavefront is their forward envelope.
  • The angle between a wavefront and the surface equals the angle between the ray and the normal.
  • Same idea gives Snell’s law for refraction: sin i/sin r = v1/v2.
  • Reflection does not change the speed, so the wavelet radius is the same as BC.

THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q14
Question 14 · Specimen 2026[3]
In Young’s double slit experiment, show that the fringe width ω (fringe separation) is given by ω = λD/d, where the terms have their usual meaning.
  1. Slits S1, S2 a distance d apart, screen at distance D; P at height y from the central point O. Draw S1N perpendicular to S2P. Path difference S2P − S1P ≈ S2N = d sinθ ≈ d tanθ = dy/D (as θ is small and D ≫ d).
    [1]
  2. Bright fringe (constructive interference) when the path difference = nλ: dyn/D = nλ ⇒ yn = nλD/d, n = 0, 1, 2, …
    [1]
  3. Fringe width = distance between successive bright fringes: ω = yn − yn−1 = nλD/d − (n − 1)λD/d = λD/d. (Dark fringes at (2n − 1)λD/2d have the same spacing.)
    [1]
Answer
ω = λD/d
Marking note The key’s derivation: tanθ ≈ sinθ, ym/D = BN/AB = mλ/d, then ω = ym − ym−1. Same result.

Concepts to recall

  • Interference: bright when the path difference = nλ, dark when = (n + ½)λ; the sources must be coherent.
  • Small angles: sinθ ≈ tanθ ≈ θ (radian) = y/D.
  • Fringe width ω = λD/d: larger for red than blue, larger for smaller d, larger D. All fringes have equal width.

With the help of a labelled diagram and Huygen’s wave theory, prove: When a parallel beam of light is incident obliquely on a plane mirror, angle of reflection is equal to angle of incidence.
  1. Let MN be the mirror and AB a plane wavefront incident obliquely, with B reaching the mirror at C in time t after A touched it. The wave speed is v, so BC = vt. [labelled diagram: AB incident wavefront, rays, normals, AC mirror line, reflected wavefront CE]

THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q15
Question 15 · Board 2025 Imp.[3]
Shyam was performing a double slit experiment to determine wavelength of monochromatic light emitted by a laser. The examiner asked him some questions based on that experiment. Shyam answered those questions as per his understanding of the experiment. State whether his answers are correct. If incorrect, write the correct answer.
(i) Examiner: What is meant by fringe width? Shyam: It is the distance between centres of a bright band and the adjacent dark band.
(ii) Examiner: What happens to fringe width if blue light is replaced with red light? Shyam: Fringe width increases.
(iii) Examiner: What will happen to the interference pattern if one of the slits is closed? Shyam: The screen will be uniformly illuminated.
  1. (i) Incorrect. Fringe width β is the distance between the centres of two consecutive bright (or two consecutive dark) fringes, β = λD/d. The distance from a bright band to the adjacent dark band is β/2.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Correct. β ∝ λ and λred (about 700 nm) > λblue (about 450 nm), so the fringe width increases.
    [1]
  3. (iii) Incorrect. With one slit closed there is no interference: the screen shows the single-slit diffraction pattern of the open slit, a broad bright central maximum with weak secondary maxima on either side, so the illumination is not uniform.
    [1]
Answer
(i) incorrect (consecutive bright fringes); (ii) correct; (iii) incorrect (single-slit diffraction pattern).

Concepts to recall

  • Fringe width β = λD/d; bright and dark fringes are equally spaced, each of width β/2 for the bright-to-dark distance.
  • β ∝ λ, ∝ D, ∝ 1/d. Red > blue in wavelength, so red fringes are wider.
  • Closing a slit removes the second wave: no superposition, so the fringes vanish and diffraction by one slit remains.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q14
Question 14 · Board 2025[3]
A parallel beam of light is travelling obliquely from an optically rarer medium to an optically denser medium.
(i) Draw a labelled diagram showing incident and refracted wavefronts. Mark angle of incidence as ‘i’ and angle of refraction as ‘r’.
(ii) Use Huygen’s wave theory to prove Snell’s law.
  1. (i) Diagram (as drawn): plane wavefront AB incident on the surface XY (rays bend towards the normal in the denser medium); refracted wavefront CE with rays; i and r marked between the rays and the normal. Wavefronts, rays and both angles correctly labelled.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Let the wavefront AB meet the surface at A first. In the time t that the point B of the wavefront takes to reach C, BC = v1t; meanwhile the secondary wavelet from A spreads into medium 2 to a radius AE = v2t. The tangent CE from C to this wavelet is the refracted wavefront.
    [1]
  3. In right-angled triangles ABC and AEC (∠BAC = i, ∠ACE = r): sin i = BC/AC = v1t/AC and sin r = AE/AC = v2t/AC. Dividing, sin i/sin r = v1/v2 = n2/n1 = constant, which is Snell’s law.
    [1]
Answer
sin i/sin r = v1/v2 = n2/n1 (Snell’s law).

Concepts to recall

  • Huygens’ principle: every point of a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets; the new wavefront is the common tangent (envelope) to them after time t.
  • Refractive index n = c/v, so a denser medium (smaller v) bends the wavefront towards the normal: r < i.
  • The angle between a wavefront and the surface equals the angle between its ray and the normal (both pairs perpendicular): that is why ∠BAC = i.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q16
Question 16 · Board 2025[3]
Two identical rectangular slits 5 mm apart are illuminated with a monochromatic light of wavelength 600 nm. The screen is kept 1·2 m away from the slits.
(i) Calculate the distance between the 5th bright fringe (band) on one side and the 3rd bright fringe on the other side of the central bright band.
(ii) What will be the change in the interference pattern if the given light is replaced with monochromatic light of wavelength 500 nm?
  1. (i) Fringe width β = λD/d = (600 × 10−9 × 1·2)/(5 × 10−3) = 1·44 × 10-4 m (0·144 mm).
    [1]
  2. The nth bright fringe is at xn = nβ from the centre. On opposite sides the separation is (5 + 3)β = 8β = 1·15 × 10-3 m ≈ 1·15 mm.
    [1]
  3. (ii) β ∝ λ. With λ = 500 nm the fringe width decreases to 0·12 mm (by the factor 5/6): the fringes become narrower and closer together (more fringes in the same width of screen); the pattern is otherwise the same, with the central fringe at the same place.
    [1]
Answer
(i) 8β = 1·15 × 10-3 m ≈ 1·15 mm; (ii) the fringes become narrower (β falls to 0·12 mm).
Common slip Taking 5 − 3 = 2 fringe widths (that is for fringes on the same side).

Concepts to recall

  • Young’s double slit: fringe width β = λD/d; bright fringes at x = nλD/d, dark at (n + ½)λD/d.
  • β ∝ λ, β ∝ D, β ∝ 1/d; the central fringe is the zeroth, so the 5th on one side and the 3rd on the other are 8β apart.
  • Convert units first: nm to m, mm to m.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC Specimen 2025 Physics Paper 1, Q13
Question 13 · Specimen 2025[3]
With the help of a neatly drawn labelled diagram, prove the law of reflection on the basis of Huygens’ wave theory.
  1. Diagram: plane mirror MN, incident wavefront OA with the angle of incidence i marked, the reflected wavefront O′B with the angle of reflection r, rays with arrows, wavefronts perpendicular to the rays.
    [1]
  2. Let the wavefront OA touch the mirror at O at t = 0 and the ray through A reach the mirror at O′ after a time t. Then AO′ = ct (or vt). In the same time the secondary wavelet from O spreads to a radius OB = ct, and the common tangent from O′ to it is the reflected wavefront O′B.
    [1]
  3. In the right-angled triangles OAO′ and O′BO (right angles at A and B): OO′ is common and AO′ = OB = ct, so the triangles are congruent (RHS). Hence ∠AOO′ = ∠BO′O, i.e. i = r (these are also the angles the rays make with the normals). The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal lie in one plane.
    [1]
Answer
i = r: the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.
Marking note CISCE’s key (with its own figure) writes “AO′ = O′B = ct”, but O′B is the reflected wavefront, not a length ct; the second equal side is OB, the radius of the wavelet from O, as used here. The marks are for the figure (1), the construction (1) and the congruence (1); any equivalent derivation is accepted.

Concepts to recall

  • Huygens: every point of a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets; the new wavefront is the common tangent (envelope) to the wavelets after a time t.
  • A ray is perpendicular to the wavefront, so the angle between a wavefront and the mirror equals the angle between the ray and the normal.
  • Same method gives Snell’s law for refraction with the wavelet radii v1t and v2t: sin i/sin r = v1/v2 = n2/n1.

Question 15 · Specimen 2025[3]
In Young’s double slit experiment, how is interference pattern affected when the following changes are made:
(i) Slits are brought closer to each other.
(ii) Screen is moved away from the slits.
(iii) Red coloured light is replaced with blue coloured light.
  1. Fringe width β = λD/d (d = slit separation, D = slit-to-screen distance).
  2. (i) d decreases, so β increases: the fringes become wider and further apart.
    [1]
  3. (ii) D increases, so β increases: the fringes become wider (the pattern is larger and fainter).
    [1]
  4. (iii) λblue < λred (about 450 nm against 650 nm), so β decreases: the fringes become narrower, closer together.
    [1]
Answer
(i) fringe width increases; (ii) fringe width increases; (iii) fringe width decreases.
Marking note CISCE’s key is wrong for (ii). It prints “(i) increases, (ii) decreases, (iii) decreases”, but moving the screen away increases D and so increases β = λD/d. Parts (i) and (iii) agree with the key. Answer (ii) as “increases”; a candidate who follows the key is unlikely to be penalised, but the physics is as stated here.

Concepts to recall

  • Fringe width β = λD/d for double slit; position of the nth bright fringe yn = nλD/d.
  • Increase of D or λ, or decrease of d all widen the pattern.
  • The central (zero-order) fringe is always bright for white light, with coloured fringes on either side because β depends on λ.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q13
Question 13 · Board 2024[3]
(i) Using Huygens’ wave theory, show that (for refraction of light):
sin isin r = Constant
where terms have their usual meaning. You must draw a neat and labelled diagram.
OR
(ii) In Young’s double slit experiment, show that:
β = λDd
where the terms have their usual meaning.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Let a plane wavefront AB be incident on a surface separating medium 1 (speed v1) from a denser medium 2 (speed v2 < v1). A reaches the surface first. Neat labelled diagram showing AB, the surface, the wavelet from A, the refracted wavefront CE, ∠i and ∠r.
    [1]
  2. In the time t that B takes to reach C, BC = v1t, while the secondary wavelet from A travels AE = v2t in medium 2. CE, the tangent from C to this wavelet, is the refracted wavefront.
    [1]
  3. Because rays are perpendicular to wavefronts, ∠BAC = i and ∠ACE = r. In right triangles ABC and AEC: sin i = BC/AC = v1t/AC and sin r = AE/AC = v2t/AC. Dividing, sin i/sin r = v1/v2 = constant (= 1n2 = n2/n1), which is Snell’s law.
    [1]
Answer
sin i/sin r = v1/v2 = constant (Snell’s law).
Common slip Marking i and r between a ray and the surface instead of the normal: the angles are those between the rays and the normal (equivalently between the wavefront and the surface).

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. S1 and S2 are coherent slits a distance d apart; the screen is at a distance D (>> d). For a point P at a distance y from the centre O, S1P2 = D2 + (y − d/2)2 and S2P2 = D2 + (y + d/2)2. Neat diagram.
    [1]
  2. Subtracting, S2P2 − S1P2 = 2yd, so path difference S2P − S1P = 2yd/(S2P + S1P) ≈ yd/D (since S1P + S2P ≈ 2D).
    [1]
  3. Bright fringe (constructive): yd/D = nλ, so yn = nλD/d. Fringe width β = yn+1 − yn = λD/d. (Dark fringes at y = (n + ½)λD/d give the same spacing.)
    [1]
Answer
β = λD/d.
Common slip Using the path difference d sinθ and then forgetting the small-angle step sinθ ≈ tanθ = y/D.

Concepts to recall

  • Huygens’ principle: every point on a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets; the new wavefront is the forward envelope (common tangent) of the wavelets after time t.
  • Refractive index n = c/v; n2/n1 = v1/v2. Frequency is unchanged on refraction, λ changes.
  • The same method proves the law of reflection (i = r) with equal speeds.
  • Conditions: coherent sources (from one wavefront), narrow slits, d << D. Constructive: path difference = nλ; destructive: (n + ½)λ.
  • β = λD/d is the same for all fringes; it grows with λ and D and falls with d (red fringes wider than blue).
  • Intensity I = 4I0 cos2(φ/2) with φ the phase difference.
