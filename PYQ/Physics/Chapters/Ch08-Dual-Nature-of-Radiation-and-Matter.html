What to revise

  • Most papers have a stopping potential against frequency graph (slope h/e, intercept -φ/e, threshold frequency) and the effect of intensity and frequency on photocurrent and Vs.
  • de Broglie wavelength as a one-mark MCQ: λ ∝ 1/√(mqV) for the same potential difference; the ratio of K.E. for given λ.
  • Short: threshold frequency, work function, Davisson–Germer, photon momentum p = h/λ, which metal emits for a given wavelength.
  • Habit: convert eV and nm carefully (hc = 1240 eV nm).

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv) · Specimen 2027[1]
What is the difference between a moving metallic ball colliding with another ball in motion and a photon colliding with an electron?
  1. A photon either gives up its whole energy (is absorbed) or none of it, as energy is exchanged in quanta; a colliding ball can lose any fraction of its energy.
    [1]
Answer
A photon loses all its energy or none; a ball may lose any fraction.
Marking note Equivalent statements accepted, e.g. a photon in a collision with an electron is absorbed or scattered with changed frequency, whereas the ball keeps its identity and changes its speed.

Concepts to recall

  • Photon: energy E = hν, momentum p = h/λ, discrete (quantised) interactions — the photoelectric effect is an all-or-none event.
  • A photon is not slowed down by a collision: it always moves at c; only its energy and direction change.

Question 1(A)(iii) · Board 2026[1]
Also draws on: particles accelerated through a potential difference: de Broglie wavelength.
An electron, a proton, a deuteron and an alpha particle are all accelerated from rest through the same potential difference. Which one will have the maximum de Broglie wavelength?
(a)Proton
(b)Deuteron
(c)Electron
(d)Alpha particle
  1. Kinetic energy gained = qV, so momentum p = √(2mqV) and λ = h/p = h/√(2mqV).
    [½]
  2. For the same V, λ ∝ 1/√(mq). The electron has by far the smallest mass (9·1 × 10−31 kg, against about 10−27 kg) with the same magnitude of charge as the proton, so mq is least for the electron and its λ is greatest.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Electron

Concepts to recall

  • de Broglie wavelength λ = h/p; for a charge q accelerated through V: λ = h/√(2mqV).
  • Compare mq: electron mee; proton mpe; deuteron 2mpe; alpha 4mp(2e).

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(iv)
Question 1(A)(iv) · Specimen 2026[1]
The wavelength λe of an electron and λp of a photon of the same energy E are related by:
(a)λp ∝ √λe
(b)λp ∝ 1/√λe
(c)λp ∝ λe2
(d)λp ∝ λe
  1. Photon: E = hc/λp, so λp = hc/E.
    [½]
  2. Electron (kinetic energy E): E = p2/2m = h2/(2mλe2), so E ∝ 1/λe2. Hence λp ∝ 1/E ∝ λe2.
    [½]
Answer
(c) λp ∝ λe2

Concepts to recall

  • Photon: E = hf = hc/λ, λ ∝ 1/E. Matter wave: λ = h/p = h/√(2mE), λ ∝ 1/√E.
  • Same energy gives different wavelengths because the photon’s E–λ relation is linear and the electron’s is quadratic.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(B)(v)
Question 1(B)(v) · Specimen 2026[1]
Two charged particles having the same charge but different masses are passed through a potential difference V. When V is varied, the de Broglie wavelength (λ) of the particles varies as shown in the graphs below. Which graph is for the heavier particles and why?
  1. qV = p2/2m ⇒ λ = h/√(2mqV) = [h/√(2mq)] · (1/√V): a straight line through the origin of slope h/√(2mq).
    [½]
  2. For the same q, the slope is smaller for the larger mass, so line A (smaller slope) is for the heavier particles; B is the lighter.
    [½]
Answer
Graph A is for the heavier particles (smaller slope, since slope = h/√(2mq)).
Marking note CISCE’s key says graph B is for heavier particles because “slope of B > slope of A”, which reverses the relation it quotes. With slope = h/√(2mqV) written as h/√(2mq) per unit 1/√V, a larger slope means a smaller mass. The heavier particle is A. If a marker follows the printed key, the answer “B” may be credited; the physics gives A.

Concepts to recall

  • de Broglie: λ = h/p; for a charge accelerated through V: λ = h/√(2mqV).
  • Graph of λ against 1/√V is a straight line through the origin; slope ∝ 1/√m at fixed charge.
  • Heavier particle, same energy, larger momentum, shorter wavelength.

If kinetic energy of moving electrons is made four times, then their de Broglie wavelength becomes:
(a)eight times.
(b)four times.
(c)two times.
(d)half.

Two particles A and B of masses mA and mB have de Broglie wavelength λA and λB. If λA = 2λB, compare their kinetic energies.

Question 8 · Board 2026[2]
The work functions for metals M1 and M2 are 1·9 eV and 5·0 eV respectively. Perform necessary calculations to find out which metal emits photoelectrons when monochromatic light of wavelength 410 nm is incident on them.
  1. Photon energy E = hc/λ = (6·6 × 10−34 × 3 × 108)/(410 × 10−9) = 4·83 × 10-19 J
    [½]
  2. In eV: E = 4·83 × 10-19/(1·6 × 10−19) = 3·02 eV
    [½]
  3. Photoelectric emission needs E ≥ φ. For M1: 3·02 eV > 1·9 eV, so photoelectrons are emitted (Kmax = 3·02 − 1·9 = 1·12 eV).
    [½]
  4. For M2: 3·02 eV < 5·0 eV, so no emission.
    [½]
Answer
Only metal M1 emits photoelectrons (photon energy 3·02 eV exceeds 1·9 eV but not 5·0 eV).
Common slip Comparing a value in joules with a work function in eV.

Concepts to recall

  • Einstein: hν = φ + Kmax; emission only if ν > ν0 (i.e. E > φ).
  • E (eV) = 1240/λ (nm): 1240/410 = 3·02 eV, a quick check.
  • Use the constants table: h = 6·6 × 10−34 J s, 1 eV = 1·6 × 10−19 J.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q8
Question 8 · Specimen 2026[2]
In the photoelectric effect, the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectron is ‘a’ and the work function of the metal is W0. If the frequency of incident radiation is made ‘K’ times, calculate the change in the maximum kinetic energy of the ejected electron.
  1. Einstein’s equation: a1 = hν − W0 (= a) and for the new frequency a2 = hKν − W0.
    [1]
  2. Change = a2 − a1 = Khν − hν = (K − 1)hν. Since hν = a + W0, this is (K − 1)(a + W0).
    [1]
Answer
ΔKEmax = (K − 1)hν = (K − 1)(a + W0)
Common slip Answering (K − 1)a; the KE is not proportional to the frequency.
Marking note Key stops at (K − 1)hν. The question names a and W0 as the given quantities, so the form (K − 1)(a + W0) is the complete one; award full marks for either.

Concepts to recall

  • Einstein: KEmax = hν − W0 = eV0 (stopping potential).
  • The work function stays the same; only the photon energy changes. Increase in KE = increase in photon energy.
  • The KE does not become K times a: the work function is subtracted once.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q8
Question 8 · Board 2025 Imp.[2]
Intensity of monochromatic UV light incident on photocathode of a photoelectric cell is increased. What is its effect on:
(i) photocurrent?
(ii) kinetic energy of the most energetic photoelectron?
  1. (i) Photocurrent increases (directly proportional to the intensity): more photons per second fall on the cathode, so more photoelectrons are emitted per second.
    [1]
  2. (ii) K.E. of the most energetic photoelectron is unchanged: Kmax = hν − φ depends only on the frequency and the work function, not on the intensity.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Photocurrent increases; (ii) Kmax remains the same.
Common slip Saying the kinetic energy also increases with intensity (the classical wave-theory expectation, which the experiment contradicts).

Concepts to recall

  • Einstein: Kmax = hν − φ = eVs; each photon ejects at most one electron.
  • Saturation photocurrent ∝ intensity (for fixed frequency above threshold); stopping potential independent of intensity.
  • Intensity changes the number of photons; frequency changes the energy of each photon.

Question 8 · Board 2025[2]
With reference to photoelectric effect, define the terms:
(i) Threshold frequency.
(ii) Work function.
  1. (i) The threshold frequency f0 is the minimum frequency of the incident radiation below which no photoelectric emission takes place from a given metal, however intense the radiation.
    [1]
  2. (ii) The work function φ is the minimum energy required to liberate an electron from the surface of the metal; φ = hf0.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Minimum frequency that can cause photoemission; (ii) minimum energy to eject an electron from the metal, φ = hf0.

  • Einstein’s photoelectric equation: hf = φ + Kmax, with φ = hf0.
  • Emission is instantaneous and occurs only for f ≥ f0; intensity decides only the number of photoelectrons.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (6)ISC Specimen 2025 Physics Paper 1, Q8
Question 8 · Specimen 2025[2]
How does stopping potential in photoelectric emission vary if
(i) the intensity of the incident radiation increases?
(ii) the frequency of incident radiation decreases?
  1. (i) Einstein: eVs = hν − φ = Kmax. Intensity only changes the number of photons (hence the photocurrent), not the energy of each photon, so the stopping potential is unchanged (independent of intensity).
    [1]
  2. (ii) Vs = (h/e)(ν − ν0): it decreases linearly as ν decreases, and becomes zero at the threshold frequency ν0 (below ν0 there is no emission).
    [1]
Answer
(i) no change; (ii) Vs decreases (linearly with ν).
Marking note CISCE’s key says only that Vs “will change with the frequency”. The direction (it decreases) is what the question asks and is what is marked here.

Concepts to recall

  • Einstein’s equation: hf = φ + Kmax, Kmax = eVs; φ = hν0.
  • Intensity affects the saturation current, not Vs; frequency affects Vs, not the saturation current.
  • Vs–ν graph: a straight line, slope h/e, intercept on the ν axis = ν0.

Question 17 · Board 2026[3]
(i) The graphs show the variation of stopping potential versus frequency of incident radiation for metals A and B (the line for A cuts the frequency axis at f0A, that for B at f0B > f0A). UV radiation of appropriate wavelength is allowed to fall on both the metals. Which metal will emit photoelectrons with higher maximum kinetic energy (Emax)? Give a reason.
(ii) State the conclusion that was drawn from the Davisson–Germer experiment.
  1. (i) Metal A.
    [1]

    [1]

Question 17 · Specimen 2026[3]
Also draws on: the photon comes from a Bohr-atom transition (Ch 9); the question asks for the stopping potential.
In a hydrogen atom, an electron jumps from the first excited state to the ground state and a photon is emitted. This photon is incident on a metal surface having a work function of 2 eV. Calculate the stopping potential of the electron emitted from the metal surface.
  1. Energy levels En = −13·6/n2 eV. First excited state n = 2: E2 = -3·4 eV; ground state E1 = −13·6 eV. Photon energy E = E2 − E1 = −3·4 + 13·6 = 10·2 eV.
    [1]
  2. Photoelectric equation: eV0 = E − W0 = 10·2 − 2 = 8·2 eV.
    [1]
  3. V0 = 8·2 eV/e = 8·2 V.
    [1]

Stopping potential V0 = 8·2 V

  • The first excited state is n = 2, the ground state n = 1.

Question 17 · Board 2025 Imp.[3]
The graph of stopping potential (V0) versus frequency (f) of incident UV radiation incident on a metallic surface is shown below. Study the graph and answer the questions that follow. (The solid line meets V0 = 0 at f = 10 × 1014 Hz; its dashed extension cuts the V0-axis at about −4·3 V.)
(i) Find the value of work function of the metal.
(ii) Determine the value of Planck’s constant ‘h’.
  1. Einstein: eV0 = hf − φ, so V0 = (h/e)f − φ/e: a straight line of slope h/e, with intercept −φ/e on the V0 axis and f0 on the f axis.
    [½]
  2. (i) Intercept on the V0 axis = −4·3 V, so φ/e = 4·3 V and φ = 4·3 eV = 4·3 × 1·6 × 10−19 = 6·88 × 10-19 J.
    [1]
  3. (ii) Slope = (0 − (−4·3))/(10 × 1014 − 0) = 4·3 × 10−15 V s. Hence h = e × slope (or h = φ/f0).
    [1]
  4. h = 1·6 × 10−19 × 4·3 × 10−15 = 6·88 × 10-34 J s ≈ 6·9 × 10−34 J s.
    [½]
Answer
(i) φ ≈ 4·3 eV (6·9 × 10-19 J); (ii) h ≈ 6·9 × 10-34 J s.
Common slip Reading the slope as h itself (it is h/e), or using the solid segment alone with an imprecise intercept.
Marking note The graph was read at 300 dpi against its grid: the V0 axis is crossed at about −4·3 V (just above the −4·5 line) and the line meets the frequency axis just before the 10 mark, at 9·7 to 10 × 1014 Hz; the slope is about 4·4 × 10−15 V s. Readings of φ between 4·2 and 4·4 eV and h between 6·7 and 7·0 × 10−34 J s are therefore consistent with the graph. The paper’s constants table does not list h, so the answer comes from the graph; an examiner allows a band about the standard 6·63 × 10−34 J s.

Concepts to recall

  • V0 against f: slope = h/e (the same for every metal), f-intercept = threshold frequency f0, V0-intercept = −φ/e.
  • Work function φ = hf0; use the dashed extension of the line, since stopping potentials below f0 do not exist physically.
  • Convert eV to J with 1 eV = 1·6 × 10−19 J (given in the paper’s table).

The graphs show the variation of the stopping potential Vs with the frequency (ν) of the incident radiations for two different photosensitive materials M1 and M2. Express work function for M1 and M2 in terms of Planck’s constant (h), threshold frequency and charge of the electron (e). If the values of stopping potential for M1 and M2 are V1 and V2 respectively then show that the slope of the lines equals (V1 − V2)/(ν01 − ν02) for a frequency ν > ν02 and also ν > ν01.
  1. At threshold the photon energy just equals the work function: φ1 = hν01 and φ2 = hν02 (as a potential: hν01/e and hν02/e volts).
    [1]
  2. Einstein’s equation eVs = hν − φ, so for the same frequency ν (ν > ν01, ν02): V1 = (h/e)ν − (h/e)ν01 and V2 = (h/e)ν − (h/e)ν02.
    [1]
  3. Subtracting: V1 − V2 = (h/e)(ν02 − ν01). Each line has slope h/e = (V1 − V2)/(ν02 − ν01), numerically equal to the stated (V1 − V2)/(ν01 − ν02) in magnitude; the lines are parallel, with slope h/e.
    [1]
Answer
φ1 = hν01, φ2 = hν02; slope h/e = |V1 − V2|/|ν01 − ν02|.
Marking note With ν01 < ν02 as drawn, V1 > V2 and the ratio (V1 − V2)/(ν01 − ν02) printed in the paper is −h/e; the sign is a slip in the question, and CISCE’s key repeats it (it also writes V01, V02 for the threshold frequencies and gets V1 − V2 = (h/e)(ν01 − ν02)). The slope is positive, as the graphs show; the statement is to be read as the magnitude. Award full marks for the derivation of h/e as the slope.

Concepts to recall

  • Vs = (h/e)ν − φ/e: slope h/e (same for every metal), intercept on the ν axis ν0 = φ/h, intercept on the Vs axis −φ/e.
  • Different metals give parallel lines; the one with the smaller threshold has the smaller work function and lies to the left.
  • Compare at the same ν: the metal with the lower φ has the larger Vs, so V1 > V2.

Question 16 · Board 2024[3]
(i) In an experiment on photo electric effect, how does stopping potential change, if at all, when intensity of incident monochromatic UV radiation is increased?
(ii) Ultraviolet light is incident on metals P, Q and R, having work functions 8 eV, 2 eV and 4 eV respectively.
(a) Which metal has lowest threshold frequency for photoelectric effect?
(b) For which metal is the value of Emax minimum?
(Note: Emax is maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectrons.)
  1. (i) The stopping potential does not change: eV0 = Kmax = hν − φ, which depends on the frequency and the metal but not on the intensity. Increasing the intensity only increases the number of photoelectrons, i.e. the saturation current.
    [1]
  2. (ii)(a) Threshold frequency ν0 = φ/h, so the metal with the smallest work function has the lowest threshold: metal Q (2 eV).
    [1]
  3. (b) For the same incident UV photon energy hν, Kmax = hν − φ is smallest for the largest work function, so Emax is minimum for metal P (8 eV).
    [1]
Answer
(i) Stopping potential remains the same. (ii)(a) Metal Q. (b) Metal P.
Marking note Strictly Emax for P would be zero (no emission) if the UV photon energy is below 8 eV. The expected answer is P, the metal with the largest work function.

Concepts to recall

  • Einstein: hν = φ + Kmax; Kmax = eV0; ν0 = φ/h.
  • Intensity → number of photons per second → saturation current. Frequency → Kmax and V0.
  • Emission occurs only if hν > φ; photons of energy below 8 eV would not release any electron from P at all.
