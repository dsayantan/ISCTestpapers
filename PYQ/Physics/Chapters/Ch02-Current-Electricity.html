What to revise

  • Almost every paper sets Kirchhoff's laws (3 marks, or 2 marks as a short circuit) and a potentiometer question (emf comparison, internal resistance, potential gradient).
  • Graph reading: V–I graph of a cell (emf and r), I–V of a conductor at two temperatures, J–E for two wires.
  • Concepts: drift speed and relaxation time, temperature dependence of resistance, metre-bridge/Wheatstone balance, cells in series and parallel.

Question 1(B)(ii) · Specimen 2027[1]
Compare resistance of an ideal voltmeter with that of an ideal ammeter.
  1. An ideal voltmeter has infinite resistance (RV = ∞) so that it draws no current; an ideal ammeter has zero resistance (RA = 0) so that it does not change the current. Hence RV >> RA.
    [1]
Answer
RV = ∞ and RA = 0.

Concepts to recall

  • Voltmeter: connected in parallel, must have a very high resistance (galvanometer + large series resistance). Ammeter: connected in series, very low resistance (galvanometer + small shunt).

Question 1(A)(i) · Board 2026[1]
A fish called an electric eel can generate a voltage up to 800 V. If it can supply a current of 5 mA, the maximum power it can deliver is:
(a)4000 W
(b)400 W
(c)40 W
(d)4 W
  1. Maximum power P = VI = 800 V × 5 × 10−3 A = 4 W
    [1]
Answer
(d) 4 W
Common slip Leaving the current in mA gives 4000, option (a).

Concepts to recall

  • Electrical power P = VI = I2R = V2/R.
  • Convert units first: 5 mA = 5 × 10−3 A (the usual slip is to use 5 A).

Question 1(B)(i) · Specimen 2026[1]
Current I flowing through a metallic wire is gradually increased. Show graphically how the heating power (P) developed in it varies with the current (I).
  1. P = I2R, so P ∝ I2 (if R is taken constant).
    [½]
  2. Graph: a parabola through the origin with P on the vertical axis and I on the horizontal axis (as drawn).
    [½]
Answer
P–I graph is a parabola through the origin, P = I2R.
Marking note CISCE’s graph is a rising curve through the origin, the same parabola. In a real wire R rises as it heats, so the curve bends up more steeply; the ideal answer is the parabola.

Concepts to recall

  • Joule heating P = I2R = VI = V2/R.
  • Which quantity is on which axis decides the shape: P against I is a parabola; P against I2 would be a straight line.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
Three cells having emfs 3 V, 1 V and 4 V are connected as shown in Figure 1 below. Their internal resistances are negligible. The equivalent emf between the terminals P and Q is:
(a)0 V
(b)4 V
(c)6 V
(d)8 V
  1. Read the plates: in all three cells the long plate (+) is on the left, so every branch has P as its positive end.
    [½]
  2. Upper branch: 3 V and 1 V in series aiding, 3 + 1 = 4 V. Lower branch: 4 V. Two equal emfs with negligible internal resistance in parallel give the same emf, so EPQ = 4 V.
    [½]
Answer
(b) 4 V
Common slip Adding all three (8 V), or reading the branches as opposing and getting 0 V.

Concepts to recall

  • Cells in series, same direction: emfs add. Cells in parallel (same polarity): equivalent emf is the common value when the emfs are equal.
  • Long plate of the symbol is the positive terminal; check polarity before adding or subtracting.
  • Opposite-polarity cells in a series chain would subtract.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
What is the effect of increasing the temperature of a piece of metal on relaxation time of free electrons?
  1. The relaxation time decreases: at higher temperature the lattice ions vibrate with greater amplitude, so the electrons collide more often and the average time between collisions falls (τ ∝ 1/√T roughly).
    [1]
Answer
Relaxation time decreases.

Concepts to recall

  • Relaxation time τ = average time between two successive collisions of an electron; drift speed vd = (eE/m)τ.
  • Resistivity ρ = m/(ne2τ): fall of τ with T explains why the resistance of a metal rises with temperature.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i) · Board 2025[1]
When a battery is connected between the terminals P and Q, as shown in Figure 1 below, it is found that no current flows through the 5 Ω resistor. Then, the value of resistor X is:
(a)10 Ω
(b)20 Ω
(c)25 Ω
(d)45 Ω
  1. No current in the 5 Ω resistor means B and D are at the same potential: the network is a balanced Wheatstone bridge with arms AB = 10 Ω, BC = 25 Ω, AD = 10 Ω, DC = X. Balance: 10/25 = 10/X, so X = 25 Ω.
    [1]
Answer
(c) 25 Ω
Common slip Adding the 10 Ω and 25 Ω (giving 35 Ω or 45 Ω) instead of comparing ratios.

Concepts to recall

  • Wheatstone bridge balance: P/Q = R/S; then the galvanometer arm (here the 5 Ω resistor) carries no current and may be ignored.
  • No current in a resistor ⇔ no potential difference across it: VB = VD.
  • Take the ratios along the two branches in the same order (top to bottom): 10 : 25 against 10 : X.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC Specimen 2025 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i) · Specimen 2025[1]
Figure 1 shows a circuit containing an ammeter A, a galvanometer G and a plug key K. When the key is closed:
(a)both G and A show deflections.
(b)neither G nor A shows deflection.
(c)G shows deflection but A does not.
(d)A shows deflection but G does not.
  1. The four resistors form a Wheatstone bridge: 2 Ω : 4 Ω = 5 Ω : 10 Ω (= 1 : 2), so the bridge is balanced. The points Q and S are at the same potential and no current flows through G.
    [½]
  2. The cell still drives current through the bridge as a whole: Req = (7 × 14)/(7 + 14) = 4·67 Ω and I = 2/4·67 = 0·43 A, and all of it passes through the ammeter A, so A is deflected.
    [½]
Answer
(d) A shows deflection but G does not.

Concepts to recall

  • Wheatstone bridge: balanced when P/Q = R/S; the galvanometer in the diagonal then reads zero.
  • Test the ratio of the two resistors on each side of the galvanometer, not just their sum.
  • The ammeter is in the main circuit, so it always carries the cell’s current whatever the bridge does.

Question 1(B)(i) · Specimen 2025[1]
Current I flowing through a metallic wire is gradually increased. Show graphically how heating power (P) developed in it varies with the current (I).
  1. Heating power P = I2R, so P ∝ I2.
    [½]
  2. Graph: a parabola through the origin with I on the x-axis and P on the y-axis, both axes labelled.
    [½]
Answer
Parabola through the origin, P ∝ I2 (see the graph).

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i) · Board 2024[1]
If potential difference between the two ends of a metallic wire is doubled, drift speed of free electrons in the wire:
(a)remains same.
(b)becomes double.
(c)becomes four times.
(d)becomes half.
  1. The wire is the same (n, A, R unchanged), so by Ohm’s law I = V/R doubles when V is doubled.
    [½]
  2. I = neAvd gives vd ∝ I, so the drift speed also doubles. (Equivalently vd = eVτ/ml ∝ V.)
    [½]
Answer
(b) becomes double.

Concepts to recall

  • I = neAvd; drift speed vd = (eE/m)τ = eVτ/(ml).
  • Ohm’s law: for a metallic conductor at constant temperature I ∝ V.
  • The relaxation time τ does not change with V (heating assumed negligible).
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(ii)
Question 1(A)(ii) · Board 2024[1]
A metre bridge is balanced with a known resistance (R) in the left hand gap and an unknown resistance (S) in the right hand gap. Balance point is found to be at a distance of l cm from the left hand side. When the battery and the galvanometer are interchanged, balance point will:
(a)shift towards left.
(b)shift towards right.
(c)remain same.
(d)shift towards left or right depending on the values of R and S.
  1. At balance R/S = l/(100 − l) (Wheatstone condition). It involves only the four resistances (the wire lengths are proportional to their resistances).
    [½]
  2. Interchanging the battery and the galvanometer leaves this condition unchanged: the bridge condition is symmetric in the two diagonals (the galvanometer still reads zero), so the balance point remains the same.
    [½]
Answer
(c) remain same.
Marking note Balance does not depend on the emf or on which diagonal holds the cell; only the sensitivity of the bridge changes.

Concepts to recall

  • Metre bridge: R/S = l/(100 − l); S = R(100 − l)/l.
  • Wheatstone bridge: P/Q = R/S, and the conditions for balance are the same if cell and galvanometer are exchanged (the conjugate-arm property).
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii) · Board 2024[1]
Three identical cells each of emf ‘e’ are connected in parallel to form a battery. What is the emf of the battery?
  1. For identical cells in parallel the emf of the combination equals the emf of a single cell: Eeq = (e/r + e/r + e/r)/(3/r) = e. (Only the internal resistance falls, to r/3.)
    [1]
Answer
The emf of the battery is e.
Common slip Writing 3e (that is the series result).

Concepts to recall

  • Cells in parallel (same polarity, same emf): E = e, req = r/n. Cells in series: E = ne, req = nr.
  • Parallel grouping is used to supply a larger current for a longer time at the same voltage.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (12)ISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii) · Board 2024[1]
Three bulbs B1 (230 V, 40 W), B2 (230 V, 60 W) and B3 (230 V, 100 W) are connected in series to a 230 V supply. Which bulb glows the brightest?
  1. Resistance of a bulb R = V2/P: R1 = 1322 Ω, R2 = 882 Ω, R3 = 529 Ω, so R1 is the greatest.
    [½]
  2. In series the same current flows through all, and the power dissipated is I2R ∝ R, so the 40 W bulb B1 glows brightest.
    [½]
Answer
B1 (the 40 W bulb) glows the brightest.
Common slip Answering B3: that is true only when the bulbs are in parallel.

Concepts to recall

  • Rated power P = V2/R gives R = V2/P: the lower the wattage, the higher the resistance.
  • Series: same I, P = I2R is largest for the largest R. Parallel: same V, P = V2/R is largest for the smallest R (the 100 W bulb).

  1. Circuit of the potentiometer wire AB: driver cell (battery) D, rheostat Rh and key K in series with the whole wire AB, with the + terminal of D at A. [1]

TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (11)ISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q4
Question 4 · Specimen 2027[2]
(i) According to Ohm’s law, V = IR, so if potential difference (V) between the two ends of a conductor is varied, will its resistance (R) change? Explain your answer in brief.
OR
(ii) Heating power developed in a resistor R is given by P = V2/R. It implies that power P ∝ 1/R, i.e., power varies inversely with R. Heating power developed in a resistor R is also given by P = I2R. It implies that power P ∝ R, i.e., power varies directly with R. Under what conditions are the above-mentioned statements valid?

Solution to the first alternative

  1. No, the resistance does not change.
    [1]
  2. R = ρl/A depends on the material, length, area and temperature of the conductor, not on V or I: when V is changed, I changes in the same ratio, so V/I stays constant.
    [1]
Answer
No: R = V/I is a property of the conductor (its material, dimensions and temperature); I ∝ V.
Marking note Valid for an ohmic conductor at constant temperature; for a non-ohmic device (diode, filament at high current) V/I does vary.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. P ∝ 1/R holds when V is constant, i.e. when the resistors are connected in parallel (same potential difference across each).
    [1]
  2. P ∝ R holds when I is constant, i.e. when the resistors are connected in series (same current through each).
    [1]
Answer
P ∝ 1/R at constant V (parallel); P ∝ R at constant I (series).

Concepts to recall

  • Ohm’s law: V ∝ I at constant temperature; the constant of proportionality is R = ρl/A.
  • R = V/I is a ratio, not a cause: a larger V only drives a larger I.
  • P = VI = I2R = V2/R; a proportionality needs the third quantity fixed.
  • Series: same I, the larger R dissipates more. Parallel: same V, the smaller R dissipates more.

TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (11)ISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q4
Question 4 · Board 2026[2]
(i) What is the effect of doubling the current flowing through a metallic wire on: (a) the drift speed of free electrons? (b) the relaxation time of free electrons?
OR
(ii) Draw a labelled circuit diagram of a potentiometer used to compare the emfs of two given cells X and Y. (Procedure and formula are NOT required.)

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) I = neAvd: for the same wire (n, A unchanged), vd ∝ I, so the drift speed doubles.
    [1]
  2. (b) The relaxation time τ (mean time between collisions) depends on the material and temperature, not on the current, so it is unchanged (the random thermal speed is far larger than vd).
    [1]
Answer
(a) Drift speed doubles. (b) Relaxation time remains the same.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Primary circuit: driver cell E, plug key K and rheostat Rh in series with the uniform wire AB; the positive terminal of the driver cell is joined to A. Labelled.
    [1]
  2. Secondary circuit: positive terminals of cells X and Y joined to A; their negative terminals to terminals 1 and 3 of a two-way key; common terminal 2 through a galvanometer G and a high resistance R to the jockey J on the wire. Labelled.
    [1]
Answer
Circuit diagram as drawn.
Marking note Both circuits must be shown. Diagram marks are for the labelled diagram; omission of the driver-cell polarity or of the high series resistance costs ½.

Concepts to recall

  • I = neAvd; vd = (eE/m)τ; conductivity σ = ne2τ/m.
  • τ falls with rising temperature but is independent of the current (assuming heating is negligible).
  • Principle: potential drop across a wire length is proportional to the length (uniform wire, steady current): V ∝ l.
  • Comparison of emfs: E1/E2 = l1/l2 (balance lengths), provided the driver emf exceeds both.
  • Every cell must be connected with its + terminal to A; mark K, Rh, G, R, J.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (11)ISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q2 (OR alternative)
Question 2 · Specimen 2026 (OR)[2]
OR alternative of Question 2; not counted in the paper's 70 marks.
(ii) The current (I)–voltage (V) graphs for a conductor are given at two different temperatures T1 and T2.
(a) At which temperature, T1 or T2, is the resistance higher?
(b) Which temperature, T1 or T2, is higher?
  1. (a) On an I–V graph the slope is I/V = 1/R = tanθ. Since θ1 > θ2, tanθ1 > tanθ2, so R at T1 is less than R at T2. The resistance is higher at T2.
    [1]
  2. (b) The resistance of a metallic conductor increases with temperature, and R at T2 is higher, so T2 is the higher temperature.
    [1]
Answer
(a) The resistance is higher at T2; (b) T2 is the higher temperature.
Marking note CISCE’s key writes “R = V/I = 1/tanθ” and calls the slope the conductance; the argument is the same.

Concepts to recall

  • I–V graph: slope = 1/R = conductance (not R). A flatter line means larger resistance.
  • Metals: ρ = ρ0[1 + α(T − T0)], resistance rises with temperature (more frequent collisions); for semiconductors it would fall.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (11)ISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q4
Question 4 · Specimen 2026[2]
(i) Two cells of the same emf E but different internal resistances r1 and r2 are connected to an external resistance R as shown in Figure 5. The voltmeter V reads zero. Obtain an expression for R in terms of r1 and r2. (Assume the voltmeter V is of infinite resistance.)
OR
(ii) Ramesh performed an experiment to determine an unknown resistance R using the circuit shown in Figure 6. X is a resistance box and PQ is a 100 cm potentiometer wire. He closed the key and inserted X = 1 Ω, 2 Ω, … and recorded the null point (l) for different values of X.
(a) Identify the principle involved in calculating R.
(b) Write down a relation required to calculate the resistance R in terms of X and l.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Cells in series aiding: total emf = 2E, total resistance = R + r1 + r2, so I = 2E/(R + r1 + r2).
    [1]
  2. The voltmeter reads the terminal voltage of cell 1: V = E − Ir1 = 0, so Ir1 = E.
    [½]
  3. 2Er1/(R + r1 + r2) = E ⇒ 2r1 = R + r1 + r2 ⇒ R = r1 − r2.
    [½]
Answer
R = r1 − r2

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) The Wheatstone bridge principle (metre-bridge form): the galvanometer shows no deflection when the bridge is balanced.
    [1]
  2. (b) At the null point, X/R = l/(100 − l), so R = X(100 − l)/l.
    [1]
Answer
(a) Wheatstone bridge principle; (b) R = X(100 − l)/l
Marking note Equation as printed in the key. The null point is at balance, the galvanometer reading zero.

Concepts to recall

  • Terminal voltage V = E − Ir (cell delivering current). It is zero when Ir = E.
  • Series cells with like polarity: emfs and internal resistances add. An ideal voltmeter draws no current.
  • A valid R needs r1 > r2.
  • Balanced bridge: P/Q = R/S. Here the arms are X and R (upper), the wire lengths l and (100 − l) (lower); wire resistance is proportional to length.
  • Resistance box on the left gap, unknown on the right: X/R = l/(100 − l). Swapping the gaps inverts the formula, so read the figure.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (11)ISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q2
Question 2 · Board 2025 Imp.[2]
(i) Two resistors, R1 = 1 Ω and R2 = 2 Ω are connected in parallel. If the power developed in the resistor R1 is 4 W, find the power developed in the resistor R2.
OR
(ii) In Figure 4 shown below, internal resistance of the battery is negligible. Calculate the Ammeter reading, assuming its resistance to be negligible. (Four 4 Ω resistors forming a bridge with a galvanometer G between A and B; 12 V battery and the ammeter in the supply lead.)

Solution to the first alternative

  1. In parallel both resistors have the same potential difference V. For R1: P1 = V2/R1, so V2 = 4 W × 1 Ω = 4 V2, V = 2 V.
    [1]
  2. P2 = V2/R2 = 4/2 = 2 W (equivalently P ∝ 1/R at constant V: half of 4 W).
    [1]
Answer
P2 = 2 W
Common slip Using P ∝ R (the series rule) and getting 8 W.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. The 12 V source is connected across the left and right junctions; A and B are the midpoints of the two 4 Ω + 4 Ω paths and G joins them. The arms satisfy 4/4 = 4/4, so the bridge is balanced: A and B are at the same potential and G carries no current, so it can be removed.
    [½]
  2. Two parallel paths of (4 + 4) = 8 Ω each: Req = (8 × 8)/(8 + 8) = 4 Ω.
    [½]
  3. Ammeter reading I = E/Req = 12/4 = 3 A.
    [1]
Answer
Ammeter reading = 3 A.
Marking note Treating the bridge as unbalanced and applying Kirchhoff’s laws gives the same 3 A, because the node potentials work out equal.

Concepts to recall

  • Power P = VI = I2R = V2/R.
  • Parallel: same V, so P ∝ 1/R. Series: same I, so P ∝ R.
  • Wheatstone bridge balance: P/Q = R/S; then no current flows through the galvanometer (equal potentials at A and B).
  • Balanced bridge: ignore G, combine each arm in series then the two arms in parallel.
  • The ammeter is ideal (zero resistance) and the battery has no internal resistance, so the supply current is E/Req.

TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (11)ISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q4
Question 4 · Board 2025[2]
(i) Figure 6 below shows an electric circuit. Apply Kirchhoff’s laws to calculate:
(a) current ‘I’ flowing through the 2 Ω resistor.
(b) emf of the cell ‘E’.
OR
(ii) In a potentiometer experiment, a cell of emf 1·25 V gives a balance point at 35 cm mark of the wire. If this cell is replaced by another cell, the balance point is at 63 cm mark. Calculate the emf of the second cell.

Solution to the first alternative
