ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (5)ISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i) · Specimen 2027[1]
A point charge −Q and three points A, B and C are shown in Figure 1 below. If V is electric potential and E is electric field intensity, we can infer that:
(a)EA > EB > EC
(b)EA < EB < EC
(c)VA < VC < VB
(d)VA > VC > VB
  1. The points lie in the order O, A, C, B, so rA < rC < rB. Field of a point charge E = kQ/r2 falls with distance, so EA > EC > EB: options (a) and (b) are wrong.
    [½]
  2. Potential of the negative charge V = −kQ/r is negative and rises towards zero as r increases, so VA < VC < VB.
    [½]
Answer
(c) VA < VC < VB
Common slip Treating potential like field: “nearer means larger” is true of |V| only; the sign makes the nearest point the lowest.

Concepts to recall

  • Point charge: E = kq/r2 (magnitude falls as 1/r2); V = kq/r with the sign of q.
  • For a negative source the potential is least (most negative) close to it and increases with distance.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (5)ISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i) · Specimen 2027[1]
Two point charges Q1 and Q2, separated by a distance, are such that there is no neutral point (where electric field is zero) in their vicinity. What is the relation between Q1 and Q2?
  1. Two like charges have a neutral point between them; two unlike charges of unequal magnitude have one on the line outside, on the side of the smaller charge. Only for equal and opposite charges is there no point where the fields cancel, so Q2 = −Q1 (they form an electric dipole).
    [1]
Answer
Q2 = −Q1 (the pair forms an electric dipole).

Concepts to recall

  • Neutral point: E1 + E2 = 0. Between like charges the fields oppose; for unlike charges they oppose only outside the pair, nearer the smaller charge.
  • For equal and opposite charges the nearer charge always dominates outside, and between them the fields add, so the field is never zero.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (5)ISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii) · Board 2026[1]
Calculate the work done in moving a point charge ‘q’ through a distance ‘d’ along the perpendicular bisector of an electric dipole, which consists of two point charges −Q and +Q separated by a distance ‘L’.
  1. Every point on the perpendicular bisector is equidistant from +Q and −Q, so the potentials cancel: V = 0 at all its points (it is an equipotential).
    [½]
  2. Work W = q(Vfinal − Vinitial) = q(0 − 0) = 0.
    [½]
Answer
W = 0
Marking note Answer “zero” with the reason (equipotential) earns the mark; the numerical values of d, L and q are not needed.

Concepts to recall

  • Work done by an external agent between two points: W = qΔV.
  • The perpendicular bisector of a dipole is an equipotential at zero potential (the field there is parallel to p, i.e. perpendicular to the path).
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (5)ISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i) · Specimen 2026[1]
Two point charges +50 nC and −50 nC separated by a distance of 1 mm are kept well inside a large sphere of radius 1 m. Electric flux emanating from the sphere is:
(a)50 × 10−12 V m
(b)50 × 10−9 V m
(c)50 × 10−6 V m
(d)Zero
  1. Gauss’s law: flux through a closed surface = (net charge enclosed)/ε0.
    [½]
  2. Net charge inside = +50 nC − 50 nC = 0, so the flux is zero (the distance between the charges and the radius of the sphere do not matter).
    [½]
Answer
(d) Zero
Common slip Using Φ = 50 nC/ε0 for one charge only.

Concepts to recall

  • Gauss’s law: Φ = qenc/ε0; only the charge inside counts.
  • A dipole has zero net charge, so the net flux through any closed surface round it is zero. Do not use the given numbers.
  • Unit of flux: V m (or N m2 C−1).
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (5)ISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i) · Board 2024[1]
In an electric dipole, what is the locus of a point having zero potential?
  1. Take any point P on the perpendicular bisector of the line joining the charges. It is equidistant (r) from +q and −q, so V = kq/r − kq/r = 0.
    [½]
  2. The locus is therefore the equatorial plane of the dipole: the plane perpendicular to the axis passing through the centre of the dipole.
    [½]
Answer
The equatorial plane (the plane perpendicular to the dipole axis through its centre).
Marking note “Perpendicular bisector plane of the line joining the two charges” is equally correct.

Concepts to recall

  • Potential of a dipole: V = p cosθ/(4πε0r2); it vanishes for θ = 90°.
  • Zero potential does not mean zero field: on the equatorial plane E is non-zero and antiparallel to p.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (13)ISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q2
Question 2 · Specimen 2027[2]
(i) A negatively charged thundercloud above the earth’s surface may be perceived as a parallel plate capacitor. The lower plate of the capacitor is the Earth’s surface, and the upper plate is the base of the thundercloud. The area of the base of the thundercloud = 4·5 × 108 m2, distance of thundercloud base from the earth’s surface = 4425 m. Calculate the capacitance of this capacitor.
OR
(ii) A tiny bird carrying a charge of q = 8 × 10−17 C dives vertically down towards a large positively charged metallic plate kept on a table in a horizontal position. What should be the surface charge density (σ) of the plate so that the bird stops in its flight, close to the plate? (Mass of the bird = 50 g)

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor (air) C = ε0A/d
    [½]
  2. = (8·85 × 10−12 × 4·5 × 108)/4425
    [½]
  3. = 9·0 × 10−7 F
    [1]
Answer
C = 9·0 × 10−7 F

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. The bird stops (is just held) when the upward electric force balances its weight: qE = mg.
    [½]
  2. Field of the large plate (the examiner’s key) E = σ/2ε0, so qσ/2ε0 = mg and σ = 2mgε0/q.
    [½]
  3. σ = (2 × 50 × 10−3 × 9·8 × 8·85 × 10−12)/(8 × 10−17)
    [½]
  4. = 1·08 × 105 C m−2
    [½]
Answer
σ = 1·08 × 105 C m−2
Common slip Leaving the mass in grams (50 instead of 0·05 kg) makes σ a thousand times too large.
Marking note The printed key gives the unit as V m−1: that is the unit of E, not of σ. Surface charge density is in C m−2, and the answer is corrected here. The key also uses E = σ/2ε0 (a sheet of charge). For the surface of a conducting plate E = σ/ε0 would give 5·42 × 104 C m−2; the key’s value 1·08 × 105 C m−2 is the one to give full marks for. (Strictly the bird is in equilibrium, not at rest, when qE = mg.)

Concepts to recall

  • C = ε0A/d for two parallel plates with air between; C = Q/V.
  • Use ε0 = 8·85 × 10−12 F m−1 from the paper’s table of constants; unit farad.
  • Large plane sheet of charge: E = σ/2ε0; surface of a charged conductor: E = σ/ε0.
  • Equilibrium of a charge in a field: qE = mg; unit of σ is C m−2.

(i) Four point charges Q1 = +17·7 μC, Q2 = −8·85 μC, Q3 = −17·7 μC and Q4 = 35·4 μC are kept as shown in Figure 1. Calculate the electric flux emanating from the closed surface S.
  1. Gauss’s law: Φ = qenclosed/ε0. Only Q1 and Q2 lie inside S; Q3 and Q4 are outside and do not contribute.

  2. qenc = 17·7 − 8·85 = 8·85 μC = 8·85 × 10−6 C

    [1]

Φ = 1·0 × 106 N m2 C−1

  • Gauss’s law: Φ = ∮E·dS = qenc/ε0.
  • Charges outside the surface contribute zero net flux (what enters also leaves).
  • Use ε0 = 8·85 × 10−12 F m−1 from the constants table; unit N m2 C−1 or V m.

Question 3 · Board 2026[2]
You are provided with three identical capacitors, each of capacitance ‘C’. If Cp is the equivalent capacitance when they are connected in parallel and Cs is the equivalent capacitance when they are connected in series, calculate the ratio Cs/Cp.
  1. Parallel: Cp = C + C + C = 3C
    [½]
  2. Series: 1/Cs = 1/C + 1/C + 1/C = 3/C, so Cs = C/3
    [1]
  3. Cs/Cp = (C/3)/(3C) = 1/9
    [½]
Answer
Cs : Cp = 1 : 9

Concepts to recall

  • Parallel: Ceq = ΣCi (same V, charges add).
  • Series: 1/Ceq = Σ1/Ci (same Q, voltages add).
  • For n identical capacitors the ratio Cs/Cp = 1/n2.

Question 2 · Specimen 2026[2]
(i) Find the capacitance of the following combinations between terminals A and B. The area of each plate is A and the separation between the nearest two plates is d.
(a) Figure 2    (b) Figure 3
  1. (a) Plates 1 and 4 are joined to A, plates 2 and 3 to B. Plates 2 and 3 are at the same potential, so no capacitor forms between them; capacitors are formed by the pairs 1–2 and 3–4, each C = ε0A/d, and they are in parallel.
    [½]
  2. CAB = 2C = 2ε0A/d
    [½]
  3. (b) Plates 1 and 3 joined to A, plates 2 and 4 to B: the pairs 1–2, 2–3, 3–4 are three capacitors in parallel.

Question 3 · Specimen 2026[2]
The arrangement of an oxygen ion and two hydrogen ions in a water molecule is shown in Figure 4. Calculate the electric dipole moment of the water molecule. Express your answer in terms of e (charge on a hydrogen ion), l and θ.
  1. The O is −2e and each H is +e, at distance l from O. Treat the molecule as two dipoles O–H, each of moment p1 = p2 = e × l directed from the negative charge (O) to the positive charge (H).
    [1]
  2. Angle between them is θ; the resultant by the parallelogram law is along the bisector: p = 2p1 cos(θ/2) = 2el cos(θ/2), directed from O along the bisector towards the hydrogens.
    [1]
Answer
p = 2el cos(θ/2)
Common slip Writing 2el sin(θ/2) (using the angle from the wrong line) or forgetting that two bonds contribute.

Concepts to recall

  • Dipole moment p = (charge) × (separation), a vector from −q to +q.
  • Dipole moments add as vectors: for two equal moments at an angle θ, p = 2p1 cos(θ/2).
  • In water θ ≈ 104.5°, l ≈ 0·96 Å, giving p ≈ 6·2 × 10−30 C m.

Question 3 · Board 2025 Imp.[2]
Two identical capacitors, each of capacitance C are connected to a battery as shown in Figure 5 below.
(i) Calculate the equivalent capacitance of the circuit.
(ii) What is the equivalent capacitance of the circuit, if the space between the two plates of one of the capacitors is completely filled with paper (of dielectric constant K = 2·5)?
  1. (i) Both capacitors are connected between the same two junctions, so they are in parallel: Ceq = C + C = 2C.
    [1]
  2. (ii) The filled capacitor becomes KC = 2·5C; the other stays C. In parallel C′eq = C + 2·5C = 3·5C.
    [1]
Answer
(i) 2C; (ii) 3·5C
Common slip Reading the two capacitors as being in series (giving C/2).

Concepts to recall

  • Parallel: Ceq = C1 + C2. Series: 1/Ceq = 1/C1 + 1/C2.
  • A dielectric of constant K filling the whole space multiplies the capacitance by K: C = Kε0A/d.
  • Trace the wiring: capacitors on different branches between the same two nodes are in parallel, not in series.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (13)ISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q4
Question 4 · Board 2025 Imp.[2]
(i) (a) Three identical point charges, each equal to Q, are situated at the three vertices of an equilateral triangle MNO, having each side equal to l. How much electrostatic potential energy is stored in this system?
(b) An electric dipole lies in a uniform electric field. When is torque acting on the dipole maximum?
OR
(ii) Two Gaussian surfaces, S1 and S2 are shown in Figure 6 below.
(a) What is the ratio of electric flux emerging out of these two surfaces?
(b) What happens to this ratio if the space occupied by each Gaussian surface is doubled?

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) There are three pairs (MN, NO, OM), each at distance l, and each pair has energy Q2/(4πε0l).
    [½]
  2. U = 3 × Q2/(4πε0l) = 3Q2/(4πε0l) (positive: the charges are like).
    [½]
  3. (b) τ = pE sinθ is maximum when sinθ = 1, i.e. θ = 90°: the dipole is perpendicular to the field; then τmax = pE.
    [1]
Answer
(a) U = 3Q2/(4πε0l); (b) when the dipole is at 90° to the field, τmax = pE.
Common slip Counting six terms (each pair twice) and getting 6Q2/(4πε0l).

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) Gauss’s law: Φ = qenc/ε0. S1 encloses −2q + q = −q, so Φ1 = −q/ε0. S2 encloses 2q + q = 3q, so Φ2 = 3q/ε0.
    [1]
  2. Ratio Φ1 : Φ2 = −q : 3q = −1 : 3 (the flux through S1 is inward).
    [½]
  3. (b) The ratio does not change (−1 : 3): the flux depends only on the charge enclosed, not on the size or shape of the surface (as long as the same charges remain inside).
    [½]
Answer
(a) Φ1 : Φ2 = −1 : 3; (b) no change.
Common slip Giving the ratio as 1 : 3 and dropping the sign, or adding magnitudes (3q : 3q = 1 : 1).

Concepts to recall

  • Energy of a system of point charges: U = Σpairs qiqj/(4πε0rij); count each pair once.
  • Torque on a dipole τ = p × E, magnitude pE sinθ; zero when parallel or antiparallel, maximum at 90°.
  • Gauss’s law: ∮E·dS = qenc/ε0; only enclosed charge contributes, with its sign.
  • Net flux is negative (inward) when the enclosed charge is negative.
  • The flux is independent of the shape and size of the closed surface.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (13)ISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q2
Question 2 · Board 2025[2]
(i) (a) What is the effect on capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor if the distance between its plates is increased?
(b) How will capacitance of a capacitor change if a dielectric slab is introduced between its plates?
  1. (a) C = ε0A/d: if d is increased, the capacitance decreases (inversely proportional to d).
    [1]
  2. (b) With a dielectric of constant K filling the gap, C = Kε0A/d = KC0, so the capacitance increases (by the factor K).
    [1]
Answer
(a) Capacitance decreases; (b) capacitance increases (C = KC0).

Concepts to recall

  • Parallel plate capacitor: C = ε0A/d in vacuum; with a dielectric slab filling the gap, C = Kε0A/d.
  • The dielectric is polarised; the induced field opposes the applied field, so for a given charge the potential difference drops and C = Q/V rises.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (13)ISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q3
Question 3 · Board 2025[2]
In case of a short electric dipole:
(i) What is the locus of a point having zero potential?
(ii) If electric field intensity at a point in axial position is E1 and at an equidistant point in equatorial position is E2, what is the ratio E1/E2?
  1. (i) The potential of a dipole at a point at angle θ to the axis is V = p cosθ/(4πε0r2); it is zero for θ = 90°. The locus of zero potential is the equatorial plane of the dipole (the plane perpendicular to the dipole axis through its centre).
    [1]
  2. (ii) Axial point: E1 = 2p/(4πε0r3); equatorial point at the same distance: E2 = p/(4πε0r3).
    [½]
  3. E1/E2 = 2.
    [½]
Answer
(i) The equatorial plane of the dipole; (ii) E1/E2 = 2.
Marking note On the axis itself (away from the centre) the potential is not zero, because the nearer charge dominates; the equatorial plane, which passes through the centre, is the full locus.

Concepts to recall

  • Short dipole: axial field 2kp/r3 (along p), equatorial field kp/r3 (opposite to p), with k = 1/4πε0.
  • Dipole potential V = kp cosθ/r2: zero on the equatorial plane, positive on the +q side.
  • The ratio 2 : 1 is the one to remember for the same distance.

Question 2 · Specimen 2025[2]
(i) A dielectric slab of dielectric constant K and thickness t is introduced between the two plates of a capacitor of plate-separation d (> t) and common area A. The capacitance of this system is given as C = ε0A / [(d − t) + t/K]. How does the capacitance C modify in each of the following cases?
(a) The dielectric slab covers half the distance of separation between the two plates.
(b) The whole space between the plates is filled with the dielectric.
  1. (a) Put t = d/2 in the given formula: C = ε0A / [(d − d/2) + d/2K]
    [½]
  2. = ε0A / [d(1/2 + 1/2K)] = 2Kε0A / [d(K + 1)]
    [½]
  3. (b) The whole space is filled: t = d, so C = ε0A / (d/K) = Kε0A/d, i.e. the capacitance becomes K times that of the empty capacitor.
    [1]
Answer
(a) C = 2Kε0A/[d(K + 1)]; (b) C = Kε0A/d.
Common slip Writing the (a) answer as KC0/2: that is wrong, because the half of the gap that stays air is not scaled by K.

Concepts to recall

  • Empty parallel-plate capacitor C0 = ε0A/d; with a full dielectric C = KC0.
  • Slab of thickness t: the air gap d − t and the slab act like two capacitors in series, giving the effective separation d − t + t/K.
  • Check limits: K = 1 gives C0 in both cases; t = 0 gives C0.

Question 3 · Specimen 2025[2]
Arrangement of an oxygen ion and two hydrogen ions in a water molecule is shown in Figure 3. Calculate the electric dipole moment of the water molecule. Express your answer in terms of e (charge on hydrogen ion), l and θ.

TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (13)ISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q2
Question 2 · Board 2024[2]
(i) A hollow sphere of radius R has a point charge q at its centre. Electric flux emanating from the sphere is X. How will the electric flux change, if at all, when
(a) radius of the sphere is doubled?
(b) charge q is replaced by an electric dipole?
OR
(ii) In case of an infinite line charge, how does intensity of electric field at a point change, if at all, when
(a) charge on it is doubled?
(b) distance of the point is halved?

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Gauss’s law: Φ = qenclosed/ε0 = X.
    [½]
  2. (a) The charge enclosed is still q, and the flux does not depend on the radius, so the flux is unchanged: it remains X.
    [½]
  3. (b) A dipole has charges +q and −q, so the net charge enclosed is zero.
    [½]
  4. Hence the net flux becomes zero (the lines that leave +q end on −q).
    [½]
Answer
(a) No change (it remains X). (b) The net flux becomes zero.
Common slip Saying the flux halves or falls to 1/4 because the surface area changes: the field falls as 1/r2 while the area grows as r2, so the product is constant.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Field of an infinite line charge of linear density λ: E = λ/(2πε0r) (from Gauss’s law with a coaxial cylinder), directed radially.
    [½]
  2. (a) Doubling the charge (λ → 2λ): E ∝ λ, so the field doubles.
    [½]
  3. (b) Halving the distance (r → r/2): E ∝ 1/r, so the field again doubles.
    [1]
Answer
(a) E becomes double. (b) E becomes double.
Common slip Using the point-charge law and saying the field becomes four times when the distance is halved: for a line charge it is only twice.

Concepts to recall

  • Gauss’s law: Φ = ∮E·dS = qenc/ε0; only the enclosed charge matters, not the size or shape of the surface.
  • Net charge of a dipole = 0, so the net flux through any closed surface enclosing it is 0 (though E itself is not zero).
  • Infinite line charge: E = λ/(2πε0r) ∝ 1/r. Point charge: ∝ 1/r2. Infinite plane sheet: independent of distance (σ/2ε0).
  • Cylindrical symmetry: flux only through the curved surface, E(2πrl) = λl/ε0.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (13)ISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q3
Question 3 · Board 2024[2]
(i) What is meant by the statement: “Relative permittivity of water is 81”?
(ii) Can a body be given a charge of 2·2 × 10−19 C? Give a reason for your answer.
  1. (i) Relative permittivity εr = ε/ε0 = 81 means that the force between two given charges kept a fixed distance apart in water is 1/81 of the force between them in vacuum (air) at the same separation; equivalently the capacitance of a capacitor filled with water is 81 times that with vacuum between its plates.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Charge is quantised: q = ne, n an integer. Here n = (2·2 × 10−19)/(1·6 × 10−19) = 1·375, which is not an integer.
    [½]
  3. So a body cannot be given this charge, since it is not an integral multiple of the electronic charge.
    [½]
Answer
(i) The force between two charges in water is 1/81 of that in vacuum; (ii) No: 2·2 × 10−19 C = 1·375 e, not an integral multiple of e.
Common slip Rounding 1·375 to 1: charge cannot be a fraction of e (quarks aside, which are never free).

Concepts to recall

  • εr = ε/ε0 = K = Cmedium/Cvacuum; force in a medium F = F0/εr.
  • Quantisation of charge: q = ±ne, e = 1·6 × 10−19 C (from the constants table).
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (8)ISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q9
Question 9 · Specimen 2027[3]
(i) Obtain an expression of intensity of electric field at a point lying on the axis of an electric dipole.
OR
(ii) An electric dipole is kept in a uniform electric field E with its axis making an angle θ with the field. Show forces acting on each charge and hence prove that torque acting is given by τ = pE sinθ.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Diagram: dipole AB (−q at A, +q at B, separation 2l, centre O) and the point P on the axis at distance r from O, with the two fields shown.
    [½]
  2. Field of +q (at distance r − l, directed away from B): E1 = kq/(r − l)2
    [½]
  3. Field of −q (at distance r + l, directed towards A): E2 = kq/(r + l)2
    [½]
  4. The two fields are along the same line and opposite, and E1 > E2, so E = E1 − E2 = kq[1/(r − l)2 − 1/(r + l)2] = kq · 4rl/(r2 − l2)2
    [1]
  5. With p = q(2l) and k = 1/4πε0: E = k · 2pr/(r2 − l2)2, along p (from −q to +q). For r >> l: E ≈ 2kp/r3.
    [½]
Answer
E = k · 2pr/(r2 − l2)2 along the dipole moment p; for r >> l, E = 2p/(4πε0r3).
Common slip Adding E1 and E2 instead of subtracting them (on the axis they point opposite ways).

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Forces: +q at A feels qE along E and −q at B feels qE opposite to E (shown in the figure).
    [1]
  2. The forces are equal and opposite, so the net force is zero, but their lines of action are different: they form a couple. Torque = force × perpendicular distance between the lines of action = qE × AC.
    [1]
  3. From the right triangle ABC, AC = AB sinθ = 2l sinθ, so τ = qE × 2l sinθ = (q × 2l)E sinθ = pE sinθ, where p = q(2l).
    [1]
Answer
τ = pE sinθ (vectorially τ = p × E).
Common slip Taking the arm as 2l cosθ: the forces are along E, so the perpendicular separation is 2l sinθ.
Marking note The key writes AC/AB = sinθ and AB = L; the length of the dipole may be written as 2l or L.

Concepts to recall

  • Electric dipole: two equal and opposite charges ±q a distance 2l apart; dipole moment p = q × 2l, direction −q to +q.
  • Superposition: E = E1 + E2. On the axis the nearer charge dominates; on the equatorial line E = kp/(r2 + l2)3/2, directed opposite to p.
  • Short dipole: Eaxial = 2Eequatorial at equal distance.
  • Torque of a couple = (one force) × (perpendicular distance between the forces).
  • τ is maximum (pE) when θ = 90° and zero when θ = 0° or 180°; stable equilibrium at θ = 0°.

V = Q/(4πε0r)

An infinite plane metallic sheet having surface charge density ‘+σ’ is placed in vacuum. P is a point at a small distance ‘r’ to its right.
(i) Write an expression for intensity of electric field at point P.
(ii) Now, an identical charged sheet having surface charge density ‘−σ’ is placed parallel to the first sheet such that the point P is to its left at the same distance ‘r’. (The point P lies between the two plates.)
(a) What is the resultant intensity of electric field at point P?
(b) What is its direction?
  1. (i) Gauss’s law for an infinite sheet gives E = σ/2ε0, independent of r, directed away from the sheet (to the right at P).
    [1]
  2. (ii)(a) Between the sheets the field of the +σ sheet (σ/2ε0, away from it) and that of the −σ sheet (σ/2ε0, towards it) point the same way and add: E = σ/2ε0 + σ/2ε0 = σ/ε0.
    [1]
  3. (ii)(b) The direction is perpendicular to the sheets, from the positively charged sheet towards the negatively charged sheet (to the right).
    [1]
Answer
(i) E = σ/2ε0; (ii)(a) E = σ/ε0; (b) from the +σ sheet to the −σ sheet.
Marking note The question’s bracket places P between the two sheets; had P been outside, the answer would have been zero.

Concepts to recall

  • Infinite plane sheet (Gauss’s law, pill-box): E = σ/2ε0, uniform, directed away from a positive sheet and towards a negative one.
  • Two oppositely charged parallel sheets: field σ/ε0 between them (fields add), zero outside (fields cancel): the parallel plate capacitor.
  • Superposition: add the two fields as vectors.
