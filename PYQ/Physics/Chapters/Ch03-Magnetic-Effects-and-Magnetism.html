What to revise

  • Proofs/derivations: field at the centre of a circular coil (Biot–Savart) and field of a long straight wire (Ampère's law) – one of them is set as a derivation in four of the seven papers (board 2024, 2025 Improvement, 2026 and specimen 2026); board 2025 asks the coil result as a numerical.
  • Numericals: converting a galvanometer into a voltmeter or ammeter, force between parallel wires, motion of a charge in B (radius, frequency), field at a point due to two wires.
  • Magnetism of materials: susceptibility against temperature, Curie temperature, soft iron against steel, relative permeability.
  • Habit: use the right-hand rule for direction every time, and say 'perpendicular to the plane' in the answer.

Question 1(A)(iii) · Specimen 2027[1]
In Figure 2 below, CD is an infinitely long and straight metallic wire carrying a current I. Point P is at a perpendicular distance r from it. Which combination of I and r gives maximum magnetic field at point P, pointing into the plane of the paper?
(a)I = 4 A towards C, with r = 0·500 m
(b)I = 4 A towards D, with r = 0·250 m
(c)I = 8 A towards C, with r = 0·125 m
(d)I = 8 A towards C, with r = 0·050 m
  1. Direction: P is to the right of the wire. For a current towards C (upwards) the right-hand rule gives B into the paper on the right of the wire, so (b), with the current towards D, gives B out of the paper and is rejected.
    [½]
  2. Magnitude B = μ0I/2πr ∝ I/r: (a) 8, (c) 64, (d) 160 (in A m−1). The largest is (d).
    [½]
Answer
(d) I = 8 A towards C, with r = 0·050 m
Common slip Comparing only I/r and overlooking direction: option (b) has the current towards D, giving B out of the paper.

Concepts to recall

  • Straight wire: B = μ0I/(2πr); direction by the right-hand grip rule (thumb along I, fingers curl along B).
  • Check the direction first, then compare I/r.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (16)ISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(ii)
Question 1(A)(ii) · Board 2026[1]
A collimated beam of protons is travelling with a constant velocity along the X-axis. A current-carrying solenoid has its axis along the X-axis too. If the beam now enters the solenoid along its axis, it will:
(a)describe a circular path.
(b)get deflected along the Z-axis.
(c)get deflected along the Y-axis.
(d)continue to travel undeflected.
  1. Inside a long solenoid the field B is uniform and parallel to the axis, i.e. along the X-axis.
    [½]
  2. The protons move along the X-axis, so the angle between v and B is 0°. Force F = qvB sin 0° = 0, so the beam is not deflected.
    [½]
Answer
(d) continue to travel undeflected.

Concepts to recall

  • Magnetic force on a moving charge: F = q(v × B), magnitude qvB sinθ.
  • No force when v is parallel or antiparallel to B; a circle needs v ⊥ B; a helix when oblique.
  • Field inside a long solenoid is uniform and along its axis (B = μ0nI).
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (16)ISC 2026 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(iv)
Question 1(A)(iv) · Board 2026[1]
Magnetic susceptibility (χ) of a material is found to decrease with the rise in its temperature. The material is:
(a)diamagnetic.
(b)paramagnetic.
(c)ferromagnetic.
(d)not magnetic.
  1. Curie’s law for a paramagnetic material: χ = C/T, so χ falls as the temperature rises. Diamagnetic susceptibility is (nearly) independent of temperature.
    [1]
Answer
(b) paramagnetic.
Marking note The textbook pairing is “χ ∝ 1/T → paramagnetic”; a ferromagnet is not described by the simple Curie law.

Concepts to recall

  • Diamagnetic: χ small, negative, independent of T. Paramagnetic: χ small, positive, χ = C/T (Curie law). Ferromagnetic: χ large, follows χ = C/(T − Tc) above the Curie temperature.
  • A material whose χ varies as 1/T is the paramagnet.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (16)ISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(ii)
Question 1(A)(ii) · Specimen 2026[1]
Three straight, parallel wires are coplanar and perpendicular to the plane of the page. The currents I1 and I3 are directed out of the page. If wire 3 experiences no force due to the currents I1 and I2, then the current in wire 2 is:
(a)I2 = 2I1 and directed into the page
(b)I2 = 0·5I1 and directed into the page
(c)I2 = 2I1 and directed out of the page
(d)I2 = 0·5I1 and directed out of the page
  1. Force on wire 3 is zero only if the net field at wire 3 is zero: B1 and B2 must be equal and opposite.
    [½]
  2. Wire 1 is at distance 2r, wire 2 at distance r from wire 3: μ0I1/(2π · 2r) = μ0I2/(2πr) ⇒ I2 = I1/2 = 0·5 I1.
    [½]
  3. Both wires lie on the same side of wire 3, so for the fields to oppose, the currents must be opposite: I1 is out, therefore I2 is into the page.
Answer
(b) I2 = 0·5 I1 and directed into the page
Marking note The printed CISCE key says (c), 2I1 out of the page. That cannot be right: a current out of the page in wire 2 produces a field at wire 3 in the same sense as wire 1’s, so nothing cancels, and wire 2 is the nearer wire so it needs the smaller current. The balance gives (b). Read from Figure 1, wire 2 is midway between wires 1 and 3. A candidate should write (b); expect the key’s (c) to be questioned.

Concepts to recall

  • Field of a long straight wire B = μ0I/(2πd); direction by the right-hand grip rule. Both wires lie on the same side of wire 3, so their fields at wire 3 are in the same sense when the currents are alike.
  • Zero force on a wire ⇔ zero resultant field at it. Compare the two fields: the nearer wire needs the smaller current.
  • Parallel currents attract, antiparallel repel: the same fact seen as a force.

The variation of magnetic susceptibility (χ) with absolute temperature (T) for a diamagnetic substance is:
(a)
(b)
(c)

Question 1(A)(ii) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
Magnetic field inside a long current carrying solenoid is B0. If each of the current (I) and the number of turns (N) in this solenoid are doubled, magnetic field B1 inside it becomes:
(a)B1 = B0/4
(b)B1 = B0/2
(c)B1 = B0
(d)B1 = 4B0
  1. Field inside a long solenoid B = μ0nI = μ0(N/l)I, with the length l unchanged.
    [½]
  2. N → 2N and I → 2I make B 2 × 2 = 4 times: B1 = 4B0.
    [½]
Answer
(d) B1 = 4B0
Marking note The question keeps the solenoid the same, so n doubles; a candidate who argues that the length also doubled would get B0×2, but the paper clearly intends 4B0.

Concepts to recall

  • B = μ0nI, where n = N/l is turns per unit length.
  • Doubling N on the same length doubles n; doubling the number of turns by lengthening the solenoid would not change n.

Question 1(A)(iii) · Board 2025 Imp.[1]
Study the data given below.
MaterialRelative Permeability

The material most suitable to make permanent magnets is:
(a)P
(b)Q
(c)R

  1. P has μr slightly less than 1 (diamagnetic) and Q has μr slightly more than 1 (paramagnetic); neither can be magnetised appreciably, so they are ruled out.
    [½]
  2. R and S are ferromagnetic. S (μr = 280 000, like permalloy or mu-metal) is a soft magnetic material: it magnetises and demagnetises very easily, suiting transformer cores and electromagnets.     [½]

Question 1(A)(ii) · Board 2025[1]
The relative permeability of substance ‘X’ is slightly less than one and that of substance ‘Y’ is slightly more than one. Then:
(a)‘X’ is paramagnetic and ‘Y’ is ferromagnetic.
(b)‘X’ is diamagnetic and ‘Y’ is ferromagnetic.
(c)‘X’ is paramagnetic and ‘Y’ is diamagnetic.

  1. μr = 1 + χ. μr slightly less than 1 means χ is small and negative: X is diamagnetic. μr slightly greater than 1 means χ is small and positive: Y is paramagnetic (a ferromagnet has μr of hundreds or thousands).

(d) X is diamagnetic and Y is paramagnetic.

Question 1(A)(iv) · Board 2025[1]
A student has made connections, as shown in Figure 2 below, so that the wires MN and ST repel each other. But it is observed that they are attracting each other. What change should the student make for the wires to repel each other?
(a)Reverse the terminals of batteries E1 and E2.
(b)Reverse the terminals of battery E1 or E2.
(c)Choose supply voltage such that E1 = E2.
(d)Add key and ammeter to the circuit.
  1. Both batteries are connected the same way round, so the currents in MN and ST are in the same direction (both downward), and parallel currents attract. Wires repel when the currents are opposite, so the current in just one of them must be reversed: reverse the terminals of E1 or of E2. (Reversing both would reverse both currents and they would still attract.)
    [1]
Answer
(b) Reverse the terminals of battery E1 or E2.
Common slip Choosing (a): reversing both currents leaves the relative direction unchanged.

Concepts to recall

  • Force between parallel currents: F/l = μ0I1I2/2πd; attraction for currents in the same direction, repulsion for opposite directions.
  • The force depends on the product I1I2, so changing the size of the emf (option c) or adding meters (option d) cannot change attraction into repulsion.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (16)ISC Specimen 2025 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(ii)
Question 1(A)(ii) · Specimen 2025[1]
A circular coil having N turns of radius R carrying a current I is used to produce a magnetic field B at its centre O. If this coil is opened and rewound such that the radius of the newly formed coil is 2R, carrying the same current I, what will be the magnetic field at the centre O?
(a)2B
(b)B
(c)B/2
(d)B/4
  1. The length of wire is fixed: N(2πR) = N′(2π·2R), so the new number of turns is N′ = N/2.
    [½]
  2. B = μ0NI/2R, so B′ = μ0(N/2)I/(2 × 2R) = B/4.
    [½]
Answer
(d) B/4
Common slip Doubling R and forgetting that N halves gives B/2, option (c).

Concepts to recall

  • Field at the centre of a coil: B = μ0NI/2R (N turns, radius R).
  • Rewinding the same wire into a bigger loop reduces the number of turns: N ∝ 1/R for a fixed length.

Question 1(A)(iii) · Board 2024[1]
Lorentz force in vector form is:
(a)F = B q v sin θ
(b)F = q (v × B)
(c)F = q (B × v)
(d)F = v (q × B)
  1. The magnetic force on a charge q moving with velocity v in a field B is F = q(v × B), perpendicular to both v and B.
    [½]

ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (16)ISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(iv)
Question 1(A)(iv) · Board 2024[1]
Assertion: When an electric current is passed through a moving coil galvanometer, its coil gets deflected.
Reason: A circular coil produces a uniform magnetic field around itself when an electric current is passed through it.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Assertion is false and Reason is true.
  1. Assertion: true. The coil sits in the field of the permanent magnet and a current-carrying coil experiences a torque τ = NIAB sinθ, so it deflects.
    [½]
  2. Reason: false. The field of a circular coil is not uniform around it (it is a dipole-like field, strong near the wires and weak far away; only near the centre is it roughly uniform). In any case the deflection is due to the torque in the magnet’s field, not to the field of the coil.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Assertion is true and Reason is false.

Concepts to recall

  • Moving coil galvanometer: τ = NIAB (radial field), balanced by the spring torque kθ, so θ ∝ I.
  • A circular current loop acts as a magnetic dipole of moment m = NIA; its field is non-uniform.
  • Assertion-reason: test each statement separately, then ask whether the Reason explains the Assertion.
ONE-MARK QUESTIONS (16)ISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv) · Board 2024[1]
Explain the meaning of the following statement: Curie temperature for soft iron is 770°C.
  1. Soft iron is ferromagnetic only up to 770°C: below this temperature it has domains and is strongly magnetised by a field.
    [½]
  2. At and above 770°C the domain structure is destroyed by thermal agitation and soft iron becomes paramagnetic (χ = C/(T − Tc)).
    [½]
Answer
Above 770°C soft iron loses its ferromagnetism and behaves as a paramagnetic substance.

Concepts to recall

  • Curie temperature Tc: the temperature above which a ferromagnetic material becomes paramagnetic (Curie–Weiss law χ = C/(T − Tc)).

Question 5 · Board 2026[2]
(i) Write an expression of the Biot–Savart law in vector form.
(ii) State Ampère’s circuital law.
  1. (i) dB = (μ0/4π) I (dl × r)/r3, where dB is the field at a point at position vector r from the current element I dl.
    [1]
  2. (ii) The line integral of the magnetic field B around any closed path equals μ0 times the total current threading the surface bounded by the path: ∮B·dl = μ0Ienc.
    [1]
Answer
(i) dB = (μ0/4π)I(dl × r)/r3; (ii) ∮B·dl = μ0Ienc.

TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (5)ISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q5
Question 5 · Specimen 2026[2]
Two moving coil galvanometers G1 and G2 are identical except that they have 50 turns and 20 turns and resistances of 10 Ω and 1 Ω respectively. Perform the necessary calculations to check which one has the greater voltage sensitivity.
  1. Voltage sensitivity β = θ/V = NBA/(kR), where k is the torsion constant and R the resistance of the galvanometer.
    [1]
  2. Since B, A and k are the same: β(G1) = 50/10 = 5 (BA/k); β(G2) = 20/1 = 20 (BA/k). β(G2) is four times β(G1): G2 has the greater voltage sensitivity.
    [1]
Answer
G2 (20 against 5, in units of BA/k).
Marking note Printed key uses β = BAN/(CR) with C the torsion constant. Any equivalent symbol is accepted.

Concepts to recall

  • Current sensitivity Is = θ/I = NBA/k; voltage sensitivity Vs = θ/V = Is/R = NBA/(kR).
  • Doubling the turns doubles Is but also doubles R, so Vs is unchanged: the ratio N/R decides.
TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (5)ISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q5
Question 5 · Board 2025 Imp.[2]
(i) How much force per unit length acts on a long current carrying conductor X due to a current flowing through another similar conductor Y, kept parallel to it in vacuum? (Derivation of the formula is not required.)
(ii) Use this expression to define an ampere.
  1. (i) For parallel conductors at separation d carrying currents IX and IY: F/l = μ0IXIY/(2πd), attractive for currents in the same direction and repulsive for opposite directions.
    [1]
  2. (ii) If IX = IY = 1 A and d = 1 m, F/l = (4π × 10−7 × 1 × 1)/(2π × 1) = 2 × 10−7 N m−1. Hence one ampere is that steady current which, flowing in each of two infinitely long, straight, parallel conductors of negligible cross-section placed 1 m apart in vacuum, makes each exert a force of 2 × 10−7 N on every metre of the other.
    [1]
Answer
(i) F/l = μ0IXIY/(2πd); (ii) 1 A gives 2 × 10−7 N m−1 at 1 m separation.
Common slip Omitting “per metre length”, “infinitely long” or “in vacuum” in the definition.

Concepts to recall

  • Force between parallel currents: same direction attract, opposite repel; F/l = μ0I1I2/(2πd), μ0 = 4π × 10−7 T m A−1.
  • The ampere is the SI base unit defined through this force (the 2019 redefinition fixes e, but the textbook definition is the one asked).

Question 5 · Board 2025[2]
Magnetic field at the centre of a circular coil is B. Calculate the magnetic field at the same point when each of the current, number of turns of the coil and its radius is doubled.
  1. Field at the centre of a coil of N turns, radius R, current I: B = μ0NI/2R.
    [1]
  2. New field: B′ = μ0(2N)(2I)/(2 × 2R) = 2 × μ0NI/2R = 2B.
    [1]
Answer
B′ = 2B.

TWO-MARK QUESTIONS (5)ISC Specimen 2025 Physics Paper 1, Q5
Question 5 · Specimen 2025[2]
Figure 5 shows two long, parallel wires carrying current in the same direction such that I1 < I2.
(i) In which direction will wire I1 move?
(ii) If the direction of the current I2 is reversed, in which direction will the wire I1 move now?
  1. (i) Parallel conductors carrying currents in the same direction attract. Wire I (carrying I1) is pulled towards wire II (carrying I2).
    [1]
  2. (ii) With I2 reversed the currents are opposite, so the wires repel: wire I moves away from wire II.
    [1]
Answer
(i) wire I moves towards wire II (attraction); (ii) wire I moves away from wire II (repulsion).
Marking note CISCE’s key says wire I “will move towards I1” and “away from I1”. That is a slip: it is wire I1 that moves, towards (or away from) the other wire carrying I2. Answers that name the direction relative to the other wire earn the marks.

Concepts to recall

  • Force per unit length between parallel wires: F/l = μ0I1I2/2πd: attractive for parallel currents, repulsive for antiparallel (this defines the ampere).
  • The two forces are equal and opposite (Newton’s third law) even though I1 ≠ I2; the condition I1 < I2 only decides who has the stronger field.
THREE-MARK QUESTIONS (14)ISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q11
Question 11 · Specimen 2027[3]
An alpha particle is moving with a velocity v. It enters a uniform magnetic field (B) as shown below. The magnetic field is perpendicular and into the plane of the paper. A uniform electric field is applied in the same region as the magnetic field so that the alpha particle passes undeviated through the combined fields.
(i) What should be the direction of the electric field?
(ii) Without any change in the electric and magnetic field, the alpha particle is replaced by a proton moving with a velocity v. Will there be any change in the path of the particle? Give a reason for your answer.
  1. (i) Magnetic force on the positive alpha particle Fm = 2e(v × B): with v to the right and B into the paper it points upwards (in the plane of the paper).
    [½]
  2. For no deviation the electric force must be equal and opposite, i.e. downwards. The charge is positive, so E is in the plane of the paper, pointing vertically downwards.
    [1]
  3. (ii) No deviation requires qE = qvB, i.e. v = E/B: the condition does not involve the charge or the mass.
    [1]
  4. The proton has the same v and the same E and B, so the condition is still met and the proton also travels undeviated: there is no change in the path.
    [½]
Answer
(i) E vertically downwards in the plane of the paper; (ii) no change: the proton too goes undeviated since v = E/B is independent of q and m.
Common slip Forgetting that the alpha particle is positive and putting E upwards.
Marking note Equivalent statement “E perpendicular to v and B, directed downwards” earns the mark. In (ii) it is the proton’s velocity being equal to the alpha’s that matters (the question gives it as v).

Concepts to recall

  • Lorentz force F = q(E + v × B); the velocity selector needs qE = qvB, so v = E/B.
  • Direction of v × B for a positive charge by the right-hand rule: x × (−z) = +y (up).
  • Charge and mass cancel: any particle with this velocity passes undeflected.

(i) State any one difference between the nature of magnetic field inside a current carrying solenoid and that along the axis of a current carrying circular coil.
(ii) A current is passed through a circular coil A in a clockwise direction, as shown in Figure 7. B is another circular coil which does not carry any current. However, when it is moved towards the coil A, current is induced in it. What is the direction of this induced current?
(iii) Why is steel preferred to soft iron in making permanent magnets?
  1. (i) The field inside a long solenoid is uniform (same everywhere), whereas the field along the axis of a circular coil is non-uniform (maximum at the centre, falling off along the axis).
    [1]
  2. (ii) The flux through B, due to the clockwise current in A (field directed away from the observer), increases as B approaches. By Lenz’s law the induced current opposes this by producing a field towards the observer, so it is anticlockwise as seen by the observer.
    [1]

    [1]
Answer
(i) uniform inside a solenoid, non-uniform along the axis of a coil; (ii) anticlockwise as seen by the observer; (iii) steel has high coercivity.

Concepts to recall

  • Solenoid: B = μ0nI inside, uniform; circular coil on the axis: B = μ0NIR2/2(R2 + x2)3/2.

  • Permanent magnets: high retentivity and high coercivity (steel, alnico); electromagnets and transformer cores: low coercivity, low hysteresis loss (soft iron).
