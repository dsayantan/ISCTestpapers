SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i)[1]
When a battery is connected between the terminals P and Q, as shown in Figure 1 below, it is found that no current flows through the 5 Ω resistor. Then, the value of resistor X is:
(a)10 Ω
(b)20 Ω
(c)25 Ω
(d)45 Ω
  1. No current in the 5 Ω resistor means B and D are at the same potential: the network is a balanced Wheatstone bridge with arms AB = 10 Ω, BC = 25 Ω, AD = 10 Ω, DC = X. Balance: 10/25 = 10/X, so X = 25 Ω.
    [1]
Answer
(c) 25 Ω
Common slip Adding the 10 Ω and 25 Ω (giving 35 Ω or 45 Ω) instead of comparing ratios.

Concepts to recall

  • Wheatstone bridge balance: P/Q = R/S; then the galvanometer arm (here the 5 Ω resistor) carries no current and may be ignored.
  • No current in a resistor ⇔ no potential difference across it: VB = VD.
  • Take the ratios along the two branches in the same order (top to bottom): 10 : 25 against 10 : X.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(ii)
Question 1(A)(ii)[1]
The relative permeability of substance ‘X’ is slightly less than one and that of substance ‘Y’ is slightly more than one. Then:
(a)‘X’ is paramagnetic and ‘Y’ is ferromagnetic.
(b)‘X’ is diamagnetic and ‘Y’ is ferromagnetic.
(c)‘X’ is paramagnetic and ‘Y’ is diamagnetic.
(d)‘X’ is diamagnetic and ‘Y’ is paramagnetic.
  1. μr = 1 + χ. μr slightly less than 1 means χ is small and negative: X is diamagnetic. μr slightly greater than 1 means χ is small and positive: Y is paramagnetic (a ferromagnet has μr of hundreds or thousands).
    [1]
Answer
(d) X is diamagnetic and Y is paramagnetic.

Concepts to recall

  • Relative permeability μr = 1 + χm.
  • Diamagnetic: χ small, negative, μr < 1 slightly. Paramagnetic: χ small, positive, μr > 1 slightly. Ferromagnetic: χ large and positive, μr ≫ 1.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(iii)
Question 1(A)(iii)[1]
If kinetic energy of moving electrons is made four times, then their de Broglie wavelength becomes:
(a)eight times.
(b)four times.
(c)two times.
(d)half.
  1. λ = h/p = h/√(2mK), so λ ∝ 1/√K. For K → 4K, λ → λ/√4 = λ/2: the wavelength becomes half.
    [1]
Answer
(d) half.
Common slip Using λ ∝ 1/K and answering ‘one-fourth’; the dependence is on the square root.

Concepts to recall

  • de Broglie wavelength λ = h/p; with p = √(2mK), λ = h/√(2mK).
  • Quick rule: λ ∝ 1/√K at fixed mass; λ ∝ 1/√V for a charge accelerated through V.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(iv)
Question 1(A)(iv)[1]
A student has made connections, as shown in Figure 2 below, so that the wires MN and ST repel each other. But it is observed that they are attracting each other. What change should the student make for the wires to repel each other?
(a)Reverse the terminals of batteries E1 and E2.
(b)Reverse the terminals of battery E1 or E2.
(c)Choose supply voltage such that E1 = E2.
(d)Add key and ammeter to the circuit.
  1. Both batteries are connected the same way round, so the currents in MN and ST are in the same direction (both downward), and parallel currents attract. Wires repel when the currents are opposite, so the current in just one of them must be reversed: reverse the terminals of E1 or of E2. (Reversing both would reverse both currents and they would still attract.)
    [1]
Answer
(b) Reverse the terminals of battery E1 or E2.
Common slip Choosing (a): reversing both currents leaves the relative direction unchanged.

Concepts to recall

  • Force between parallel currents: F/l = μ0I1I2/2πd; attraction for currents in the same direction, repulsion for opposite directions.
  • The force depends on the product I1I2, so changing the size of the emf (option c) or adding meters (option d) cannot change attraction into repulsion.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(v)
Question 1(A)(v)[1]
N-type semiconductor is that which has:
(a)majority of holes as charge carriers.
(b)majority of free electrons as charge carriers.
(c)trivalent element added as an impurity.
(d)an equal number of holes and free electrons.
  1. An n-type semiconductor is made by doping a pentavalent impurity (P, As, Sb) into Si or Ge. Each donor atom contributes a free electron, so free electrons are the majority carriers and holes the minority carriers.
    [1]
Answer
(b) majority of free electrons as charge carriers.

Concepts to recall

  • n-type: pentavalent donor, majority carriers electrons, minority carriers holes. p-type: trivalent acceptor, majority carriers holes.
  • The crystal as a whole stays electrically neutral; ‘n’ stands for negative carriers, not for a charged crystal.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(vi)
Question 1(A)(vi)[1]
Given below are two statements marked, Assertion and Reason. Read the two statements and choose the correct option.
Assertion: When a convex lens made of glass is completely immersed in water, its focal length increases.
Reason: Refractive index of glass with respect to water is greater than that of glass with respect to air.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Lens-maker’s formula: 1/f = (mng − 1)(1/R1 − 1/R2), where mng = ng/nm is the index of glass relative to the surrounding medium.
    [½]
  2. Air: ang = 1·5. Water: wng = 1·5/1·33 = 1·13, which is smaller. So (n − 1) falls from 0·5 to about 0·13 and f becomes about four times larger: the Assertion is true. The Reason says the relative index is greater in water than in air; that is false.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Assertion is true and Reason is false.
Marking note With nglass = 1·5 and nwater = 1·33, fwater/fair = 0·5/0·13 ≈ 4.

Concepts to recall

  • Focal length of a lens in a medium: 1/f = (nlens/nmedium − 1)(1/R1 − 1/R2); the closer the two indices, the weaker the lens.
  • A lens with the same index as its surroundings has infinite focal length (it disappears).
  • Assertion–reason: check each statement on its own first, then whether the second explains the first.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(vii)
Question 1(A)(vii)[1]
Given below are two statements marked, Assertion and Reason. Read the two statements and choose the correct option.
Assertion: Diffraction of light is difficult to observe in everyday situations but can be observed in laboratory conditions.
Reason: To produce diffraction of waves, size of an obstacle must be comparable to the wavelength of the waves.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Reason: appreciable diffraction needs an obstacle or aperture of size comparable to λ; this is true. Light has λ of about 5 × 10−7 m, whereas everyday obstacles are enormously larger, so bending is not noticed; in the laboratory, slits of width of the order of λ are used. So the Assertion is true and the Reason is exactly why: (a).
    [1]
Answer
(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.

Concepts to recall

  • Diffraction is marked when the aperture width a is comparable to λ (a ∼ λ); the first minimum of a single slit is at sinθ = λ/a.
  • Ray optics holds when a ≫ λ; that is why light seems to travel in straight lines (and sound, with λ of metres, bends round corners).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i)[1]
How does the resistance of a semiconductor crystal vary with its temperature?
  1. The resistance of a semiconductor decreases as its temperature rises (negative temperature coefficient): heating breaks covalent bonds and generates more electron–hole pairs, so the number of carriers increases much faster than the lattice scattering.
    [1]
Answer
Resistance decreases with rise in temperature.

Concepts to recall

  • Metals: ρ increases with T (more collisions, n nearly fixed). Semiconductors: n grows roughly as e−Eg/2kT, so ρ falls with T.
  • Negative temperature coefficient of resistance: thermistors.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii)[1]
Figure 3 below shows an ideal transformer. Explain why current flowing through secondary coil is greater than that in primary coil.
  1. The figure shows fewer turns on the secondary than on the primary (Ns < Np): a step-down transformer. For an ideal transformer there is no power loss, VpIp = VsIs, and Vs/Vp = Ns/Np < 1. Hence Is = Ip(Np/Ns) > Ip: the voltage is stepped down, so the current is stepped up by the same factor.
    [1]
Answer
Ns < Np, so Vs < Vp and, since VpIp = VsIs, Is > Ip.

Concepts to recall

  • Ideal transformer: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np = Ip/Is; output power = input power.
  • Step-down: Ns < Np, lower voltage and higher current. Step-up: the opposite.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii)[1]
State any one difference between a primary rainbow and a secondary rainbow.
  1. Any one: (1) A primary rainbow is formed by one internal reflection inside each drop; a secondary rainbow by two internal reflections. (2) In the primary the red is on the outer edge and violet on the inner; in the secondary the order is reversed (violet outer, red inner). (3) The primary is brighter; the secondary is fainter (more light lost in the extra reflection). (4) The primary subtends about 42° (red) at the observer’s eye, the secondary about 51°.
    [1]
Answer
Primary: one internal reflection, red outside; secondary: two internal reflections, violet outside, fainter.
Marking note Award the mark for any one correct distinction.

Concepts to recall

  • A rainbow is dispersion plus total internal reflection (internal reflection) inside raindrops; the observer has the Sun behind.
  • Each extra internal reflection reverses the colour order and costs intensity.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv)[1]
Hubble telescope employs a large parabolic mirror as an objective. State any one advantage of using a mirror in place of a lens in such a telescope.
  1. Any one: a mirror has no chromatic aberration (reflection does not depend on wavelength); a parabolic mirror is free of spherical aberration; a large mirror can be supported from the back and is lighter than a large lens, and it has only one surface to be ground and polished, so a large aperture (more light, better resolution) is possible.
    [1]
Answer
No chromatic aberration (or: large aperture is easier to make and support; no spherical aberration with a parabolic shape).
Marking note Any one of the listed advantages earns the mark.

Concepts to recall

  • Reflecting telescope: objective is a concave (parabolic) mirror; resolving power ∝ aperture, so a large aperture is needed for faint, distant objects.
  • Lens defects: chromatic aberration (n depends on λ) and spherical aberration; mirrors do not show the first.

Question 1(B)(v)[1]
Name any one phenomenon where moving particles behave like waves.
  1. Electron diffraction (Davisson–Germer experiment) shows moving electrons behaving like waves; also acceptable: electron microscope, neutron diffraction, interference of electrons or atoms in a double-slit arrangement.
    [1]
Answer
Diffraction of electrons (Davisson–Germer experiment).

  • Confirmed by Davisson–Germer (electron diffraction from a nickel crystal) and G.P. Thomson (electrons through thin foil).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(vi)
Question 1(B)(vi)[1]
What is the minimum energy a gamma ray (γ) photon should possess to produce an electron-positron pair?
  1. Pair production needs at least the rest energy of the pair: Emin = 2mec2 = 2 × 9·1 × 10−31 × (3 × 108)2 = 1·64 × 10-13 J = 1·02 MeV.
    [1]
Answer
Emin = 2mec2 ≈ 1·02 MeV (1·64 × 10−13 J).
Marking note The constants table of this paper lists the proton mass but not the electron mass; me = 9·1 × 10−31 kg is the standard value. Accept 1·02 MeV. (Using the proton mass would give 1·88 GeV, which is the proton–antiproton threshold.)

Concepts to recall

  • Pair production: γ → e− + e+ in the field of a nucleus; threshold E = 2mec2 = 1·02 MeV (0·511 MeV for each particle).
  • Convert with 1 eV = 1·6 × 10−19 J.

Question 1(B)(vii)[1]
In an energy band diagram of a certain material, forbidden band is absent. Identify this material.
  1. If there is no forbidden gap, the valence band and conduction band overlap, so electrons move freely into the conduction band: the material is a conductor (a metal).
    [1]
Answer
A conductor (metal).

Concepts to recall

  • Band picture: insulators Eg > 3 eV; semiconductors Eg ∼ 0·1–3 eV; conductors: bands overlap, Eg = 0.

SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q2
Question 2[2]
(i) (a) What is the effect on capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor if the distance between its plates is increased?
(b) How will capacitance of a capacitor change if a dielectric slab is introduced between its plates?
OR
(ii) Figures 4 and 5 represent the combination of two identical cells having negligible internal resistance.
(a) In which of the two combinations, emf of the battery is greater?
(b) When a resistor ‘R’ is connected between the terminals P and Q, I1 and I2 are the currents flowing through ‘R’ in Figure 4 and Figure 5 respectively. Obtain the ratio I1/I2.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) C = ε0A/d: if d is increased, the capacitance decreases (inversely proportional to d).
    [1]
  2. (b) With a dielectric of constant K filling the gap, C = Kε0A/d = KC0, so the capacitance increases (by the factor K).
    [1]
Answer
(a) Capacitance decreases; (b) capacitance increases (C = KC0).

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) Figure 4: the two cells are in series, with the + plate of one joined to the − plate of the next, so the emf adds: Eeq = E + E = 2E. Figure 5: identical cells in parallel give Eeq = E. The emf is greater in Figure 4.
    [1]
  2. (b) Internal resistance is negligible: Figure 4, I1 = 2E/R; Figure 5, I2 = E/R.
    [½]
  3. I1/I2 = (2E/R)/(E/R) = 2.
    [½]
Answer
(a) Figure 4; (b) I1/I2 = 2.
Marking note In both figures the long plate of each cell is on the left, so the series pair is aiding and the parallel pair has like terminals joined.

Concepts to recall

  • Parallel plate capacitor: C = ε0A/d in vacuum; with a dielectric slab filling the gap, C = Kε0A/d.
  • The dielectric is polarised; the induced field opposes the applied field, so for a given charge the potential difference drops and C = Q/V rises.
  • Cells in series (aiding): emf = sum, internal resistances add. Identical cells in parallel: emf stays E, internal resistance becomes r/n.
  • With r = 0 the current is simply emf/R.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q3
Question 3[2]
In case of a short electric dipole:
(i) What is the locus of a point having zero potential?
(ii) If electric field intensity at a point in axial position is E1 and at an equidistant point in equatorial position is E2, what is the ratio E1/E2?
  1. (i) The potential of a dipole at a point at angle θ to the axis is V = p cosθ/(4πε0r2); it is zero for θ = 90°. The locus of zero potential is the equatorial plane of the dipole (the plane perpendicular to the dipole axis through its centre).
    [1]
  2. (ii) Axial point: E1 = 2p/(4πε0r3); equatorial point at the same distance: E2 = p/(4πε0r3).
    [½]
  3. E1/E2 = 2.
    [½]
Answer
(i) The equatorial plane of the dipole; (ii) E1/E2 = 2.
Marking note On the axis itself (away from the centre) the potential is not zero, because the nearer charge dominates; the equatorial plane, which passes through the centre, is the full locus.

Concepts to recall

  • Short dipole: axial field 2kp/r3 (along p), equatorial field kp/r3 (opposite to p), with k = 1/4πε0.
  • Dipole potential V = kp cosθ/r2: zero on the equatorial plane, positive on the +q side.
  • The ratio 2 : 1 is the one to remember for the same distance.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q4
Question 4[2]
(i) Figure 6 below shows an electric circuit. Apply Kirchhoff’s laws to calculate:
(a) current ‘I’ flowing through the 2 Ω resistor.
(b) emf of the cell ‘E’.
OR
(ii) In a potentiometer experiment, a cell of emf 1·25 V gives a balance point at 35 cm mark of the wire. If this cell is replaced by another cell, the balance point is at 63 cm mark. Calculate the emf of the second cell.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) Kirchhoff’s current law at junction Q: current in = current out. In: 3 A (from P) + I (from S, up the 2 Ω branch); out: 4 A (through the 1 Ω resistor). So 3 + I = 4 and I = 1 A.
    [1]
  2. (b) Kirchhoff’s voltage law for the loop Q → R → S → Q: the 4 A current flows from Q to R through 1 Ω (−4 V); the cell E drives the current from R to S (+E); from S to Q the 1 A flows through 2 Ω in the direction of travel (−2 V). −4 + E − 2 = 0, so E = 6 V.
    [1]
Answer
(a) I = 1 A (from S to Q); (b) E = 6 V.
Common slip Sign slip in the loop equation: the 2 Ω branch is traversed with the current (−2) in loop QRSQ, but against it (+2) in loop PQSP.
Marking note The arrows in the figure fix the polarity of E: it drives current from R to S, i.e. its positive terminal is at S.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. In a potentiometer the emf of a cell is proportional to its balancing length (V = kl, same potential gradient k): E1/E2 = l1/l2.
    [1]
  2. E2 = E1l2/l1 = 1·25 × 63/35 = 2·25 V.
    [1]
Answer
E2 = 2·25 V.
Marking note Any equivalent method (potential gradient k = 1·25/35 V per cm) earns full marks.

Concepts to recall

  • KCL: ΣI at a junction = 0 (current in = current out).
  • KVL: Σ(emf) = Σ(IR) round a closed loop; take a rise in potential across a cell from − to + and a fall of IR across a resistor in the direction of current.
  • Check with a second loop: P → Q → S → P gives −3×4 + 1×2 + 10 = 0, which confirms the 10 V cell and I = 1 A.
  • Potentiometer principle: potential difference across a length l of a uniform wire carrying steady current is proportional to l, so E ∝ l at balance.
  • Comparison of emfs: E1/E2 = l1/l2; the working cell must have emf greater than both.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q5
Question 5[2]
Magnetic field at the centre of a circular coil is B. Calculate the magnetic field at the same point when each of the current, number of turns of the coil and its radius is doubled.
  1. Field at the centre of a coil of N turns, radius R, current I: B = μ0NI/2R.
    [1]
  2. New field: B′ = μ0(2N)(2I)/(2 × 2R) = 2 × μ0NI/2R = 2B.
    [1]
Answer
B′ = 2B.
Common slip Forgetting that R is in the denominator: doubling all three does not give 8B.

Concepts to recall

  • Centre of a circular coil: B = μ0NI/2R (from Biot–Savart; every element is at distance R and perpendicular to it).
  • Proportional reasoning: B ∝ N, B ∝ I, B ∝ 1/R; so the factor is 2 × 2 × ½ = 2.

Question 6[2]
The objective of a telescope consists of two lenses kept in contact. One lens has an optical power of +2·0 D whereas the other lens has an optical power of −1·5 D. Calculate focal length of the objective.
  1. For thin lenses in contact the powers add: P = P1 + P2 = +2·0 + (−1·5) = +0·5 D.
    [1]
  2. f = 1/P = 1/0·5 = 2 m (= 200 cm), converging.
    [1]
Answer
f = 2 m (200 cm).

Concepts to recall

  • Power P = 1/f (in metres), unit dioptre (D); converging lens positive, diverging negative.
  • Lenses in contact: 1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2, i.e. P = P1 + P2.
  • A telescope objective has a long focal length (large fo): magnification m = fo/fe.

A satellite sends signals to a dish antenna on the ground (see the image).

With reference to photoelectric effect, define the terms:

(ii) Work function.

SECTION C – 27 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q9
Question 9[3]
An infinite plane metallic sheet having surface charge density ‘+σ’ is placed in vacuum. P is a point at a small distance ‘r’ to its right.
(i) Write an expression for intensity of electric field at point P.
(ii) Now, an identical charged sheet having surface charge density ‘−σ’ is placed parallel to the first sheet such that the point P is to its left at the same distance ‘r’. (The point P lies between the two plates.)
(a) What is the resultant intensity of electric field at point P?
(b) What is its direction?
  1. (i) Gauss’s law for an infinite sheet gives E = σ/2ε0, independent of r, directed away from the sheet (to the right at P).
    [1]
  2. (ii)(a) Between the sheets the field of the +σ sheet (σ/2ε0, away from it) and that of the −σ sheet (σ/2ε0, towards it) point the same way and add: E = σ/2ε0 + σ/2ε0 = σ/ε0.
    [1]
  3. (ii)(b) The direction is perpendicular to the sheets, from the positively charged sheet towards the negatively charged sheet (to the right).
    [1]
Answer
(i) E = σ/2ε0; (ii)(a) E = σ/ε0; (b) from the +σ sheet to the −σ sheet.
Marking note The question’s bracket places P between the two sheets; had P been outside, the answer would have been zero.

Concepts to recall

  • Infinite plane sheet (Gauss’s law, pill-box): E = σ/2ε0, uniform, directed away from a positive sheet and towards a negative one.
  • Two oppositely charged parallel sheets: field σ/ε0 between them (fields add), zero outside (fields cancel): the parallel plate capacitor.
  • Superposition: add the two fields as vectors.
SECTION C – 27 MARKSISC 2025 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q10
Question 10[3]
(i) Harry sets up a circuit as shown in Figure 7 below. He measures potential difference ‘V’ across the variable resistor R with an instrument Y. He also measures current ‘I’ flowing through R with another instrument X.
(a) Identify the instruments X and Y.
(b) Using the graph of V against I shown below, calculate (1) emf (E) of the cell. (2) Internal resistance (r) of the cell.
OR
(ii) The graph below shows the variation of current density (J) with electric field (E) applied to two different metallic wires A and B.
(a) Which one of the wires, A or B, has higher resistivity?
(b) For a certain value of electric field (E), in which wire ‘A’ or ‘B’ is drift velocity greater? Give a reason for your answer.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) X is in series with the cell and R: it is an ammeter. Y is connected across R, in parallel: it is a voltmeter.
    [1]
  2. (b)(1) V = E − Ir. When I = 0 the terminal voltage equals the emf: the graph cuts the V axis at 2·5 V, so E = 2·5 V.
    [1]

  1. (a) J = σE, so the slope of a J–E line is the conductivity σ = 1/ρ. A has the smaller slope, hence the smaller conductivity and the higher resistivity: wire A.
    [1]

    [1]

(a) A; (b) B, because for the same E its J is greater (vd = J/ne).

  • Ammeter: low resistance, in series. Voltmeter: high resistance, in parallel with the element.

Question 14[3]
A parallel beam of light is travelling obliquely from an optically rarer medium to an optically denser medium.
(i) Draw a labelled diagram showing incident and refracted wavefronts. Mark angle of incidence as ‘i’ and angle of refraction as ‘r’.
(ii) Use Huygen’s wave theory to prove Snell’s law.
  1. (i) Diagram (as drawn): plane wavefront AB incident on the surface XY (rays bend towards the normal in the denser medium); refracted wavefront CE with rays; i and r marked between the rays and the normal. Wavefronts, rays and both angles correctly labelled.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Let the wavefront AB meet the surface at A first. In the time t that the point B of the wavefront takes to reach C, BC = v1t; meanwhile the secondary wavelet from A spreads into medium 2 to a radius AE = v2t. The tangent CE from C to this wavelet is the refracted wavefront.
    [1]
  3. In right-angled triangles ABC and AEC (∠BAC = i, ∠ACE = r): sin i = BC/AC = v1t/AC and sin r = AE/AC = v2t/AC. Dividing, sin i/sin r = v1/v2 = n2/n1 = constant, which is Snell’s law.
    [1]
Answer
sin i/sin r = v1/v2 = n2/n1 (Snell’s law).

Concepts to recall

  • Huygens’ principle: every point of a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets; the new wavefront is the common tangent (envelope) to them after time t.
  • Refractive index n = c/v, so a denser medium (smaller v) bends the wavefront towards the normal: r < i.
  • The angle between a wavefront and the surface equals the angle between its ray and the normal (both pairs perpendicular): that is why ∠BAC = i.

    [1]
  2. ε = (1/π) × 3 × π = 3 V.
    [1]
  3. (a)(2) The direction of the induced current is found by Lenz’s law (equivalently Fleming’s right-hand rule): here v × B points up the page, so the current in the rod flows from N to M and M is the positive end.
    [1]
  4. (b) Induced emf |ε| = L|dI/dt|, so L = ε/(dI/dt).
