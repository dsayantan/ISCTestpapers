SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i)[1]
Three cells having emfs 3 V, 1 V and 4 V are connected as shown in Figure 1 below. Their internal resistances are negligible. The equivalent emf between the terminals P and Q is:
(a)0 V
(b)4 V
(c)6 V
(d)8 V
  1. Read the plates: in all three cells the long plate (+) is on the left, so every branch has P as its positive end.
    [½]
  2. Upper branch: 3 V and 1 V in series aiding, 3 + 1 = 4 V. Lower branch: 4 V. Two equal emfs with negligible internal resistance in parallel give the same emf, so EPQ = 4 V.
    [½]
Answer
(b) 4 V
Common slip Adding all three (8 V), or reading the branches as opposing and getting 0 V.

Concepts to recall

  • Cells in series, same direction: emfs add. Cells in parallel (same polarity): equivalent emf is the common value when the emfs are equal.
  • Long plate of the symbol is the positive terminal; check polarity before adding or subtracting.
  • Opposite-polarity cells in a series chain would subtract.

Question 1(A)(ii)[1]
Magnetic field inside a long current carrying solenoid is B0. If each of the current (I) and the number of turns (N) in this solenoid are doubled, magnetic field B1 inside it becomes:
(a)B1 = B0/4
(b)B1 = B0/2
(c)B1 = B0
(d)B1 = 4B0
  1. Field inside a long solenoid B = μ0nI = μ0(N/l)I, with the length l unchanged.
    [½]
  2. N → 2N and I → 2I make B 2 × 2 = 4 times: B1 = 4B0.
    [½]
Answer
(d) B1 = 4B0
Marking note The question keeps the solenoid the same, so n doubles; a candidate who argues that the length also doubled would get B0×2, but the paper clearly intends 4B0.

Concepts to recall

  • B = μ0nI, where n = N/l is turns per unit length.
  • Doubling N on the same length doubles n; doubling the number of turns by lengthening the solenoid would not change n.

Question 1(A)(iii)[1]
Study the data given below.
MaterialRelative Permeability
P0·9999906
Q1·000021
R600
S280,000
The material most suitable to make permanent magnets is:
(a)P
(b)Q
(c)R
(d)S
  1. P has μr slightly less than 1 (diamagnetic) and Q has μr slightly more than 1 (paramagnetic); neither can be magnetised appreciably, so they are ruled out.
    [½]
  2. R and S are ferromagnetic. S (μr = 280 000, like permalloy or mu-metal) is a soft magnetic material: it magnetises and demagnetises very easily, suiting transformer cores and electromagnets. R (μr of a few hundred, like steel) is a hard material with high retentivity and coercivity, which a permanent magnet needs.
    [½]
Answer
(c) R
Common slip Choosing S because it has the largest permeability: that is the best material for a core, not for a permanent magnet.
Marking note Only permeability is tabulated, so R is chosen as the ferromagnet of moderate permeability, as for steel. This reading is the usual textbook one but the data do not state retentivity, so it is the one doubtful item of Section A.

Concepts to recall

  • Permanent magnet: high retentivity and high coercivity (steel, alnico). Electromagnet or transformer core: high permeability, low retentivity and coercivity (soft iron, permalloy).
  • μr < 1: diamagnetic; μr slightly above 1: paramagnetic; μr >> 1: ferromagnetic.

Question 1(A)(iv)[1]
de Broglie wavelength of moving particles does not depend on their:
(a)velocity.
(b)mass.
(c)size.
(d)kinetic energy.
  1. λ = h/p = h/(mv) = h/√(2mK): it depends on the mass, the velocity and the kinetic energy.
    [½]
  2. The size of the particle does not enter the expression, so λ does not depend on size.
    [½]
Answer
(c) size.

Concepts to recall

  • λ = h/p; in terms of kinetic energy K: h/√(2mK); through potential V: h/√(2mqV).
  • Only momentum (mass and velocity) matters.

Question 1(A)(v)[1]
If temperature of a block of a semiconducting material is increased, its resistance:
(a)decreases.
(b)increases.
(c)remains constant.
(d)first increases and then decreases.
  1. On heating, covalent bonds break and more electron–hole pairs are produced, so the carrier density ne + nh rises exponentially. This far outweighs the small fall in relaxation time, so conductivity rises and the resistance decreases.
    [1]
Answer
(a) decreases.

Concepts to recall

  • Semiconductor: negative temperature coefficient of resistance (carrier density grows rapidly with T).
  • Metal: resistance increases with T because the relaxation time falls and the carrier density is fixed.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(vi)
Question 1(A)(vi)[1]
Given below are two statements marked, Assertion and Reason. Read the two statements and choose the correct option.
Assertion: Phase difference between two overlapping waves to produce a bright band 0 is 2π, 4π, 6π, 8π, …… (2m−1)π.
Reason: When crest of one wave falls on crest of another wave, amplitude of the resulting wave decreases.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Assertion: bright bands need constructive interference, phase difference 0, 2π, 4π, … = 2mπ. The general term printed, (2m−1)π, is an odd multiple of π, which gives dark bands. As written the Assertion is false.
    [½]
  2. Reason: crest on crest is constructive interference, so the amplitude increases (to a1 + a2); the Reason is false.
    [½]
Answer
(d) Both Assertion and Reason are false.
Marking note The printed series ends in (2m−1)π, which describes dark bands, so the Assertion is taken as false and the answer is (d). If the line is read as intending 2mπ it would be option (c); the Reason is false either way.

Concepts to recall

  • Constructive interference: Δφ = 2mπ (path difference mλ), amplitude a1 + a2. Destructive: Δφ = (2m−1)π (path difference (2m−1)λ/2), amplitude |a1 − a2|.
  • For assertion-reason questions test each statement on its own, down to the last term of a series.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(vii)
Question 1(A)(vii)[1]
Given below are two statements marked, Assertion and Reason. Read the two statements and choose the correct option.
Assertion: When a beam of white light is passed through a prism, it gets split into its constituent colours.
Reason: Refractive index of glass is different for different wavelengths of light.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Both Assertion and Reason are false.
  1. Both statements are true: white light splits into its colours (dispersion), and the refractive index of glass varies with wavelength (n larger for violet than for red, Cauchy: n = A + B/λ2).
    [½]
  2. Because n differs, the colours are refracted through different angles; this is precisely why the beam splits, so the Reason explains the Assertion.
    [½]
Answer
(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.

Concepts to recall

  • Dispersion: n depends on λ; nviolet > nred, so violet deviates most.
  • In a vacuum all colours travel at the same speed and do not disperse; dispersion occurs only in a material medium.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i)[1]
What is the effect of increasing the temperature of a piece of metal on relaxation time of free electrons?
  1. The relaxation time decreases: at higher temperature the lattice ions vibrate with greater amplitude, so the electrons collide more often and the average time between collisions falls (τ ∝ 1/√T roughly).
    [1]
Answer
Relaxation time decreases.

Concepts to recall

  • Relaxation time τ = average time between two successive collisions of an electron; drift speed vd = (eE/m)τ.
  • Resistivity ρ = m/(ne2τ): fall of τ with T explains why the resistance of a metal rises with temperature.

SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv)[1]
Figure 3 below shows an optical pin kept in front of a lens mirror combination. It is found that the final image formed by the lens mirror combination coincides with the object pin. Draw the ray diagram showing the formation of the final image (I).
  1. The final image coincides with the pin only if the rays return along their own paths. Diagram (a): the pin P is at the principal focus F of the convex lens L, so the rays from P emerge from the lens as a parallel beam, fall normally on the plane mirror M, are reflected straight back along the same paths and the lens converges them to P again: the final image I is at P.
    [½]
  2. Diagram (b), equally acceptable: P is at a distance for which the lens forms a real image I′ exactly on the mirror; M reflects the rays as if from a luminous point at I′, and the lens, by reversibility of rays, converges them back on P, so I coincides with P. (Labelled rays with arrows.)
    [½]
Answer
Ray diagram as drawn: rays retrace their paths and the final image I coincides with the pin P.
Marking note The question does not give the distances, so either construction earns the marks; the labelled diagram with arrows on the rays, the pin, lens and mirror marked, and I shown coinciding with P is what is credited.

Concepts to recall

  • Coincidence of image and object (no parallax) in the lens-mirror method: the rays must retrace their path, which happens when they fall normally on the mirror (pin at the focus of the lens, so the beam between lens and mirror is parallel).
  • Reversibility of light: a ray returned along its path goes back to its source; a plane mirror at the image I′ makes I′ a luminous object for the return journey.
  • In the laboratory the distance from the pin to the lens, when there is no parallax with its image, is the focal length of the lens.

SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(vii)
Question 1(B)(vii)[1]
State any one difference in the energy band diagram of a semiconductor and that of an insulator.
  1. In a semiconductor the forbidden gap Eg between the valence and conduction bands is small (about 1 eV, less than 3 eV: 0·7 eV for Ge, 1·1 eV for Si), whereas in an insulator it is very large (greater than about 3 eV, e.g. 6 eV for diamond). (Any one.)
    [1]
Answer
Forbidden gap: small (< 3 eV) in a semiconductor, large (> 3 eV) in an insulator.
Marking note Also accepted: at 0 K a semiconductor behaves as an insulator; at room temperature some electrons occupy the conduction band of a semiconductor but practically none do in an insulator.

Concepts to recall

  • Band theory: valence band (filled), conduction band (empty or partly filled), forbidden gap Eg. Conductor: bands overlap or conduction band partly filled.
  • Semiconductors conduct when thermal energy lifts electrons across the small gap; in an insulator thermal energy at room temperature cannot.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q2
Question 2[2]
(i) Two resistors, R1 = 1 Ω and R2 = 2 Ω are connected in parallel. If the power developed in the resistor R1 is 4 W, find the power developed in the resistor R2.
OR
(ii) In Figure 4 shown below, internal resistance of the battery is negligible. Calculate the Ammeter reading, assuming its resistance to be negligible. (Four 4 Ω resistors forming a bridge with a galvanometer G between A and B; 12 V battery and the ammeter in the supply lead.)

Solution to the first alternative

  1. In parallel both resistors have the same potential difference V. For R1: P1 = V2/R1, so V2 = 4 W × 1 Ω = 4 V2, V = 2 V.
    [1]
  2. P2 = V2/R2 = 4/2 = 2 W (equivalently P ∝ 1/R at constant V: half of 4 W).
    [1]
Answer
P2 = 2 W
Common slip Using P ∝ R (the series rule) and getting 8 W.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. The 12 V source is connected across the left and right junctions; A and B are the midpoints of the two 4 Ω + 4 Ω paths and G joins them. The arms satisfy 4/4 = 4/4, so the bridge is balanced: A and B are at the same potential and G carries no current, so it can be removed.
    [½]
  2. Two parallel paths of (4 + 4) = 8 Ω each: Req = (8 × 8)/(8 + 8) = 4 Ω.
    [½]
  3. Ammeter reading I = E/Req = 12/4 = 3 A.
    [1]
Answer
Ammeter reading = 3 A.
Marking note Treating the bridge as unbalanced and applying Kirchhoff’s laws gives the same 3 A, because the node potentials work out equal.

Concepts to recall

  • Power P = VI = I2R = V2/R.
  • Parallel: same V, so P ∝ 1/R. Series: same I, so P ∝ R.
  • Wheatstone bridge balance: P/Q = R/S; then no current flows through the galvanometer (equal potentials at A and B).
  • Balanced bridge: ignore G, combine each arm in series then the two arms in parallel.
  • The ammeter is ideal (zero resistance) and the battery has no internal resistance, so the supply current is E/Req.

Question 3[2]
Two identical capacitors, each of capacitance C are connected to a battery as shown in Figure 5 below.
(i) Calculate the equivalent capacitance of the circuit.
(ii) What is the equivalent capacitance of the circuit, if the space between the two plates of one of the capacitors is completely filled with paper (of dielectric constant K = 2·5)?
  1. (i) Both capacitors are connected between the same two junctions, so they are in parallel: Ceq = C + C = 2C.
    [1]
  2. (ii) The filled capacitor becomes KC = 2·5C; the other stays C. In parallel C′eq = C + 2·5C = 3·5C.
    [1]
Answer
(i) 2C; (ii) 3·5C
Common slip Reading the two capacitors as being in series (giving C/2).

Concepts to recall

  • Parallel: Ceq = C1 + C2. Series: 1/Ceq = 1/C1 + 1/C2.
  • A dielectric of constant K filling the whole space multiplies the capacitance by K: C = Kε0A/d.
  • Trace the wiring: capacitors on different branches between the same two nodes are in parallel, not in series.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q4
Question 4[2]
(i) (a) Three identical point charges, each equal to Q, are situated at the three vertices of an equilateral triangle MNO, having each side equal to l. How much electrostatic potential energy is stored in this system?
(b) An electric dipole lies in a uniform electric field. When is torque acting on the dipole maximum?
OR
(ii) Two Gaussian surfaces, S1 and S2 are shown in Figure 6 below.
(a) What is the ratio of electric flux emerging out of these two surfaces?
(b) What happens to this ratio if the space occupied by each Gaussian surface is doubled?

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) There are three pairs (MN, NO, OM), each at distance l, and each pair has energy Q2/(4πε0l).
    [½]
  2. U = 3 × Q2/(4πε0l) = 3Q2/(4πε0l) (positive: the charges are like).
    [½]
  3. (b) τ = pE sinθ is maximum when sinθ = 1, i.e. θ = 90°: the dipole is perpendicular to the field; then τmax = pE.
    [1]
Answer
(a) U = 3Q2/(4πε0l); (b) when the dipole is at 90° to the field, τmax = pE.
Common slip Counting six terms (each pair twice) and getting 6Q2/(4πε0l).

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) Gauss’s law: Φ = qenc/ε0. S1 encloses −2q + q = −q, so Φ1 = −q/ε0. S2 encloses 2q + q = 3q, so Φ2 = 3q/ε0.
    [1]
  2. Ratio Φ1 : Φ2 = −q : 3q = −1 : 3 (the flux through S1 is inward).
    [½]
  3. (b) The ratio does not change (−1 : 3): the flux depends only on the charge enclosed, not on the size or shape of the surface (as long as the same charges remain inside).
    [½]
Answer
(a) Φ1 : Φ2 = −1 : 3; (b) no change.
Common slip Giving the ratio as 1 : 3 and dropping the sign, or adding magnitudes (3q : 3q = 1 : 1).

Concepts to recall

  • Energy of a system of point charges: U = Σpairs qiqj/(4πε0rij); count each pair once.
  • Torque on a dipole τ = p × E, magnitude pE sinθ; zero when parallel or antiparallel, maximum at 90°.
  • Gauss’s law: ∮E·dS = qenc/ε0; only enclosed charge contributes, with its sign.
  • Net flux is negative (inward) when the enclosed charge is negative.
  • The flux is independent of the shape and size of the closed surface.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q5
Question 5[2]
(i) How much force per unit length acts on a long current carrying conductor X due to a current flowing through another similar conductor Y, kept parallel to it in vacuum? (Derivation of the formula is not required.)
(ii) Use this expression to define an ampere.
  1. (i) For parallel conductors at separation d carrying currents IX and IY: F/l = μ0IXIY/(2πd), attractive for currents in the same direction and repulsive for opposite directions.
    [1]
  2. (ii) If IX = IY = 1 A and d = 1 m, F/l = (4π × 10−7 × 1 × 1)/(2π × 1) = 2 × 10−7 N m−1. Hence one ampere is that steady current which, flowing in each of two infinitely long, straight, parallel conductors of negligible cross-section placed 1 m apart in vacuum, makes each exert a force of 2 × 10−7 N on every metre of the other.
    [1]
Answer
(i) F/l = μ0IXIY/(2πd); (ii) 1 A gives 2 × 10−7 N m−1 at 1 m separation.
Common slip Omitting “per metre length”, “infinitely long” or “in vacuum” in the definition.

Concepts to recall

  • Force between parallel currents: same direction attract, opposite repel; F/l = μ0I1I2/(2πd), μ0 = 4π × 10−7 T m A−1.
  • The ampere is the SI base unit defined through this force (the 2019 redefinition fixes e, but the textbook definition is the one asked).

Question 6[2]
A parallel beam of monochromatic light of wavelength 450 nm is incident normally on a rectangular slit of width 0·9 μm. Calculate angular width of the central bright band.
  1. Single slit: first minimum at a sinθ = λ, so sinθ = λ/a = (450 × 10−9)/(0·9 × 10−6) = 0·5.
    [1]
  2. θ = 30°; angular width of the central band = 2θ = 60° (= 1·05 rad).
    [1]
Answer
Angular width 2θ = 60° ≈ 1·05 rad
Marking note With the small-angle formula 2λ/a the answer is 1 rad (57·3°); this is the answer many books give. Either value is accepted, but the exact one is 60° since θ = 30° is not a small angle.

Concepts to recall

  • Single-slit diffraction: minima at a sinθ = nλ; central maximum lies between the first minima on either side, angular width 2θ = 2λ/a (small angles).
  • Here λ/a = 0·5 is not small, so use sinθ = 0·5 exactly.
  • Linear width on a screen at distance D: 2λD/a.

Question 7[2]
(Picture: a hand pointing a remote control at a television set.) (i) Name the electromagnetic wave emitted by the remote control operating the TV set.
(ii) State any one property common to all electromagnetic waves.
  1. (i) Infrared radiation (a pulse-coded infrared beam from an infrared LED).
    [1]
  2. (ii) Any one: all electromagnetic waves travel with the speed of light c = 3 × 108 m s−1 in vacuum; they are transverse waves; they are not deflected by electric or magnetic fields (they carry no charge); they carry energy and momentum; they need no medium.
    [1]

(i) Infrared waves; (ii) all travel at c in vacuum (or are transverse).

SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q8
Question 8[2]
Intensity of monochromatic UV light incident on photocathode of a photoelectric cell is increased. What is its effect on:
(i) photocurrent?
(ii) kinetic energy of the most energetic photoelectron?
  1. (i) Photocurrent increases (directly proportional to the intensity): more photons per second fall on the cathode, so more photoelectrons are emitted per second.
    [1]
  2. (ii) K.E. of the most energetic photoelectron is unchanged: Kmax = hν − φ depends only on the frequency and the work function, not on the intensity.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Photocurrent increases; (ii) Kmax remains the same.

  • Saturation photocurrent ∝ intensity (for fixed frequency above threshold); stopping potential independent of intensity.
  • Intensity changes the number of photons; frequency changes the energy of each photon.
SECTION C – 27 MARKSISC 2025 Improvement Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q9
Question 9[3]
Derive an expression for electric field intensity (E) at a point which lies on the axis of an electric dipole (having length 2l and dipole moment P) and which is at a distance ‘r’ from its centre.
  1. Let the dipole be −q at A and +q at B, separated by 2l, with centre O. P is on the axis (the line AB produced) at distance r from O (r > l). Labelled figure.
    [1]
  2. Field of +q (distance r − l): E1 = 14πε0q(r − l)2, along p (away from +q). Field of −q (distance r + l): E2 = 14πε0q(r + l)2, towards −q, opposite to p.
    [1]
  3. Both lie along the axis, so E = E1 − E2 = q4πε0[1(r − l)2 − 1(r + l)2] = q4πε04rl(r2 − l2)2. With p = q(2l): E = 14πε02pr(r2 − l2)2, along p.
    [1]
Answer
E = (1/4πε0) · 2pr/(r2 − l2)2, along p; for r >> l, E = 2p/(4πε0r3).
Common slip Writing p = ql instead of q(2l) (the length here is 2l), or adding instead of subtracting the two fields.

Concepts to recall

  • Dipole moment p = q(2l), directed from −q to +q.
  • Axial field is along p; equatorial field is p/[4πε0(r2 + l2)3/2] opposite to p. For a short dipole the axial field is twice the equatorial one at the same distance.
  • Superposition: the nearer charge (+q) gives the larger field, so the resultant points along p.
