SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i)[1]
A point charge −Q and three points A, B and C are shown in Figure 1 below. If V is electric potential and E is electric field intensity, we can infer that:
(a)EA > EB > EC
(b)EA < EB < EC
(c)VA < VC < VB
(d)VA > VC > VB
  1. The points lie in the order O, A, C, B, so rA < rC < rB. Field of a point charge E = kQ/r2 falls with distance, so EA > EC > EB: options (a) and (b) are wrong.
    [½]
  2. Potential of the negative charge V = −kQ/r is negative and rises towards zero as r increases, so VA < VC < VB.
    [½]
Answer
(c) VA < VC < VB
Common slip Treating potential like field: “nearer means larger” is true of |V| only; the sign makes the nearest point the lowest.

Concepts to recall

  • Point charge: E = kq/r2 (magnitude falls as 1/r2); V = kq/r with the sign of q.
  • For a negative source the potential is least (most negative) close to it and increases with distance.

Question 1(A)(iii)[1]
In Figure 2 below, CD is an infinitely long and straight metallic wire carrying a current I. Point P is at a perpendicular distance r from it. Which combination of I and r gives maximum magnetic field at point P, pointing into the plane of the paper?
(a)I = 4 A towards C, with r = 0·500 m
(b)I = 4 A towards D, with r = 0·250 m

(d)I = 8 A towards C, with r = 0·050 m

Match the entries in Column I with those in Column II.

SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(v)
Question 1(A)(v)[1]
Identify the phenomena that occur when a P-N junction diode is forward biased.
(P) The width of the depletion layer decreases.
(Q) The height of the potential barrier is lowered.
(R) The effective resistance of the diode increases significantly.
(S) The reverse current flows mainly due to the diffusion of majority charge carriers.
(a)Both (P) and (Q)
(b)Both (R) and (S)
(c)Both (P) and (S)
(d)Both (Q) and (R)
  1. Forward bias opposes the built-in field: the depletion layer becomes narrower (P) and the barrier height is lowered (Q).
    [½]
  2. The resistance falls sharply, so (R) is false; (S) is false since majority-carrier diffusion gives the forward current, not a reverse current.
    [½]
Answer
(a) Both (P) and (Q)

Concepts to recall

  • Forward bias: p to +, n to −; depletion width and barrier decrease; low resistance; large majority-carrier current above the knee voltage.
  • Reverse bias: depletion width and barrier increase; tiny minority-carrier leakage current.

SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(vii)
Question 1(A)(vii)[1]
Assertion: The focal length of a convex mirror will increase, if the mirror is placed in water.
Reason: The focal length of a convex mirror is equal to half its radius of curvature.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true, and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true, but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Assertion is false and Reason is true.
  1. Reason: for a spherical mirror f = R/2, which is true.
    [½]
  2. Reflection does not depend on the refractive index of the surrounding medium, so f = R/2 is unchanged in water: the Assertion is false.
    [½]
Answer
(d) Assertion is false and Reason is true.
Common slip Carrying over what happens to a lens in water (its focal length increases) to a mirror.

Concepts to recall

  • Mirror: f = R/2 in any medium, because only reflection is involved. A lens is different: its f depends on nlens/nmedium.
  • Assertion–reason: test each statement alone, then ask whether the reason explains the assertion.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i)[1]
Two point charges Q1 and Q2, separated by a distance, are such that there is no neutral point (where electric field is zero) in their vicinity. What is the relation between Q1 and Q2?
  1. Two like charges have a neutral point between them; two unlike charges of unequal magnitude have one on the line outside, on the side of the smaller charge. Only for equal and opposite charges is there no point where the fields cancel, so Q2 = −Q1 (they form an electric dipole).
    [1]
Answer
Q2 = −Q1 (the pair forms an electric dipole).

Concepts to recall

  • Neutral point: E1 + E2 = 0. Between like charges the fields oppose; for unlike charges they oppose only outside the pair, nearer the smaller charge.
  • For equal and opposite charges the nearer charge always dominates outside, and between them the fields add, so the field is never zero.

Compare resistance of an ideal voltmeter with that of an ideal ammeter.
  1. An ideal voltmeter has infinite resistance (RV = ∞) so that it draws no current; an ideal ammeter has zero resistance (RA = 0) so that it does not change the current. Hence RV >> RA.
    [1]
Answer
RV = ∞ and RA = 0.

  • Voltmeter: connected in parallel, must have a very high resistance (galvanometer + large series resistance). Ammeter: connected in series, very low resistance (galvanometer + small shunt).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii)[1]
How can the focal length of a given convex lens be measured in Physics laboratory using only a metre scale?
  1. Distant object method: focus the image of a distant object (a tree or a window across the street) on a screen (wall or paper) by moving the lens; the parallel rays from the distant object meet at the focus.
    [½]
  2. Measure the distance between the lens and the sharp image with the metre scale: this is the focal length f.
    [½]
Answer
f = distance from lens to the sharp image of a distant object.
Marking note The 2027 key names the “Distant Object Method”; the explanation of the procedure is what earns the mark.

Concepts to recall

  • Rays from a distant object are parallel, so the image forms at the principal focus: v = f when u → ∞.
  • It is a rough value (error of a few mm); the u–v method or the displacement method is more accurate.

Question 1(B)(iv)[1]
What is the difference between a moving metallic ball colliding with another ball in motion and a photon colliding with an electron?
  1. A photon either gives up its whole energy (is absorbed) or none of it, as energy is exchanged in quanta; a colliding ball can lose any fraction of its energy.
    [1]
Answer
A photon loses all its energy or none; a ball may lose any fraction.
Marking note Equivalent statements accepted, e.g. a photon in a collision with an electron is absorbed or scattered with changed frequency, whereas the ball keeps its identity and changes its speed.

Concepts to recall

  • Photon: energy E = hν, momentum p = h/λ, discrete (quantised) interactions — the photoelectric effect is an all-or-none event.
  • A photon is not slowed down by a collision: it always moves at c; only its energy and direction change.

SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q1(B)(vi)
Question 1(B)(vi)[1]
Explain why resistance of a semiconductor material decreases when its temperature is increased.
  1. At a higher temperature more electrons gain enough energy to break their covalent bonds and cross the forbidden gap into the conduction band, so the number of free charge carriers (electrons and holes) rises sharply.
    [½]
  2. Resistivity ρ = 1/(neμ) falls because n increases much faster than the mobility falls, so the resistance decreases.
    [½]
Answer
More carriers are released with temperature, so resistance falls.

Concepts to recall

  • Metals: n fixed, collisions rise with T, so resistance rises. Semiconductors: n rises exponentially with T, so resistance falls (negative temperature coefficient).
  • Energy gap: about 1·1 eV (Si), 0·7 eV (Ge).

SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q2
Question 2[2]
(i) A negatively charged thundercloud above the earth’s surface may be perceived as a parallel plate capacitor. The lower plate of the capacitor is the Earth’s surface, and the upper plate is the base of the thundercloud. The area of the base of the thundercloud = 4·5 × 108 m2, distance of thundercloud base from the earth’s surface = 4425 m. Calculate the capacitance of this capacitor.
OR
(ii) A tiny bird carrying a charge of q = 8 × 10−17 C dives vertically down towards a large positively charged metallic plate kept on a table in a horizontal position. What should be the surface charge density (σ) of the plate so that the bird stops in its flight, close to the plate? (Mass of the bird = 50 g)

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor (air) C = ε0A/d
    [½]
  2. = (8·85 × 10−12 × 4·5 × 108)/4425
    [½]
  3. = 9·0 × 10−7 F
    [1]
Answer
C = 9·0 × 10−7 F

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. The bird stops (is just held) when the upward electric force balances its weight: qE = mg.
    [½]
  2. Field of the large plate (the examiner’s key) E = σ/2ε0, so qσ/2ε0 = mg and σ = 2mgε0/q.
    [½]
  3. σ = (2 × 50 × 10−3 × 9·8 × 8·85 × 10−12)/(8 × 10−17)
    [½]
  4. = 1·08 × 105 C m−2
    [½]
Answer
σ = 1·08 × 105 C m−2
Common slip Leaving the mass in grams (50 instead of 0·05 kg) makes σ a thousand times too large.
Marking note The printed key gives the unit as V m−1: that is the unit of E, not of σ. Surface charge density is in C m−2, and the answer is corrected here. The key also uses E = σ/2ε0 (a sheet of charge). For the surface of a conducting plate E = σ/ε0 would give 5·42 × 104 C m−2; the key’s value 1·08 × 105 C m−2 is the one to give full marks for. (Strictly the bird is in equilibrium, not at rest, when qE = mg.)

Concepts to recall

  • C = ε0A/d for two parallel plates with air between; C = Q/V.
  • Use ε0 = 8·85 × 10−12 F m−1 from the paper’s table of constants; unit farad.
  • Large plane sheet of charge: E = σ/2ε0; surface of a charged conductor: E = σ/ε0.
  • Equilibrium of a charge in a field: qE = mg; unit of σ is C m−2.

Draw a labelled diagram of an arrangement used in Physics laboratory to compare emfs of two given cells X and Y.
  1. Circuit of the potentiometer wire AB: driver cell (battery) D, rheostat Rh and key K in series with the whole wire AB, with the + terminal of D at A. [1]
    [1]
  2. Secondary circuit: cells X and Y with their + terminals joined to A, a two-way key (or keys K1, K2), a galvanometer G (with protective resistance) and jockey J on AB; all parts labelled. [1]
    [1]

SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q4
Question 4[2]
(i) According to Ohm’s law, V = IR, so if potential difference (V) between the two ends of a conductor is varied, will its resistance (R) change? Explain your answer in brief.
OR
(ii) Heating power developed in a resistor R is given by P = V2/R. It implies that power P ∝ 1/R, i.e., power varies inversely with R. Heating power developed in a resistor R is also given by P = I2R. It implies that power P ∝ R, i.e., power varies directly with R. Under what conditions are the above-mentioned statements valid?

Solution to the first alternative

  1. No, the resistance does not change.
    [1]
  2. R = ρl/A depends on the material, length, area and temperature of the conductor, not on V or I: when V is changed, I changes in the same ratio, so V/I stays constant.
    [1]
Answer
No: R = V/I is a property of the conductor (its material, dimensions and temperature); I ∝ V.
Marking note Valid for an ohmic conductor at constant temperature; for a non-ohmic device (diode, filament at high current) V/I does vary.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. P ∝ 1/R holds when V is constant, i.e. when the resistors are connected in parallel (same potential difference across each).
    [1]
  2. P ∝ R holds when I is constant, i.e. when the resistors are connected in series (same current through each).
    [1]
Answer
P ∝ 1/R at constant V (parallel); P ∝ R at constant I (series).

Concepts to recall

  • Ohm’s law: V ∝ I at constant temperature; the constant of proportionality is R = ρl/A.
  • R = V/I is a ratio, not a cause: a larger V only drives a larger I.
  • P = VI = I2R = V2/R; a proportionality needs the third quantity fixed.
  • Series: same I, the larger R dissipates more. Parallel: same V, the smaller R dissipates more.

Question 5[2]
Explain why a blue coloured spark appears in a switch contact when a circuit containing a load like a heater, geyser or an electric iron is switched off.
  1. When the switch is opened the current falls very rapidly. The wiring and load have self-inductance, so a large induced (back) emf e = −LdI/dt appears across the gap between the contacts.
    [1]
  2. This large emf produces a discharge through the air: the air molecules are ionised and the excited molecules emit light (blue) as they return to their ground states.
    [1]
Answer
A large self-induced emf on break causes an electric discharge in the air at the contacts; the ionised air gives off a blue light.
Marking note Mention of back emf / self-induction and of ionisation of air gets the two marks.

Concepts to recall

  • Self-induction: emf = −L dI/dt; it opposes the change of current, and dI/dt is very large when a circuit is broken suddenly.
  • Excited atoms or molecules emit light of definite colours when they return to lower levels.

SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q7
Question 7[2]
(i) Why the current flowing is extremely small during reverse biasing of a junction diode?
OR
(ii) Extrinsic semiconductors are neither good nor bad conductors of electricity. Why are they still considered useful?

Solution to the first alternative

  1. In reverse bias the barrier is raised and the majority carriers cannot cross it.
    [1]
  2. The only current is due to minority charge carriers, which are very few in number, so the current is extremely small (of the order of μA).
    [1]
Answer
The reverse current is carried only by minority carriers, which are very few.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Their conductivity can be controlled and tuned precisely by the type and concentration of the dopant added.
    [1]
  2. Hence diodes, transistors, solar cells and other devices can be made from them (p-n junctions), which have wide applications in electronics.
    [1]
Answer
Their conductivity can be controlled by doping, which makes diodes, transistors, solar cells etc. possible.

Concepts to recall

  • Reverse bias: p to −, n to +; depletion layer widens, barrier height rises.
  • Reverse saturation current is independent of the reverse voltage until breakdown (temperature-dependent).
  • Extrinsic semiconductor: pure Si/Ge doped with a pentavalent (n-type) or trivalent (p-type) impurity; conductivity rises by orders of magnitude with a doping of 1 in 108.
  • Joining p and n type gives the junction, the basis of all electronic devices.

Two particles A and B of masses mA and mB have de Broglie wavelength λA and λB. If λA = 2λB, compare their kinetic energies.
  1. λ = h/p and K = p2/2m, so K = h2/(2mλ2).

SECTION C – 27 MARKSISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q9
Question 9[3]
(i) Obtain an expression of intensity of electric field at a point lying on the axis of an electric dipole.
OR
(ii) An electric dipole is kept in a uniform electric field E with its axis making an angle θ with the field. Show forces acting on each charge and hence prove that torque acting is given by τ = pE sinθ.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Diagram: dipole AB (−q at A, +q at B, separation 2l, centre O) and the point P on the axis at distance r from O, with the two fields shown.
    [½]
  2. Field of +q (at distance r − l, directed away from B): E1 = kq/(r − l)2
    [½]
  3. Field of −q (at distance r + l, directed towards A): E2 = kq/(r + l)2
    [½]
  4. The two fields are along the same line and opposite, and E1 > E2, so E = E1 − E2 = kq[1/(r − l)2 − 1/(r + l)2] = kq · 4rl/(r2 − l2)2
    [1]
  5. With p = q(2l) and k = 1/4πε0: E = k · 2pr/(r2 − l2)2, along p (from −q to +q). For r >> l: E ≈ 2kp/r3.
    [½]
Answer
E = k · 2pr/(r2 − l2)2 along the dipole moment p; for r >> l, E = 2p/(4πε0r3).
Common slip Adding E1 and E2 instead of subtracting them (on the axis they point opposite ways).

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Forces: +q at A feels qE along E and −q at B feels qE opposite to E (shown in the figure).
    [1]
  2. The forces are equal and opposite, so the net force is zero, but their lines of action are different: they form a couple. Torque = force × perpendicular distance between the lines of action = qE × AC.
    [1]
  3. From the right triangle ABC, AC = AB sinθ = 2l sinθ, so τ = qE × 2l sinθ = (q × 2l)E sinθ = pE sinθ, where p = q(2l).
    [1]
Answer
τ = pE sinθ (vectorially τ = p × E).
Common slip Taking the arm as 2l cosθ: the forces are along E, so the perpendicular separation is 2l sinθ.
Marking note The key writes AC/AB = sinθ and AB = L; the length of the dipole may be written as 2l or L.

Concepts to recall

  • Electric dipole: two equal and opposite charges ±q a distance 2l apart; dipole moment p = q × 2l, direction −q to +q.
  • Superposition: E = E1 + E2. On the axis the nearer charge dominates; on the equatorial line E = kp/(r2 + l2)3/2, directed opposite to p.
  • Short dipole: Eaxial = 2Eequatorial at equal distance.
  • Torque of a couple = (one force) × (perpendicular distance between the forces).
  • τ is maximum (pE) when θ = 90° and zero when θ = 0° or 180°; stable equilibrium at θ = 0°.
SECTION C – 27 MARKSISC Specimen 2027 Physics Paper 1, Q10
Question 10[3]
(i) Three identical cells each of emf 1·5 V and internal resistance of 2 Ω are connected in series to form a battery B. This battery is connected to an ammeter A (of negligible resistance) and five other resistors as shown in Figure 4 below. Calculate the reading of the ammeter A.
OR

(i) State any one difference between the nature of magnetic field inside a current carrying solenoid and that along the axis of a current carrying circular coil.

  1. (i) The field inside a long solenoid is uniform (same everywhere), whereas the field along the axis of a circular coil is non-uniform (maximum at the centre, falling off along the axis).
    [1]
  2. (ii) The flux through B, due to the clockwise current in A (field directed away from the observer), increases as B approaches. By Lenz’s law the induced current opposes this by producing a field towards the observer, so it is anticlockwise as seen by the observer.
    [1]
  3. (iii) Steel has a large coercivity (and retentivity), so it is difficult to demagnetise, and it retains its magnetism; soft iron loses its magnetism easily.

A physics teacher drew a ray diagram on the board as shown below and asked the following questions to the students. State whether the answer of the student is correct or incorrect. Correct the incorrect response with an explanation in brief.
(i) Teacher: There is a point P on the screen where the path difference between two interfering light waves is exactly 1·2 × 10−6 m. If the light emitted by the source has a wavelength of 600 nm, can anyone tell me whether a bright or a dark fringe will form at the point P? Student: Dark fringe will be obtained.
(ii) Teacher: Two students performed the same Young’s double slit experiment. One student obtained wider fringes. What is the possible reason for this observation? Student: The student who obtained wider fringes must have increased the distance between the two slits.
(iii) Teacher: If one of the slits is closed with a finger while performing the experiment, what change will be noticed on the screen? Student: The screen will get illuminated uniformly.
  1. (i) Incorrect. Path difference / λ = (1·2 × 10−6)/(600 × 10−9) = 2, a whole number of wavelengths (nλ with n = 2): constructive interference, so a bright fringe forms at P.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Incorrect. Fringe width β = λD/d: wider fringes come from a decrease in the slit separation d (or an increase in D or λ). Increasing d makes the fringes narrower.
    [1]
  3. (iii) Incorrect. With one slit closed there is no interference; the single open slit gives a diffraction pattern on the screen (a broad central maximum with weaker secondary maxima), not a uniform illumination.
    [1]
Answer
(i) incorrect, bright fringe (path difference = 2λ); (ii) incorrect, d must have been decreased; (iii) incorrect, a diffraction pattern is seen.
Common slip Reading 2 as an even number and calling the fringe dark: dark needs a half-integral number of wavelengths.
Marking note In (ii) any of: decrease d, increase D, use longer wavelength; but the student’s statement (increase of d) is wrong.

Concepts to recall

  • Constructive interference: path difference = nλ (bright); destructive: (n + ½)λ (dark).
  • Fringe width β = λD/d (D slit–screen distance, d slit separation).
  • A single slit of width comparable to λ diffracts the light: central maximum twice as wide as the others and much brighter.

    [1]

    [1]

    [1]
