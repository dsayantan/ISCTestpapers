SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i)[1]
If potential difference between the two ends of a metallic wire is doubled, drift speed of free electrons in the wire:
(a)remains same.
(b)becomes double.
(c)becomes four times.
(d)becomes half.
  1. The wire is the same (n, A, R unchanged), so by Ohm’s law I = V/R doubles when V is doubled.
    [½]
  2. I = neAvd gives vd ∝ I, so the drift speed also doubles. (Equivalently vd = eVτ/ml ∝ V.)
    [½]
Answer
(b) becomes double.

Concepts to recall

  • I = neAvd; drift speed vd = (eE/m)τ = eVτ/(ml).
  • Ohm’s law: for a metallic conductor at constant temperature I ∝ V.
  • The relaxation time τ does not change with V (heating assumed negligible).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(ii)
Question 1(A)(ii)[1]
A metre bridge is balanced with a known resistance (R) in the left hand gap and an unknown resistance (S) in the right hand gap. Balance point is found to be at a distance of l cm from the left hand side. When the battery and the galvanometer are interchanged, balance point will:
(a)shift towards left.
(b)shift towards right.
(c)remain same.
(d)shift towards left or right depending on the values of R and S.
  1. At balance R/S = l/(100 − l) (Wheatstone condition). It involves only the four resistances (the wire lengths are proportional to their resistances).
    [½]
  2. Interchanging the battery and the galvanometer leaves this condition unchanged: the bridge condition is symmetric in the two diagonals (the galvanometer still reads zero), so the balance point remains the same.
    [½]
Answer
(c) remain same.
Marking note Balance does not depend on the emf or on which diagonal holds the cell; only the sensitivity of the bridge changes.

Concepts to recall

  • Metre bridge: R/S = l/(100 − l); S = R(100 − l)/l.
  • Wheatstone bridge: P/Q = R/S, and the conditions for balance are the same if cell and galvanometer are exchanged (the conjugate-arm property).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(iii)
Question 1(A)(iii)[1]
Lorentz force in vector form is:
(a)F = B q v sin θ
(b)F = q (v × B)
(c)F = q (B × v)
(d)F = v (q × B)
  1. The magnetic force on a charge q moving with velocity v in a field B is F = q(v × B), perpendicular to both v and B.
    [½]
  2. (a) is only a magnitude, (c) has the wrong order of the cross product (reversed direction), (d) is meaningless (q is a scalar).
    [½]
Answer
(b) F = q (v × B)

Concepts to recall

  • F = q(v × B); magnitude qvB sinθ. Direction: right-hand rule for positive q, reversed for negative q.
  • Cross product is not commutative: B × v = −(v × B).
  • With an electric field as well: F = q(E + v × B).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(iv)
Question 1(A)(iv)[1]
Assertion: When an electric current is passed through a moving coil galvanometer, its coil gets deflected.
Reason: A circular coil produces a uniform magnetic field around itself when an electric current is passed through it.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Assertion is false and Reason is true.
  1. Assertion: true. The coil sits in the field of the permanent magnet and a current-carrying coil experiences a torque τ = NIAB sinθ, so it deflects.
    [½]
  2. Reason: false. The field of a circular coil is not uniform around it (it is a dipole-like field, strong near the wires and weak far away; only near the centre is it roughly uniform). In any case the deflection is due to the torque in the magnet’s field, not to the field of the coil.
    [½]
Answer
(c) Assertion is true and Reason is false.

Concepts to recall

  • Moving coil galvanometer: τ = NIAB (radial field), balanced by the spring torque kθ, so θ ∝ I.
  • A circular current loop acts as a magnetic dipole of moment m = NIA; its field is non-uniform.
  • Assertion-reason: test each statement separately, then ask whether the Reason explains the Assertion.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(v)
Question 1(A)(v)[1]
When a ray of white light is incident obliquely on the first surface of a prism, then:
(a)red colour is deviated most.
(b)green colour is deviated most.
(c)yellow colour is deviated most.
(d)violet colour is deviated most.
  1. Deviation δ = (n − 1)A for a thin prism, and in general increases with the refractive index.
    [½]
  2. Refractive index is greatest for the shortest wavelength (Cauchy: n = a + b/λ2), so violet (nv > nr) is deviated most and red least.
    [½]
Answer
(d) violet colour is deviated most.

Concepts to recall

  • Dispersion: nviolet > nred, so δviolet > δred. Order of deviation: V I B G Y O R (most to least).
  • Dispersive power ω = (nv − nr)/(ny − 1).
  • In vacuum all colours travel at the same speed, but in glass violet travels slowest.

Question 1(A)(vi)[1]
The de-Broglie wavelength (λ) associated with a moving electron having kinetic energy (E) is given by:
(a)2h/√(2mE)
(b)2√(2mE)/h
(c)h/√(2mE)
(d)√(2mhE)
  1. λ = h/p and kinetic energy E = p2/2m, so p = √(2mE).
    [½]
  2. Hence λ = h/√(2mE). Check of the others: (a) has a stray factor 2, (b) is the reciprocal (dimensionally 1/length), (d) has h in the numerator under the root.
    [½]
Answer
(c) h/√(2mE)

Concepts to recall

  • de Broglie: λ = h/p = h/mv = h/√(2mE).
  • Accelerated through V: λ = h/√(2meV); for an electron λ = 12·27/√V Å.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(A)(vii)
Question 1(A)(vii)[1]
The majority charge carriers in a P-type semiconductor are:
(a)electrons.
(b)holes.
(c)protons.
(d)ions.
  1. A p-type semiconductor is made by doping Si or Ge with a trivalent (acceptor) impurity; each impurity atom creates a vacancy (hole) in the covalent bonding.
    [½]
  2. So holes are the majority carriers and the thermally generated electrons are the minority carriers (nh >> ne). The crystal as a whole is neutral.
    [½]
Answer
(b) holes.

Concepts to recall

  • p-type: trivalent dopant (B, Al, Ga, In), majority carriers holes. n-type: pentavalent dopant (P, As, Sb), majority carriers electrons.
  • Mass-action law: nenh = ni2.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(i)
Question 1(B)(i)[1]
In an electric dipole, what is the locus of a point having zero potential?
  1. Take any point P on the perpendicular bisector of the line joining the charges. It is equidistant (r) from +q and −q, so V = kq/r − kq/r = 0.
    [½]
  2. The locus is therefore the equatorial plane of the dipole: the plane perpendicular to the axis passing through the centre of the dipole.
    [½]
Answer
The equatorial plane (the plane perpendicular to the dipole axis through its centre).
Marking note “Perpendicular bisector plane of the line joining the two charges” is equally correct.

Concepts to recall

  • Potential of a dipole: V = p cosθ/(4πε0r2); it vanishes for θ = 90°.
  • Zero potential does not mean zero field: on the equatorial plane E is non-zero and antiparallel to p.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii)[1]
Three identical cells each of emf ‘e’ are connected in parallel to form a battery. What is the emf of the battery?
  1. For identical cells in parallel the emf of the combination equals the emf of a single cell: Eeq = (e/r + e/r + e/r)/(3/r) = e. (Only the internal resistance falls, to r/3.)
    [1]
Answer
The emf of the battery is e.
Common slip Writing 3e (that is the series result).

Concepts to recall

  • Cells in parallel (same polarity, same emf): E = e, req = r/n. Cells in series: E = ne, req = nr.
  • Parallel grouping is used to supply a larger current for a longer time at the same voltage.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii)[1]
Three bulbs B1 (230 V, 40 W), B2 (230 V, 60 W) and B3 (230 V, 100 W) are connected in series to a 230 V supply. Which bulb glows the brightest?
  1. Resistance of a bulb R = V2/P: R1 = 1322 Ω, R2 = 882 Ω, R3 = 529 Ω, so R1 is the greatest.
    [½]
  2. In series the same current flows through all, and the power dissipated is I2R ∝ R, so the 40 W bulb B1 glows brightest.
    [½]
Answer
B1 (the 40 W bulb) glows the brightest.
Common slip Answering B3: that is true only when the bulbs are in parallel.

Concepts to recall

  • Rated power P = V2/R gives R = V2/P: the lower the wattage, the higher the resistance.
  • Series: same I, P = I2R is largest for the largest R. Parallel: same V, P = V2/R is largest for the smallest R (the 100 W bulb).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(iv)
Question 1(B)(iv)[1]
Explain the meaning of the following statement: Curie temperature for soft iron is 770°C.
  1. Soft iron is ferromagnetic only up to 770°C: below this temperature it has domains and is strongly magnetised by a field.
    [½]
  2. At and above 770°C the domain structure is destroyed by thermal agitation and soft iron becomes paramagnetic (χ = C/(T − Tc)).
    [½]
Answer
Above 770°C soft iron loses its ferromagnetism and behaves as a paramagnetic substance.

Concepts to recall

  • Curie temperature Tc: the temperature above which a ferromagnetic material becomes paramagnetic (Curie–Weiss law χ = C/(T − Tc)).
  • Ferro: domains, large positive χ. Para: small positive χ ∝ 1/T. Dia: small negative χ, independent of T.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(v)
Question 1(B)(v)[1]
What type of wavefronts are associated with a point source of light?
  1. A point source in an isotropic medium sends light equally in all directions, so all points at the same distance from it are in the same phase.
    [½]
  2. The wavefronts are spherical (concentric spheres centred on the source; they look like circles in a plane section).
    [½]
Answer
Spherical wavefronts.

Concepts to recall

  • Wavefront: locus of points in the same phase. Point source: spherical; line (slit) source: cylindrical; a distant source, or the emergent beam of a lens with the source at its focus: plane.
  • Rays are perpendicular to the wavefront.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(vi)
Question 1(B)(vi)[1]
What is ‘Pair production’?
  1. The conversion of a high-energy gamma-ray photon into an electron and a positron (a particle–antiparticle pair), γ → e− + e+.
    [½]
  2. It takes place in the strong electric field close to a nucleus (which takes up the momentum) and needs photon energy of at least 2mec2 = 1·02 MeV.
    [½]
Answer
A gamma-ray photon of energy ≥ 1·02 MeV, near a nucleus, changes into an electron–positron pair.

Concepts to recall

  • Pair production: hν ≥ 2mc2 (threshold); any extra energy appears as kinetic energy. Mass–energy equivalence E = mc2.
  • Annihilation is the reverse process: e− + e+ → 2γ.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q1(B)(vii)
Question 1(B)(vii)[1]
In semiconductor physics, what is the function of a rectifier?
  1. A rectifier is a p-n junction diode circuit that allows current to flow in one direction only (it conducts when forward biased and blocks when reverse biased).
    [½]
  2. Its function is to convert an alternating voltage/current (ac) into a unidirectional (pulsating dc) voltage/current.
    [½]
Answer
A rectifier converts alternating current into direct (unidirectional) current.

Concepts to recall

  • Half-wave rectifier: one diode, output in one half-cycle only. Full-wave: two diodes (centre-tapped) or a bridge of four, output in both half-cycles.
  • A filter (capacitor/inductor) after the rectifier smooths the pulsating dc.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q2
Question 2[2]
(i) A hollow sphere of radius R has a point charge q at its centre. Electric flux emanating from the sphere is X. How will the electric flux change, if at all, when
(a) radius of the sphere is doubled?
(b) charge q is replaced by an electric dipole?
OR
(ii) In case of an infinite line charge, how does intensity of electric field at a point change, if at all, when
(a) charge on it is doubled?
(b) distance of the point is halved?

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Gauss’s law: Φ = qenclosed/ε0 = X.
    [½]
  2. (a) The charge enclosed is still q, and the flux does not depend on the radius, so the flux is unchanged: it remains X.
    [½]
  3. (b) A dipole has charges +q and −q, so the net charge enclosed is zero.
    [½]
  4. Hence the net flux becomes zero (the lines that leave +q end on −q).
    [½]
Answer
(a) No change (it remains X). (b) The net flux becomes zero.
Common slip Saying the flux halves or falls to 1/4 because the surface area changes: the field falls as 1/r2 while the area grows as r2, so the product is constant.

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. Field of an infinite line charge of linear density λ: E = λ/(2πε0r) (from Gauss’s law with a coaxial cylinder), directed radially.
    [½]
  2. (a) Doubling the charge (λ → 2λ): E ∝ λ, so the field doubles.
    [½]
  3. (b) Halving the distance (r → r/2): E ∝ 1/r, so the field again doubles.
    [1]
Answer
(a) E becomes double. (b) E becomes double.
Common slip Using the point-charge law and saying the field becomes four times when the distance is halved: for a line charge it is only twice.

Concepts to recall

  • Gauss’s law: Φ = ∮E·dS = qenc/ε0; only the enclosed charge matters, not the size or shape of the surface.
  • Net charge of a dipole = 0, so the net flux through any closed surface enclosing it is 0 (though E itself is not zero).
  • Infinite line charge: E = λ/(2πε0r) ∝ 1/r. Point charge: ∝ 1/r2. Infinite plane sheet: independent of distance (σ/2ε0).
  • Cylindrical symmetry: flux only through the curved surface, E(2πrl) = λl/ε0.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q3
Question 3[2]
(i) What is meant by the statement: “Relative permittivity of water is 81”?
(ii) Can a body be given a charge of 2·2 × 10−19 C? Give a reason for your answer.
  1. (i) Relative permittivity εr = ε/ε0 = 81 means that the force between two given charges kept a fixed distance apart in water is 1/81 of the force between them in vacuum (air) at the same separation; equivalently the capacitance of a capacitor filled with water is 81 times that with vacuum between its plates.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Charge is quantised: q = ne, n an integer. Here n = (2·2 × 10−19)/(1·6 × 10−19) = 1·375, which is not an integer.
    [½]
  3. So a body cannot be given this charge, since it is not an integral multiple of the electronic charge.
    [½]
Answer
(i) The force between two charges in water is 1/81 of that in vacuum; (ii) No: 2·2 × 10−19 C = 1·375 e, not an integral multiple of e.
Common slip Rounding 1·375 to 1: charge cannot be a fraction of e (quarks aside, which are never free).

Concepts to recall

  • εr = ε/ε0 = K = Cmedium/Cvacuum; force in a medium F = F0/εr.
  • Quantisation of charge: q = ±ne, e = 1·6 × 10−19 C (from the constants table).
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q4
Question 4[2]
(i) What type of transformer is used in a mobile phone charger?
(ii) Why is the core of a transformer made of soft iron and not of steel?
  1. (i) A step-down transformer: it reduces the 220/230 V mains to a few volts (about 5 V) before the rectifier in the charger.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Soft iron has high permeability and low retentivity with a narrow hysteresis loop, so the hysteresis (energy) loss per cycle is small and it is easily magnetised and demagnetised by the ac. Steel, with high retentivity and a wide loop, would waste much energy as heat.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Step-down transformer. (ii) Soft iron has a small hysteresis loss (low retentivity, high permeability); steel would lose much energy as heat.

Concepts to recall

  • Transformer: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np = Ip/Is (ideal). Step-down: Ns < Np.
  • Energy losses: copper loss (I2R), eddy currents (laminated core), hysteresis (soft iron), flux leakage.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q5
Question 5[2]
(i) Name the electromagnetic radiation whose frequency is 1011 Hz.
(ii) What is the speed of radio waves in vacuum?
  1. (i) Wavelength λ = c/f = (3 × 108)/1011 = 0·003 m = 3 mm. This lies in the range of about 1 mm to 1 m (109 to 1012 Hz): microwaves.
    [1]
  2. (ii) All electromagnetic waves, radio waves included, travel in vacuum with the same speed c = 3 × 108 m s−1.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Microwaves (λ = 3 mm). (ii) 3 × 108 m s−1.

Concepts to recall

  • c = fλ; EM spectrum by frequency: radio < 109 Hz < microwave (109–1012) < infrared < visible (~1014) < UV < X-ray < gamma.
  • In vacuum c = 1/√(μ0ε0) for all EM waves; they differ in λ and f only.

Question 6[2]
Draw a labelled graph showing the variation in intensity of diffracted light with diffracting angle in a single slit Fraunhofer diffraction experiment.
  1. Axes labelled: intensity I on the vertical axis, diffraction angle θ (or sin θ) on the horizontal axis; a central maximum of greatest intensity at θ = 0 that is twice as wide as the others.
    [1]
  2. Subsidiary maxima on both sides, rapidly decreasing in intensity, separated by minima (zero intensity) marked at sin θ = ±λ/a, ±2λ/a, …
    [1]
Answer
Graph as drawn: a broad central maximum with weak, narrower secondary maxima on both sides.

SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q7
Question 7[2]
(i) Figure 1 above is the energy level diagram for hydrogen atom. Study the transitions shown and answer the following questions.
(a) State the type of spectrum obtained.
(b) Name the series of spectrum obtained.
OR
(ii) In a nuclear reactor, state the use of the following:
(a) Graphite rods
(b) Cadmium rods

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) The arrows point downwards: the electron falls from a higher level (n = 3 and n = 4) to a lower one and the atom emits photons of definite energies, 1·89 eV and 2·55 eV, so the spectrum is a line emission spectrum.
    [1]
  2. (b) Both transitions end on the second level (n = 2): 3 → 2 and 4 → 2. Transitions to n = 2 form the Balmer series (in the visible region).
    [1]
Answer
(a) Emission (line) spectrum. (b) Balmer series.
Marking note The printed level energies agree with −13·6/n2 (−3·4, −1·51, −0·85 eV).

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) Graphite rods act as the moderator: they slow down the fast neutrons produced in fission to thermal energies (about 0·025 eV) by elastic collisions, without absorbing them, so that they can cause further fission of 235U.
    [1]
  2. (b) Cadmium rods are the control rods: cadmium absorbs neutrons strongly, so pushing the rods in or out controls the number of neutrons and hence the rate of the chain reaction (they can also shut the reactor down).
    [1]
Answer
(a) Graphite: moderator (slows down fast neutrons). (b) Cadmium: control rods (absorb neutrons to control the chain reaction).

Concepts to recall

  • Emission: electron falls to a lower level, hν = Ei − Ef. Absorption: upward transitions.
  • Series by final level: Lyman n = 1 (UV), Balmer n = 2 (visible), Paschen n = 3 (IR), Brackett 4, Pfund 5. 1/λ = R(1/nf2 − 1/ni2).
  • Energy of level: En = −13·6/n2 eV.
  • Reactor parts: fuel (enriched U), moderator (graphite, heavy water), control rods (Cd, B), coolant, shielding.
  • Multiplication factor k: k = 1 steady (controlled), k > 1 growing (bomb-like), k < 1 dies out.
  • Thermal neutrons have much larger fission cross-section for 235U than fast ones.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q8
Question 8[2]
With reference to a semiconductor diode, define the following terms:
(i) depletion region
(ii) potential barrier
  1. (i) Depletion region: the thin region on both sides of a p-n junction, depleted of mobile charge carriers (electrons and holes diffuse across and recombine), leaving only immobile ionised acceptor and donor atoms.
    [1]
  2. (ii) Potential barrier: the potential difference (about 0·7 V for silicon, 0·3 V for germanium) developed across the depletion region by the fixed ions; it opposes further diffusion of majority carriers across the junction.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Region near the junction free of mobile charge carriers. (ii) The potential difference across this region that opposes the diffusion of majority carriers.

Concepts to recall

  • Formation: diffusion of electrons (n to p) and holes (p to n) → recombination → uncovered ions → built-in field E from n to p; diffusion current balances drift current at equilibrium.
  • Forward bias narrows the depletion region and lowers the barrier; reverse bias widens it and raises the barrier.

Question 9[3]
Obtain an expression for equivalent capacitance C when three capacitors C1, C2 and C3 are connected in series.
  1. Connect the three capacitors one after the other across a source of potential difference V (figure). The plate joined to the positive terminal acquires +Q; by induction the far plate of each capacitor takes −Q and the next plate +Q, so every capacitor carries the same charge Q (the inner plates are isolated and stay neutral overall).
    [1]
  2. The potential differences add: V = V1 + V2 + V3, with V1 = Q/C1, V2 = Q/C2, V3 = Q/C3.
    [1]
  3. If C is the equivalent capacitance, V = Q/C. So Q/C = Q/C1 + Q/C2 + Q/C3, and cancelling Q: 1/C = 1/C1 + 1/C2 + 1/C3.
    [1]
Answer
1/C = 1/C1 + 1/C2 + 1/C3, i.e. C = C1C2C3/(C1C2 + C2C3 + C3C1)
Common slip Starting with “the voltage is the same across each”: that is the parallel combination.

Concepts to recall

  • Series: same charge, voltages add, 1/Ceq = Σ1/Ci; Ceq is smaller than the smallest capacitance.
  • Parallel: same voltage, charges add, Ceq = ΣCi.
  • Definition C = Q/V; energy U = ½CV2 = Q2/2C.
SECTION C – 27 MARKSISC 2024 Physics Paper 1 (861A), Q10
Question 10[3]
(i) Figure 2 shows two batteries E1 and E2 having emfs of 18 V and 10 V and internal resistances of 1 Ω and 2 Ω respectively. W1, W2 and W3 are uniform metallic wires AC, FD and BE having resistance of 8 Ω, 6 Ω and 10 Ω respectively. B and E are midpoints of the wires W1 and W2. Using Kirchhoff’s laws of electrical circuits, calculate the current flowing in the wire W3.
OR
(ii) Figure 3 shows a potentiometer circuit in which the driver cell D has an emf of 6 V and internal resistance of 2 Ω. The potentiometer wire AB is 10 m long and has a resistance of 28 Ω. The series resistance Rs is of 20 Ω. Calculate: (a) the current IP flowing in the potentiometer wire AB when the jockey (J) does not touch the wire AB. (b) emf of the cell X if the balancing length AC is 4·5 m.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. B and E are midpoints, so AB = BC = 4 Ω and FE = ED = 3 Ω. Junction B (KCL): I3 = I1 + I2.
    [1]
  2. Loop A→B→E→F through E1 (KVL): 18 = I1(1 + 4 + 3) + 10I3, i.e. 8I1 + 10I3 = 18. Loop C→B→E→D through E2: 10 = I2(2 + 4 + 3) + 10I3, i.e. 9I2 + 10I3 = 10.
    [1]

  1. (a) With the jockey off the wire the primary circuit has the driver cell (internal resistance 2 Ω), Rs and the wire in series: IP = 6/(2 + 20 + 28) = 6/50 = 0·12 A.

Question 13[3]

sin isin r = Constant
where terms have their usual meaning. You must draw a neat and labelled diagram.
OR
(ii) In Young’s double slit experiment, show that:
β = λDd
where the terms have their usual meaning.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Let a plane wavefront AB be incident on a surface separating medium 1 (speed v1) from a denser medium 2 (speed v2 < v1). A reaches the surface first. Neat labelled diagram showing AB, the surface, the wavelet from A, the refracted wavefront CE, ∠i and ∠r.
    [1]
  2. In the time t that B takes to reach C, BC = v1t, while the secondary wavelet from A travels AE = v2t in medium 2. CE, the tangent from C to this wavelet, is the refracted wavefront.
    [1]
  3. Because rays are perpendicular to wavefronts, ∠BAC = i and ∠ACE = r. In right triangles ABC and AEC: sin i = BC/AC = v1t/AC and sin r = AE/AC = v2t/AC. Dividing, sin i/sin r = v1/v2 = constant (= 1n2 = n2/n1), which is Snell’s law.
    [1]
Answer
sin i/sin r = v1/v2 = constant (Snell’s law).
Common slip Marking i and r between a ray and the surface instead of the normal: the angles are those between the rays and the normal (equivalently between the wavefront and the surface).

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. S1 and S2 are coherent slits a distance d apart; the screen is at a distance D (>> d). For a point P at a distance y from the centre O, S1P2 = D2 + (y − d/2)2 and S2P2 = D2 + (y + d/2)2. Neat diagram.
    [1]
  2. Subtracting, S2P2 − S1P2 = 2yd, so path difference S2P − S1P = 2yd/(S2P + S1P) ≈ yd/D (since S1P + S2P ≈ 2D).
    [1]
  3. Bright fringe (constructive): yd/D = nλ, so yn = nλD/d. Fringe width β = yn+1 − yn = λD/d. (Dark fringes at y = (n + ½)λD/d give the same spacing.)
    [1]
Answer
β = λD/d.
Common slip Using the path difference d sinθ and then forgetting the small-angle step sinθ ≈ tanθ = y/D.

Concepts to recall

  • Huygens’ principle: every point on a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets; the new wavefront is the forward envelope (common tangent) of the wavelets after time t.

  • The same method proves the law of reflection (i = r) with equal speeds.
  • Conditions: coherent sources (from one wavefront), narrow slits, d << D. Constructive: path difference = nλ; destructive: (n + ½)λ.
  • β = λD/d is the same for all fringes; it grows with λ and D and falls with d (red fringes wider than blue).
  • Intensity I = 4I0 cos2(φ/2) with φ the phase difference.

    [1]
  2. (ii) Correct. “Graph of y against x” has y on the vertical axis, so 1/v on Y and 1/u on X. Since 1/v − 1/u = 1/f, the graph is a straight line of slope 1 with intercept 1/f on the 1/v axis.
    [1]

    [1]
