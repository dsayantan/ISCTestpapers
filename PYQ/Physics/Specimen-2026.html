SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(i)
Question 1(A)(i)[1]
Two point charges +50 nC and −50 nC separated by a distance of 1 mm are kept well inside a large sphere of radius 1 m. Electric flux emanating from the sphere is:
(a)50 × 10−12 V m
(b)50 × 10−9 V m
(c)50 × 10−6 V m
(d)Zero
  1. Gauss’s law: flux through a closed surface = (net charge enclosed)/ε0.
    [½]
  2. Net charge inside = +50 nC − 50 nC = 0, so the flux is zero (the distance between the charges and the radius of the sphere do not matter).
    [½]
Answer
(d) Zero
Common slip Using Φ = 50 nC/ε0 for one charge only.

Concepts to recall

  • Gauss’s law: Φ = qenc/ε0; only the charge inside counts.
  • A dipole has zero net charge, so the net flux through any closed surface round it is zero. Do not use the given numbers.
  • Unit of flux: V m (or N m2 C−1).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(ii)
Question 1(A)(ii)[1]
Three straight, parallel wires are coplanar and perpendicular to the plane of the page. The currents I1 and I3 are directed out of the page. If wire 3 experiences no force due to the currents I1 and I2, then the current in wire 2 is:
(a)I2 = 2I1 and directed into the page
(b)I2 = 0·5I1 and directed into the page
(c)I2 = 2I1 and directed out of the page
(d)I2 = 0·5I1 and directed out of the page
  1. Force on wire 3 is zero only if the net field at wire 3 is zero: B1 and B2 must be equal and opposite.
    [½]
  2. Wire 1 is at distance 2r, wire 2 at distance r from wire 3: μ0I1/(2π · 2r) = μ0I2/(2πr) ⇒ I2 = I1/2 = 0·5 I1.
    [½]
  3. Both wires lie on the same side of wire 3, so for the fields to oppose, the currents must be opposite: I1 is out, therefore I2 is into the page.
Answer
(b) I2 = 0·5 I1 and directed into the page
Marking note The printed CISCE key says (c), 2I1 out of the page. That cannot be right: a current out of the page in wire 2 produces a field at wire 3 in the same sense as wire 1’s, so nothing cancels, and wire 2 is the nearer wire so it needs the smaller current. The balance gives (b). Read from Figure 1, wire 2 is midway between wires 1 and 3. A candidate should write (b); expect the key’s (c) to be questioned.

Concepts to recall

  • Field of a long straight wire B = μ0I/(2πd); direction by the right-hand grip rule. Both wires lie on the same side of wire 3, so their fields at wire 3 are in the same sense when the currents are alike.
  • Zero force on a wire ⇔ zero resultant field at it. Compare the two fields: the nearer wire needs the smaller current.
  • Parallel currents attract, antiparallel repel: the same fact seen as a force.

Question 1(A)(iii)[1]
The variation of magnetic susceptibility (χ) with absolute temperature (T) for a diamagnetic substance is:
(a)
(b)
(c)
(d)
  1. Diamagnetic susceptibility is small, negative and independent of temperature (χ does not vary with T).
    [½]
  2. So the graph of |χ| against T is a straight line parallel to the T-axis: option (a). (b) and (c) are the 1/T curves of a paramagnet and a ferromagnet above its Curie point; (d) has χ ∝ T.

SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(iv)
Question 1(A)(iv)[1]
The wavelength λe of an electron and λp of a photon of the same energy E are related by:
(a)λp ∝ √λe
(b)λp ∝ 1/√λe
(c)λp ∝ λe2
(d)λp ∝ λe
  1. Photon: E = hc/λp, so λp = hc/E.
    [½]
  2. Electron (kinetic energy E): E = p2/2m = h2/(2mλe2), so E ∝ 1/λe2. Hence λp ∝ 1/E ∝ λe2.
    [½]
Answer
(c) λp ∝ λe2

Concepts to recall

  • Photon: E = hf = hc/λ, λ ∝ 1/E. Matter wave: λ = h/p = h/√(2mE), λ ∝ 1/√E.
  • Same energy gives different wavelengths because the photon’s E–λ relation is linear and the electron’s is quadratic.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(v)
Question 1(A)(v)[1]
In which of the following figures is the p-n diode forward biased?
(a)Only (P), (Q) and (S)
(b)Only (R)
(c)Only (P) and (R)
(d)Only (Q) and (S)
  1. A diode is forward biased when its anode (the triangle’s base) is at a higher potential than its cathode (the bar).
    [½]
  2. Reading the circuits as drawn: P anode +10 V, cathode +5 V; Q anode 10 V, cathode 0 V; R anode −5 V, cathode −12 V; S anode 0 V, cathode +5 V (reverse). On the figures exactly as printed P, Q and R all test forward and only S is reverse, a set that none of (a) to (d) lists. CISCE’s key accepts (b), Only (R).
    [½]
Answer
(b) Only (R), as CISCE’s key gives it
Marking note Doubt about the printed question. The figures as printed give forward bias in P, Q and R (anode at the higher potential in each), which matches no option; the key’s (b) can only be reached if the diode arrows in P and Q are read the other way. A candidate should write the key’s answer, (b).

Concepts to recall

  • Forward bias: p-side (anode) more positive than n-side (cathode); depletion layer narrows, current flows once the knee voltage (0·7 V Si, 0·3 V Ge) is passed.
  • In the symbol the arrow points from p to n, the direction of conventional current in forward bias.
  • Compare potentials at the two ends of the diode; the resistor only drops voltage after current flows.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(vi)
Question 1(A)(vi)[1]
When a beam of white light is incident on a prism, the prism:
(a)only disperses the incident light.
(b)only deviates the incident light.
(c)deviates as well as disperses incident light.
(d)neither deviates nor disperses incident light.
  1. Refraction through a prism bends every colour towards the base, so the beam is deviated.
    [½]
  2. The refractive index differs for each wavelength (n larger for violet), so the colours are bent by different amounts and spread into a spectrum: it also disperses the light.
    [½]
Answer
(c) deviates as well as disperses incident light.

Concepts to recall

  • Deviation δ = (n − 1)A for a thin prism; δviolet > δred because nviolet > nred.
  • Dispersion is the splitting of white light into its colours because n depends on wavelength (Cauchy).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(A)(vii)
Question 1(A)(vii)[1]
Assertion: The focal length of a convex mirror will increase if the mirror is placed in water.
Reason: The focal length of a convex mirror of radius R is equal to R/2.
(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.
(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.
(d)Assertion is false and Reason is true.
  1. Reason: for a spherical mirror f = R/2, a statement of geometry. It is true.
    [½]
  2. Reflection takes place at the surface and the refractive index of the surrounding medium does not enter f = R/2, so the focal length of a mirror does not change in water. The Assertion is false.
    [½]
Answer
(d) Assertion is false and Reason is true.

Concepts to recall

  • Mirror: f = R/2 in any medium (reflection, not refraction). Lens: f depends on nlens/nmedium, so a lens’s focal length does change in water.
  • Assertion–reason: judge each statement separately, then decide whether the Reason explains the Assertion.

Question 1(B)(i)[1]
Current I flowing through a metallic wire is gradually increased. Show graphically how the heating power (P) developed in it varies with the current (I).
  1. P = I2R, so P ∝ I2 (if R is taken constant).
    [½]
  2. Graph: a parabola through the origin with P on the vertical axis and I on the horizontal axis (as drawn).
    [½]
Answer
P–I graph is a parabola through the origin, P = I2R.
Marking note CISCE’s graph is a rising curve through the origin, the same parabola. In a real wire R rises as it heats, so the curve bends up more steeply; the ideal answer is the parabola.

Concepts to recall

  • Joule heating P = I2R = VI = V2/R.
  • Which quantity is on which axis decides the shape: P against I is a parabola; P against I2 would be a straight line.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(B)(ii)
Question 1(B)(ii)[1]
State one method to minimise flux loss in a transformer.
  1. Wind the primary and secondary coils one over the other (interleaving) so that nearly all the flux of the primary links the secondary; or use a core of high permeability (soft iron) which confines the flux.
    [1]
Answer
Winding primary and secondary on top of each other, on a high-permeability core.
Marking note Key: interleaved windings and a core of high permeability. A different loss (laminating the core) is a different answer and earns no mark here.

Concepts to recall

  • Flux leakage: not all of the primary’s flux links the secondary. Remedy: close coupling and a high-permeability closed core.
  • Do not confuse with eddy-current loss (laminated core), hysteresis loss (soft iron), copper loss (thick wire), humming (tight clamping).
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(B)(iii)
Question 1(B)(iii)[1]
Why are giant telescopes of reflecting type? Give any one scientific reason.
  1. Any one: images formed by a reflecting telescope are free from chromatic aberration (a mirror does not disperse light); or free from spherical aberration (a parabolic mirror); or brighter, because a large mirror can be made and supported from behind, so light-gathering power is high.
    [1]
Answer
A mirror gives images free from chromatic aberration (or spherical aberration / brighter images).
Marking note Any one of the three reasons printed in the key earns the mark.

Concepts to recall

  • Resolving power ∝ aperture D/λ; light gathered ∝ D2. A large lens sags under its weight and is supported only at its rim; a mirror is supported from behind.
  • Mirrors have no chromatic aberration and a parabolic mirror removes spherical aberration.

Question 1(B)(iv)[1]
Give any one example where a ray of light travelling from one optical medium to another travels undeviated.
  1. A ray incident along the normal to the interface (angle of incidence 0°) passes into the second medium without change of direction; or when the two media have the same refractive index, μ1 = μ2.
    [1]
Answer
A ray incident normally on the surface of separation (or μ1 = μ2).

Concepts to recall

  • Snell’s law: μ1 sin i = μ2 sin r. No deviation if i = 0 (so r = 0) or μ1 = μ2 (so r = i).
  • The speed changes on entering the second medium but the direction need not.
SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(B)(v)
Question 1(B)(v)[1]
Two charged particles having the same charge but different masses are passed through a potential difference V. When V is varied, the de Broglie wavelength (λ) of the particles varies as shown in the graphs below. Which graph is for the heavier particles and why?
  1. qV = p2/2m ⇒ λ = h/√(2mqV) = [h/√(2mq)] · (1/√V): a straight line through the origin of slope h/√(2mq).
    [½]
  2. For the same q, the slope is smaller for the larger mass, so line A (smaller slope) is for the heavier particles; B is the lighter.
    [½]
Answer
Graph A is for the heavier particles (smaller slope, since slope = h/√(2mq)).
Marking note CISCE’s key says graph B is for heavier particles because “slope of B > slope of A”, which reverses the relation it quotes. With slope = h/√(2mqV) written as h/√(2mq) per unit 1/√V, a larger slope means a smaller mass. The heavier particle is A. If a marker follows the printed key, the answer “B” may be credited; the physics gives A.

Concepts to recall

  • de Broglie: λ = h/p; for a charge accelerated through V: λ = h/√(2mqV).
  • Graph of λ against 1/√V is a straight line through the origin; slope ∝ 1/√m at fixed charge.
  • Heavier particle, same energy, larger momentum, shorter wavelength.

What happens when an electron collides with a positron?
  1. The electron and positron annihilate each other; their mass is converted into energy emitted as two gamma-ray photons (each 0·51 MeV when they are at rest).

They annihilate, producing gamma-ray photons.

SECTION A – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q1(B)(vii)
Question 1(B)(vii)[1]
What is the direction of flow of electrons in a solar cell?
  1. Photons create electron–hole pairs near the junction; the junction field sweeps electrons towards the n-type material and holes towards the p-type material. Inside the cell electrons flow to the n side; through an external load they return from the n side (negative terminal) to the p side.
    [1]
Answer
Electrons move towards the n-type material (and holes towards the p-type); through the external circuit electrons flow from n to p.
Marking note The key: “Electrons move towards the n-type material, and holes towards the p-type material”. Either statement of the direction earns the mark.

Concepts to recall

  • Solar cell: a p-n junction operated with no bias, which converts light into electricity. Emf is about 0·5–0·6 V for Si.
  • The depletion-layer field points from n to p, so it carries electrons to n and holes to p.
  • Conventional current in the load is from p to n, opposite to the electron flow.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q2
Question 2[2]
(i) Find the capacitance of the following combinations between terminals A and B. The area of each plate is A and the separation between the nearest two plates is d.
(a) Figure 2    (b) Figure 3
OR
(ii) The current (I)–voltage (V) graphs for a conductor are given at two different temperatures T1 and T2.
(a) At which temperature, T1 or T2, is the resistance higher?
(b) Which temperature, T1 or T2, is higher?

Solution to the first alternative

  1. (a) Plates 1 and 4 are joined to A, plates 2 and 3 to B. Plates 2 and 3 are at the same potential, so no capacitor forms between them; capacitors are formed by the pairs 1–2 and 3–4, each C = ε0A/d, and they are in parallel.
    [½]
  2. CAB = 2C = 2ε0A/d
    [½]
  3. (b) Plates 1 and 3 joined to A, plates 2 and 4 to B: the pairs 1–2, 2–3, 3–4 are three capacitors in parallel.
    [½]
  4. CAB = 3C = 3ε0A/d
    [½]
Answer
(a) 2ε0A/d; (b) 3ε0A/d

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) On an I–V graph the slope is I/V = 1/R = tanθ. Since θ1 > θ2, tanθ1 > tanθ2, so R at T1 is less than R at T2. The resistance is higher at T2.
    [1]
  2. (b) The resistance of a metallic conductor increases with temperature, and R at T2 is higher, so T2 is the higher temperature.
    [1]
Answer
(a) The resistance is higher at T2; (b) T2 is the higher temperature.
Marking note CISCE’s key writes “R = V/I = 1/tanθ” and calls the slope the conductance; the argument is the same.

Concepts to recall

  • Parallel-plate capacitor C = ε0A/d (air).
  • First find which plates are joined: plates at the same potential form no capacitor between them. Capacitors that share the same two terminals are in parallel and add: Ceq = ∑C.
  • Count the gaps that have opposite plates facing each other (alternate plates joined together gives n − 1 capacitors for n plates).
  • I–V graph: slope = 1/R = conductance (not R). A flatter line means larger resistance.
  • Metals: ρ = ρ0[1 + α(T − T0)], resistance rises with temperature (more frequent collisions); for semiconductors it would fall.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q3
Question 3[2]
The arrangement of an oxygen ion and two hydrogen ions in a water molecule is shown in Figure 4. Calculate the electric dipole moment of the water molecule. Express your answer in terms of e (charge on a hydrogen ion), l and θ.
  1. The O is −2e and each H is +e, at distance l from O. Treat the molecule as two dipoles O–H, each of moment p1 = p2 = e × l directed from the negative charge (O) to the positive charge (H).
    [1]
  2. Angle between them is θ; the resultant by the parallelogram law is along the bisector: p = 2p1 cos(θ/2) = 2el cos(θ/2), directed from O along the bisector towards the hydrogens.
    [1]
Answer
p = 2el cos(θ/2)
Common slip Writing 2el sin(θ/2) (using the angle from the wrong line) or forgetting that two bonds contribute.

Concepts to recall

  • Dipole moment p = (charge) × (separation), a vector from −q to +q.
  • Dipole moments add as vectors: for two equal moments at an angle θ, p = 2p1 cos(θ/2).
  • In water θ ≈ 104.5°, l ≈ 0·96 Å, giving p ≈ 6·2 × 10−30 C m.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q4
Question 4[2]
(i) Two cells of the same emf E but different internal resistances r1 and r2 are connected to an external resistance R as shown in Figure 5. The voltmeter V reads zero. Obtain an expression for R in terms of r1 and r2. (Assume the voltmeter V is of infinite resistance.)
OR
(ii) Ramesh performed an experiment to determine an unknown resistance R using the circuit shown in Figure 6. X is a resistance box and PQ is a 100 cm potentiometer wire. He closed the key and inserted X = 1 Ω, 2 Ω, … and recorded the null point (l) for different values of X.
(a) Identify the principle involved in calculating R.
(b) Write down a relation required to calculate the resistance R in terms of X and l.

Solution to the first alternative

  1. Cells in series aiding: total emf = 2E, total resistance = R + r1 + r2, so I = 2E/(R + r1 + r2).
    [1]
  2. The voltmeter reads the terminal voltage of cell 1: V = E − Ir1 = 0, so Ir1 = E.
    [½]
  3. 2Er1/(R + r1 + r2) = E ⇒ 2r1 = R + r1 + r2 ⇒ R = r1 − r2.
    [½]
Answer
R = r1 − r2

Solution to the OR alternative

  1. (a) The Wheatstone bridge principle (metre-bridge form): the galvanometer shows no deflection when the bridge is balanced.
    [1]
  2. (b) At the null point, X/R = l/(100 − l), so R = X(100 − l)/l.
    [1]
Answer
(a) Wheatstone bridge principle; (b) R = X(100 − l)/l
Marking note Equation as printed in the key. The null point is at balance, the galvanometer reading zero.

Concepts to recall

  • Terminal voltage V = E − Ir (cell delivering current). It is zero when Ir = E.
  • Series cells with like polarity: emfs and internal resistances add. An ideal voltmeter draws no current.
  • A valid R needs r1 > r2.
  • Balanced bridge: P/Q = R/S. Here the arms are X and R (upper), the wire lengths l and (100 − l) (lower); wire resistance is proportional to length.
  • Resistance box on the left gap, unknown on the right: X/R = l/(100 − l). Swapping the gaps inverts the formula, so read the figure.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q5
Question 5[2]
Two moving coil galvanometers G1 and G2 are identical except that they have 50 turns and 20 turns and resistances of 10 Ω and 1 Ω respectively. Perform the necessary calculations to check which one has the greater voltage sensitivity.
  1. Voltage sensitivity β = θ/V = NBA/(kR), where k is the torsion constant and R the resistance of the galvanometer.
    [1]
  2. Since B, A and k are the same: β(G1) = 50/10 = 5 (BA/k); β(G2) = 20/1 = 20 (BA/k). β(G2) is four times β(G1): G2 has the greater voltage sensitivity.
    [1]
Answer
G2 (20 against 5, in units of BA/k).
Marking note Printed key uses β = BAN/(CR) with C the torsion constant. Any equivalent symbol is accepted.

Concepts to recall

  • Current sensitivity Is = θ/I = NBA/k; voltage sensitivity Vs = θ/V = Is/R = NBA/(kR).
  • Doubling the turns doubles Is but also doubles R, so Vs is unchanged: the ratio N/R decides.
SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q6
Question 6[2]
Two similar convex lenses are made up of two different materials as shown in Figures 7 and 8. Find the number of images formed in the following set-ups: (i) Figure 7   (ii) Figure 8
  1. (i) The lens is cut along a plane through the axis direction, so the two parts lie side by side across the aperture. Each part has its own refractive index and hence its own focal length: each forms its own image. Two images.
    [1]
  2. (ii) The lens is cut across its thickness; every ray crosses both materials in turn. The pair acts as one lens (the two refractions combine), with a single focal length: one image.
    [1]
Answer
(i) Two images; (ii) one image.

Question 7[2]
Name the electromagnetic wave used in: (a) radars    (b) crystallography
  1. (a) Radars use microwaves (short wavelength, about 1 mm to 30 cm, which can be beamed and reflected from aircraft, ships and rain).

SECTION B – 14 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q8
Question 8[2]
In the photoelectric effect, the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectron is ‘a’ and the work function of the metal is W0. If the frequency of incident radiation is made ‘K’ times, calculate the change in the maximum kinetic energy of the ejected electron.
  1. Einstein’s equation: a1 = hν − W0 (= a) and for the new frequency a2 = hKν − W0.
    [1]
  2. Change = a2 − a1 = Khν − hν = (K − 1)hν. Since hν = a + W0, this is (K − 1)(a + W0).
    [1]
Answer
ΔKEmax = (K − 1)hν = (K − 1)(a + W0)
Common slip Answering (K − 1)a; the KE is not proportional to the frequency.
Marking note Key stops at (K − 1)hν. The question names a and W0 as the given quantities, so the form (K − 1)(a + W0) is the complete one; award full marks for either.

Concepts to recall

  • Einstein: KEmax = hν − W0 = eV0 (stopping potential).
  • The work function stays the same; only the photon energy changes. Increase in KE = increase in photon energy.
  • The KE does not become K times a: the work function is subtracted once.
SECTION C – 27 MARKSISC Specimen 2026 Physics Paper 1, Q9
Question 9[3]
Obtain an expression for the electric potential (V) at a point near a point charge ‘Q’.
  1. Potential at P (distance r from Q) = work done in bringing a unit positive test charge from infinity to P. At a point B at distance x from Q the force on the unit charge is F = (1/4πε0)(Q × 1)/x2, directed away from Q (for Q > 0).

The physically complete answer is the absorption one given here; the key’s threshold idea (E ≥ Eg acts, E < Eg does nothing) is the same, so give the marks to either reading. (i): key 3 and 1.

Concepts to recall

  • Band gap Eg: minimum energy to lift an electron from the valence to the conduction band. LED colour: Eg = hc/λ; threshold voltage ≈ Eg/e.
  • Photodiode / solar cell: a photon with E ≥ Eg creates an electron–hole pair, which the junction field separates; E < Eg does nothing. Cut-off wavelength λc = hc/Eg.
  • Light emitters (LEDs) use forward bias, light detectors (photodiodes) reverse bias, solar cells no bias.
