(i)1 mark

Let R be the relation on the set ℝ of real numbers defined by R = {(a, b) : a ≤ b2}. Then R is: (Understand)

(a)reflexive only(b)symmetric only(c)transitive only(d)neither reflexive, nor symmetric, nor transitive
Answer

Note In answering Multiple Choice Questions, candidates have to write either the correct option number or the explanation against it. Please note that only ONE correct answer should be written.

(d) neither reflexive, nor symmetric, nor transitive

Reflexive? Take a = ½: ½ ≤ ¼ is false, so (½, ½) ∉ R. Not reflexive.

Symmetric? (1, 2) ∈ R since 1 ≤ 4, but (2, 1) ∉ R since 2 > 1. Not symmetric.

Transitive? (3, 2) ∈ R (3 ≤ 4) and (2, 1.5) ∈ R (2 ≤ 2.25), but (3, 1.5) ∉ R since 3 > 2.25. Not transitive.

Type 1 (relation classified by a rule), S2027 Q1(i), B2023/B2024/B2026 Q1(i). NCERT Ex 1.1 has this R; the classic “fails all three” case.

(v)1 mark

Statement I: The function f(x) = x3 + 3x2 + 3x + 7 is strictly increasing on ℝ.

Statement II: If a function f is strictly increasing on an interval, then f′(x) > 0 at every point of that interval.

(Understand)
(a)Statement I is true and Statement II is false.(b)Statement I is false and Statement II is true.(c)Both the statements are true.(d)Both the statements are false.
Answer

(a) Statement I is true and Statement II is false.

I: f′(x) = 3x2 + 6x + 3 = 3(x + 1)2 ≥ 0, and it vanishes only at the single point x = −1, so f is strictly increasing on ℝ. True.

II: the same f is strictly increasing on ℝ, yet f′(−1) = 0. Strictly increasing implies only f′(x) ≥ 0. False.

Solve the differential equation 2xy dydx = x2 + y2. (Apply)

Answer

Question 93 marks

A small workshop makes wooden toy cars and toy trains. Each car needs 2 hours of cutting and 1 hour of finishing; each train needs 1 hour of cutting and 3 hours of finishing. On a day at most 10 hours of cutting and 15 hours of finishing are available. The profit is ₹30 on a car and ₹20 on a train, and everything made is sold.

(i)1 mark

Formulate the problem as a linear programming problem to maximise the profit. (Apply)

Answer

Let x cars and y trains be made per day.

Maximise Z = 30x + 20y subject to 2x + y ≤ 10 (cutting), x + 3y ≤ 15 (finishing), x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0.

(ii)1 mark

Draw the feasible region and find its corner points. (Apply)

Answer

Lines 2x + y = 10 through (5, 0), (0, 10) and x + 3y = 15 through (15, 0), (0, 5). Both inequalities contain the origin. They meet where x = 15 − 3y and 2(15 − 3y) + y = 10 ⇒ y = 4, x = 3.

Corner points: O(0, 0), A(5, 0), B(3, 4), C(0, 5).

(iii)1 mark

Find the number of cars and trains that maximise the profit, and the maximum profit. (Analyse)

Answer
Corner pointZ = 30x + 20y
O(0, 0)0
A(5, 0)150
B(3, 4)170 (maximum)
C(0, 5)100

The region is bounded, so the maximum exists at a corner. 3 cars and 4 trains a day give the maximum profit ₹170.

Types 166/163 (formulate and solve; corner-point table), S2027 Q9 slot, B2024 Q22, B2026 Q22. NCERT Ex 12.2 pattern.
Question 103 marks

If y = sin−1x√1 − x2, prove that (1 − x2) d2ydx2 − 3x dydx − y = 0. (Apply)

Answer

Given √1 − x2 y = sin−1 x.

Differentiating with respect to x:

√1 − x2 y′ + y · −x√1 − x2 = 1√1 − x2

Multiplying by √1 − x2: (1 − x2) y′ − xy = 1.

Differentiating again:

(1 − x2) y″ − 2x y′ − (y + x y′) = 0
⇒ (1 − x2) d2ydx2 − 3x dydx − y = 0. Hence proved.

Find the range of f. (Understand)

Answer

f(x) = (x − 2)2 + 1 ≥ 1, with equality at x = 2, and f takes every value above 1. Range = [1, ∞).

(ii)1 mark

Answer

No. f(0) = f(4) = 5, so f is not one-one (a horizontal line y = 5 cuts the graph twice); also f is not onto ℝ, since 0 is not in the range. A function has an inverse only if it is a bijection.

Answer

On [2, ∞), f is strictly increasing (f′(x) = 2x − 4 > 0 for x > 2) and its values fill [1, ∞), so g is a bijection.

y = (x − 2)2 + 1 ⇒ x − 2 = √y − 1 (x − 2 ≥ 0) ⇒ g−1(x) = 2 + √x − 1, x ≥ 1.
