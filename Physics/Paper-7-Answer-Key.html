Question 143 marks

Using Huygens' wave theory and a labelled diagram, show that the angle of reflection is equal to the angle of incidence. (Understand)

Answer
Plane wavefront AB incident on the reflecting surface; A′C is the reflected wavefront.
  1. AB is a plane wavefront incident obliquely on the plane reflecting surface; when its end A touches the surface the other end B is still BA′ = vt away from it. After time t, B reaches A′.
  2. In the same time the secondary wavelet from A spreads in the same medium to a radius AC = vt. The tangent A′C from A′ to this wavelet is the reflected wavefront.
  3. In the right-angled triangles ABA′ and A′CA: BA′ = AC (= vt), AA′ is common, and the angles at B and C are 90°. Hence the triangles are congruent and ∠BAA′ = ∠CA′A.
  4. These angles are the angles of incidence i and reflection r (each is the angle between a wavefront and the surface, equal to the angle between the corresponding ray and the normal), so i = r.

For a prism of refracting angle A, show that the refractive index of its material is n = sin[(A + δm)/2]/sin(A/2), where δm is the angle of minimum deviation. (Understand)

The curve rises steeply for light nuclei, is flat near 8·8 MeV for A = 50–80 (maximum at 56Fe) and falls slowly for heavy nuclei.
