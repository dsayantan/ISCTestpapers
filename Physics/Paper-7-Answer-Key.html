(ii)1 mark

Given below are two statements marked, Assertion and Reason. Read the two statements and choose the correct option.

Assertion: The kinetic energy of a charged particle describing a circular path in a uniform magnetic field changes continuously with time.
Reason: The velocity of such a particle changes continuously with time, because its direction keeps changing. (Analyse)

(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true, and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true, but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.(d)Assertion is false and Reason is true.
Answer

(d) Assertion is false and Reason is true.

The magnetic force is always perpendicular to the velocity, so it does no work and the speed, hence the kinetic energy, stays constant: the Assertion is false. The velocity (a vector) does change continuously in direction: the Reason is true.

PB24 Q1(A)(iv) (assertion that the kinetic energy of a charged particle in a circular path in a magnetic field does not remain constant, with the reason about its velocity), with the Assertion false and the Reason true, as set; MG01 (S27 1A(i)).
(iii)1 mark

A 50 Hz a.c. voltage source is applied to a series combination of a resistor of resistance P and a component B of reactance Y. The current leads the voltage by π/4. Which of the following is true? (Apply)

(a)B is an inductor and Y = P(b)B is a capacitor and Y = 2P(c)B is a capacitor and Y = P(d)B is an inductor and Y = 2P
Answer

(c) B is a capacitor and Y = P

The current leads the voltage only in a capacitive circuit, so B is a capacitor; tan φ = XC/R = Y/P = tan 45° = 1, so Y = P.

(iv)1 mark

Identify the correct statements about a photon. (Understand)

(P)  Its rest mass is zero and its energy is entirely kinetic.
(Q)  Its energy is hν and its momentum is h/λ.
(R)  Its speed in vacuum depends on its frequency.
(S)  It is deflected by an electric field.

(a)Both (Q) and (R)(b)Both (P) and (Q)(c)Both (R) and (S)(d)Both (P) and (S)
Answer

(b) Both (P) and (Q)

A photon has no rest mass, so all its energy hν is kinetic, and p = E/c = h/λ. Its speed in vacuum is c for every frequency (R is false) and, being neutral, it is not deflected by an electric field (S is false).

(vi)1 mark

Given below are two statements marked, Assertion and Reason. Read the two statements and choose the correct option.

Assertion: The graph of v against u for the real images formed by a concave mirror lies in the third quadrant.
Reason: For a real image formed by a concave mirror, both u and v are negative in the Cartesian sign convention. (Analyse)

(a)Both Assertion and Reason are true, and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.(b)Both Assertion and Reason are true, but Reason is not the correct explanation for Assertion.(c)Assertion is true and Reason is false.(d)Assertion is false and Reason is true.
Answer

(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true, and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.

Object and real image both lie in front of the mirror, i.e. on the side from which the light comes, so u < 0 and v < 0: the point (u, v) is in the third quadrant. The Reason is exactly why the Assertion holds.

(vii)1 mark

Monochromatic light is used in Young's double slit experiment and then in a single-slit diffraction experiment. Identify the correct statements. (Understand)

(P)  The bright fringes of the double-slit pattern are of equal width.
(Q)  In the single-slit pattern the central maximum is the widest and the brightest.
(R)  All the maxima of the single-slit pattern are of equal intensity.

(a)Both (Q) and (R)(b)Both (P) and (S)(c)Both (R) and (S)(d)Both (P) and (Q)

A parallel plate capacitor is charged to a potential V by a battery. The battery is then disconnected and the plates are pulled slightly farther apart. Does the capacitance increase, decrease or remain the same? Explain. (Understand)

Answer

The capacitance decreases: C = ε0A/d ∝ 1/d. (The charge Q stays constant because the battery is disconnected, so the potential difference V = Q/C rises.)

(ii)1 mark

A 24 V battery, a 6 Ω resistor and an ammeter are connected in series. The ammeter has a coil of resistance 180 Ω shunted by 20 Ω. Find the reading of the ammeter. (Apply)

Answer

Ammeter resistance = 180 × 20/(180 + 20) = 18 Ω; total resistance = 6 + 18 = 24 Ω; I = 24/24 = 1 A (the ammeter reads the total current).

(iii)1 mark

A single slit of width 0·10 mm gives a certain diffraction pattern with light of wavelength 400 nm. To what width must the slit be changed to obtain the same pattern with light of wavelength 600 nm? (Apply)

Answer

The positions of the minima are given by a sin θ = nλ, so the pattern is the same when λ/a is unchanged: a′ = a(λ′/λ) = 0·10 × 600/400 = 0·15 mm.

PB24 Q1(B)(i) (blue light of wavelength λ and slit width w; green light of 1·5λ: what width gives the original pattern back?) with numbers; WO07 (B26 Q15).
(iv)1 mark

How does the refractive index of the material of a prism depend on the angle of the prism and the angle of minimum deviation? (Recall)

Answer

n = sin[(A + δm)/2] / sin(A/2), where A is the angle of the prism and δm the angle of minimum deviation.

PB24 Q1(B)(ii) (refractive index of a prism's material in terms of the angle of deviation and the angle of the prism in minimum deviation); RO01 (B25 Q15).
(v)1 mark

An electron revolves in the second orbit of a hydrogen atom. Given that the radius of the first orbit is 0·53 × 10−10 m, calculate the de Broglie wavelength of the electron. (Apply)

Answer

r2 = 22 × 0·53 × 10−10 = 2·12 × 10−10 m. The orbit holds a whole number of waves: nλ = 2πr, so λ = 2πr2/2 = 6·66 × 10−10 m

PB24 Q1(B)(iii) (de Broglie wavelength of the electron in the first orbit, radius 0·53 × 10−10 m), taken for the second orbit; DN10 (B26 1A(iii)).
(vi)1 mark

State two conditions necessary for nuclear fusion. (Recall)

Answer

(1) A very high temperature (about 107–109 K), so that the nuclei have enough kinetic energy (about 0·4 MeV) to overcome their Coulomb repulsion; (2) a very high particle density and enough confinement time, so that collisions are frequent enough for the reaction to sustain itself.

PB24 Q8 (two necessary conditions for nuclear fusion); AN07.
(vii)1 mark

Name the diode that is used as a voltage regulator and state the bias in which it is operated. (Recall)

Answer

The Zener diode, operated in reverse bias, in its breakdown region (the voltage across it stays almost constant there).

Question 62 marks

Young's double-slit experiment is performed first in air and then in a liquid. The 6th bright fringe in the liquid is found to coincide with the 4th dark fringe in air. Find the refractive index of the liquid. (Apply)

Answer
  1. Fringe widths: βair = λD/d and βliquid = λD/μd = βair/μ.

Question 113 marks

Using the Biot–Savart law, obtain an expression for the magnetic field at the centre of a circular coil of radius R having N turns and carrying a current I. (Understand)

Answer

  2. For every element of the coil, dl is tangential and the line to the centre O is a radius, so θ = 90° and r = R. All the elements give dB in the same direction (along the axis, by the right-hand rule), so the contributions simply add.
    B = ∫ μ04πI dlR2 = μ0I4πR2(2πR) = μ0I2R
  3. For a coil of N turns: B = μ0NI/2R, along the axis, in the sense given by the right-hand rule.

Question 133 marks

An optics student uses a compound microscope. The examiner gives instructions and asks questions. State whether the response of the student in each case is correct or incorrect. Give a reason for your answer. (Evaluate)

(i)1 mark
Examiner: Increase the magnification produced by the objective lens.
Student: Decrease the distance between the object and the objective, keeping in mind that this distance must remain greater than the focal length of the objective.
Answer

Correct. mo = vo/uo = fo/(fo − uo) in magnitude: it grows as uo decreases towards fo (the image moves farther away), and the object must stay outside fo for the image to be real.

Answer

Incorrect. The resolving power of a microscope is proportional to 1/λ, so light of a shorter wavelength must be used.

Student: f1 = 1·5 cm as the objective and f2 = 80 cm as the eyepiece.
Answer

Incorrect. Both lenses of a compound microscope must have short focal lengths, the objective the shorter (M ≈ (L/fo)(D/fe)). The objective should be f1 = 1·5 cm and the eyepiece f3 = 5 cm; an 80 cm eyepiece would give a magnification of only 1 + 25/80 ≈ 1·3.

Question 143 marks

Using Huygens' wave theory and a labelled diagram, show that when a plane wave is refracted from a medium of refractive index n1 into one of refractive index n2, n1 sin i = n2 sin r. (Understand)

Answer
Plane wavefront AB incident on the interface; A′C is the refracted wavefront (here v2 < v1).
  1. AB is a plane wavefront in medium 1 (speed v1). When its end A reaches the interface, B is still BA′ = v1t away; B reaches A′ after a time t.
  2. In the same time t the secondary wavelet from A spreads in medium 2 to a radius AC = v2t. The tangent A′C from A′ to this wavelet is the refracted wavefront.
  3. In the right-angled triangles ABA′ and ACA′ (right angles at B and C), the angle at A in the first is i and the angle at A′ in the second is r:
    sin i = BA′AA′ = v1tAA′,   sin r = ACAA′ = v2tAA′
  4. Dividing, sin i/sin r = v1/v2. With n = c/v, v1/v2 = n2/n1, so n1 sin i = n2 sin r.

What are eddy currents? Write one use of them. (Recall)

Answer

Eddy currents are the currents induced in the body of a piece of metal (as closed circulating paths) when the magnetic flux through it changes, whether because the metal moves in a field or because the field is varied; by Lenz's law they oppose the change that produces them.

Use (any one): induction furnace (to melt metals), electromagnetic braking in trains, damping in a moving-coil galvanometer, or induction cooktops.
