Answer the following questions briefly:

Why can two equipotential surfaces never intersect? (Understand)

Answer

At a point of intersection the potential would have two different values, and the field (normal to the surface) two directions — which is impossible.

Equipotentials (ES17/ES20): C22 (A–R), S24 Q3, B26 1B(iii).

(iii)1 mark

Name the phenomenon on which an optical fibre works, and state the two conditions for it. (Recall)

Answer

Total internal reflection: light must travel from the denser to the rarer medium, and the angle of incidence must exceed the critical angle.

TIR (RO20): B20 1B(vi), B19 1B(vi), S24 Q6(i), C35.
(iv)1 mark

The de Broglie wavelength of an electron is λ. What will it become if the kinetic energy of the electron is made four times as large? (Apply)

Answer

λ = h/√(2mK) ∝ 1/√K, so it becomes λ/2.

(v)1 mark

Why must the neutrons produced in the fission of uranium-235 be slowed down in a reactor? (Understand)

Answer

Slow (thermal) neutrons are far more likely to be captured by 235U and cause fission than fast ones, so slowing them with a moderator keeps the chain reaction going.

Reactor (AN13): B24 Q7(ii) (graphite), B25 Q19(ii)(a) (Apsara), S23 Q16, C84.

What happens to the width of the depletion layer of a p-n junction when it is forward biased? (Recall)

Answer

It decreases: the applied voltage opposes the barrier and pushes majority carriers into the junction.

Question 72 marks
(i)1 mark

Distinguish between an intrinsic and an extrinsic semiconductor. (Understand)

Answer

Intrinsic: pure, ne = nh, conductivity low. Extrinsic: doped with a trivalent or pentavalent impurity, so one kind of carrier dominates and conductivity is much higher.

An n-type semiconductor has a large number of free electrons. Is it negatively charged? Explain. (Understand)

No. Each free electron leaves behind a positive donor ion, so the crystal as a whole is neutral.

Give one reason why large research telescopes use a concave mirror as the objective. (Recall)
