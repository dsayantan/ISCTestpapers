How much work is done in moving a charge of 5 μC through 10 cm along the equatorial line of an electric dipole? (Understand)

What is meant by motional emf? (Recall)

Answer

The emf induced across the ends of a conductor when it moves through a magnetic field so as to cut the field lines; for a rod of length l moving with velocity v perpendicular to B, ε = Blv.

A mirror objective has no chromatic aberration (any one of: a parabolic mirror is free from spherical aberration; a large-aperture mirror is easier to make and support, so the image is brighter).

(vi)1 mark

Why is a photodiode operated in reverse bias? (Understand)

Answer

In reverse bias the current is the small minority-carrier current, so the extra carriers produced by light cause a large fractional change in current; the change is easily detected. (In forward bias the large majority current would swamp it.)

Question 72 marks
(i)1 mark

What is meant by the depletion region of a p-n junction? (Recall)

Answer

The thin region on either side of the junction which has no free charge carriers (electrons and holes have diffused across and recombined) and contains only immobile ions.

(ii)1 mark

How is the potential barrier across the junction formed? (Understand)

Answer

The uncovered immobile ions — negative acceptor ions on the p-side and positive donor ions on the n-side — set up an electric field from n to p. This field opposes further diffusion, giving a potential difference V0 (the barrier).

Depletion region and the barrier potential.
S23 Q8 was set near-verbatim as B24 Q8; also B20 Q22(a)(i), B26 Q20(iii), C38.

Question 113 marks

Using the Biot–Savart law, obtain an expression for the magnetic field at the centre of a circular coil of radius R having N turns and carrying a current I. (Understand)

State Bohr’s postulates for the circular motion of the electron in a hydrogen atom and the quantisation of its angular momentum. Using them, obtain an expression for the radius of the nth orbit. (Apply)

Answer
Bohr’s first postulate (centripetal force is the Coulomb force): mv2/r = ke2/r2 ... (1)½
Quantisation of angular momentum: mvr = nh/2π ⇒ v = nh/2πmr ... (2)½
From (1): mv2r = ke2. Put v from (2): m(nh/2πmr)2r = ke21
rn = n2h24π2mke2 = ε0n2h2πme2, so rn ∝ n21

Prove that 1 u = 931·5 MeV, where the terms have their usual meaning. (Apply)
